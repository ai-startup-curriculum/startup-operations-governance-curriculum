# 6. The RIF / layoff playbook under WARN

> Board authorisation, impact analysis, WARN Act notice (federal 60-day plus state mini-WARN), the OWBPA disclosure schedule for the age-40+ pool, the executive-communications choreography, the severance and outplacement package, and the anti-signalling operating norms during and after a layoff.

## Motivation

An individual involuntary termination (chapter 05) is a personnel decision. A **Reduction in Force** — a coordinated separation of a meaningful fraction of the workforce — is a corporate action that touches securities disclosure obligations, federal and state notice statutes, the compensation committee, the board, and the corporation's reputation with its remaining employees, its investors, its customers, and the press. Every element of the individual-termination playbook still applies, and a second layer of legal and communications obligations comes on top.

The failure modes are consequential:

- The corporation counts headcount against federal WARN Act thresholds only, misses that its California operations trigger Cal-WARN at a lower threshold, and hits the impacted employees with same-day notice; the state Department of Labor Standards Enforcement (DLSE) or the state attorney general's office opens an action, and back pay plus penalties is owed to each employee for the notice period the corporation skipped.
- The corporation offers a group severance package with a 5-day consideration period; more than half the affected employees are age 40+; the OWBPA-required 45-day consideration period was skipped; the releases are challenged and defeated; the corporation has paid severance and cannot enforce the release.
- The all-hands announcement lands 24 hours *after* individual notice conversations were supposed to start but the manager schedule is behind; the news breaks on social media; affected employees see it before they hear from their manager; the reputational hit is real.
- The corporation misjudges the impact analysis, the demographic slice shows a 60% concentration of the layoff on employees over 50, and the pattern surfaces later in an EEOC pattern-and-practice inquiry.
- The corporation cuts too shallow, needs to run a second RIF within 90 days, and both markets (talent and capital) read the second cut as evidence of runaway losses.

This chapter builds the operating playbook that turns a RIF from a chaotic emergency into a controlled operation — legally clean, communicated with dignity, and cut deep enough that a second cut is not needed six months later.

> **Legal-not-legal-advice caveat.** The WARN Act, state mini-WARN statutes, OWBPA, and the state layer of severance and release requirements are dense and fact-specific. Every RIF should be executed with employment counsel review and, for material RIFs, with securities counsel review on Reg-FD / MNPI dimensions. See [resources.md](./resources.md) for primary-source citations.

## The seven operating decisions

1. **Board authorisation.** Who approves the RIF, and what is the authorisation trail.
2. **Impact analysis.** Function-by-function / level-by-level / demographic-by-demographic view of who is affected.
3. **WARN Act compliance.** Federal WARN, state mini-WARN, and the notice mechanics.
4. **OWBPA disclosure.** The age-40+ pool and the group-release disclosure schedule.
5. **Executive-communications choreography.** All-hands, individual conversations, and the 24–48 hour delivery window.
6. **The severance / benefits / outplacement package.**
7. **Anti-signalling operating norms** during and after.

## Decision 1 — Board authorisation

A RIF of any material size is a decision that touches the board.

**Formal authorisation.**

- **CEO-authored RIF memo** to the board — the business rationale (revenue miss, extended runway objective, strategic pivot, cost-reduction commitment tied to fundraising), the impact analysis (percentage of workforce, cost savings, function-by-function view), the recommended package, the WARN Act analysis, the communications plan, and the estimated one-time cash impact.
- **Board consent or minute** — for a material RIF, either an explicit board resolution authorising the RIF or a documented board discussion in the minutes of a board meeting. The compensation committee is typically the primary board committee involved (severance authority for executives is a comp-committee matter — see [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)); the full board is typically informed and often asked to consent for materially large RIFs.
- **Audit committee brief** — if the corporation is nearing an IPO or is public, the audit committee is briefed on the accounting and disclosure consequences (see the securities section below).

**Materiality threshold.** There is no bright-line rule for what triggers board authorisation vs. head-of-people / CEO discretion. Sensible starting points:

- Under ~5% of workforce and no C-level exit: CEO discretion with head-of-people execution.
- 5–15% of workforce or a C-level involvement: board notice; compensation committee formal involvement.
- More than 15% or any impact that would be a material 8-K disclosure for a public company: full-board consent.

**Securities / disclosure interaction (public and near-public companies).** A material RIF may trigger Item 2.05 (Costs Associated with Exit or Disposal Activities) or Item 5.02 (Departure of Directors or Principal Officers) of Form 8-K. Public-company RIFs are executed with securities counsel and IR (investor relations) in the room from day one. Reg FD requires that any RIF communication that reaches broad audiences be handled without selective disclosure of material non-public information — the sequencing of internal comms and external comms matters.

Even at pre-IPO stages, a material RIF is typically communicated to the lead investor and the board observers before the general employee announcement — not because it is required, but because the alternative (lead investor reads about it on LinkedIn) damages the relationship for the next round.

## Decision 2 — Impact analysis

The impact analysis is the internal document that answers "who is affected, why, and does the pattern hold up." It is authored jointly by the CEO, CFO, head of people, and general counsel, and it is the artifact that the RIF is executed from.

**Standard slices:**

- **By function.** Percentage cut per function, absolute headcount per function, function leader's justification for that cut.
- **By level.** Distribution of affected employees across levels — is the cut concentrated at IC levels, at management levels, evenly distributed?
- **By tenure.** Distribution across tenure buckets (0–1 year, 1–3 years, 3+ years).
- **By work location.** Distribution across states, cities, remote / hybrid / in-office. Critical for WARN Act analysis (which turns on "single site of employment").
- **By demographic slice.** Distribution across gender, race / ethnicity, age (40+), disability, and other protected categories, compared to the workforce baseline. Not a quota mechanism — a pattern-detection mechanism that surfaces disparate-impact risk before the RIF is executed. If the layoff skews significantly against a protected category, either the selection criteria need to be revisited or the corporation needs a documented, non-discriminatory business justification for the pattern that would survive an EEOC pattern-and-practice inquiry.
- **By cost savings.** Total cash savings (salary + benefits) per affected employee, cumulative annualised savings, one-time severance cost, net savings in the first year.

**Selection criteria documentation.** For every affected employee, the reason for selection is documented against the selection framework the corporation adopted. Two mainstream selection-criteria patterns:

- **Position-elimination criteria.** The affected role is eliminated; whoever holds the role is affected. Straightforward to document.
- **Employee-selection criteria.** More than one employee holds the same role; the corporation selects which to eliminate. Selection must be based on documented, non-discriminatory criteria (performance rating, tenure, cross-functional utility, skill mix required for the go-forward organisation). Selection criteria authored *after* the individual selections are made are legally suspect; the criteria should be authored, documented, and applied consistently before names are placed against them.

**HRBP / general counsel review.** The impact analysis is reviewed by the HRBP (for demographic-slice patterns and selection-criteria defensibility) and general counsel or employment counsel (for WARN and OWBPA compliance, and for pattern-and-practice risk).

## Decision 3 — WARN Act compliance

The **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (WARN Act, 29 U.S.C. §§ 2101–2109; DOL regulations at 20 C.F.R. Part 639) is the federal notice statute for large layoffs.

**Federal WARN — the coverage threshold.** WARN applies to an employer that has **100 or more employees**, excluding part-time employees who work fewer than 20 hours per week or who have been employed for fewer than 6 of the last 12 months. (Alternative test: 100 or more employees including part-timers, that work in aggregate at least 4,000 hours per week exclusive of overtime.) An employer under this threshold is not subject to federal WARN.

**Federal WARN — the notice triggers.** Federal WARN requires at least **60 calendar days** advance written notice of:

- **A plant closing** — a permanent or temporary shutdown of a single site of employment (or one or more facilities / operating units within a single site) that results in an employment loss for **50 or more full-time employees** during any 30-day period.
- **A mass layoff** — a reduction in force at a single site of employment during any 30-day period that results in an employment loss for either **500 or more employees**, or **50–499 employees if those employees constitute at least 33% of the active full-time workforce** at the single site.

"Employment loss" includes a termination other than for cause, a layoff of more than 6 months, or a reduction in hours of work of more than 50% during each month of any 6-month period.

**Aggregation.** Individual employment losses that occur within any **90-day period** are aggregated for WARN purposes, unless the employer can show the losses are the result of separate and distinct causes. This prevents an employer from breaking a single mass layoff into a series of sub-50-person cuts to escape notice.

**Notice recipients.** The 60-day notice must be delivered to:

- Each affected employee (or their union representative if unionised).
- The state dislocated-worker unit (usually the state Department of Labor's rapid-response coordinator).
- The chief elected official of the local government unit in which the site is located.

**Notice content.** Written notice includes the name and address of the affected employment site, whether the planned action is expected to be permanent or temporary, the expected date of the first separation, the schedule of subsequent separations if any, the job titles of positions to be affected, and the number of affected employees in each classification. The notice to the state and local officials must additionally include the name of the union or bargaining representative if any, and the name and phone number of a corporate contact for further information.

**Statutory exceptions.** Federal WARN provides three narrow exceptions that reduce (but do not eliminate) the 60-day notice period:

1. **Faltering company exception** — for plant closings only; the employer was actively seeking capital or business at the time notice would have been required, and giving notice would have precluded the employer from obtaining that capital or business.
2. **Unforeseeable business circumstances exception** — the closing or mass layoff is caused by business circumstances that were not reasonably foreseeable at the time the 60-day notice would have been required (e.g., a sudden and dramatic economic downturn, a principal customer cancelling a major contract, an unexpected regulatory change).
3. **Natural disaster exception** — the closing or mass layoff is a direct result of a natural disaster.

An exception reduces the notice period but does not eliminate the notice obligation — the employer must still give **as much notice as is practicable** and, in the notice, must give a brief statement of the reason for reducing the notification period.

**Penalties.** An employer that violates the WARN Act notice provisions is liable to each affected employee for **back pay and benefits for each day of the violation**, up to 60 days, plus an additional **civil penalty of up to $500 per day** payable to the local government unit (which may be avoided if the employer pays each affected employee within 3 weeks after the closing / layoff). Employees may also recover attorneys' fees.

**State mini-WARN — the layer on top of federal.** Several states have their own WARN-analogue statutes, and in most cases the state statute is *stricter* than the federal WARN Act. The most important:

- **California WARN Act** (Cal. Labor Code §§ 1400–1408) — covers "covered establishments" with 75+ employees currently or in the preceding 12 months; requires **60-day notice** for a "mass layoff" (50+ employees terminated in any 30-day period, with no 33% threshold), a plant relocation, or a plant termination. Cal-WARN does not have the same "unforeseeable business circumstances" exception as federal WARN (the exception was narrowed by the California courts in *International Brotherhood of Boilermakers v. NASSCO Holdings Inc.* and related decisions). Notice to affected employees, California Employment Development Department (EDD), and local officials. <!-- needs-research: verify current Cal-WARN judicial interpretation of "unforeseeable business circumstances" and whether the pandemic-era emergency amendments have expired or been made permanent. -->
- **New York WARN Act** (NY Labor Law §§ 860–860-i) — covers employers with **50+ employees** (excluding part-time employees); requires **90-day notice** (not 60) for a mass layoff (25+ employees representing at least 33%, or 250+ regardless of percentage), plant closing (25+), relocation (25+ employees moving 50+ miles), or a covered reduction in work hours. Stricter than federal on both the employer-size threshold and the notice period.
- **New Jersey WARN Act / Millville Dallas Airmotive Plant Job Loss Notification Act** (N.J.S.A. §§ 34:21-1 to 34:21-7) — covers employers with **100+ employees** (any employee, not just full-time); requires **90-day notice** for a mass layoff (50+ full-time or part-time employees, no percentage threshold), plant closing, or transfer. New Jersey uniquely **mandates severance** — 1 week of pay per year of service — for a covered event, and the mandated severance is doubled if the employer fails to give adequate notice.
- **Wisconsin Business Closing and Mass Layoff Law** (Wis. Stat. § 109.07) — covers employers with **50+ employees**; requires **60-day notice** for a business closing (25+) or mass layoff (25+ or 25% of workforce, whichever is greater, at a single site).
- **Illinois WARN Act** (820 ILCS 65) — covers employers with **75+ employees**; requires **60-day notice** for a plant closing (25+) or mass layoff (33% or 250+).
- **Other states with mini-WARN statutes** — Iowa, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Hampshire, Ohio, Oregon, Tennessee, Vermont, and others have variants; scope, thresholds, and notice periods differ. <!-- needs-research: verify the current-year scope of each state mini-WARN before quoting specific thresholds and notice periods; the state landscape includes recent amendments and is worth confirming against each state Department of Labor's current guidance. -->

**Practical consequence.** A federally-non-triggering RIF (say, 45 employees at a 200-person company) does not trigger federal WARN — but a 45-person California cut at a 200-person company that has 90 employees in California *may* trigger Cal-WARN even though the count would not trigger federal. Every RIF's WARN analysis must go state-by-state, not federal-only.

**Sequencing.** Because notice must precede termination by 60 days (federal) or 90 days (NY / NJ), the 60- or 90-day notice window is the *first* fixed point on the RIF calendar. Backing off from a target execution date, the notice must go out 60 (or 90) days before the intended employment loss. Working forward from the corporation's decision to RIF, the earliest realistic execution is 60–90 days out — unless the corporation is invoking one of the WARN exceptions or is paying "pay in lieu of notice" and terminating immediately (in which case the corporation must still deliver notice to state / local officials and be prepared to defend the exception).

## Decision 4 — OWBPA disclosure for the age-40+ pool

Every group termination that offers a release of claims to affected employees triggers **OWBPA** (see [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) for the individual-release version).

The relevant additions for a group termination:

- **45-day consideration period.** If the release is offered as part of "an exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees," each employee age 40+ has at least **45 days** to consider the agreement (versus 21 days for an individual release).
- **7-day revocation period** applies as always.
- **OWBPA disclosure schedule.** The employer must inform each age-40+ affected employee, in writing, of:
  1. The class, unit, or group of individuals covered by the program;
  2. The eligibility factors for the program;
  3. Any applicable time limits; and
  4. The job titles and ages of all individuals eligible or selected for the program, and the ages of all individuals in the same job classification or organizational unit who are not eligible or selected for the program (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)(H)).

**Practical execution.** The corporation prepares a "OWBPA disclosure schedule" as an appendix to the severance-and-release agreement offered to age-40+ affected employees. The schedule is typically a two-column list — job title / age of individuals selected, and job title / age of individuals in the same classification / organizational unit not selected. The definition of "organizational unit" is fact-specific; typically it is the smallest business unit within which the selection decisions were made (a team, a function, a department). Employment counsel should review the disclosure schedule before it goes out.

**Consequence of omission.** A severance agreement that omits the OWBPA disclosure — or that provides less than a 45-day consideration period on a group release — is not enforceable as a waiver of ADEA claims (see e.g. *Oubre v. Entergy Operations, Inc.*, 522 U.S. 422 (1998), on the strict-compliance rule for OWBPA waivers). The corporation has paid severance and cannot invoke the release against an ADEA claim.

## Decision 5 — Executive-communications choreography

The delivery-day choreography is the second-highest-stakes moment of the RIF, after the impact-analysis decision itself. Getting it right is a communications, logistics, and empathy exercise.

**The T-minus timeline (mainstream pattern).**

- **T-7 days:** Board / compensation committee confirms authorisation. Legal review of individual notice packets and severance-and-release agreements complete. HRBP, IT, and finance operations aligned on the day-of runbook.
- **T-2 to T-1 days:** Manager training. Every manager who will deliver an individual notice conversation is given the script, the runbook, the answers to expected questions, and access to HRBP support on-day.
- **T-1 day evening or T-day morning:** Executive team confirms final list; any last-minute changes documented.
- **T-day morning:**
  - **All-hands or CEO video message** to the entire workforce announcing the RIF — the business rationale, the scope, the process from here. Timed *before* individual notice conversations begin, so no affected employee learns from the all-hands after they have received individual notice, and no unaffected employee finds out from social media.
  - **Manager 1:1s** with affected employees begin immediately after the all-hands, scheduled in a coordinated 60–90 minute window. Each conversation follows the [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) delivery-day script: manager + HRBP; 15 minutes; clear communication of the decision, the package, the timeline, next steps.
  - **Unaffected employees** receive follow-up communication from their manager within a few hours confirming they are unaffected. The corporation-wide announcement that named the number of affected employees left a gap ("am I one of them?") that is closed by direct manager confirmation.
- **T-day + 1 to T-day + 3:** Q&A sessions for the remaining organisation; HRBP office hours for affected and unaffected employees; individual follow-ups for questions about severance, COBRA, and equity.
- **T-day + 7 to T-day + 45:** Signed severance-and-release agreements returned within the applicable consideration window (45 days for age-40+ group; 21 days for age-40+ single-employee; shorter for under-40); revocation windows observed; payments released on the schedule the packages specify.

**Delivery mode.**

- **In-person** for employees co-located in the office. Video call for remote employees, with the manager and HRBP both on the call.
- **Never delivered via email as the first touch.** Every affected employee gets a scheduled 1:1 with a real person for the initial notice.
- **Sequencing matters.** Affected employees are notified in a coordinated window. Once notice begins, information will leak — Slack, LinkedIn, personal networks — within the hour. The tighter the notification window, the more the corporation can control the sequence.

**Comms to remaining employees.** The corporation-wide message the same day covers: what happened (the RIF, the business reason), what is next (the go-forward plan, the strategic reset), what is not changing (values, product direction, customer commitments), and what leadership is doing to support affected colleagues (severance, outplacement, references). Follow-up all-hands within 1–2 weeks — the remaining employees are watching leadership signal and cadence closely, and any drop-off is read as a further signal of trouble.

**Comms to customers, partners, investors.** For public companies, coordinated with IR / PR under Reg FD. For private companies, the CEO reaches key customers and partners the same day or next day. For all companies, the lead investors and board should be informed before the all-hands announcement.

## Decision 6 — The severance / benefits / outplacement package

The RIF severance package should be **at least as generous as the corporation's default individual-termination severance policy** from [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md), and typically more so — the affected employees did nothing wrong, and the package is one of the primary ways the corporation demonstrates continued respect for the departing group.

**Package elements (mainstream Series-B+ pattern).**

- **Severance pay.** 2–4 weeks of base pay per year of service, minimum 8–12 weeks. Executive severance per [chapter 07](./07-executive-team-offboarding.md) and [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).
- **Health-plan continuation.** Corporation-subsidised COBRA premium for the length of the severance period, sometimes longer.
- **Equity treatment.** Post-termination exercise window per the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) — some corporations offer an *extended* PTE window for RIF terminations (12–24 months instead of the standard 90 days), which is a material and appreciated benefit. Vested acceleration is unusual outside of executive severance; document any acceleration in the impact-analysis approval.
- **PTO payout.** Per corporation policy and applicable state law (see [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md)).
- **Bonus payment.** Prorated bonus if the RIF lands after the bonus performance period but before payment.
- **Outplacement services.** A common addition — a 3–6 month engagement with a firm like [Randstad RiseSmart](https://www.randstadrisesmart.com/), [Lee Hecht Harrison (LHH)](https://www.lhh.com/), [INTOO](https://www.intoo.com/us/), [Careerminds](https://careerminds.com/), or [Right Management](https://www.right.com/) that provides the affected employee with a career coach, resume review, interview preparation, and job-search resources. Typical cost: $500–$3,000 per affected employee for a group programme. High reputational-return investment.
- **References.** A written commitment that the corporation will provide standard references for affected employees; often paired with a manager-authored recommendation on LinkedIn if warranted.
- **Alumni / re-hire signalling.** For strong performers among the affected, an explicit alumni-programme invitation and a "you would be welcomed back" signal per [chapter 08](./08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md).

**Package structure.** A defensible package is uniform across the affected group (adjusted for role and tenure) with narrow, documented exceptions. A package that varies substantially across similarly-situated affected employees on subjective bases is exposure to disparate-treatment claims.

## Decision 7 — Anti-signalling operating norms

A RIF is a signal — to the affected employees, to the remaining team, to customers, to investors, to the market. The anti-signalling norms are about controlling what signal the corporation *wants* to send and preventing the signals it does not want.

**During:**

- **The CEO leads the all-hands and every subsequent follow-up.** The CEO is visible, present, and personally accountable. Delegating the announcement to the head of people or a corporate email is read as executive absence and is remembered.
- **The head of people, general counsel, and CFO are visible in office hours.** Affected employees have questions about severance, taxes, equity, benefits — the leadership team is available, not hiding behind an FAQ.
- **Managers are trained and supported.** Managers who are asked to deliver difficult conversations without preparation deliver them badly, and the failures propagate. Manager training on T-2 / T-1 is not optional.
- **No hiring freeze theater.** If the corporation is going to keep hiring for critical roles (typically it should — a RIF is a rebalance, not a stop), say so in the announcement. Silent hiring after a public RIF is read as bad faith.
- **No same-week promotions or executive comp announcements.** The optics of an executive raise in the same week as a layoff are catastrophic; a promotion cycle in the same week reads as tone-deaf. Delay non-critical positive-comp events by two to four weeks.

**After:**

- **Cadenced follow-through.** All-hands every 1–2 weeks for the following 4–8 weeks to keep the remaining organisation informed of the go-forward plan. Radio silence after a RIF is read as chaos.
- **Retention risk management.** The head of people identifies retention risks in the remaining organisation — key employees who might use the moment to leave — and the executive team runs targeted retention conversations, often paired with a retention bonus authorised through the comp committee.
- **A "no-second-cut" commitment or the honest absence of it.** If the corporation can honestly say "this cut is the right depth and we do not anticipate another," it should. If it cannot, it should not say the opposite either — dishonest reassurance destroys trust when the second cut arrives.
- **Alumni relationships and rehire signalling.** The affected employees are the corporation's alumni network from tomorrow. Investment in outplacement, references, and continued relationship pays back in referrals, boomerang hires, and reputational recovery.

## Concrete example: a 60-person cut at a 220-person Series-B company

A Series-B SaaS company. Head count 220, distributed across California (95), New York (55), Washington (30), Texas (20), and remote (20). Revenue miss; extended runway objective set at 18 months post-cut. Board authorises a 27% cut — 60 employees across every function.

- **Board authorisation.** CEO delivers a RIF memo to the board 10 days ahead of execution; comp committee formally approves the executive-severance elements; full board consents in writing to the RIF plan.
- **Impact analysis.**
  - By function: Engineering 15 of 80, Sales 20 of 50, Marketing 8 of 25, CS 6 of 25, G&A 11 of 40.
  - By state: CA 28, NY 18, WA 8, TX 3, remote 3.
  - By age: 22 of the 60 are age 40+ — OWBPA group-release rules apply for those employees.
  - Demographic-slice review shows a small over-representation of one protected category in one function; the HRBP and general counsel review the selection criteria for that function and re-run one selection.
- **WARN analysis.**
  - Federal WARN: 220 employees, mass-layoff threshold at "single site of employment" — need to run the analysis per site. California CA site (95) has 28 affected; federal-WARN "mass layoff" test at that site is 50+ AND 33% (28 is under 50) — federal WARN not triggered at that site. Similar analysis for NY (18 of 55).
  - California WARN: covers 75+-employee "covered establishments"; California has 95 employees at one covered establishment; 28 terminations at that establishment exceeds the 50-employee threshold — actually 28 is under 50, so Cal-WARN also not triggered at that site. <!-- needs-research: confirm current Cal-WARN "covered establishment" definition and threshold interaction; the California threshold is 50 employees terminated in a 30-day period at a "covered establishment," not 33%. -->
  - New York WARN: 55 NY employees, 18 affected — the NY WARN threshold is 25+ representing at least 33%, and 18 is under 25. NY WARN not triggered.
  - Even where WARN is not triggered, the corporation elects to give 30 days' notice to affected employees as a matter of practice and adds a corresponding "pay in lieu" component for anyone accepting an earlier departure.
- **OWBPA execution.** For the 22 age-40+ affected employees, severance agreements include the 45-day consideration period, 7-day revocation window, OWBPA disclosure schedule showing job titles and ages of employees selected and not selected in each organisational unit, and consultation-with-counsel advice. Age-under-40 employees receive a shorter consideration period.
- **Comms choreography.** T-day: 9 AM all-hands (CEO delivers the announcement); 9:30–11:00 AM window for individual notice conversations (manager + HRBP); 11:00 AM manager-check-ins with unaffected reports; 2:00 PM town hall for questions; 4:00 PM CEO summary email. Days 2–7: HRBP office hours; individual questions; severance-and-release paperwork distributed to affected employees.
- **Package.** 3 weeks per year of service, minimum 12 weeks; 6-month subsidised COBRA; extended PTE window (12 months) for options; 6-month outplacement engagement with a national provider (~$2,000 / employee); LinkedIn recommendations from managers where warranted.
- **Anti-signalling.** No exec-comp changes for 60 days; no promotion announcements for the current cycle deferred by two weeks; CEO holds two follow-up all-hands over the next 30 days.

Total elapsed time from board authorisation to closed severance-and-release for the last age-40+ employee: ~65 days (driven by the 45-day OWBPA consideration + 7-day revocation window).

## Summary

- A RIF is a corporate action, not a personnel decision. Board authorisation (or at minimum board notice) is warranted for anything material.
- The impact analysis is authored jointly by CEO / CFO / head of people / general counsel and covers function, level, tenure, work location, demographic slice, and cost savings. Demographic-slice review is a pattern-detection instrument, not a quota mechanism.
- **Federal WARN Act** — 100+ employees, 60-day notice, plant closing (50+ at a single site) or mass layoff (500+, or 50–499 at 33%+). Three narrow exceptions still require notice as soon as practicable.
- **State mini-WARN** — California (75+, 60 days, 50+ threshold), New York (50+, 90 days, 25+ / 33%), New Jersey (100+, 90 days, mandatory severance), Wisconsin (50+, 60 days, 25+), Illinois (75+, 60 days, 25+ or 33%), and others. Every RIF's WARN analysis is state-by-state.
- **OWBPA** — group termination triggers 45-day consideration period for age-40+ employees, plus written disclosure of job titles and ages of individuals selected and not selected in the affected organisational unit. Strict compliance under *Oubre*.
- Executive-communications choreography — CEO-delivered all-hands, coordinated individual notice conversations within 60–90 minutes, unaffected-employee manager check-ins the same day, cadenced follow-through for 4–8 weeks.
- Package — severance at least at the individual-termination default, COBRA subsidy, outplacement engagement, extended PTE window, LinkedIn references, alumni signalling.
- Anti-signalling norms — CEO visible, no same-week exec-comp announcements, honest treatment of the "second cut" question, alumni-relationship investment.
