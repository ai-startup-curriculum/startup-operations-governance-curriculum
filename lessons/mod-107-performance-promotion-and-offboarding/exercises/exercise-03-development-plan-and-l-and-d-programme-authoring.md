# Exercise 03 — Development plan and L&D programme authoring

> Estimated time: **~4 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — Development, growth plans, and the L&D programme](../03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)

## Problem statement

Marigold Bio is a Delaware C-corp at Series-B, 190 employees, split roughly 50/40/10 across R&D (wet-lab plus computational), business functions (BD, ops, finance, people, legal), and G&A. Head of People is designing the corporation's first structured development / growth-plan / L&D programme. Two triggers made the work board-visible:

- **Exit-interview data.** Over the last four quarters, "no clear growth path" and "manager not investing in my development" have been the top two cited reasons for voluntary resignation among mid-tenure ICs (2–4 years at Marigold). Attrition has run 22% annualised in that cohort — meaningfully above the ~10–11% voluntary-attrition rate Pin's 2026 analysis reports for Series A/B companies (see https://www.pin.com/blog/attrition-rate-guide/).
- **Growth-conversation absence.** In the last performance cycle, only 4 of Marigold's 22 managers held a documented growth conversation with their direct reports. The rest either merged it with the performance-rating conversation or skipped it.

Two constraints matter:

- **Manager corps.** 22 managers. Roughly half became managers for the first time at Marigold; the other half are experienced but were promoted before Marigold had any manager-development infrastructure. There is no L&D lead FTE today; the head of people carries it.
- **L&D budget.** The CEO and CFO have approved ~1.4% of payroll as the programmatic L&D budget for next year — below the ATD *State of the Industry* direct-learning-expenditure benchmark (recent editions report figures in the ~2–3% of payroll range; see https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/highlights-from-atd-s-2026-state-of-the-industry-report) but a defensible Series-B starting point. The exercise assumes this level of budget as fixed.

Author the development / growth-plan / L&D programme.

## Tax, FLSA, and employment-record posture for the L&D stipend, mandatory manager training, and cohort-programme reimbursements

Chapter 03 names the dollar ranges for stipends and the platform categories but leaves the tax, wage-and-hour, and employment-record treatment of the programme's cash and time flows to the author. Pin the postures below before executing Part B (IDP template field design), Part C (manager-training calendar), Part D (stipend structure and eligible-expense categories), and Part F (cost model and success metrics). Where a statutory parameter is current-year-sensitive (the § 127 cap, the state-training-mandate hours, the IRS Notice 2016-30 PDS list), flag it `<!-- needs-research -->` at the point of use rather than inventing the figure.

- **IRC § 127 Educational Assistance Program posture for the stipend.** Under 26 U.S.C. § 127, an employer-provided educational-assistance programme excludes up to **$5,250 per employee per calendar year** of qualifying tuition, fees, books, supplies, and equipment from the employee's W-2 wages — but only if (i) a written plan document exists, (ii) the plan does not discriminate in favour of highly-compensated employees (HCEs) per § 414(q), (iii) no more than 5% of the benefit flows to >5% owners, and (iv) employees have reasonable notice. Marigold's Part D stipend design must say whether the plan adopts a § 127 written plan (Rippling supports this as a benefit-plan configuration) and, if so, where the $5,250-per-year cap intersects the proposed IC-tier / manager-tier / exec-tier ceilings. Amounts above the § 127 cap, or amounts spent outside the plan's eligible categories, flow through to the employee's W-2 as taxable compensation unless they qualify as a § 132(d) working-condition fringe (next bullet). (The $5,250 cap remains in effect for 2026 and will begin adjusting for inflation in $50 increments for taxable years beginning after 2026 per recent IRS guidance; see https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/irs-clarifies-tax-free-educational-assistance-cap-to-adjust-with-inflation-beginning-in-2027/ and https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/updates-to-frequently-asked-questions-about-educational-assistance-programs.)
- **IRC § 132(d) working-condition fringe for job-related education and materials.** Under 26 U.S.C. § 132(d) and Treas. Reg. § 1.162-5, education that maintains or improves skills *required in the employee's current trade or business* is excludable from W-2 wages with no dollar cap and no written-plan requirement — but education that qualifies the employee for a **new** trade or business is **not** a § 132(d) fringe. The Priya example IDP (bench scientist → scientific team lead) straddles the line: a leadership course that teaches team-lead skills for her existing scientific discipline is § 132(d)-eligible; a course that qualifies her for a different scientific discipline or a non-scientific MBA is not. The Daniel example (BD IC → BD manager) is cleaner — people-management training for a BD function Daniel is already in satisfies the § 132(d) test. The Part D eligible-expense categories must distinguish the two postures explicitly (Rippling Expense category tags: `job-related-skill`, `current-role-maintenance`, `development-stretch`, `new-trade-qualification`) so the payroll treatment flows correctly without post-hoc reclassification.
- **FLSA compensable-time treatment for mandatory manager training (29 CFR §§ 785.27–785.32).** Training time for a **non-exempt** employee counts as hours worked — and must be paid, including overtime if applicable — unless **all four** 29 CFR § 785.27 tests are met: (i) attendance is outside the employee's regular working hours, (ii) attendance is in fact voluntary, (iii) the course is not directly related to the employee's current job, and (iv) the employee performs no productive work during the training. Marigold's 22-manager corps is uniformly exempt under the FLSA executive exemption per the mod-106 chapter 03 FLSA classification posture, so the January 2027 two-day remediation cohort and the ongoing monthly manager forum do not trigger FLSA overtime liability for the managers themselves. But the Part C delivery crew (any non-exempt L&D coordinator, facilities support, or admin covering the cohort logistics) is subject to the compensable-training rule in the other direction: time spent supporting mandatory training **is** hours worked for the non-exempt support role and must be scheduled inside those employees' standard workweek or tracked for overtime in Rippling. (The regulation's four-factor test is well-settled, but the "voluntariness" element is the most frequently-litigated piece — training employees reasonably believe is required to keep their job fails the voluntariness prong even if nominally optional; see https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/us-dol-further-defines-what-constitutes-compensable-training-time.)
- **State-mandated harassment-prevention training applicability.** Chapter 03 names the state-mandated-harassment-prevention requirement as a required compliance-training line separate from developmental L&D; the per-state cadences and hours differ. Delaware (Marigold's state of incorporation) does not currently have a private-sector mandated-training hour requirement, but if Marigold has remote or hybrid employees in **California** (2 hours biennial for supervisors and 1 hour biennial for non-supervisors under Government Code § 12950.1, with new-hire training within 6 months), **New York State** (annual, under Labor Law § 201-g, interactive, covering state model content), **New York City** (annual for employers of 15+, under the Stop Sexual Harassment in NYC Act), **Illinois** (annual, under SB 75 / 775 ILCS 5/2-109(B)), **Connecticut** (CHRO rules under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 46a-54), **Maine**, or **Washington**, the compliance-platform roster (EVERFI / Emtrain / Traliant / Ethena per chapter 03) must be configured to assign the state-specific curriculum against each employee's Rippling work-location record, not against the corporation's HQ state. Facts section does not pin Marigold's physical-presence surface by state; the Part D compliance-platform rollout must verify the current-year statute cites for each state in which Marigold has a covered employee before finalising the vendor assignment. (As of 2026, the headline requirements: California — 2 hrs supervisors / 1 hr non-supervisors every 2 years at employers of 5+; New York State — annual interactive training for all employees; Illinois — annual for all employers; see the state roundup at https://www.paychex.com/articles/compliance/mandated-sexual-harassment-prevention-training and https://emtrain.com/blog/sexual-harassment/sexual-harassment-training-requirements-by-city-state-and-province/.)
- **IDP and growth-conversation documents as employment records.** IDPs, growth-conversation shared docs, and the Lattice-exported "growth-conversation-held" boolean are employment records under the EEOC recordkeeping rules at 29 CFR § 1602.14 (retain for **at least one year** from the record's creation or the personnel action it relates to, whichever is later; two years for federal contractors under OFCCP rules at 41 CFR § 60-1.12). Because these records can be subpoenaed in a discrimination, retaliation, or wrongful-termination claim, the Part B template must keep IDP field content **development-oriented** and **not** restate performance gaps or any adverse-action language already recorded in the performance-management surface in Lattice. The chapter 03 "separate module" rule is reinforced by this: performance goals and ratings sit in Lattice's performance space (discoverable as performance records); IDPs sit in a separate Lattice Goals space (discoverable as development records); conflating the two means a plaintiff's counsel can argue either surface is a performance evaluation, and the corporation loses the ability to say "this was coaching, not adverse action." Retention policy for both surfaces follows the mod-103 chapter 07 litigation-hold protocol on top of the baseline retention minimum.
- **ADEA / disparate-impact review of IDPs, promotion-track investments, and the rotation programme.** Any pattern in which L&D investment, sponsor introductions, cohort-programme enrolment, or rotation-programme slots concentrates on a non-representative demographic slice is a disparate-impact exposure surface under Title VII (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(k)), the ADEA (29 U.S.C. § 623), and California FEHA (Cal. Gov. Code § 12940) — particularly relevant to the Priya-track scientific-team-lead pipeline (where the wet-lab bench-scientist corps skews to a demographic distinct from the computational team) and to the rotation programme (where "which L3 associate gets the computational tour" is a managerial-discretion decision). The Part F success-metrics dashboard must include, as a non-vanity leading indicator, a demographic-slice review of IDP coverage, cohort-programme enrolment, and rotation-programme candidacy against the underlying population for each slice. Manager-discretion decisions that visibly skew the slice route to the head of people and to outside employment counsel per the mod-107 chapter 04 anti-abuse norm and the mod-103 chapter 07 guidance before the investment is committed. Do not treat this as DEI-programme substance (which is mod-108's surface); treat it as compliance review of the L&D-programme operating decisions themselves.
- **Cohort-programme clawback / repay-if-leave letter posture.** Clawback or repay-if-leave letters for a $5–10k Reforge cohort, a scientific-leadership programme, or an executive-MBA investment are a Series-B common practice but are **not uniformly enforceable** across US jurisdictions. California narrowly enforces clawback only where the agreement reflects a bona fide loan or a true training-cost reimbursement proportionate to the time saved by not completing the engagement (Business and Professions Code § 16600's non-compete prohibition has been read to constrain clawback language that operates as a de facto stay-with-the-employer covenant); New York and New Jersey enforce clawback more readily but scrutinise the proportionality and the business-interest rationale; Washington's RCW 49.44.410 restricts non-compete enforceability but is less clear on pure clawback. Marigold's Part D stipend design must say whether clawback applies at all; the recommended Series-B posture is **no clawback under $5,000** of cumulative calendar-year L&D investment per employee, a **pro-rated 24-month reimbursement** letter for any single investment of $5,000 or more (scientific-leadership programmes, $2k+ Reforge cohorts, executive coaching above the mainline BetterUp tier), and **California employees handled as a state-specific carve-out** (no clawback above the de minimis threshold unless outside employment counsel signs off on the structure as a bona fide loan). The FTC's 2024 Non-Compete Rule was vacated by the Northern District of Texas on 2024-08-20 before its 2024-09-04 effective date, the FTC dismissed its appeals in September 2025, and the rule was formally removed from the Code of Federal Regulations on 2026-02-12 (see https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/12/2026-02866/revision-of-the-negative-option-rule-withdrawal-of-the-cars-rule-removal-of-the-non-compete-rule-to); the rule as written did not categorically prohibit training-repayment agreements, so TRA enforceability continues to turn on state law and case-by-case Section 5 FTC Act review.
- **Coaching-vendor and individual-coach engagement contracting.** External coaching delivered through **BetterUp** or **Better Manager** is a vendor-platform engagement — the coaches are the platform's W-2s or independent contractors, Marigold pays a per-seat fee, no 1099-NEC obligation falls on Marigold, and the vendor agreement is a standard SaaS-plus-services contract per the [mod-109 chapter 03](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/03-saas-service-contract-structure.md) template. **Individual-coach engagements** (one coach, one manager, direct contract) require the individual to be a true independent contractor under the DOL FLSA economic-reality test and, if a W-9 is on file and cumulative payments meet the IRC § 6041 reporting threshold in a calendar year, Marigold issues a **1099-NEC** by January 31 of the following year. Note that OBBBA § 70433(a) raised the § 6041 threshold from $600 to $2,000 for payments made on or after 2026-01-01, indexed for inflation in $100 increments thereafter (see https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/17/2026-07519/increase-in-threshold-for-requiring-information-reporting-with-respect-to-certain-payees-extension), so a coaching engagement paying $1,500 in 2026 does not trigger 1099-NEC reporting even though it would have under the pre-2026 rule. Individual-coach contracts also must include a confidentiality clause (coaching conversations are privileged in the professional-norm sense but have no statutory privilege in US law — the contract is the only enforceable instrument) and an IP / work-product clause consistent with the mod-109 vendor-contract template. The 2026-12-18 coaching-vendor selection must land on the contract structure (platform vs. individual) before procurement signs.
- **Peer-coaching time as an implicit cost in the Part F model.** If the Part C coaching-engagement decision includes **internal peer coaching** as a layer (or as a fallback for a budget-constrained scenario), the time of the mentoring manager is a real labour cost that must flow through the Part F cost model even though no cash leaves the corporation. Price the line as `(mentoring hours per manager per month) × (12 months) × (mentor-manager count) × (manager loaded hourly rate)` and name the loaded-hourly-rate basis explicitly (standard practice: fully-loaded salary ÷ 2,080 hours × 1.3 for benefits and overhead). Peer-coaching time is **additive** to the mentor's existing load — a healthy peer-coaching layer is designed so no mentor commits more than 4 hours per month; above that threshold, the mentor's primary-role performance measurably erodes per the chapter 03 and chapter 01 guidance on allocated-time transparency. Do not let the Part F cost model treat peer-coaching as a zero-cost substitute for paid external coaching when comparing against the Series-B benchmark; call out the time cost even if it does not change the cash figure.

## Requirements

### Part A — Semi-annual growth-conversation instrument

1. **Cadence and calendar.** Anchor the growth-conversation cycle to Marigold's performance-review cycle (assume the mod-106 chapter 04 calendar). Growth conversations should land 6 weeks after each formal review closes — pick specific weeks.
2. **Written prompt.** Author the five-question written prompt the employee receives 48 hours ahead. Address explicitly why growth conversations are held separately from performance-review conversations (chapter 03: mixing collapses both).
3. **Conversation format.** 60-minute 1:1; the manager's role (listen, propose, commit); the employee's role; the shared-doc summary format.
4. **The manager's failure mode.** Author the specific pattern managers use to duck growth conversations (rescheduling, merging with the review, running short) and the head-of-people intervention when the pattern surfaces.

### Part B — Individual Development Plan (IDP) template

Author the five-field IDP template:

1. **Growth goal.** Specifically named; the pitfall of "grow as a leader" vs. "scope from lead-engineer projects to multi-team programmes."
2. **Why.** Promotion path, role change, or personal-mastery outcome. Ties back to the promotion architecture in [chapter 02](../02-promotion-architecture.md).
3. **How.** Stretch project, training, coaching engagement, rotation, mentorship. Two or three concrete activities per goal.
4. **Support.** Manager / corporation commitment — coaching engagement, L&D-budget allocation, sponsor introduction, project scoping.
5. **Review checkpoint.** 90-day mid-cycle review and 6-month growth-conversation re-review.

Include two example IDPs — one for a mid-level bench scientist growing toward a scientific-team-lead role; one for a business-function IC growing toward a management role. The examples are Marigold-specific and use realistic goals.

### Part C — Manager-coaching programme

Design the three-layer manager-development programme:

1. **New-manager training.** Content, format, and duration (chapter 03 references a one-time onboarding programme within 90 days of promotion). Include a topic-by-topic outline.
2. **Ongoing manager training.** Monthly manager forum + quarterly manager-development workshop + annual manager offsite. Author the topic rotation for a full year — 12 months of forum topics, 4 quarterly workshops, one annual offsite theme.
3. **1:1 coaching.** Author the coaching-engagement decision — BetterUp, Better Manager, individual coach, or internal peer coaching. Include a per-manager annual budget and a cost model for the full 22-manager corps.

### Part D — L&D budget allocation and platform selection

1. **Per-employee stipend.** Author the annual per-employee L&D stipend (chapter 03 references $1–3k at Series-A; Marigold is Series-B). Address structure — use-it-or-lose-it vs. two-year rollover; approval workflow; eligible expense categories (courses, books, conferences, coaching). Higher tiers for manager and exec levels.
2. **Programmatic budget.** Author the split of the ~1.4%-of-payroll programmatic budget across: manager training (Part C), DEI and compliance training (hand off to [mod-108](../../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/) for design), new-hire onboarding L&D (hand off to [mod-104 chapter 06](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-first-90-day-onboarding.md)), tuition-reimbursement, and cohort programmes.
3. **Platform decision matrix.** Populate the chapter 03 platform categories for Marigold — general-purpose learning platform, cohort programme, coaching platform, compliance / required-training platform. For each: the specific vendor selected (from the resources list), the target population, and the annual cost.
4. **The selection rule.** Chapter 03 warns against paying for four overlapping platforms; author Marigold's selection rule and the veto power of the head of people over function-lead platform requests.

### Part E — Internal-mobility programme

Author the internal-mobility programme:

1. **Internal job board.** How new open reqs are posted internally first; the internal-first window (chapter 03: one week); the internal-referral flag; the interview process for internal candidates.
2. **Transfer policy.** Tenure-in-role requirement (typically 12 months); manager-consent norms (transfers do not require manager approval but do require notification); transition timeline (4–8 weeks); comp adjustment mechanic.
3. **Cross-functional rotation programme.** For Marigold specifically, design a rotation programme that would let a wet-lab scientist do a 3-month rotation on the computational team and vice versa — the specific scoping, the return rights, the backfill mechanic.
4. **Manager-signalling norms.** How managers are trained to treat internal-transfer inquiries as career development, not disloyalty; the head-of-people intervention if a manager blocks transfers de facto.
5. **The measurable outcomes.** Internal-mobility as a share of total hires; time-to-first-move; where these metrics appear in the quarterly people-ops review.

### Part F — Cost model and 12-month rollout

1. **Cost model.** A line-by-line annual cost breakdown of the full programme — per-employee stipend rolled up, coaching engagements, platform licences, cohort budgets, event costs. Compare to the ~1.4%-of-payroll target.
2. **12-month rollout calendar.** Which components go live when — the new-manager training first, the platform decisions second, the growth conversations in cycle two, the rotation programme in the second half. Marigold cannot roll everything out on day one.
3. **Success metrics.** The specific metrics reported to the CEO and board each quarter — growth-conversation completion rate, IDP-active count, L&D-stipend utilisation, manager-coaching engagement participation, internal-mobility percentage, and (over time) attrition among the mid-tenure IC cohort.
