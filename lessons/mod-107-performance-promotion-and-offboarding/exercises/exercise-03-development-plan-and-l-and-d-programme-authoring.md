# Exercise 03 — Development plan and L&D programme authoring

> Estimated time: **~4 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — Development, growth plans, and the L&D programme](../03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)

## Problem statement

Marigold Bio is a Delaware C-corp at Series-B, 190 employees, split roughly 50/40/10 across R&D (wet-lab plus computational), business functions (BD, ops, finance, people, legal), and G&A. Head of People is designing the corporation's first structured development / growth-plan / L&D programme. Two triggers made the work board-visible:

- **Exit-interview data.** Over the last four quarters, "no clear growth path" and "manager not investing in my development" have been the top two cited reasons for voluntary resignation among mid-tenure ICs (2–4 years at Marigold). Attrition has run 22% annualised in that cohort — meaningfully above the Series-B people-ops benchmark. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current Series-B benchmark for annual voluntary attrition among mid-tenure ICs; the exercise flags this as a needs-research placeholder rather than a canonical number. -->
- **Growth-conversation absence.** In the last performance cycle, only 4 of Marigold's 22 managers held a documented growth conversation with their direct reports. The rest either merged it with the performance-rating conversation or skipped it.

Two constraints matter:

- **Manager corps.** 22 managers. Roughly half became managers for the first time at Marigold; the other half are experienced but were promoted before Marigold had any manager-development infrastructure. There is no L&D lead FTE today; the head of people carries it.
- **L&D budget.** The CEO and CFO have approved ~1.4% of payroll as the programmatic L&D budget for next year — an amount consistent with the ATD industry benchmark. <!-- needs-research: confirm current-year ATD *State of the Industry* percentage-of-payroll benchmark. --> The exercise assumes this level of budget as fixed.

Author the development / growth-plan / L&D programme.

## Requirements

### Part A — Semi-annual growth-conversation instrument

1. **Cadence and calendar.** Anchor the growth-conversation cycle to Marigold's performance-review cycle (assume the mod-106 chapter 04 calendar). Growth conversations should land 6 weeks after each formal review closes — pick specific weeks.
2. **Written prompt.** Author the five-question written prompt the employee receives 48 hours ahead. Address explicitly why growth conversations are held separately from performance-review conversations (chapter 03: mixing collapses both).
3. **Conversation format.** 60-minute 1:1; the manager's role (listen, propose, commit); the employee's role; the shared-doc summary format.
4. **The manager's failure mode.** Author the specific pattern managers use to duck growth conversations (rescheduling, merging with the review, running short) and the head-of-people intervention when the pattern surfaces.

### Part B — Individual Development Plan (IDP) template

Author the five-field IDP template:

1. **Growth goal.** Specifically named; the pitfall of "grow as a leader" vs. "scope from lead-engineer projects to multi-team programmes."
2. **Why.** Promotion path, role change, or personal-mastery outcome. Ties back to the promotion architecture in [chapter 02](../02-promotion-architecture.md).
3. **How.** Stretch project, training, coaching engagement, rotation, mentorship. Two or three concrete activities per goal.
4. **Support.** Manager / corporation commitment — coaching engagement, L&D-budget allocation, sponsor introduction, project scoping.
5. **Review checkpoint.** 90-day mid-cycle review and 6-month growth-conversation re-review.

Include two example IDPs — one for a mid-level bench scientist growing toward a scientific-team-lead role; one for a business-function IC growing toward a management role. The examples are Marigold-specific and use realistic goals.

### Part C — Manager-coaching programme

Design the three-layer manager-development programme:

1. **New-manager training.** Content, format, and duration (chapter 03 references a one-time onboarding programme within 90 days of promotion). Include a topic-by-topic outline.
2. **Ongoing manager training.** Monthly manager forum + quarterly manager-development workshop + annual manager offsite. Author the topic rotation for a full year — 12 months of forum topics, 4 quarterly workshops, one annual offsite theme.
3. **1:1 coaching.** Author the coaching-engagement decision — BetterUp, Better Manager, individual coach, or internal peer coaching. Include a per-manager annual budget and a cost model for the full 22-manager corps.

### Part D — L&D budget allocation and platform selection

1. **Per-employee stipend.** Author the annual per-employee L&D stipend (chapter 03 references $1–3k at Series-A; Marigold is Series-B). Address structure — use-it-or-lose-it vs. two-year rollover; approval workflow; eligible expense categories (courses, books, conferences, coaching). Higher tiers for manager and exec levels.
2. **Programmatic budget.** Author the split of the ~1.4%-of-payroll programmatic budget across: manager training (Part C), DEI and compliance training (hand off to [mod-108](../../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/) for design), new-hire onboarding L&D (hand off to [mod-104 chapter 06](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-first-90-day-onboarding.md)), tuition-reimbursement, and cohort programmes.
3. **Platform decision matrix.** Populate the chapter 03 platform categories for Marigold — general-purpose learning platform, cohort programme, coaching platform, compliance / required-training platform. For each: the specific vendor selected (from the resources list), the target population, and the annual cost.
4. **The selection rule.** Chapter 03 warns against paying for four overlapping platforms; author Marigold's selection rule and the veto power of the head of people over function-lead platform requests.

### Part E — Internal-mobility programme

Author the internal-mobility programme:

1. **Internal job board.** How new open reqs are posted internally first; the internal-first window (chapter 03: one week); the internal-referral flag; the interview process for internal candidates.
2. **Transfer policy.** Tenure-in-role requirement (typically 12 months); manager-consent norms (transfers do not require manager approval but do require notification); transition timeline (4–8 weeks); comp adjustment mechanic.
3. **Cross-functional rotation programme.** For Marigold specifically, design a rotation programme that would let a wet-lab scientist do a 3-month rotation on the computational team and vice versa — the specific scoping, the return rights, the backfill mechanic.
4. **Manager-signalling norms.** How managers are trained to treat internal-transfer inquiries as career development, not disloyalty; the head-of-people intervention if a manager blocks transfers de facto.
5. **The measurable outcomes.** Internal-mobility as a share of total hires; time-to-first-move; where these metrics appear in the quarterly people-ops review.

### Part F — Cost model and 12-month rollout

1. **Cost model.** A line-by-line annual cost breakdown of the full programme — per-employee stipend rolled up, coaching engagements, platform licences, cohort budgets, event costs. Compare to the ~1.4%-of-payroll target.
2. **12-month rollout calendar.** Which components go live when — the new-manager training first, the platform decisions second, the growth conversations in cycle two, the rotation programme in the second half. Marigold cannot roll everything out on day one.
3. **Success metrics.** The specific metrics reported to the CEO and board each quarter — growth-conversation completion rate, IDP-active count, L&D-stipend utilisation, manager-coaching engagement participation, internal-mobility percentage, and (over time) attrition among the mid-tenure IC cohort.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 03 is the primary reference. The four operating decisions (growth conversation, IDP, manager coaching, L&D budget / tools) plus the internal-mobility programme are the frame.
- The growth conversation must be *separate* from the performance-review conversation — this is the single most important design point in the chapter. Do not let the mid-year review absorb it.
- Marigold has a wet-lab / computational-R&D split that most SaaS-oriented programmes do not have. The rotation programme in Part E and the example IDP in Part B should reflect that.
- The manager-coaching programme (Part C) is the highest-ROI investment per chapter 03. Do not shortcut the cost.
- Do not invent specific platform pricing. Chapter 03 references ranges; where a specific number is cited, take it from the chapter or flag `<!-- needs-research -->`.
- The programmatic-budget split (Part D) requires deferring to sibling modules — mod-108 owns DEI-training substance, mod-104 owns new-hire-onboarding substance. Name the deferrals explicitly.
- The internal-mobility programme (Part E) has to survive the "engineering manager wants to hold on to Priya" test. The transfer policy needs to make it hard for a manager to *block* a transfer even if they can *slow* one.
- The success metrics (Part F) are what the head of people gets asked about at the next board meeting. Pick metrics that are measurable, movable, and meaningful — not vanity metrics.
- The exit-interview data referenced in the problem statement closes back into this programme via [chapter 08](../08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md). Reference the loop but do not re-author it.
