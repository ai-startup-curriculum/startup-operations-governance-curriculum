# Exercise 07 — Executive-team offboarding playbook drill

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [07 — Executive-team offboarding](../07-executive-team-offboarding.md)

## Problem statement

You are the head of people at Highwater Systems, a Delaware C-corp at Series-C, 250 employees. Highwater is on an IPO track — targeting an S-1 filing in 12–18 months. The CEO has concluded, over the course of two quarters, that the current CFO is not the right operator for the Series-D fundraise and IPO track. The audit-committee chair has aligned with the CEO. The compensation-committee chair has pre-cleared the executive-severance package framework.

The current CFO — Vidya, tenure 3 years, age 49 — has been effective at the fractional-CFO-graduation phase but the audit-committee-and-public-company operating cadence requires a different operator. There is no misconduct issue. Vidya is respected internally; her direct reports (Controller, VP FP&A, Head of Finance Ops) are strong. Highwater has identified an internal candidate — the current VP FP&A — to step up on an interim basis while a permanent CFO search runs.

Two additional dimensions matter:

- **The CFO holds a board-observer seat** on the audit committee (not a full board seat), created when Highwater expanded the audit committee at Series-B. The observer arrangement needs to be resolved as part of the transition.
- **Highwater is 12 months from an intended S-1 filing.** Every 8-K disclosure event before then feeds directly into the S-1 narrative and is scrutinised by underwriters. This transition needs to land cleanly.

Author the executive-team offboarding playbook end-to-end — the CEO-CFO difficult-conversation script, the board-communication choreography, the exec-severance-and-release execution with attention to CoC / indemnification / observer-seat resignation, and the employee-and-external comms.

## Requirements

### Part A — CEO preparation memo

Before the difficult conversation, the CEO needs to align internally and prepare. Author the memo the CEO writes for themselves (not sent) covering:

1. **The gap or change that led to the decision.** Specific, honest, and defensible in a board meeting. Address the "different operator for the next phase" framing without slipping into either euphemism or blame.
2. **What has been tried in the last 3–6 months.** Coaching, expanded staff support, direct feedback from CEO. Document the sequence honestly.
3. **What the corporation will say publicly about the departure.** The framing that will land in the employee announcement, the 8-K if filed, and the S-1 later.
4. **The internal alignment checklist.** Board chair, lead independent director, audit-committee chair, comp-committee chair, and (before the CEO-CFO conversation) outside employment counsel.

### Part B — CEO-CFO difficult-conversation script

Author the specific script for the difficult conversation per chapter 07:

1. **Setting.** Private, in-person, at a moment that does not immediately precede a public event.
2. **Length.** 30–45 minutes.
3. **Structure (five steps).**
   - CEO opens with the decision — the specific words.
   - CEO gives the *why* in one to two paragraphs — honest, direct, specific, not litigious. The specific words.
   - CEO acknowledges what the executive has contributed — genuine, not perfunctory. The specific paragraph.
   - CEO transitions to the how — package walkthrough by head of people.
   - CEO and CFO close on next steps — communications timing, transition responsibilities, effective date.
4. **The mutual-agreement framing.** Author the specific offer of a "step-down" or "transition" arrangement — the executive's cooperation on messaging in exchange for a package. Address explicitly what changes if Vidya refuses the mutual-agreement framing.
5. **The Horowitz-canon test.** Apply the chapter 07 paraphrase — "do not make it about them, make it about the company; do not litigate the past, communicate the decision; take responsibility for the hire and the situation; the executive is going to talk about how you handled this for the rest of their career, handle it like that matters." Point to the specific script lines that satisfy the test.

### Part C — Board-communication choreography

Author the board-communication timeline:

1. **Day -14 to -7.** CEO aligns with board chair and audit-committee chair.
2. **Day -7 to -3.** Comp-committee chair confirms package parameters.
3. **Day -3 to -1.** Separation-and-release paperwork moves toward execution; CEO holds difficult conversation with Vidya.
4. **Day 0.** Written board notice or memo circulated to the full board with the departure summary, transition plan, and communications plan. Author the memo.
5. **Day 0 to +1.** If board consent is required, execute via unanimous written consent under DGCL § 141(f). Author the resolution text for both the CFO transition (accepting resignation, appointing interim CFO) and the audit-committee-observer-seat resolution.
6. **Day 0 to +2.** Internal employee announcement.
7. **Day 0 to +2.** External announcement if warranted.
8. **Day 0 to +4.** 8-K filed if required.

Address the audit-committee-specific involvement per exchange listing standards (Nasdaq 5605(c)(3); NYSE 303A.07) — the audit committee has an independent line to the CFO, and CFO turnover is a matter the audit committee needs to be involved in from the start.

Address the S-1 sensitivity — every disclosure event before filing feeds into the S-1 narrative and is scrutinised by underwriters. Author the specific dialogue with securities counsel about the 8-K content and the S-1 disclosure implication.

### Part D — Exec severance-and-release agreement

Author the executive severance-and-release agreement per chapter 07 and chapter 05:

1. **Cash severance.** 12 months of base salary plus 12 months of target bonus prorated (per Vidya's employment agreement — assume this is what her offer letter provides; if not, comp-committee approval required for the specific package structure). Delivered as salary continuation or lump sum — pick and defend.
2. **COBRA subsidy.** 12 months of corporation-paid COBRA premium.
3. **Equity treatment.**
   - Equity acceleration per Vidya's employment agreement — assume 12 months of accelerated vesting on involuntary termination. Address the specific share counts (do not fabricate; state "per Vidya's grant agreement, calculated by [equity administrator, e.g., Carta] as of the termination date").
   - Post-termination exercise window — 12 months per Highwater's plan (an extended window at the cost of ISO tax treatment for the affected options). Reference [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) for the substantive equity policy.
   - CoC-acceleration mechanics — even though there is no pending change of control today, the standalone-vs-CoC severance-clause interaction should be documented in the release so future events do not create ambiguity.
4. **Indemnification continuation** — DGCL § 145; the individual indemnification agreement (assumed executed at appointment); the corporation's continuing obligation reaffirmed.
5. **D&O coverage reaffirmation** — coverage during Vidya's period of service; tail / run-off coverage if a change of control occurs; standard side A / B / C treatment.
6. **Board-observer-seat resignation.** Formal resignation letter effective on a specified date; the audit-committee seat is amended by board consent.
7. **Transition-services agreement.** 30–60-day advisor period during which Vidya continues to be available for transition support; compensation continued at salary or converted to consulting fee; equity vests during transition (or does not, per policy).
8. **OWBPA compliance.** Vidya is 49; author the 21-day consideration period, 7-day revocation window, and written consultation notice per single-employee (not group) OWBPA release.
9. **Speak Out Act carve-out and California Silenced No More Act carve-out** (Vidya works in San Francisco — Highwater HQ). Per chapter 05.
10. **NLRA / *McLaren Macomb* consideration.** Vidya is a supervisor / executive; the *McLaren Macomb* framework has historically been more permissive for supervisors. Address the analysis and the corporation's decision to include or omit the Section 7 carve-out.
11. **Mutual non-disparagement.** Chapter 07 warns against a broad clause binding the corporation too tightly. Author the narrowly-tailored mutual non-disparagement — Vidya does not disparage Highwater's business, product, or services; Highwater's specifically-named executives (CEO, board members) do not disparage Vidya's professional performance. Do not agree to a blanket corporation-wide non-disparagement that would constrain what the corporation can say when the next reference check arrives.
12. **Non-compete / non-solicit.** California prohibits non-competes (see [mod-103 chapter 06](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/06-non-compete-landscape-and-alternatives.md)); non-solicit of Highwater employees is enforceable more narrowly. Author what applies.
13. **Consideration.** The severance package is consideration for the release; standard non-admission clause.
14. **Continuing PIIA obligations** per [mod-103 chapter 04](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/04-employee-piia.md).

### Part E — Employee-communications playbook

Author the internal announcement:

1. **The written announcement.** Sent by the CEO to the whole company on the day of the transition. Structure per chapter 07 — what, why, what next, recognition. Author the specific text.
2. **The recognition paragraph.** Written honestly, specifically. Chapter 07 notes this is often the piece the departing executive most wants to see and the piece the corporation most sloppily writes. Get it right.
3. **The cliché avoidance.** Do not use "Vidya has decided to pursue new opportunities." Author a shorter, more honest sentence.
4. **Live all-hands.** Within 24 hours of the announcement. Author the CEO's opening remarks and the top three questions the CEO should be prepared to answer.
5. **Team-specific comms for Vidya's direct reports.** Additional 1:1s or small-group conversations the same day. Author the framing.
6. **Confidentiality of the separation package.** Explicitly not disclosed to the general employee population. Confirm the practice.

### Part F — External-communications choreography

Highwater is not yet public but is on an S-1 track. Author the external comms:

1. **8-K decision.** Highwater is not a public company today, so Form 8-K disclosure is not currently required. If Highwater has filed a confidential S-1 or has publicly filed for an IPO, the 8-K obligation may kick in earlier. Address the current-state decision and the trigger point at which it changes.
2. **Press release.** Whether to issue one. For a Series-C CFO transition on an IPO track, the answer is typically yes — the transition will be picked up by trade press and by potential underwriters, and controlling the narrative is worth doing. Author the press release draft (300–500 words).
3. **IR / lead-investor outreach.** Lead investors and board observers briefed the day of the internal announcement. Author the specific talking points.
4. **Customer / partner outreach.** Vidya has spoken at industry conferences and is externally-visible; key customer accounts should be briefed by the CEO or successor within 48 hours. Author the talking points.
5. **S-1 disclosure implication.** Coordinate with securities counsel — the departure will need to be disclosed in the "Management" section of the S-1 and the "Recent Developments" section if the S-1 is filed within a defined window. Flag the coordination requirement.

### Part G — Executive-offboarding checklist

Populate every line item from the chapter 07 checklist for Vidya's specific case:

- CEO decision documented; board chair, audit-committee chair, comp-committee chair alignment before the conversation.
- Separation-and-release paperwork drafted with employment counsel, including CoC-acceleration analysis, indemnification continuation, board-observer-seat resignation, non-solicit terms scoped to California.
- CEO-Vidya difficult conversation held.
- Board written notice or board consent circulated per DGCL § 141(f); comp-committee approval of package.
- Audit-committee brief.
- Employee announcement drafted; recognition paragraph reviewed with Vidya.
- Live all-hands scheduled within 24 hours of announcement.
- Direct-report 1:1s scheduled the same day.
- External comms (press release, IR outreach, customer briefings) coordinated per profile.
- Separation-and-release signed within OWBPA 21-day consideration window; 7-day revocation window observed.
- Final pay (California, day-of-termination per Cal. Labor Code § 201 — but Vidya's transition ends after a 30-60 day advisor period, so address the timing carefully).
- COBRA notice per [chapter 05](../05-termination-playbook.md).
- Equity administration per [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); Carta or equivalent administrator notified.
- Audit-committee-observer-seat resignation letter executed and filed; audit-committee composition updated.
- D&O tail / indemnification confirmation letter delivered to Vidya.
- Alumni-programme signalling per [chapter 08](../08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md).

### Part H — 30-day retrospective

After the transition closes, run a 30-day retrospective:

1. **What worked in the choreography.**
2. **What the CEO would do differently.**
3. **What the head of people would do differently.**
4. **Feedback signals from the workforce** (what the town-hall Q&A surfaced; what direct-report 1:1s revealed; any pulse-survey signal).
5. **Feedback signals from the board** (audit-committee reaction; lead-investor reaction).
6. **The one lesson to institutionalise** for the next executive transition.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 07 is the primary reference. The five operating decisions (difficult-conversation script, board choreography, exec severance execution, employee comms, external comms) are the frame.
- The Horowitz-canon test is not decorative — it is the design principle for the difficult-conversation script in Part B. Every line should satisfy the test.
- The board choreography is non-negotiable per chapter 07 — board notice before employee announcement, audit-committee involvement for a CFO transition, comp-committee approval of any above-policy severance. Get the sequence right.
- The DGCL § 141(f) unanimous written consent resolution needs to cover the acceptance of Vidya's resignation as CFO, the appointment of the interim CFO (the current VP FP&A), and the amendment of the audit-committee composition to reflect the observer-seat resignation. Author each resolution.
- The CoC-acceleration analysis is important even in the absence of a pending change of control. The standalone-vs-CoC severance-clause interaction must be documented in the release so a future event (M&A, IPO) does not create ambiguity about what the executive is entitled to. Refer to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) for the substantive equity policy; the exercise names the interaction but does not author the equity-plan substance.
- The audit-committee-observer-seat is a distinct legal artifact from a full board seat. The resignation mechanic is a formal letter to the audit-committee chair and the board chair; the board consent amending the observer arrangement is separate from the officer-resignation resolution.
- The S-1 sensitivity is real. Highwater is 12 months from an intended filing; every 8-K, every press release, and every internal communication about the CFO transition may end up in the S-1 or in underwriter diligence. Coordinate with securities counsel from day one.
- The recognition paragraph in the employee announcement is the piece the corporation most often gets wrong. The failure mode is generic praise ("Vidya has been a great partner"); the target is specific praise ("Vidya built the FP&A function from scratch, ran two clean audits, and led the corporation through the shift to public-company-track reporting"). Get it specific.
- The mutual non-disparagement is a trap chapter 07 warns about. A broad clause binding "Highwater" to non-disparagement will constrain what the corporation can say when the next reference check comes in. Narrow the clause to specifically-named executives (CEO, board members) rather than "the corporation."
- OWBPA applies (Vidya is 49) — 21-day consideration for a single-employee release, 7-day revocation, written consultation notice. Not the 45-day group-release version. Get the OWBPA math right.
- California workplace + California Silenced No More Act (SB 331) + federal Speak Out Act carve-outs all apply. State the specific carve-out language.
- The transition-services (advisor) period is a common Series-C+ CFO transition pattern. It also raises timing questions on final pay under California Labor Code § 201 (the technical termination-of-employment date matters for the same-day-final-pay rule). Address the timing carefully.
- Do not fabricate specific share counts, dollar amounts, or Vidya's specific grant terms. State "per Vidya's grant agreement, calculated by the equity administrator" where specifics apply.
- The 30-day retrospective (Part H) is a light-touch instrument that closes the loop with the executive team and the head of people; do not overspec it.
