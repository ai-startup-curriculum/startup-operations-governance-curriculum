# Exercise 04 — PIP authoring and conclusion-decision drill

> Estimated time: **~4 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — The Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)](../04-pip-mechanics.md)

## Problem statement

You are the head of people at Riverpoint Systems, a Delaware C-corp at Series-B, 130 employees, engineering-heavy, distributed workforce (California, Colorado, New York, Texas). Four PIP-adjacent cases have landed on your desk in the same two-week window. Not all four are appropriate PIP triggers, and even the appropriate ones need careful authoring. You will make a triage decision for each, author the PIP for the ones that proceed, and design a decision-making mechanic for the conclusion window.

**Case A — Priya, L4 Engineer (Colorado, tenure 3.5 years).** Her manager (Jonas, first-time manager, promoted 8 months ago) has flagged that Priya has been shipping late for two quarters, missing code-review commitments, and delivering PRs that require substantial rework. Jonas has raised the issue in three 1:1s over the last six weeks and sent a written coaching-memo email two weeks ago. Priya acknowledges the gap but says the team's sprint scope is unrealistic. Jonas wants to open a PIP.

**Case B — Marcus, L5 Sales AE (New York, tenure 1 year).** His sales manager wants to PIP him because he missed quota by 20% in Q3. Marcus hit quota in Q1 and Q2 and has closed two of Riverpoint's largest deals in the last six months. His territory was recently expanded and he is still ramping on the new segment. The manager has not previously raised a concern with Marcus. Sales-comp clawback and quota-relief mechanics live in the GTM curriculum, not this module — but the *decision to open a PIP or not* is squarely within mod-107.

**Case C — Alex, L4 Designer (California, tenure 2 years).** Alex has been argumentative in cross-functional meetings, has snapped at a peer twice in the last month, and was overheard making a derogatory comment about a colleague. Alex's manager thinks the pattern is a performance issue and wants to PIP. The head of engineering has raised the incident-behaviour issue separately as a potential misconduct issue.

**Case D — Robyn, L3 Customer Support Rep (Texas, tenure 8 months, age 52).** Robyn's manager wants to PIP her for slow ticket-resolution times. Robyn is the only employee over 50 on the Customer Support team, and she is the only Customer Support Rep to be flagged in this cycle. She has been at Riverpoint 8 months; the team's ticket-volume target has been raised twice since her hire. Her manager is 28 and has raised concerns to you privately about "generational fit."

Author the triage decision for each case, the PIPs for those that proceed, and the conclusion-decision mechanic for cases that reach the 30- / 60- / 90-day boundary.

## Requirements

### Part A — Case triage memo

Author a 2–3 page memo covering each of the four cases. For each, state:

1. **Recommendation.** Open PIP; do not open PIP (informal coaching continues); do not open PIP (misconduct — investigate); do not open PIP (role-fit or ramp — different intervention); or defer pending further review.
2. **Reasoning.** Anchored to the chapter 04 trigger criteria. Address each of the chapter's five decisions where relevant — trigger, structure, support pattern, conclusion outcome, anti-abuse norm.
3. **The alternative if PIP is not opened.** For each case that does not proceed to PIP, what intervention does proceed and how is it documented.
4. **Legal-risk flags.** For each, flag the EEOC / state-law exposure surface (California FEHA, ADEA, potential retaliation) with reference to [mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) chapters 07 and 08.

For Case B (Marcus), address the GTM handoff explicitly — the ramp-fail vs. capability-fail distinction that the GTM curriculum owns. Do not author the sales-specific PIP substance; identify what needs GTM-curriculum-informed review.

For Case C (Alex), address the misconduct-vs-performance split from chapter 04. Do not merge investigation into PIP.

For Case D (Robyn), the demographic pattern is the most important thing on the page. Address the anti-abuse norm 4 from chapter 04 (demographic-slice review) directly and describe the specific escalation before any PIP is opened. Do not sanitise the manager's "generational fit" language — flag it.

### Part B — PIP authoring for the case(s) that proceed

For each case where your triage recommendation is "Open PIP," author the full PIP document per chapter 04's five components. Standard sections:

1. **Employee name, role, level, start / end date.**
2. **Statement of performance gap** — specific, evidence-anchored (not "communication issues").
3. **History of prior coaching** — dates of 1:1s and coaching conversations; summary of gaps discussed. Use realistic dates aligned to the case narrative.
4. **PIP goals** — 3–5 measurable, time-bound, observable goals per the chapter 04 "someone reading the goal must be able to say yes or no" standard.
5. **Support commitments** — from the manager and the corporation. Two-sided PIP.
6. **Check-in cadence** — 30-minute weekly manager-employee 1:1s; HRBP monthly; written summary in the shared PIP doc.
7. **Conclusion outcomes** — what a successful completion looks like; what "not on track" looks like; what happens at the end.
8. **Signatures** — employee (acknowledgment, not agreement), manager, HRBP.

For Priya (Case A, assuming triage says proceed): a 60-day PIP with the specific engineering-team goals per chapter 04's concrete example. Address the "team's sprint scope is unrealistic" pushback from Priya — how does the PIP goal design accommodate that reality without becoming un-measurable?

### Part C — HRBP-and-manager support pattern

For each PIP you authored, specify the roles-and-responsibilities across the employee, manager, and HRBP:

1. **Employee's role.** Engagement expectations; how to raise concerns.
2. **Manager's role.** Weekly check-in preparation; support commitments; escalation to HRBP.
3. **HRBP's role.** Coach the manager; review the initial PIP; sit in on the opening conversation; monthly 1:1 with the manager; monthly 1:1 with the employee.
4. **Escalation lines.** When the HRBP escalates to the head of people; when the head of people escalates to the general counsel or outside employment counsel.

Since Jonas (Priya's manager) is a first-time manager 8 months in, author the additional support scaffolding — HRBP joins every check-in for the first two weeks; head of people reviews Jonas's check-in notes weekly; Jonas has an emergency HRBP line if the check-in goes off the rails.

### Part D — Conclusion-decision framework

Chapter 04 identifies three conclusion outcomes — successful completion, extension, release. Author the decision framework:

1. **Data the committee reviews at conclusion.** Written PIP-goal-by-goal assessment from the manager; the employee's response (if any); HRBP's read on the coaching quality; independent peer / stakeholder input where available; retention risk read.
2. **Who is in the room.** Manager, HRBP, second-line manager, head of people. For Riverpoint at Series-B, a 4-person conclusion review.
3. **The three-outcome decision.** The specific bar for each: what does "met the goals" mean vs. "materially progressing" vs. "did not meet."
4. **The extension policy.** One 30-day extension available; do not extend twice per chapter 04. What triggers the extension; what the tightened goals look like; the written notice to the employee.
5. **The release-outcome execution.** Handoff to [chapter 05](../05-termination-playbook.md) — severance-and-release execution per corporation policy, equity treatment per [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/), timing (delivery within one week of PIP conclusion).
6. **The severance policy for PIP-conclusion terminations.** Address the chapter 04 policy question: does Riverpoint offer standard performance severance for a PIP-conclusion release, or does it offer a modestly larger package? Document the policy the corporation will operate.

### Part E — Anti-abuse operating norms

Chapter 04 identifies six anti-abuse norms. Author the operating mechanic for each at Riverpoint:

1. **PIP is a coaching tool** — the corporation's policy on the mutual-separation-and-severance alternative when the outcome is pre-decided; who authorises the mutual-separation offer.
2. **PIP has a real chance of success** — how Riverpoint measures and reports the PIP success rate (one-third to one-half per chapter 04); the head of people's quarterly PIP-outcome report.
3. **PIP is opened by the manager, not by the HRBP** — the operating norm that prevents "HR says I have to do this."
4. **Demographic-slice review at the programme level** — how the head of people slices PIP openings by gender, race / ethnicity, age (40+), disability, and other protected categories per [mod-103 chapter 07](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/07-eeoc-protected-category-framework.md). Quarterly cadence; the specific escalation if a single function or manager is over-representing a protected group.
5. **The employee's side is documented** — the shared PIP doc template that has an explicit space for the employee's written response per check-in.
6. **The PIP does not surprise** — the specific check that the head of people runs before a PIP is opened, confirming the informal-coaching sequence from [chapter 01](../01-performance-management-operating-system.md) actually happened.

### Part F — Post-PIP outcome for Priya

Assume Priya's 60-day PIP has run. At the day-55 conclusion-review meeting, the evidence is:

- Goal 1 (sprint delivery): met in 6 of 8 sprints; the 2 misses were scoped-down mid-sprint per the goal's "written re-scope proposal" clause.
- Goal 2 (PR review turnaround): met — 92% within one business day.
- Goal 3 (PR rework rate): partial — 55% first-round-accepted (target was ≥70%).

The manager's read is "materially better but not fully there." Priya's engagement has been good; she has attended every check-in, delivered on the support commitments, and asked for HRBP support twice.

Author the conclusion-decision analysis — successful completion, one-cycle extension, or release. Defend the decision. Include:

1. The specific reading against the chapter 04 three-outcome framework.
2. The extension-goal design (if extend) or the release-execution runbook (if release).
3. The retention-and-stabilisation plan if the outcome is successful completion (30-60 day stabilisation window; higher-touch 1:1 cadence).
4. The communication to Priya — the manager's script for the conclusion conversation.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 04 is the primary reference. The trigger criteria (decision 1), structure (decision 2), support pattern (decision 3), conclusion decision (decision 4), and anti-abuse norms (decision 5) are the substantive spine.
- The four cases are deliberately mixed — not all four should get a PIP. The triage is the exercise's core.
- Case B (Marcus, sales AE, quota miss after territory change): the ramp-fail vs. capability-fail distinction is a GTM-curriculum matter. Do not author the sales-PIP substance. The exercise wants the recognition that this is a GTM handoff and the decision to defer.
- Case C (Alex, misconduct-adjacent): the harassment / misconduct investigation runs separately from any performance PIP per chapter 04. If misconduct is substantiated, direct termination per [chapter 05](../05-termination-playbook.md). Do not merge the two processes.
- Case D (Robyn, age 52, sole over-50 employee on the team, manager cites "generational fit"): the demographic pattern and the manager's language are red flags. The right answer is not "don't open a PIP" — it is "do not open a PIP until the head of people has done a demographic-slice review of the Customer Support function, has coached the manager on the language, and has separately investigated whether the ticket-volume target increase is being applied consistently across the team."
- The written PIP documents are the load-bearing artifact — they are what will be reviewed in an EEOC charge, in a wrongful-termination claim, or in an outside-counsel review. Be specific.
- The support commitments (from the manager and the corporation) are the two-sided part of the PIP. A PIP that only lists employee goals with no corporate commitment is a stopwatch, not a coaching instrument.
- The demographic-slice review is a pattern-detection instrument, not a quota mechanism. Do not authorize a rating change or a PIP decision on demographic grounds — investigate the pattern and address the underlying issue.
- The conclusion decision for Priya (Part F) is a real judgment call. There is a case for extension (materially progressing, engagement is good) and a case for successful completion (2 of 3 goals met, one partial with good-faith effort). The exercise does not score which decision you make; it scores whether the reasoning is defensible.

- `conclusion-decision-framework.md` — Part D, including the release-outcome handoff to chapter 05 and the corporation severance policy for PIP-conclusion terminations.

- `priya-conclusion-decision.md` — Part F, the day-55 conclusion analysis and decision.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. The triage memo (Part A) covers all four cases with a specific recommendation and reasoning; the reasoning is anchored to chapter 04.
2. Case B is deferred to the GTM curriculum with the ramp-fail vs. capability-fail distinction named; the sales-PIP substance is not authored here.
3. Case C separates the misconduct investigation from any performance PIP; the handoff to a misconduct investigation and (if substantiated) [chapter 05](../05-termination-playbook.md) direct termination is explicit.
4. Case D triggers a demographic-slice review before any PIP is opened; the manager's "generational fit" language is flagged; the ticket-volume target's consistent application across the team is investigated.
5. Priya's PIP (Part B) has all five chapter 04 components — statement of gap, coaching history, measurable goals, support commitments, check-in cadence, conclusion outcomes, signatures. Goals are specific enough that "yes / no" is answerable at the end.
6. The HRBP-and-manager support pattern (Part C) addresses Jonas's first-time-manager status with additional scaffolding.
7. The conclusion-decision framework (Part D) documents the three-outcome decision bar, the extension policy, and the release-outcome handoff to chapter 05 (with equity treatment deferred to mod-105).
8. The severance policy for PIP-conclusion terminations is documented as a policy, not left to case-by-case negotiation.
9. The anti-abuse operating norms (Part E) have a specific operating mechanic for each of the six norms.
10. Priya's conclusion decision (Part F) picks one of the three outcomes and defends it; if extension, the tightened goals are authored; if successful completion, the stabilisation window is designed; if release, the runbook handoff is explicit.
11. Legal-risk flags (Part A) reference [mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) chapters 07 and 08 explicitly.
12. Equity treatment on release is deferred to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); severance-and-release execution is deferred to [chapter 05](../05-termination-playbook.md); both deferrals are named.
13. Nothing left as `[TBD]` or `[FILL IN]`.
