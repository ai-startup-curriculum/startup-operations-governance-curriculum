# 5. Voluntary and involuntary termination playbook

> The exit interview, the involuntary decision framework, the delivery-day script, the severance-and-release agreement (post-Speak Out Act, OWBPA-compliant for the 40+ pool), and the final-pay / COBRA mechanics.

## Motivation

Every corporation eventually terminates employment — voluntarily on the employee's side, involuntarily on the corporation's side, or by mutual agreement. Each pathway has:

- A **before**: the decision or resignation itself, and how it is captured.
- A **during**: the delivery-day conversation and the paperwork.
- An **after**: the final pay, benefits continuation, equity treatment, and the alumni-relationship signal.

The failure modes are legal, financial, and reputational at the same time:

- A voluntary resignation is accepted informally by email, no exit interview is captured, and the corporation loses the single most information-rich moment in the employee lifecycle.
- An involuntary termination is delivered by an unprepared manager alone in a room; the meeting runs 90 minutes; the employee hears "let's talk about how we can fix this"; the corporation now has a wrongful-termination claim and a demoralised team.
- The severance agreement is copied from a 2018 template; it contains a blanket non-disparagement and a non-disclosure clause that cover sexual-harassment allegations; the Speak Out Act 2022 voids those clauses to the extent they apply; the corporation has spent legal budget on an agreement whose most contentious clauses are unenforceable.
- The corporation offers severance to a 62-year-old employee under a template with a 21-day consideration period and forgets the OWBPA disclosure requirement for the age-40+ pool; the release is challenged and defeated.
- Final pay in California is not delivered on the last day; the corporation owes waiting-time penalties at the employee's daily rate for up to 30 days.

This chapter builds the playbook that makes each termination — voluntary or involuntary — a boring, correct, defensible operation.

> **Legal-not-legal-advice caveat.** Termination and severance mechanics are dense with federal and state law and the state layer is highly variable. Every fact pattern in this chapter references specific statutes; before executing a termination in a given jurisdiction, verify current law with employment counsel. See [resources.md](./resources.md) for the primary-source citations.

## The two branches

Termination decomposes into two branches:

- **Voluntary**: the employee resigns. The corporation's job is to receive the resignation, capture the exit interview, and execute a clean offboarding.
- **Involuntary**: the corporation initiates the termination for cause, for performance, or because the role no longer exists (individual role elimination — for group role elimination see [chapter 06](./06-rif-and-layoff-playbook-under-warn.md)).

Some terminations sit ambiguously between the two — the "mutual separation" where an employee is offered a package to resign rather than proceed through a PIP or performance-plus-severance track. Practically, mutual separations are handled with the involuntary-branch mechanics (severance-and-release documentation, delivery choreography) with the outcome framed as a voluntary resignation.

## Branch A — Voluntary resignation and the exit interview

**Receive the resignation.** The mainstream pattern:

- Employee delivers resignation in a 1:1 with their manager, ideally with two weeks' notice (though at-will means neither party is legally required — see [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/)).
- Manager acknowledges the resignation, thanks the employee, does not press for the reason in the moment.
- Manager notifies HRBP / head of people the same day. HRBP acknowledges resignation to employee via email within 24 hours and initiates the offboarding checklist.
- If the resignation was verbal, HRBP confirms in writing (email is sufficient) with the last day of employment.

**The exit-interview instrument.** Every departing employee is offered an exit interview — never mandatory, always meaningful. The mainstream pattern is a 30–60 minute conversation in the last week of employment, conducted by:

- The HRBP or head of people (not the direct manager — the manager is often part of the reason for departure);
- Or, for very senior employees, the CEO or another executive outside the reporting chain.

A defensible exit-interview instrument covers:

1. **What is driving the decision?** (Multiple choice: compensation, growth path, manager, team, product / mission, personal / family, external offer, other; plus a narrative field.)
2. **What worked?** Culture, manager, team, tooling, growth, comp, benefits — where were we strong.
3. **What did not work?** Same axes.
4. **What is one thing you would change if you were CEO for a day?**
5. **Would you rehire yourself into a similar role at this company in 12–24 months?** (Signals alumni-network interest and helps mark the file — see [chapter 08](./08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md).)
6. **Anything you want to raise about your manager, team, or a specific incident?** (A last, protected opportunity to flag misconduct that the employee did not feel safe raising while employed.)

**Data handling.** Exit-interview data is confidential to the head of people and the HRBP; aggregated data (patterns, themes) is shared with executives quarterly. Do not share raw exit-interview text with the departing employee's manager — it collapses the honesty of the instrument for the next departing employee.

**Feedback loop.** The exit-interview data closes back into the performance-management operating system, the manager-training programme, and the corporation's culture programme in [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/). See [chapter 08](./08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md) for the mechanics.

**Voluntary offboarding checklist.** The HRBP runs a standard checklist:

- Confirm last day, transition plan, and knowledge-transfer artifacts.
- Coordinate final-pay delivery (see below).
- Deliver COBRA notice and benefits-continuation paperwork (see below).
- Communicate equity treatment — post-termination exercise window per [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) equity policy.
- Access revocation: coordinate with IT for account offboarding at end of last day.
- Return of corporation property (laptop, hardware, badges).
- Alumni-network invitation and continued-benefits summary (LinkedIn recommendation from manager if warranted, alumni Slack channel if the corporation runs one).

## Branch B — Involuntary termination: the decision framework

Every involuntary termination fits one of three categories, and the process is meaningfully different for each.

### Category 1 — Misconduct

Termination for a specific policy violation, dishonesty, harassment, safety violation, or criminal act.

**Process.**

- Investigation before termination. The HRBP or an outside investigator conducts a documented investigation (interview witnesses, review evidence, interview the accused with an opportunity to respond). Standard tools: [i-Sight / Case IQ](https://www.i-sight.com/), [HR Acuity](https://www.hracuity.com/), or a documented Google Doc for smaller companies.
- Decision by the head of people, general counsel, and (typically) the CEO after the investigation concludes.
- Termination delivered as summary termination — typically the same day, sometimes with immediate access revocation.
- Severance is generally not offered for termination-for-cause; if it is offered (to secure a release of claims), it is meaningfully smaller than the corporation's standard performance severance.
- Employment counsel review is essentially always warranted before a for-cause termination is delivered — the misconduct-termination category is the highest legal-risk category and the highest likelihood of subsequent EEOC / state-agency claim.

### Category 2 — Performance

Termination after a PIP or after documented performance issues that were coached but not resolved (see [chapter 04](./04-pip-mechanics.md)).

**Process.**

- Manager, HRBP, and second-line manager align on the decision.
- Head of people signs off.
- Termination delivered via the delivery-day script below within days of PIP conclusion or the coaching decision.
- Severance is typically offered per the corporation's default severance-and-release policy — see the severance-and-release section below.
- Employment counsel review of the severance-and-release agreement is standard, especially for age-40+ employees (OWBPA requirements).

### Category 3 — Role elimination (individual)

Termination because the specific role no longer exists — a reorganisation collapses the position, the function was outsourced, the strategic priority shifted.

**Process.**

- Executive team documents the business justification for the role elimination (kept in the corporate record; may be relevant to subsequent discrimination-claim defence).
- Head of people confirms there is no reasonable internal-mobility fit for the employee (see [chapter 03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)).
- Termination delivered via the delivery-day script.
- Severance typically at or above the corporation's default level; the "no-fault" nature of role elimination often warrants a somewhat more generous package.
- If more than a handful of role eliminations happen in the same window, evaluate whether the collection triggers WARN Act obligations under [chapter 06](./06-rif-and-layoff-playbook-under-warn.md).

## The delivery-day choreography

The single failure mode that turns a defensible termination into a lawsuit is a poorly-run delivery-day meeting.

**Preparation (day before).**

- Manager, HRBP, and head of people rehearse the meeting. A single-page script is written and reviewed.
- Access-revocation timing is coordinated with IT — typically at the moment the meeting ends, so the employee cannot be locked out mid-meeting or leave the meeting to a still-live account they can be tempted to misuse.
- Severance and release paperwork is prepared (see below); a paper copy and a PDF are ready to hand over at the end of the meeting.
- The manager or HRBP identifies who will handle immediate post-meeting logistics: escorting the employee, arranging return of property, transportation home if warranted.

**The meeting itself.**

- **Attendees:** Manager, HRBP (or head of people at pre-Series-A / Series-A scale where there is no HRBP), and — in most jurisdictions — a witness. The witness is typically the HRBP or head of people; the manager should not deliver a termination alone.
- **Location:** Private room. In-person if the employee is in the office; video call if remote. Never in a public space, never on the employee's team channel.
- **Length:** Under 15 minutes. A termination meeting is not a coaching conversation and it is not a debate. The decision is made; the meeting communicates the decision.
- **Structure:**
  1. Manager opens — "We're here because we've made the decision to end your employment with the corporation, effective [today / end of day]. This is not a conversation about whether — the decision is made. I want to walk you through what happens next."
  2. Manager gives the one-sentence reason (framed factually: "the decision follows the conclusion of the PIP that closed on [date]" or "the role is being eliminated as part of the team restructure"). No blame, no anger, no elaboration.
  3. HRBP takes over on logistics — last day, final pay, severance-and-release paperwork ("here is the package we are offering, in exchange for a release of claims; you have [X] days to consider it, and we recommend you consult with counsel"), COBRA notice, equity treatment, property return, access timing.
  4. HRBP asks whether the employee has questions on logistics. Not on the decision.
  5. HRBP closes — "We know this is difficult. We appreciate the work you've done here. Someone will meet you at the door to help you gather your things."
- **Empathy, finality, clarity.** These are the three qualities of a well-delivered termination meeting. Empathy: acknowledge that this is hard. Finality: the decision is made and is not up for debate today. Clarity: the employee walks out knowing exactly what happens in the next hour, day, week.

**Post-meeting logistics.**

- IT revokes access at meeting close.
- Manager notifies the team the same day (within 1–2 hours) with a short factual message ("X is no longer with the corporation, effective today; we will discuss transition plans in tomorrow's standup") — do not disclose the reason.
- HRBP schedules a follow-up 48–72 hours out to answer any questions the employee has on severance / benefits / COBRA / equity.
- Property return, alumni programme signalling, and reference-check policy per the corporation's standard offboarding process.

## The severance-and-release agreement

Severance is not a legal requirement in the US in most non-RIF situations (see [chapter 06](./06-rif-and-layoff-playbook-under-warn.md) for the RIF context). Companies offer it for three reasons:

1. **To secure a release of claims** (the primary reason from a legal-risk standpoint).
2. **To treat the departing employee well** (retention of the alumni relationship, cultural signal to remaining employees).
3. **To smooth the financial transition** to next employment.

### The default severance policy

Every corporation should have a documented default severance policy — the schedule the head of people can execute without a bespoke negotiation every time. A mainstream Series-B pattern:

- **Non-executive:** 2 weeks of base pay per year of service, with a minimum of 4 weeks and a maximum of 16 weeks. Health-plan continuation subsidy for the equivalent COBRA period. Standard release-of-claims requirement.
- **Manager / director:** minimum 8 weeks. Otherwise same structure.
- **VP+ executive:** governed by the executive severance policy per [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) and typically covered by the executive's employment agreement — see [chapter 07](./07-executive-team-offboarding.md).

The default is a policy, not a promise — non-executive employees typically do not have a contractual right to severance; the policy exists to standardise the offer.

### The release-of-claims agreement

Standard content, with statutory-required elements:

- **Release language.** Employee releases the corporation from all known and unknown claims arising from employment, subject to statutory carve-outs. The release cannot waive certain non-waivable rights (right to file a charge with the EEOC or similar state agencies, right to unemployment insurance, right to workers' compensation, right to vested retirement benefits).
- **Consideration.** The severance payment is consideration for the release. Without severance or another benefit above what the employee is already entitled to, the release is unenforceable.
- **Consultation notice.** "The corporation advises you to consult with counsel of your choice before signing." Required by OWBPA for age-40+ (see below), sound practice for everyone.
- **Consideration period.** Time to consider the offer — length varies by law (see OWBPA below).
- **Revocation period.** Time to revoke after signing — again varies by law.
- **Non-admission clause.** The agreement is not an admission of wrongdoing by either party.
- **Return of property and confidentiality clauses.** Continuing confidentiality obligations under the employee's PIIA from [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/); return-of-property confirmation.
- **Non-disparagement clause.** Historically standard; substantially constrained by recent federal law and NLRB decisions — see the next section.

### Post-Speak Out Act carve-outs for sexual assault and sexual harassment

The **Speak Out Act of 2022** (Pub. L. 117-224, codified at 42 U.S.C. §§ 19401–19404) voids the enforceability of *pre-dispute* nondisclosure and non-disparagement clauses that relate to a sexual-assault or sexual-harassment dispute. Practical consequence for the severance agreement:

- A nondisclosure or non-disparagement clause that would prevent a departing employee from speaking about a sexual-assault or sexual-harassment matter is void as against public policy when it purports to bind *before* the dispute has arisen. (The Speak Out Act does not, on its face, invalidate a *post-dispute* settlement negotiated after a specific dispute is on the table.)
- Modern severance agreements include an **explicit carve-out** — "nothing in this agreement prevents the employee from disclosing or discussing facts related to any alleged sexual assault or sexual harassment" — both because the Speak Out Act would render a broad clause unenforceable and because the carve-out is often a legal requirement or best practice under overlapping state law.

Related federal framework worth naming:

- **Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021** (Pub. L. 117-90, codified at 9 U.S.C. §§ 401–402), signed 2022 — voids *pre-dispute* arbitration agreements and class-action waivers as applied to sexual-assault and sexual-harassment claims, at the election of the claimant.
- **Internal Revenue Code § 162(q)** — no tax deduction for settlements or attorneys' fees paid in connection with sexual-harassment or sexual-abuse matters if the settlement is subject to a nondisclosure agreement. Practically, this discourages the NDA-plus-settlement pattern for these claims.

**State-law overlay.** Multiple states have gone further than federal law. California's **Silenced No More Act** (SB 331, effective 2022) prohibits confidentiality clauses in settlement and separation agreements that would restrict the employee's ability to discuss any unlawful workplace conduct — not only sexual-harassment claims but all Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) protected-category claims. New York, New Jersey, Illinois, Oregon, Washington, and others have analogous statutes with varying scope. The severance-agreement template should include a state-law-specific carve-out for any state in which the departing employee worked — do not rely on the federal Speak Out Act as the only carve-out. <!-- needs-research: verify the current status and precise scope of California SB 331, New York General Obligations Law § 5-336, New Jersey S121, Illinois Workplace Transparency Act, Oregon Workplace Fairness Act, Washington RCW 49.44.210, and other state analogues before publishing a canonical list — the state landscape is actively evolving. -->

### OWBPA and the age-40+ release

The **Older Workers Benefit Protection Act** (OWBPA), which amended the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), imposes specific requirements for the waiver of ADEA claims by employees age 40 or older (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)). For a release of ADEA claims to be knowing and voluntary, it must:

1. Be **written in a manner calculated to be understood**.
2. Specifically refer to rights or claims arising **under the ADEA**.
3. **Not waive future claims** — only claims arising up to the effective date.
4. Be in exchange for **consideration in addition to** anything the employee is already entitled to.
5. **Advise the employee in writing to consult an attorney** before signing.
6. Provide the employee at least **21 days to consider the agreement** if the release is offered to a single employee, or at least **45 days** if the release is offered as part of an "exit incentive or other employment termination program offered to a group or class of employees" (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)(F)).
7. Provide at least **7 days after signing to revoke** the agreement, during which time the agreement is not enforceable.

For a group termination that triggers the 45-day period, the OWBPA also requires the corporation to provide, in writing to each affected employee, **information about the class of employees covered by the program** — job titles and ages of individuals eligible or selected for the program, and the same for individuals in the same job classification or organizational unit who were not selected (29 U.S.C. § 626(f)(1)(H)). This is the "OWBPA disclosure" that becomes central to RIF execution in [chapter 06](./06-rif-and-layoff-playbook-under-warn.md).

**Practical consequences.** The severance template branches by age: age-40+ single-employee releases use a 21-day consideration period with a 7-day revocation window; age-under-40 releases can use shorter periods (e.g., 5–7 business days) but should still advise the employee to consult counsel. Executing a 5-day consideration period against a 45-year-old employee produces an unenforceable release; the corporation has paid severance and received nothing in exchange.

### Non-disparagement and the NLRB overlay

Beyond the Speak Out Act sexual-harassment carve-out, a further constraint on non-disparagement clauses in severance agreements comes from the National Labor Relations Board. In *McLaren Macomb*, 372 NLRB No. 58 (2023), the NLRB held that severance agreements offered to non-supervisory employees that contain overly broad confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses violate Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act — because they tend to chill employees' protected right to discuss terms and conditions of employment with coworkers. Practical consequence:

- Non-disparagement and confidentiality clauses in severance agreements for non-supervisory employees should be narrowly tailored — typically limited to disparagement of the corporation's products and services, or to genuine trade-secret confidentiality — and should include an explicit Section 7 carve-out ("nothing in this agreement prohibits the employee from engaging in activity protected by Section 7 of the National Labor Relations Act, including discussion of wages, hours, and working conditions").
- The *McLaren Macomb* framework applies primarily to non-supervisory employees; agreements with supervisory / managerial employees have historically been treated with more latitude.
- The severance-agreement template should have counsel review at least annually to reflect NLRB and court developments in this area — the space is actively litigated.

## Final pay and COBRA mechanics

### Final pay

Federal law does not set a general timing rule for final wages after termination; state law does, and the state variance is meaningful.

- **California** (Cal. Labor Code §§ 201–203): involuntary termination — final wages, including accrued vacation, due **on the day of termination**. Voluntary resignation with 72+ hours notice — due **on the last day of work**; without such notice — due **within 72 hours**. Waiting-time penalty under § 203 up to **30 days of wages** for wilful failure to pay.
- **New York** (NY Labor Law § 191): final wages due **not later than the next regularly scheduled payday**.
- **Illinois** (820 ILCS 115/5): final wages due at time of separation if possible, but in any event no later than the next regularly scheduled payday.
- **Texas** (Tex. Labor Code § 61.014): involuntary termination — within **6 days**; voluntary — next regularly scheduled payday.
- **Massachusetts** (M.G.L. c. 149, § 148): involuntary — same day; voluntary — next regularly scheduled payday.
- **Other states.** Consult the state department of labor before finalising the final-pay checklist for any jurisdiction. <!-- needs-research: cross-reference each state's current final-pay statute; the state-by-state table above is illustrative and must be verified per jurisdiction before publication or reliance. -->

**Accrued but unused vacation / PTO.** Also state-variable. In California, accrued vacation is a vested wage that must be paid out at termination; in many other states, payout depends on the corporation's PTO policy and the terms of the employee handbook. The PTO-payout policy for each state of operation should be documented and consistent.

**PEO / HRIS execution.** The payroll platform (Justworks, Rippling, Gusto, Deel, Workday) handles the final-check mechanics — most support a "termination workflow" that issues the final check per state rules. The head of people confirms the final-pay date is on the correct calendar day per the terminating employee's state.

### COBRA and benefits continuation

The **Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1985** (COBRA — ERISA §§ 601–608, 29 U.S.C. §§ 1161–1168; IRC § 4980B) requires most private-sector group health plans that cover 20 or more employees to offer continuation coverage to qualified beneficiaries after certain qualifying events.

**Employer coverage threshold.** COBRA applies to group health plans of employers that had 20 or more employees on more than 50% of typical business days in the prior calendar year. Corporations under 20 employees are not subject to federal COBRA — but many states have "mini-COBRA" statutes that cover smaller employers (California Cal-COBRA, New York, and others).

**Qualifying event and coverage duration.** Termination of employment (other than for gross misconduct) is a qualifying event; coverage duration is up to **18 months** from the qualifying event.

**Notice mechanics.**

- **General notice** at plan enrollment (typically handled by the HRIS / benefits broker at hire).
- **Employer notice to plan administrator** within **30 days** of the qualifying event.
- **Plan administrator notice to qualified beneficiary** within **14 days** of receiving the employer notice.
- **Election period** — qualified beneficiary has **60 days** from later of (a) date of notice or (b) date of loss of coverage to elect COBRA.
- **Payment mechanics** — first premium due within 45 days of election; subsequent premiums monthly with a 30-day grace period.

**Cost.** Under COBRA the beneficiary pays 100% of the premium (both the previous employee share and the previous employer share) plus up to a 2% administrative fee. A corporation may choose to subsidise some or all of the COBRA premium as part of the severance package — this is a discretionary policy decision.

**Vendor execution.** Most benefits brokers and PEOs (Sequoia, Justworks, TriNet, Rippling Benefits, Gusto) either administer COBRA themselves or coordinate with a COBRA-administration vendor (e.g., WEX, Businessolver). The head of people confirms COBRA notice was delivered within the statutory windows; the HRBP tracks each open COBRA event to closure.

**State mini-COBRA layer.** Multiple states (California Cal-COBRA, Connecticut, New York, Texas, others) impose continuation-coverage requirements on employers not subject to federal COBRA, and some extend the duration beyond 18 months. Verify the state statutes for every state in which the corporation employs someone. <!-- needs-research: catalogue current state mini-COBRA statutes and coverage durations; California Cal-COBRA (Cal. ## Equity treatment at termination

Governed by the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) — this chapter defers to that policy for the substantive rules and covers only the *operational* touch points:

- **Post-termination exercise (PTE) window.** ISOs must be exercised within **3 months** of termination to preserve ISO tax treatment under IRC § 422(a)(2); many plans permit a longer window (12–24 months, or in some cases through option expiration) at the cost of ISO status. Confirm the plan's PTE window and communicate it to the departing employee at termination with the exact deadline in writing.
- **Vested vs. unvested.** Unvested equity is typically forfeited at termination; vested equity is either exercisable (options) or continues to be held (RSUs post-liquidity, common stock). The termination confirmation letter should state the exact number of vested shares as of the termination date.
- **Acceleration.** For non-executive terminations, acceleration is rare. For executive terminations, single-trigger and double-trigger acceleration may apply per the executive's grant agreement — see [chapter 07](./07-executive-team-offboarding.md) and [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).

## The involuntary-termination checklist (single-page summary)

The HRBP should carry a single-page checklist that runs from decision to close:

- [ ] Decision documented and approved by head of people (and general counsel for misconduct).
- [ ] Employment counsel review of severance / release agreement (always for age-40+; strongly advised for all).
- [ ] Severance and release paperwork prepared, with state-law carve-outs.
- [ ] Delivery-day meeting scheduled; attendees, script, and IT-access-revocation timing confirmed.
- [ ] Final-pay calculation completed per state law and PTO payout policy.
- [ ] COBRA notice paperwork prepared.
- [ ] Equity summary (vested / unvested, PTE deadline) confirmed with mod-105 policy.
- [ ] Delivery-day meeting held.
- [ ] Same-day: IT access revoked; team notification sent; property return coordinated.
- [ ] 24–48 hours: HRBP follow-up scheduled to answer benefits / equity / severance questions.
- [ ] Severance-and-release signed and returned within the applicable consideration window; revocation window observed before payment.
- [ ] COBRA notice delivered within statutory window; election tracked.
- [ ] Alumni-programme signalling per [chapter 08](./08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md).
- [ ] Post-termination exit-interview offered (voluntary — most involuntarily-terminated employees decline, but the offer is standard).

## Concrete example: a Series-B performance termination

A Series-B B2B company. An L4 engineer completed a 60-day PIP (chapter 04); the PIP outcome is release. The engineer is 34, based in California, on the corporation's standard offer letter and PIIA.

- **Category:** Performance.
- **Approvals:** Manager + HRBP + Director of Engineering + head of people; employment-counsel review of severance and release (with California carve-outs) confirmed.
- **Severance package:** Standard default — 6 weeks base pay (3 years of service, 2 weeks / year, minimum 4); 6 weeks of COBRA premium subsidy; standard release-of-claims with California SB 331 carve-out and NLRA Section 7 carve-out; no non-compete (California prohibits — see [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/)); non-disparagement narrowed to product / service disparagement.
- **Consideration window:** 5 business days (employee is under 40; OWBPA does not apply; California does not impose a longer consideration window for standard performance separations). Documented advice to consult counsel.
- **Delivery day:** Manager + HRBP, 15-minute meeting, video call (engineer is remote). Access revoked at meeting end. Team notification within the hour.
- **Final pay:** Delivered on the day of termination per Cal. Labor Code § 201, including accrued PTO payout. Executed via Rippling termination workflow.
- **COBRA:** Notice delivered by benefits broker within 14 days of the corporation's notice to the plan administrator (which was sent same-day as termination); 60-day election window opens on receipt.
- **Equity:** 90-day PTE window per the corporation's plan; vested-share summary in the termination-confirmation email; corporation's stock-plan administrator (Carta) notified.
- **Signed release** returned within 5 days; 7-day revocation window skipped (California does not require; employee is under 40); severance payment processed on the next payroll after the release effective date.

Total elapsed time from PIP-conclusion decision to closed severance-and-release: ~10 business days. Total legal exposure: minimal — the process followed the corporation's documented template, the PIP evidence supports the performance rationale, and the release covers all standard claims.

## Summary

- Voluntary and involuntary termination are two branches with a shared operating spine (final pay, COBRA, equity, alumni).
- The voluntary branch is anchored by the exit interview — the single most information-rich moment in the employee lifecycle, and one most companies waste.
- Involuntary termination sorts into misconduct, performance, and role elimination — each has a distinct approval, evidence, and severance shape.
- The delivery-day meeting is a 15-minute, three-person, empathy-finality-clarity conversation. It is not a debate; the decision is made before the meeting.
- The severance-and-release agreement must reflect the **Speak Out Act 2022** (pre-dispute NDA / non-disparagement voided for sexual assault / harassment), the **OWBPA** requirements for age-40+ (21-day consideration for single-employee release, 45-day for group programme, 7-day revocation, OWBPA disclosure schedule), the state Silenced No More statutes, and the **NLRA Section 7 / *McLaren Macomb*** constraint on non-supervisory-employee non-disparagement.
- Final pay is state-variable — California requires same-day for involuntary, and § 203 waiting-time penalties are real. COBRA notice runs on 30-day / 14-day / 60-day windows and is administered by the benefits broker or PEO.
- Equity mechanics defer to the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); this chapter owns the operational touch points.
- See [exercise-05](./exercises/exercise-05-termination-playbook-and-severance-release-drafting.md) for the drafting drill.
