# 7. Executive-team offboarding

> The CEO-executive difficult-conversation script, the board-communication choreography, the exec-severance-and-release execution with material board-decision items (CoC acceleration, indemnification continuation, board-seat resignation), and the employee-communications playbook.

## Motivation

Executive departures are qualitatively different from any other termination the corporation handles. The executive holds material non-public information, has direct or indirect fiduciary duties (as an officer under DGCL § 142 — see [mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) and mod-111), often sits on a compensation agreement negotiated years ago whose terms are load-bearing today, and their departure is a signal to the board, to the employees, to customers, and (for public or IPO-track companies) to the market.

The failure modes are especially costly:

- The CEO decides on Monday that the CFO must go and delivers the news that afternoon; the board hears about it Wednesday from the CFO's LinkedIn post; the audit committee (which the CFO serves) is embarrassed, the board is angry, and the credibility hit lasts a year.
- The corporation terminates a VP whose grant agreement provides double-trigger CoC acceleration; the corporation is quietly in early diligence for a strategic transaction; the termination triggers acceleration under the grant that the corporation was not modelling — the option pool is now under-provisioned by 2% of fully-diluted shares.
- The exec-severance-and-release is negotiated in the room by the CEO without employment counsel present; the CEO agrees to a mutual non-disparagement clause that binds the corporation broadly enough that the corporation is now constrained in what it can say when the next reference check comes in on the departing exec.
- The employee announcement of the exec departure runs in cliché ("Alex has decided to pursue new opportunities") when everyone in the building knows the exec was managed out; the remaining executives lose credibility for the next difficult message.

This chapter builds the playbook for the difficult conversation, the board process around it, the severance mechanics with attention to the equity and indemnification interactions with [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) and mod-111, and the internal / external communications choreography.

## The five operating decisions

1. **The CEO-executive difficult-conversation script.** How the CEO delivers the decision to the departing executive.
2. **The board-communication choreography.** When and how the board is informed, and where board authorisation is required.
3. **Exec severance and release execution.** The mechanics, with attention to CoC acceleration, indemnification continuation, and board-seat resignation.
4. **Employee-communications playbook.** How the corporation announces the departure internally.
5. **External-communications choreography.** Investors, customers, press — where the exec role and the corporation's profile require external comms.

## Decision 1 — The CEO-executive difficult-conversation script

Executive-level "you need to go" conversations are, in practice, the hardest conversations a CEO delivers. Two canonical references from the founder / CEO literature: Ben Horowitz's *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (Chapter 5, "Take Care of the People, the Products, and the Profits — in That Order"; sections on firing an executive) and Reid Hoffman's *The Alliance* and related writing on "tour of duty" framing for executive engagements ending. These are not doctrinal — they are the accepted operating canon that most first-time CEOs learn from.

The mainstream script pattern:

**Preparation.**

- The CEO drafts a memo for themselves (not sent) that answers three questions: (a) what specifically is the gap or the change that has led to this decision? (b) what has been tried in the last 3–6 months? (c) what will the corporation say publicly about the departure?
- The CEO aligns with the board chair (or lead independent director) and, for CFO / GC-level departures, with the audit-committee chair or the compensation-committee chair, *before* the conversation with the executive.
- The CEO aligns with the head of people, general counsel, and outside employment counsel on the offer package.
- A separation-and-release draft is prepared before the conversation happens. The CEO does not offer to "figure out the package later" in the room — that pattern leads to a bespoke, negotiated, expensive outcome.

**The conversation itself.**

- **Setting.** Private, in-person if possible, at a moment that does not immediately precede a public event (board meeting, earnings call, all-hands).
- **Length.** 30–45 minutes — longer than a non-executive termination because the executive has earned the space to react, ask questions, and process — shorter than a coaching conversation.
- **Structure:**
  1. CEO opens with the decision — "I've made the decision that it's time for us to move on from your role as [title], effective [date to be discussed]." No throat-clearing, no build-up, no ambiguity about whether this is a conversation about performance or a decision.
  2. CEO gives the *why* in one to two paragraphs. Honest, direct, and specific — but not litigious. "The go-forward business needs a different capability" or "the [X] we discussed for the last two quarters has not landed, and we are out of runway to keep trying" — not "you failed at [X]." The point is to communicate the decision clearly, not to relitigate the last six months.
  3. CEO acknowledges what the executive has contributed. Genuinely, not perfunctorily. Executives who feel unseen on their way out are the ones who become active detractors after.
  4. CEO transitions to the how: "The head of people is going to walk you through the package we are offering. I want us to leave this conversation with a clear plan for the transition, the communications, and the timing."
  5. Head of people (present, or joining after the CEO's opening) walks through the offer — severance, benefits continuation, equity treatment (including any CoC-acceleration mechanics), indemnification continuation, non-disparagement / mutual-non-disparagement, transition-services agreement if any.
  6. CEO and executive close on next steps — communications timing, transition responsibilities, effective date.
- **The mutual-agreement framing.** The mainstream pattern is to close on a mutually-agreed "step-down" or "transition" arrangement rather than an involuntary termination — with the executive's cooperation on messaging in exchange for a package. This framing is favoured by both parties: the executive avoids "fired" on the record; the corporation gets a clean transition and typically a broader mutual release.
- **When a mutual agreement is not possible.** If the executive rejects the offer or the fact pattern is misconduct-based, the corporation proceeds with an involuntary termination — with counsel involvement from that moment forward, board notice on an expedited schedule, and a much tighter approach to communications.

**Horowitz canon (paraphrased).** "Do not make it about them — make it about the company. Do not litigate the past — communicate the decision. Take responsibility for the hire and the situation, not blame for the outcome. The executive is going to talk about how you handled this for the rest of their career. Handle it like that matters."

## Decision 2 — Board-communication choreography

Executive departures are board matters. The choreography is:

**Board notice before executive announcement.** The CEO informs the board (or at minimum the board chair, the lead independent director, and any relevant committee chairs) *before* the departure is announced internally or externally. This is a non-negotiable norm — for both fiduciary reasons (board members are officers of the corporation whose duty of care requires them to be informed on material matters) and relationship reasons (a board member who reads about a C-level departure on Twitter is a board member who will not trust the CEO on the next difficult message).

**Board authorisation when material.** Certain executive departures require formal board or committee action:

- **CFO / General Counsel / CEO / other named executive officer** — often listed by name in the corporation's constitutional documents (bylaws) or in the corporation's SEC filings (for public / near-public companies). Departure and replacement typically requires board consent or resolution under DGCL § 142 (see [mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) and mod-111 for the officer-appointment framework).
- **Executive-severance terms above the corporation's default policy** — typically requires compensation-committee authorisation under the committee's charter per [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/). Severance packages that materially exceed pre-authorised severance policy limits require an explicit resolution.
- **Board-seat resignation** — if the departing executive holds a board seat, the resignation is executed via a formal resignation letter delivered to the board chair, effective on the date specified. The board may need to fill the resulting vacancy under DGCL § 223 or amend the bylaws if the seat was reserved for the role.
- **8-K / SEC disclosure** — for public companies, departure of a "named executive officer" (as defined in Item 5.02 of Form 8-K) requires disclosure within 4 business days. The 8-K contents typically include the departure date and, if severance is material, the terms of the separation agreement. Coordination with securities counsel and IR is required from the moment the CEO decides on the departure.

**Board committee involvement.**

- **Compensation committee.** Reviews and approves the executive-severance package if it exceeds the pre-authorised policy. See [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) for the comp-committee charter and delegation framework.
- **Audit committee.** Involved specifically for CFO or Controller departures — the audit committee has an independent line to the CFO / Controller under exchange listing standards (Nasdaq 5605(c)(3); NYSE 303A.07), and any turnover in that seat is a matter the audit committee should be involved in from the start.
- **Nominating & governance committee (or full board where no such committee exists).** Involved in the recruitment and succession planning for the replacement.

**Timing.** For a mutually-agreed executive departure, the mainstream sequence:

- Day -7 to -3: CEO aligns with board chair and relevant committee chairs.
- Day -3 to -1: CEO holds difficult conversation with executive; separation-and-release paperwork moves toward execution.
- Day 0: Written board notice (email or memo) circulated to the full board with the departure summary, transition plan, and communications plan.
- Day 0 to +1: If board authorisation is required, board consent (via unanimous written consent, or via a formally-noticed meeting for a material matter) is executed.
- Day 0 to +2: Internal employee announcement (see decision 4).
- Day 0 to +2: External announcement if warranted (see decision 5).
- Day 0 to +4 (public companies): 8-K filed if required.

## Decision 3 — Exec severance and release execution

The executive severance-and-release mechanically resembles the [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) individual-release template, with three material additions.

### Addition 1 — Change-of-control (CoC) acceleration and severance interaction

Executive grant agreements — from the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) — often include CoC-triggered acceleration. Two canonical patterns:

- **Single-trigger acceleration.** Unvested equity accelerates automatically on a change of control. Uncommon for executive grants at venture-backed companies; more common at pre-IPO for founders.
- **Double-trigger acceleration.** Unvested equity accelerates on the combined occurrence of (a) a change of control and (b) an involuntary termination or resignation-for-good-reason within a specified window (typically 12 months) after the change of control.

**Interactions with executive severance:**

- A **standalone executive-severance clause** (typical for VP+ hires) — cash severance and often equity acceleration triggered on involuntary termination or resignation-for-good-reason, independent of any CoC. Common terms: 6–12 months of base pay + bonus; 6–12 months of health-plan continuation; 6–12 months of equity vesting-acceleration for a straight involuntary termination. The corporation may not have this in place for all executives; for those without a contractual severance, the corporation may still offer a package as a matter of policy.
- **Combined with CoC acceleration**, an executive terminated outside a CoC context receives the standalone severance treatment; the same executive terminated within a CoC window receives the double-trigger acceleration plus (if not overlapping) the standalone severance.

**Practical execution.** The severance-and-release agreement must (a) identify each equity award, (b) state which awards accelerate under each trigger, (c) recite the corporation's calculation of vested / unvested / accelerated share counts as of the termination date, (d) specify the post-termination exercise window for each option grant, and (e) note any modifications to the plan-default terms.

**Common failure.** Executives negotiate their offer letters years before departure, the corporation has evolved the equity plan since, and the grant agreements have terms that conflict with the current plan. The corporation and the departing executive both need employment counsel review of the actual grant agreement in force, not a summary.

### Addition 2 — Indemnification continuation

Officers of a Delaware corporation are typically covered by indemnification obligations under DGCL § 145 (see [mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/)) and often by an individual **indemnification agreement** executed at appointment. Indemnification survives departure — a former officer remains indemnified for actions taken during their service.

The separation agreement should:

- **Reaffirm indemnification** — the corporation acknowledges its continuing indemnification obligation under the corporation's bylaws, the DGCL, and any indemnification agreement.
- **Reaffirm D&O insurance coverage** — the departing executive is a covered person under the corporation's D&O tower (both the ongoing "side A / B / C" coverage and any "tail" / run-off coverage that would apply to a change of control). Confirm that the corporation will maintain D&O insurance covering the executive's period of service on standard terms.
- **Confirm access to corporate records** — the departing executive may need to defend a subsequent claim brought against them for acts taken during service; the separation agreement typically acknowledges the executive's right to access records reasonably necessary for defence.

Indemnification is not something the executive is asking for as a favour — it is a right they have as a former officer, and the separation agreement documents the continuation of that right rather than granting it.

### Addition 3 — Board-seat resignation, transition, and non-compete / non-solicit

- **Board-seat resignation.** If the executive holds a board seat, they execute a formal resignation letter effective on a specified date. For public / near-public companies, the resignation may itself require 8-K disclosure under Item 5.02(a).
- **Transition-services agreement.** For a senior executive, a common pattern is a 30–90 day "transition services" or "advisory" period during which the executive continues to be available (typically on a limited-time basis) to support the transition. Compensation is typically continued at the standard salary or converted to a consulting fee; equity may continue to vest during this period. The transition arrangement is often the practical mechanism for delivering CoC-triggered treatment or bridging to a specific vesting event.
- **Non-compete / non-solicit.** State law varies — see [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/). California, North Dakota, Oklahoma, and Minnesota substantially prohibit non-competes; the FTC's non-compete rule remains in ongoing litigation and its ultimate status is uncertain. Non-solicit clauses are more broadly enforceable but also state-variable. For executive severance packages, any non-compete / non-solicit obligation should be tightly scoped, backed by explicit consideration in the package, and reviewed against the executive's employment state — a non-compete that would be enforceable in New York may be void as against public policy in California. <!-- needs-research: verify current status of the FTC non-compete rule (16 C.F.R. ## Decision 4 — Employee-communications playbook

Executive departures signal to the workforce more than any other termination. The workforce is trying to read four things from the announcement: what happened, why, what does it mean for the company, what does it mean for me. The playbook exists to answer those four questions clearly without saying anything the corporation cannot back up.

**The written announcement.** Sent by the CEO to the whole company, typically the same day the executive's role transitions. Structure:

- **What.** "Alex Chen is transitioning out of the [role] effective [date]. [Interim / permanent] leadership arrangement: [name / plan]."
- **Why.** One to two sentences of the honest, cleared reason. If the departure was mutual, "Alex and I have decided together that it's time for [role]-in-transition wording"; if performance-driven, some version that does not violate any mutual-non-disparagement in the separation agreement and does not litigate the past in public; if role-elimination, the org-change context.
- **What next.** Interim reporting arrangement, search process, transition plan.
- **Recognition.** One paragraph of what the executive contributed — genuine, specific. This paragraph is often the piece the departing executive most wants to see, and it is often the piece the corporation most sloppily writes.

**Cliché avoidance.** "Alex has decided to pursue new opportunities" is a cliché that everyone reads through. If the corporation cannot say anything honest, it should say very little rather than say something obviously euphemistic. "Alex is transitioning out of the role, and we're announcing the interim reporting arrangement below" is a shorter and more honest sentence than the cliché.

**Live all-hands.** Following the written announcement, the CEO holds a live all-hands (or a called meeting for the affected teams if the exec was function-specific) within 24 hours. The all-hands answers the "what does it mean for us" question directly — the go-forward plan, the interim arrangement, the search process. The CEO takes questions.

**Team-specific comms.** The executive's direct reports get an additional 1:1 or small-group conversation the same day — they need more context (transition responsibilities, near-term priorities) and more time to process.

**Confidentiality of the separation package.** The details of the executive severance are almost always kept confidential to the CEO, head of people, general counsel, comp committee, and (for public companies) the audit committee. The employee announcement does not describe the package.

## Decision 5 — External-communications choreography

For public / near-public companies, executive departures may require:

- **8-K filing** under Item 5.02 for departure of a named executive officer, within 4 business days.
- **Press release** if the departure is material to the corporation's public profile or if the executive is a public figure (typical for CEO, CFO, or high-profile roles).
- **IR outreach** to key institutional investors and analysts, briefed by the CEO or head of IR, coordinated with securities counsel to avoid Reg FD violations.
- **Customer / partner outreach** for executives with public customer-facing roles (CRO, Head of Product, CCO) — key customer accounts are typically briefed by the CEO or the successor within a business day.

For pre-public companies, the external comms depth is a function of the executive's public profile. A CEO departure at a Series-B company is a story regardless; a VP Engineering departure may not require any external comms beyond routine networking updates.

## The exec-offboarding checklist (single-page summary)

- [ ] CEO decision documented; board chair and relevant committee chairs aligned before the conversation.
- [ ] Separation-and-release paperwork drafted with employment counsel, including CoC-acceleration analysis, indemnification continuation, board-seat resignation (if applicable), non-compete / non-solicit terms scoped to state.
- [ ] CEO-executive difficult conversation held.
- [ ] Board written notice or board consent circulated per the materiality of the departure.
- [ ] Compensation committee approval of package (if above pre-authorised policy).
- [ ] Audit committee brief (if CFO / Controller departure).
- [ ] Employee announcement drafted; recognition paragraph reviewed with departing exec if possible.
- [ ] Live all-hands scheduled within 24 hours of announcement.
- [ ] Direct-report 1:1s scheduled the same day.
- [ ] External comms (8-K, press release, IR outreach, customer briefings) coordinated per profile.
- [ ] Separation-and-release signed within OWBPA consideration window (45 days if age 40+ and offered as part of a program; 21 days if age 40+ and single-employee); revocation window observed.
- [ ] Final pay, COBRA notice, and equity administration per [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md).
- [ ] Board-seat resignation letter executed and filed with corporate records; bylaws / board composition updated per DGCL § 223.
- [ ] D&O tail / indemnification confirmation letter delivered to executive.
- [ ] Alumni-programme signalling per [chapter 08](./08-alumni-rehire-eligibility-and-feedback-loop.md).

## Concrete example: a CFO transition at Series-C

A Series-C SaaS company. Board and CEO have concluded over the course of two quarters that the current CFO is not the right operator for the pending Series-D fundraise and IPO-track. The CEO and audit-committee chair have aligned on the direction; the compensation committee has pre-cleared the exec-severance package framework.

- **Board choreography.** CEO aligns with board chair and audit-committee chair four weeks in advance; comp-committee chair confirms package parameters two weeks in advance; full board is briefed via written notice the day of the CEO-CFO conversation; board consents (by unanimous written consent under DGCL § 141(f)) two days later to the transition, the interim CFO appointment (an existing VP Finance stepping up), and the search for a permanent CFO.
- **CEO-CFO conversation.** Held in the CEO's office on a Wednesday afternoon. 40 minutes. CEO opens with the decision; head of people joins after 10 minutes to walk through the package. Mutually agreed transition: CFO steps out of the CFO role effective in 30 days; provides transition-advisor services on a limited-time basis for an additional 60 days.
- **Package.** 12 months of base salary; 12 months of target bonus prorated; 12 months of accelerated equity vesting (per the CFO's employment agreement); 12 months of COBRA subsidy; standard release-of-claims with California SB 331 carve-out (CFO is based in California), Speak Out Act carve-out, and NLRA carve-out; mutual non-disparagement with narrow scope; reaffirmation of indemnification and D&O coverage; consulting fee for transition-advisor period.
- **Employee announcement.** Written email from CEO the day of the board consent; live all-hands the same afternoon; interim CFO announcement in the same communication. The recognition paragraph is drafted with the departing CFO's input.
- **External comms.** 8-K filed within 4 business days under Item 5.02 disclosing the CFO's departure, the interim CFO appointment, and the separation-agreement terms (mandatory disclosure). Press release the day of the 8-K. IR briefings to top-10 institutional investors within 48 hours.
- **Signed release** returned within 40 days (well inside the 45-day OWBPA window — the CFO is 46); 7-day revocation window observed; severance payments commence on the next payroll after the release effective date.

Total elapsed time from board pre-alignment to fully-executed separation: ~65 days.

## Summary

- Executive offboarding is a board matter, not a personnel matter. Board notice before employee announcement is non-negotiable.
- The CEO-executive conversation follows the Horowitz canon — direct, honest, specific about the decision, acknowledging what the executive contributed, transitioning to the how. Mutual-agreement framing is the mainstream target.
- Exec severance execution has three additions on top of the [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) individual template — CoC-acceleration and severance-interaction mechanics per the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); indemnification continuation and D&O coverage; board-seat resignation and any transition-services or non-compete / non-solicit terms.
- Board authorisation is required for material terms — comp-committee approval for above-policy severance, full-board consent for material transitions, audit-committee involvement for CFO / Controller departures, 8-K for public-company named executive officers.
- Employee-communications playbook — honest written announcement with a genuine recognition paragraph, live all-hands within 24 hours, direct-report 1:1s the same day. Confidentiality of the package details is standard.
- External comms for public / near-public: 8-K within 4 business days, press release if material, IR and customer outreach coordinated with securities counsel.
