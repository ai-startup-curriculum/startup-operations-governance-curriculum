# 8. Alumni, rehire eligibility, and the exit-interview feedback loop

> Every departing employee is either the strongest source of future hires and referrals — or a public detractor. The alumni programme, the rehire-eligibility decision framework, and the exit-interview feedback loop are the operating instruments that determine which.

## Motivation

The final chapter of an employee's lifecycle at the corporation is the beginning of a longer relationship. In a healthy people function:

- Alumni become referrers — often the single most productive channel for the corporation's next generation of hires. LinkedIn's own workforce research and Lever / Greenhouse sourcing benchmarks consistently show employee-referral hires close faster, ramp faster, retain longer, and cost meaningfully less to hire than externally-sourced hires. Alumni referrals are a specific subset that carries the same benefits.
- Alumni become customers, partners, and reputation ambassadors. The former head of engineering who left on good terms and is now a VP at a target customer is a warm intro rather than a cold outbound.
- Alumni return. **Boomerang** hires — employees who leave and rejoin — are, on average, faster-ramping and higher-retaining than net-new hires, because they know the culture, the product, and the founding team, and the corporation knows them. A well-run alumni programme sees 5–15% of open reqs in year N+2 filled by boomerangs.
- Exit interviews close a feedback loop into the performance-management operating system in [chapter 01](./01-performance-management-operating-system.md), the manager-training programme in [chapter 03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md), and the culture / engagement programme in [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/). The alternative is: the same manager causes the same three resignations for the same three reasons over the next year.

In an unhealthy people function, alumni become detractors — public negative reviews on Glassdoor, LinkedIn posts referencing the layoff, adversarial reference-check outcomes for the corporation's remaining employees, and a slow leak into the corporation's reputational credit that shows up as a lower yield on offer letters two years later.

This chapter builds the operating system that keeps the alumni relationship on the productive side of that line.

## The three operating decisions

1. **The alumni-network programme.** How the corporation stays connected with former employees.
2. **The exit-interview data-collection loop.** How exit-interview signal turns into changes in the performance-and-management operation.
3. **The rehire-eligibility decision framework.** How the corporation decides which former employees are welcome back.

## Decision 1 — The alumni-network programme

The alumni-network programme is a lightweight, low-cost operating asset that most Series-A / Series-B companies underinvest in. The mainstream pattern is not sophisticated — it does not need to be.

**The core elements.**

- **An alumni Slack channel or LinkedIn group.** A single shared space in which former employees stay in touch, share opportunities, and remain adjacent to the corporation's community. Invitation is delivered at exit; membership is voluntary. Ownership sits typically with the head of people or with a designated alumni-programme lead (at ~200+ employees).
- **A quarterly alumni email.** A short update from the CEO or head of people — company news, product launches, hiring priorities, notable alumni milestones. Cadence: quarterly. Length: short (the failure mode is a 3,000-word newsletter that nobody reads).
- **An alumni portal or landing page.** Simple. A page on the corporation's website that (a) lists open reqs with an internal-referral note, (b) points to the alumni Slack / LinkedIn group, (c) provides a contact for reference questions, (d) links to any alumni benefits (extended PTE window per [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/), continued discount on the corporation's product, etc.).
- **An annual alumni event.** For Series-B+ companies where the alumni footprint has reached ~100+ former employees. A dinner, a happy hour, a small conference. The point is the relationships, not the event.

**What the programme does not need.** A dedicated FTE (until well past growth stage), a bespoke platform (a Slack channel and an email list are enough), formal governance (the head of people or CEO owns the tone).

**Signalling from the exit day.** The alumni relationship starts on the last day, not later. The offboarding checklist from [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) includes: an explicit alumni-programme invitation, a "we would be glad to have you back if the moment is right" signal from the manager (for eligible-to-rehire employees — see decision 3), a LinkedIn recommendation from the manager if warranted, and the practical continuing benefits (extended PTE window if the corporation offers it, extended learning-platform access if practical).

## Decision 2 — The exit-interview data-collection loop

Exit interviews (see [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md)) are the input; a functioning feedback loop is what turns them into change.

**The data-collection instrument.** Covered in [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md). The essential fields for the feedback loop are:

- **Reason categorisation.** Structured multiple-choice — compensation, growth path, manager, team, product / mission, personal, external offer, other. Enables month-over-month trending.
- **Manager-level attribution.** With the caveat that manager attribution is confidential to the head of people. Enables identification of specific managers whose direct reports are leaving disproportionately.
- **Themes.** Narrative fields on what worked and what did not — coded manually by the head of people at each quarterly review, or with an AI-assisted coding pass at higher volumes.
- **Rehire-eligibility flag.** Marked in the file by the HRBP (see decision 3).

**The quarterly review cycle.**

- **Data review.** Head of people reviews exit-interview data quarterly, aggregated across the quarter. Slices reviewed: by function, by manager, by tenure, by reason category, by demographic.
- **Executive brief.** Head of people delivers a quarterly executive brief covering (a) reason-category trends, (b) manager-level attribution where a pattern warrants attention, (c) programmatic gaps the exit data is signalling (compensation, growth path, manager quality, culture), and (d) recommended interventions.
- **Board / comp-committee brief.** For growth-stage companies with a formal people-committee or comp-committee, an annual summary of exit data is worth including in the board people-metrics review.

**The feedback-to-action loop.**

- **Manager-level attribution → manager coaching.** A manager whose direct reports are exiting at a rate materially above the corporation baseline is a manager who needs an HRBP conversation, an assessment against the manager competencies, and often a coaching engagement (BetterUp / Better Manager per [chapter 03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)) or, in extreme cases, a PIP per [chapter 04](./04-pip-mechanics.md).
- **Compensation-driven exits → comp-band review.** A recurring pattern of compensation-driven exits is a signal to the comp cycle in [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/04-annual-comp-cycle.md) that the corporation's bands are lagging market for the affected role.
- **Growth-path-driven exits → promotion or L&D review.** A recurring pattern of growth-driven exits signals a gap in the promotion architecture ([chapter 02](./02-promotion-architecture.md)) or the development programme ([chapter 03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)).
- **Team / culture-driven exits → culture programme.** Handoff to [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/) with the specific themes documented.

**The failure mode.** Exit data is collected, filed, and never referenced. The head of people is busy; the executive team does not ask; the trends compound silently. The counter-norm: every quarterly people-review meeting has an exit-data agenda item, and the head of people has one intervention to propose per quarter based on it. If nothing is proposed, the data is not being read.

## Decision 3 — The rehire-eligibility decision framework

Not every former employee should be welcomed back. The rehire-eligibility decision framework is the operating rule that prevents the corporation from re-hiring an employee whose original departure was for reasons that would still apply — while keeping the door open for the majority who are simply moving through the industry.

**The three-category framework.**

- **Eligible for rehire (default for good-standing departures).** Voluntary resignations in good standing, RIF-affected employees, and involuntary terminations for role elimination or mutual separation typically fall here. The employee's file is marked "eligible" and the alumni-programme signals apply.
- **Eligible for rehire with review.** Voluntary resignations after a documented performance gap that had not yet escalated to a PIP; involuntary terminations after a PIP that concluded in release; mutual separations after a difficult period. The employee is not automatically welcome back — a hiring manager or recruiter considering the employee triggers a review with the head of people (and the former direct manager if still with the corporation) before making an offer. The bar to overturn the "with review" flag is a demonstrable change in circumstance and a specific role fit.
- **Not eligible for rehire.** Terminations for misconduct (harassment, safety violation, dishonesty, criminal act) and terminations for cause under the corporation's for-cause policy. The employee is not eligible; a recruiter or hiring manager who surfaces the former employee is redirected before an offer conversation begins.

**Who decides.** The HRBP or head of people marks the flag at exit (or as soon after as possible). Changing the flag later requires the head of people's approval. The former direct manager's opinion is an input, not the decision — a manager who wants to rehire a former report is not on their own permitted to override an earlier not-eligible flag.

**Legal-risk considerations.** The rehire-eligibility flag is a *documented, non-discriminatory* categorisation. Reviewing the rehire pool with the HRBP for pattern by protected category (as the exit-interview data is reviewed) is worth doing at least annually — a rehire-eligibility flag that skews on demographic lines is a signal to investigate before applying it in the next round of hiring decisions.

**Communication of the flag to the former employee.** The corporation generally does not tell the former employee their rehire-eligibility status explicitly. The signal is embedded in the exit conversation ("we would be glad to see you again" vs. a shorter neutral close). For the "with review" and "not eligible" categories, the corporation does not typically deliver the categorisation to the former employee — the private, documented, internal categorisation is the operating instrument.

**Boomerang hire mechanics.** When an eligible-for-rehire alumnus applies for a new role:

- Standard hiring pipeline per [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) with the interview shortcut typical for internal or referred candidates.
- The former employee's file is reviewed (performance history, reason for original departure, rehire-eligibility flag).
- Prior tenure is often recognised for benefits purposes (tenure-based PTO accrual, 401(k) vesting service credit, sabbatical eligibility) per the corporation's rehire policy.
- Prior tenure is typically *not* recognised for equity refresh purposes — the boomerang comes in with new-hire equity treatment, not a refresh calculated on continuous tenure.
- Onboarding is adapted — the boomerang does not need to go through a full new-hire onboarding but does need to be re-integrated into current systems, current org, current strategic priorities per [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-first-90-day-onboarding.md).

## The alumni / feedback-loop / rehire-eligibility one-page

- [ ] Alumni Slack / LinkedIn group is live; invitation delivered at exit.
- [ ] Quarterly alumni email is calendared and delivered.
- [ ] Alumni landing page on the corporation's website is current.
- [ ] Exit-interview instrument covers reason category, manager attribution, themes, and rehire-eligibility flag.
- [ ] Quarterly exit-data review is calendared; executive brief delivered.
- [ ] Manager-level attribution is used to inform manager coaching / manager PIP conversations.
- [ ] Compensation-driven exits are fed to the comp-cycle in [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/04-annual-comp-cycle.md).
- [ ] Growth-path-driven exits are fed to the promotion and L&D programmes ([chapters 02](./02-promotion-architecture.md) and [03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)).
- [ ] Culture-driven exits are handed off to [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/).
- [ ] Rehire-eligibility flag is set at exit; changing the flag requires head-of-people approval.
- [ ] Rehire pool is reviewed at least annually for demographic-slice pattern.
- [ ] Boomerang-hire policy documents which prior-tenure benefits carry forward.

## Concrete example: alumni programme at a 200-person Series-B

A 200-person Series-B B2B company. The head of people runs a lightweight alumni programme:

- **Alumni Slack.** ~130 members (former employees over the past 4 years). Head of People and a rotating alumni-programme lead moderate. Standard channels: #jobs, #general, #product-updates, #ama-with-founders.
- **Quarterly email.** Short update from the CEO. ~40% open rate.
- **Landing page.** On the corporation's website. Lists open reqs, alumni Slack invitation, contact for reference questions, and continued 30% discount on the corporation's product for alumni.
- **Annual alumni dinner.** Held each October in the corporation's HQ city. ~40 attendees.
- **Exit-interview review.** Quarterly review by the head of people; monthly slice-review of manager attribution.
- **Interventions in the last year.**
  - Two managers surfaced as having ~40% of their direct-report exits cite the manager as a factor; both are now in coaching engagements with BetterUp.
  - A pattern of comp-driven exits from mid-level Product Managers led to a mid-year adjustment of the PM band per [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/02-cash-and-equity-bands-per-level-per-geography.md).
  - A pattern of growth-path exits from senior Design led to the introduction of a "Principal Designer" level in the leveling framework.
- **Boomerangs.** 4 alumni rehired in the past 12 months (~8% of total hires). All in senior IC or manager roles. Each ramped to full productivity in ~30 days (vs. ~90 for new hires).
- **Rehire-eligibility pool.** ~85% marked eligible; ~10% eligible-with-review; ~5% not-eligible.

Total programme cost: ~$15k / year in event costs, ~$0 in software (Slack + LinkedIn are already in place), ~50 hours / year of head-of-people time. Return: ~4 boomerangs, ~15 referrals from active alumni, and the intangible reputational benefit of an alumni network that talks positively about the corporation.

## Summary

- Alumni are the corporation's most-durable talent-pipeline asset. The programme does not need to be sophisticated — Slack channel, quarterly email, landing page, occasional event.
- The alumni relationship starts on the last day, embedded in the offboarding checklist from [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md).
- The exit-interview feedback loop is the mechanism by which exit data becomes change in the performance-and-management operation. Quarterly review, executive brief, specific interventions.
- Rehire-eligibility is a documented three-category framework — eligible, eligible-with-review, not-eligible. Set at exit, changed only with head-of-people approval, reviewed annually for demographic pattern.
- Boomerang hires are among the highest-quality hires the corporation makes. Preserve them by preserving the alumni relationship.
