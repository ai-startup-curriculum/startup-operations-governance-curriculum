# 4. The Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

> A coaching instrument with a real chance of recovering a struggling employee — not a paper trail whose only purpose is to make the eventual termination defensible. If the PIP is the latter, the corporation is already in trouble.

## Motivation

The PIP is the most misused performance instrument in the startup playbook. The two failure patterns:

- **The paper-trail PIP.** The manager and the HRBP have already decided the employee will be terminated; the PIP exists to build 60 days of documentation. The employee reads the PIP and knows immediately what it is. The corporation gets sued at higher rates because juries and administrative agencies read paper-trail PIPs as pretext for a discrimination claim; the employee's morale collapses immediately; the rest of the team learns that "PIP" means "fired," and the instrument becomes unusable for its actual purpose.
- **The avoided PIP.** The manager has known for six months that the employee is not meeting the bar but has never had the hard conversation, never documented the gap, and never opened a PIP. When the employee's performance finally forces a decision, the corporation has no documentation, no defensible termination pathway, and often no realistic option other than a large severance package to buy a peaceful exit.

A well-designed PIP programme is the *first* documented coaching escalation after informal coaching has failed. Opened early, structured honestly, staffed with real support, and closed on the evidence — it recovers a meaningful fraction of the employees it is opened on, and it makes the involuntary-termination cases that do proceed (chapter 05) both cleaner and more defensible.

## The five operating decisions

1. **Trigger criteria.** When is a PIP the right escalation, versus continued informal coaching?
2. **Structure.** How long, what goals, what check-in cadence, what evidence?
3. **Manager and HRBP support pattern.** Who is coaching whom, and how much?
4. **Conclusion decision.** At day 30 / 60 / 90 — recover, extend, or release?
5. **Anti-abuse operating norms.** How does the corporation prevent the PIP from becoming a paper trail?

## Decision 1 — Trigger criteria

A PIP is the *escalated* step in a coaching sequence, not the first step. The trigger pattern:

1. **Informal coaching in 1:1s.** The manager has raised the specific performance gap with the employee across at least 2–3 1:1s, has been explicit about the gap ("the design specs you shipped last month missed the review bar in these three ways"), has proposed and delivered support (pairing, coaching, additional review cycles), and has documented the conversations in the shared 1:1 doc.
2. **Written coaching / verbal warning.** A follow-up email or written check-in that summarises the specific gap, the coaching support, and what "back on track" would look like. This is often the last step before a PIP and is sometimes formalised as a "verbal warning" or "coaching memo."
3. **PIP triggered.** The gap has persisted through the informal coaching sequence, the manager and HRBP have reviewed the case together, and the manager can articulate:
   - The specific performance gap (against goals, competencies, or role expectations).
   - The evidence for the gap (concrete examples, not vibes).
   - The support previously provided (informal coaching, resources, time).
   - The specific measurable outcomes that would resolve the gap.

**PIP is not for misconduct.** Misconduct — harassment, safety violations, dishonesty, policy breach — is handled through investigation and (typically) direct termination, not through a performance-improvement plan. Mixing the two collapses both processes and destroys the coaching signal a PIP is supposed to send. See [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md).

**PIP is not for role fit.** If the employee is capable but in the wrong role, the intervention is often an internal transfer (see [chapter 03](./03-development-growth-plans-and-l-and-d-programme.md)) or an honest conversation about the mismatch, not a PIP. Opening a PIP on someone who is a great engineer in the wrong team is a category error.

**PIP is not for demographic-adverse decisions.** If the pattern of who gets PIPed skews on protected-category lines (gender, race / ethnicity, age 40+, national origin, disability, religion — see [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/)), that is not a PIP mechanics problem, it is an EEOC-exposure problem. The HRBP should be running a demographic-slice review of PIP openings against the workforce baseline, quarterly at minimum. See the anti-abuse norms below.

## Decision 2 — Structure

A well-structured PIP has five components.

**Length.** The three canonical lengths: **30 days**, **60 days**, or **90 days**. The choice is a function of the gap:

- **30 days** for a discrete, specific gap where 30 days of focused work is enough to demonstrate recovery (e.g., "meet all four sprint commitments over the next four sprints").
- **60 days** for a broader competency or output-consistency gap where the employee needs a longer runway to sustainably shift.
- **90 days** for a role-level competency gap where the employee needs a full quarter of work to demonstrate operating at level.

A PIP shorter than 30 days is not a PIP, it is a termination with paperwork. A PIP longer than 90 days is losing structural rigor and probably signals that the underlying question is role fit rather than performance.

**Measurable goals.** Every PIP goal must be observable, specific, and time-bound. The failure mode is a goal like "improve communication with the team." A defensible PIP goal is "attend and prepare for weekly team stand-ups; post a weekly written status update in the team channel by Friday of each week; respond to peer PR review requests within one business day." Someone reading the goal at the end of the PIP window must be able to say "yes, this happened" or "no, it did not."

**Weekly check-ins.** A 30-minute manager–employee 1:1 every week for the duration of the PIP, with a written summary in the shared PIP doc. The check-in reviews progress against each goal, surfaces obstacles, and adjusts support. Weekly is the mainstream cadence — biweekly is common but tends to let too much time pass between correction cycles.

**Support commitments.** The PIP is a two-sided document — the employee's goals and the manager's / corporation's commitments. The manager may commit to: additional pairing time, expedited PR review turnaround, a specific peer collaborator, time on a specific coaching platform, an HRBP monthly check-in. If the corporation is not committing anything, the "PIP" is a stopwatch, not a coaching instrument.

**Written PIP document.** The artifact itself — signed by the employee (acknowledging receipt, not agreement), the manager, and the HRBP. Standard sections:

- Employee name, role, level, start date of PIP, end date.
- Statement of performance gap (specific, evidence-anchored).
- History of prior coaching (dates of 1:1s and coaching conversations, summary of gaps discussed).
- PIP goals (numbered, measurable, time-bound).
- Support commitments (from the manager and the corporation).
- Check-in cadence and format.
- Conclusion outcomes: what a successful completion looks like, what "not on track" looks like, what happens at the end.
- Signatures.

## Decision 3 — Manager and HRBP support pattern

The PIP has three participants: the employee, the manager, and the HRBP. Each has a distinct role.

**The employee's role.** Engage in the coaching in good faith, deliver against the PIP goals, ask for support explicitly when they are blocked, and communicate honestly at each check-in. An employee who is not willing to engage on the PIP is signalling that the underlying decision may already be made on their side too — a conversation the manager should acknowledge rather than duck.

**The manager's role.** Own the coaching. Show up prepared for each weekly check-in with specific observations against each goal. Provide the committed support. Escalate blockers to HRBP quickly. Document each check-in in the shared PIP doc. Do not use the PIP as a distancing instrument — a manager who cools their behaviour toward the employee during the PIP is signalling the outcome regardless of what the paperwork says.

**The HRBP's role.** Coach the manager. The single most common PIP failure mode is a first-time manager attempting a PIP without HRBP support and doing it badly. The HRBP should:

- Review the initial PIP goals for specificity and defensibility before the PIP is delivered.
- Sit in on the PIP-opening conversation (or debrief with the manager immediately after).
- Do a monthly 1:1 with the manager during the PIP to check-in on the coaching quality.
- Do a 1:1 with the *employee* at least once during the PIP — the employee needs a channel outside the manager to raise concerns.
- Own the demographic-slice review at the programme level.

A people function without HRBP capacity — pre-Series-A, typically — has to compensate. The head of people usually plays the HRBP role directly for every PIP. Opening a PIP with no HRBP coverage at all is the single fastest way to lose the case at the EEOC.

## Decision 4 — Conclusion decision

Every PIP has one of three outcomes at the conclusion window.

**Successful completion — employee returns to standing.** The employee met the PIP goals. The manager and HRBP jointly confirm the outcome; the manager closes the PIP in the shared doc with a written summary; the employee returns to standard performance-management cadence. A common follow-through is a 30–60 day "stabilisation window" during which the manager keeps a higher-touch 1:1 cadence to consolidate the recovery. Do not open a second PIP on the same employee for the same gap within 6 months of a successful conclusion — that pattern reads as manufactured and destroys the credibility of the process.

**Extension — one 30-day extension available.** The employee is materially progressing but has not fully closed the gap. A single 30-day extension is defensible, with tightened goals and continued weekly check-ins. Do not extend twice; a second extension is a signal that either the gap was scoped wrong or the outcome was already decided.

**Release — employment is terminated at PIP conclusion.** The employee did not meet the PIP goals. The manager, HRBP, and (typically) the second-line manager and head of people jointly confirm the outcome. Timeline: the termination is delivered within the week following PIP conclusion — the delivery-day choreography, script, severance, and release are all handled per [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md). Two important sub-decisions:

- **Severance offer.** The corporation's default severance-and-release policy (chapter 05) typically applies. Some corporations offer a modestly larger severance for a PIP-conclusion termination than for a for-cause termination — this is a policy decision that should be documented company-wide, not negotiated case-by-case.
- **Equity treatment.** Post-termination exercise window is governed by the equity policy in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) — typically 90 days for ISOs unless the plan permits a longer window. Any severance-triggered acceleration is also governed by mod-105, not by this module.

## Decision 5 — Anti-abuse operating norms

The PIP process becomes toxic when it is used as a covert termination instrument. The operating norms that protect the process:

**Norm 1: The PIP is a coaching tool.** If the manager and HRBP have already decided the outcome is termination, do not open a PIP — offer a mutual-separation-and-severance arrangement per [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) instead. The mutual separation is more honest, typically faster, often less expensive in total cost (severance vs. 60–90 days of full pay plus severance), and does not corrupt the PIP instrument for the next employee.

**Norm 2: The PIP has a real chance of success.** A well-run PIP programme should see somewhere between one-third and one-half of PIPs conclude with successful return to standing. If a corporation's PIP success rate is close to zero, the PIP is being used as a paper trail — either because the trigger criteria are wrong, the goals are unwinnable, or the coaching support is absent.

**Norm 3: The PIP is opened by the manager, not by the HRBP.** The HRBP coaches; the manager owns. A PIP opened over the manager's objection ("HR says I have to do this") is a PIP the manager will not coach on, and the outcome is predetermined.

**Norm 4: Demographic-slice review at the programme level.** The HRBP and head of people review PIP openings by gender, race / ethnicity, age (40+), disability, and other protected-category lines against the workforce baseline. If a single function or manager is over-representing a protected group in PIP openings, that is a signal to investigate the manager's calibration and the function's culture before opening the next PIP.

**Norm 5: The employee's side is documented.** The employee's disagreement or context on any check-in is captured in the shared PIP doc — either as the employee's written response or as a summary the manager acknowledges. A one-sided PIP doc is a document with a compromised evidentiary value in any subsequent dispute.

**Norm 6: The PIP does not surprise.** Nothing in the PIP conversation should be new information. If the employee is hearing about the performance gap for the first time when the PIP is opened, the manager has failed the coaching sequence in [chapter 01](./01-performance-management-operating-system.md) and needs to reset with informal coaching first before escalating to a PIP.

## Concrete example: a 60-day mid-level PIP

A Series-B engineering team. A mid-level engineer (L4) has been shipping late for two quarters, missing code-review commitments, and delivering PRs that require substantial rework. The manager has raised the gap in three 1:1s over the last six weeks and delivered a written coaching memo two weeks ago. The gap persists. Manager and HRBP decide to open a 60-day PIP.

- **Length:** 60 days.
- **Goals:**
  1. Deliver committed sprint work by the sprint boundary in five of the next eight sprints; document any at-risk sprint by Wednesday of that sprint with a written re-scope proposal.
  2. Complete assigned PR reviews within one business day for the next eight weeks.
  3. Reduce first-round PR rework by delivering PRs that meet the team's PR-checklist criteria — measured by the ratio of accepted-without-rework PRs (target ≥ 70%).
- **Support:** Weekly 60-minute pairing session with the team's Senior engineer; expedited turnaround from the manager on any PR review the engineer requests; monthly 1:1 with the HRBP.
- **Check-ins:** 30-minute manager 1:1 every Friday; written summary in the shared PIP doc; HRBP joins every third check-in.
- **Conclusion:** Manager, HRBP, and Director of Engineering meet on day 55 to review; decision documented; outcome delivered on day 60 or 61.

Outcome A (successful): Engineer met all three goals; PIP closed; 30-day stabilisation window with continued weekly 1:1s; standard performance-management cadence resumes.

Outcome B (release): Engineer missed goals 1 and 3; PIP closed; termination delivered within the week per [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md) with the corporation's default severance-and-release package.

## Summary

- PIP is the *escalated* coaching step after informal coaching has failed — not the first step, not a substitute for a hard 1:1.
- PIP is not for misconduct (investigate and typically terminate directly) and not for role fit (transfer or honest conversation).
- Structure: 30 / 60 / 90 days; measurable goals; weekly check-ins; two-sided document with the corporation's support commitments visible.
- Manager owns the coaching; HRBP coaches the manager; employee has a direct channel to HRBP outside the manager.
- Conclusion outcomes are recover, extend once, or release. A PIP with a near-zero success rate is being misused.
- Anti-abuse norms — coaching tool not paper trail, real chance of success, no surprise information, demographic-slice review at the programme level.
- If the outcome has already been decided at the PIP-opening moment, do not open a PIP — offer a mutual separation per [chapter 05](./05-termination-playbook.md).
