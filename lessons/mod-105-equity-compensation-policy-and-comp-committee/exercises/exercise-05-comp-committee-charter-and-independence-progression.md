# Exercise 05 — Comp committee charter and independence progression

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [05 — The compensation committee](../05-compensation-committee.md)

## Problem statement

Breakwater Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp building a specialised GPU-fabric platform for AI-infra customers. The corporation just closed a $68M Series-B co-led by two established venture firms, bringing total raised to ~$95M. Post-closing employee headcount is ~150 (expected to reach ~210 within twelve months on the board-approved plan), of whom twenty-eight are senior-director level or above and six are C-suite officers. The S-1 horizon the board is managing toward is 24–36 months.

The current board of directors, immediately post-closing, has five seats:

- The founder-CEO (W-2 officer; founder common stock plus unvested RSAs).
- The founder-CTO (W-2 officer; founder common stock).
- The Series-A lead-investor director (general partner at the Series-A fund; the fund holds a ~14% as-converted stake).
- The Series-B lead-investor director (general partner at one of the two Series-B co-leads; the two Series-B funds together hold ~22% as-converted).
- One independent director — a former public-company CFO added at Series-A as part of a side-letter commitment, with no financial or affiliate relationship to any investor fund and no consulting agreement with Breakwater.

No compensation committee currently exists. For the entire history of the corporation, the full board has approved all exec-comp decisions (initial packages, refresh grants, promotion grants, bonus payouts) by unanimous written consent or at the regular quarterly meeting. The board has agreed in principle, as a Series-B closing condition negotiated with the Series-B lead, to stand up a chartered compensation committee effective immediately post-closing, with a stated commitment to progress the committee toward three-independent-director composition before the S-1 is filed.

Your role: you are the General Counsel (or outside corporate counsel engaged by the GC) with support from the VP People. Produce the Series-B committee charter, the independence-progression plan through to pre-IPO, the first-year committee agenda, the advisor-engagement decision, the resolution templates for the Series-B closing board meeting, and a memo to the full board explaining the trajectory.

## Requirements

### Part A — Initial committee charter at Series-B

Draft a written compensation-committee charter adopted effective at the Series-B closing. Cover:

1. **Composition** — three directors by name / role. Name specifically which three of the five current directors sit on the committee at adoption, which director is Chair, who is Secretary (likely the GC or Corporate Secretary — see [mod-111 chapter 03](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/03-corporate-secretary-function.md)), and the written rationale for the composition choice.
2. **Independence posture** — a candid written statement that the Series-B committee is *not* fully independent under NYSE / Nasdaq listing standards. Name which directors are considered independent for pre-IPO purposes, which are not, and the specific reason each is or is not (affiliate / investor / employee / consulting relationship — the Rule 10C-1 factors below).
3. **Cadence** — minimum quarterly meetings, executive-session norms when discussing CEO comp, UWC authority, phone / video participation rules.
4. **Delegated authority and purview** — specific decision-making scope the committee owns (exec-comp packages, equity-plan administration including quarterly refresh / promotion batches, benchmarking vendor approval, exec-severance plan administration) and the matters that return to the full board. Specify a delegation threshold the Committee Chair may act on between meetings (e.g., single-grant share-count cap, aggregate dollar cap) and flag the specific numbers `<!-- needs-research -->`.
5. **Materials pack** — the standing contents of the pre-meeting materials pack (benchmarking data, grant-batch worksheet, exec-comp tally sheet, minutes from prior meeting, action-item tracker).
6. **Interaction with the audit and governance committees** — how the comp committee hands off to the audit committee on 10b5-1 / insider-trading / exec perquisite disclosure questions, and to the nominating-and-governance committee on director compensation. Cross-reference [mod-111 chapter 03](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/03-corporate-secretary-function.md) and [chapter 10 — ownership boundary map](../10-ownership-boundary-map.md).
7. **Member retainer and chair premium** — the retainer each committee member receives for committee service, the Chair premium, and whether meeting fees apply (flag the specific dollar numbers `<!-- needs-research -->`).

### Part B — Independence-progression plan Series-B → Series-C → pre-IPO

Produce a dated progression plan showing how committee composition and independence posture shift across the next 24–36 months. Cover:

1. **At Series-B closing (today)**: the three-director composition from Part A; independence posture as stated.
2. **At Series-C close (target: ~15 months post-Series-B)**: a specific commitment — e.g., add a second qualifying independent director to the board as a condition of the Series-C term sheet, seat that director on the comp committee, and rotate off one investor director. Specify who rotates and why.
3. **Twelve-to-eighteen months before S-1 filing**: shift to a three-independent-director committee that meets the exchange standard. Name the specific trigger (board-approved S-1 kickoff resolution, selection of lead underwriter, formal engagement of IPO counsel).
4. **At S-1 filing / listing**: full three-independent-director composition under the applicable listing rule, with the written independence determinations in the board minutes.
5. **Legal reference set** — the plan must cite, by section number: **NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.05**, **Nasdaq Rule 5605(d)**, **Dodd-Frank § 952 (15 U.S.C. § 78j-3)**, and **SEC Rule 10C-1 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10C-1)**. For each citation, state in one sentence what the rule requires and how Breakwater's progression plan satisfies it (or relies on the newly-public-company cure period and why).
6. **Dependency on recruiting independent directors** — the recruiting lead-time, the compensation package the corporation will offer an independent director (annual cash retainer plus RSA grant — flag specific numbers `<!-- needs-research -->`), and the search firm or network the corporation will use.

### Part C — First-year committee agenda

Produce a written first-year agenda — four quarterly meetings, each with specific topics, materials, and decisions. Cover:

1. **Q1 (first meeting, ~45 days post-closing)** — adopt charter via resolution; approve annual-comp-cycle framework; approve prior-year exec bonuses; approve Q1 refresh and promotion grant batch; receive benchmarking-vendor onboarding briefing.
2. **Q2** — mid-year compensation review; approve Q2 refresh / promotion batch; approve any new exec-hire packages that closed in-quarter; mid-year benchmarking spot-check against the Series-B hiring plan.
3. **Q3** — full Compensia / Radford refresh presentation; approve the comp-cycle framework for the following year (merit budget, equity refresh envelope, promotion-grant policy); approve Q3 refresh / promotion batch; begin option-pool-forecast discussion ahead of Q4.
4. **Q4** — approve next-year total comp budget (cash + equity); approve the option-pool-top-up ask that will go to the full board and stockholders (see [chapter 04 — the option-pool refresh](../04-option-pool-refresh.md)); approve any exec-severance-plan amendments; conduct the committee's annual self-evaluation.

For each meeting, specify: the materials pack deliverables, the executive-session segment (meeting without the CEO), the decisions by resolution versus discussion-only items, and the standing-report items that will be read into the next full-board meeting.

### Part D — Compensia / Radford advisor engagement

Produce a written advisor-engagement decision document. Cover:

1. **Scope** — benchmarking-only (annual compensation data refresh and peer-group construction) versus full-service (benchmarking plus CEO-comp advisory, severance-package design, equity-plan-structure advisory, CD&A drafting support pre-IPO). State which scope Breakwater is engaging at Series-B and the trigger that will expand scope later.
2. **Vendor selection** — Compensia, Radford (AON), Pave, Carta Total Compensation, or an equivalent. Name the specific vendor selected, the two or three alternatives considered, and the three decision factors that drove the selection.
3. **Fee structure** — annual retainer versus per-engagement fees; flag the specific numbers `<!-- needs-research -->`. State the committee's expectation of annual fee ranges and the escalation / scope-change workflow.
4. **Independence assessment under SEC Rule 10C-1(b)(4)** — document the six independence factors the committee must consider before engaging a compensation consultant (the provision of other services, fees as a percentage of the consultant's total revenue, policies to prevent conflicts, business or personal relationships with committee members, ownership of company stock, and business or personal relationships with executive officers). State the committee's finding on each factor and the overall independence conclusion.
5. **Conflict-of-interest disclosure** — the written certification the advisor provides annually; the committee's annual re-evaluation; the disclosure path into the eventual CD&A under Reg S-K Item 407(e)(3)(iv).

### Part E — Resolution templates for the Series-B closing board meeting

Produce resolution templates (board resolutions, suitable for adoption at the Series-B closing board meeting and inclusion in the minute book) for each of the following:

1. **Adoption of the charter** — board resolution establishing the compensation committee under DGCL § 141(c), delegating specified authority, and adopting the written charter attached as an exhibit.
2. **Delegation of authority to the Committee Chair** — a specific between-meetings authorisation for the Chair, with the share-count and dollar thresholds from Part A (flag the specific numbers `<!-- needs-research -->`).
3. **Adoption of the exec-severance plan** — a resolution adopting (or ratifying) the current exec-severance plan and delegating its ongoing administration to the committee.
4. **Adoption of the annual compensation cycle** — a resolution approving the annual-comp-cycle framework described in [chapter 06 — the annual comp cycle](../06-annual-comp-cycle.md) and delegating cycle execution to the committee.
5. **Approval of equity-plan amendment** — a resolution approving the equity-plan amendment required at Series-B (option-pool top-up and any administrative amendments), recommending it to the stockholders for approval, and delegating ongoing administration to the committee.

Each template should include the recital-and-resolve structure the Corporate Secretary will use in the minute book.

### Part F — Memo to the full board

Produce a one-page memo from the General Counsel to the full board explaining:

- Why the committee is being stood up at the Series-B closing (the Delaware entire-fairness exposure that the full-board-only process is accumulating as exec-comp amounts grow; the Series-B lead-investor condition; the S-1-readiness timeline).
- What specifically changes at Series-B — the composition, the delegation, the cadence, the advisor engagement.
- What will change at Series-C and pre-IPO — a two-paragraph preview of the progression plan.
- The specific board asks at the closing meeting — adopt the charter, adopt the resolutions in Part E, approve the advisor engagement, approve the independent-director recruiting commitment for Series-C.
- The `<!-- needs-research -->` items flagged in the package and how they will be resolved before the first committee meeting.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 05 is the primary reference. The chapter walks through the Series-A → Series-B/C → pre-IPO independence trajectory, the required charter contents, and the Dodd-Frank / Rule 10C-1 advisor-independence framework.
- The composition answer in Part A is not obvious. The Series-B closing committee cannot be fully independent under listing standards (only one of the five directors is independent), and seating an inside officer-director is defensible pre-IPO but should be documented as a known posture that will shift at Series-C. Investor-director service is common at Series-B / C and is not disqualifying for a pre-IPO committee.
- Do not manufacture specific advisor fees, independent-director retainers, or market-benchmarked grant thresholds. Where the exercise asks for a number (Chair delegation share cap, member retainer, advisor retainer, Compensia / Radford fee range), take the chapter's ranges where available and flag the rest with `<!-- needs-research -->` to resolve against current-market data at the first committee meeting.
- The independence-progression plan is a dated commitment, not a vague statement. It should name the specific triggers (Series-C term-sheet negotiation, S-1 kickoff resolution, listing date) and the composition at each milestone.
- The Rule 10C-1(b)(4) independence assessment for Compensia / Radford is a six-factor analysis. The chapter names the six factors; the committee must document the finding on each.
- The resolution templates in Part E are the artefacts the Corporate Secretary will drop into the Series-B closing minute book. They should be adoption-ready, with blanks only where `<!-- needs-research -->` markers appear.
- Cross-reference [chapter 10 — the ownership boundary map](../10-ownership-boundary-map.md) for which decisions the committee owns vs. which return to the full board, and [mod-111 chapter 05 — fiduciary duties](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md) for the entire-fairness-doctrine driver.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. The Series-B charter names three specific directors by role (or named seat), identifies the Chair and Secretary, and includes a candid written independence posture that does not overclaim.
2. The delegation to the Committee Chair specifies a numeric threshold (single-grant share cap and / or aggregate dollar cap) — flagged `<!-- needs-research -->` is acceptable, left as `[TBD]` is not.
3. The independence-progression plan cites by section number **NYSE § 303A.05**, **Nasdaq Rule 5605(d)**, **Dodd-Frank § 952 (15 U.S.C. § 78j-3)**, and **SEC Rule 10C-1**, with a one-sentence statement of each rule's requirement.
4. The progression plan is dated — Series-B closing, Series-C close (target month), S-1 kickoff (target month), listing — not written as "eventually" / "at some point."
5. The first-year agenda has four quarterly meetings with specific topics, decisions, and materials for each quarter, including an annual self-evaluation at Q4.
6. The advisor-engagement decision names a specific vendor (Compensia, Radford, Pave, Carta, or defensible substitute), a specific scope (benchmarking-only or full-service), and includes a six-factor Rule 10C-1(b)(4) independence assessment.
7. The resolution templates in Part E are adoption-ready and address all five required resolutions, with blanks only at `<!-- needs-research -->` markers.
8. The board memo states at least four specific board asks for the Series-B closing meeting.
9. Every market number cited (Chair delegation threshold, member retainer, Chair premium, independent-director retainer, advisor fee) is either taken from chapter 05's ranges or flagged with a `<!-- needs-research -->` marker.
10. Nothing left as `[TBD]` or `[FILL IN]`.
