# Exercise 02 — Refresh and promotion grant cadence decision

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — Refresh, promotion, and retention grants](../03-refresh-promotion-and-retention-grants.md)

## Problem statement

Marbletop Robotics is a Delaware C-corp building autonomous fulfilment hardware. It closed a $110M Series-C eleven months ago, has 248 employees (142 engineering / applied-research, 38 operations and manufacturing, 34 GTM, 22 G&A, 12 product / design), and did the bulk of its hiring during Series-A (36 months ago, ~55 hires) and Series-B (22 months ago, ~110 hires). The initial new-hire grants were standard 4-year grants with a 1-year cliff; no refresh grant has yet been made to any employee outside of a small number of ad hoc awards.

The Head of People has reported to the CEO that she is having "visible retention-risk conversations" with roughly 15-20 critical ICs — the majority on the applied-research / perception team, with a cluster of senior platform engineers and two mission-critical manufacturing-process leads. Several of these ICs are from the Series-A cohort now sitting between month 33 and month 39 of their original 48-month vest; a larger group from the Series-B cohort will cross the month-24 mark within the next six months and the month-30 inflection within twelve.

No formal refresh cadence has been adopted. Promotion grants over the last eighteen months have been issued ad hoc — managers propose a share count, the CEO approves in a 1:1 — producing visible inconsistency: two L4-to-L5 engineering promotions in the last quarter received grants that differed by roughly 2.6x in share count. Six weeks ago, a retention grant was issued to a staff perception engineer who walked in with a competing offer from a well-funded autonomy competitor; the grant was approved by the CEO over Slack and processed by legal without comp-committee approval, surfacing after the fact in the quarterly 83(b) / Section-16-adjacent review.

The comp committee met last week and asked the Head of People and the CFO to come back within 30 days with a policy proposal covering (a) a refresh cadence, (b) a sized refresh pool, (c) a promotion-grant structure, and (d) a retention-grant policy with explicit approval mechanics. Your role: you are the Head of People (or the operating partner retained by the comp committee to produce the proposal). Produce the policy package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Diagnose the "year-3 cliff" risk

Produce a written diagnosis of Marbletop's current forward-vest exposure. Cover:

1. **Cohort inventory.** For the Series-A cohort (~55 hires, now month 33-39) and the Series-B cohort (~110 hires, now month 19-25), estimate the headcount currently in or entering the 12-month pre-cliff window (month 30 to month 42) over the next 6 and 12 months. State your attrition / role-change assumptions explicitly.
2. **Critical-IC identification.** Define the criteria you will use to designate the ~15-20 critical ICs the Head of People has already flagged, and name the functions / role archetypes you expect to appear on the list. The chapter's "forward-vest inflection" language from the refresh-cadence section should anchor the framing.
3. **Dollar / dilution cost of a 2-year forward-looking vest refresh** for the critical-IC population and separately for the full Series-A + Series-B cohort entering the window. Flag the inputs you cannot resolve from the exercise brief alone with `<!-- needs-research -->` markers — current 409A FMV, current Series-C post-money fully-diluted share count, L4 / L5 / L6 target total-equity bands at Series-C scale, performance-quartile distribution assumptions.
4. **The three-to-five specific failure modes** already visible or expected within 90 days if no policy is adopted. Name them against the chapter's failure patterns ("departure spike in month-30-to-42," "manager-by-manager improvisation on promotion grants," "founder-discretionary retention grants above threshold without committee approval").

### Part B — Pick a refresh cadence

Pick one of the three cadences from the chapter (annual top-up, milestone-based, year-3 or year-4 cliff refresh) and justify the choice. Cover:

1. The specific cadence you are recommending and the anchor date (fiscal-year end, calendar-year end, employee anniversary, or other).
2. The chapter's trade-off framing applied to Marbletop specifically — stage (Series-C), headcount (~250), the visibility of the year-3 cliff to recruiters in the autonomy space, and the operating cost of running the cadence.
3. The first-cycle target forward-looking vest (the chapter's worked example uses two years; justify your choice if different).
4. The vesting structure on refresh grants — new 4-year vest from grant date vs. tacked-on — and the cliff policy on refreshes.
5. Interaction with the annual comp cycle covered in [../06-annual-comp-cycle.md](../06-annual-comp-cycle.md) — where refresh grants land in the cycle calendar, who the pre-read packet goes to, when the comp committee approves the parameters versus the individual grants.

### Part C — Size the refresh pool

Produce a sized refresh pool for the first full cycle. Cover:

1. The sizing convention (percent-of-fully-diluted or percent-of-initial-grants) and the specific percent you are recommending for the first cycle. Marbletop is a Series-C at ~250 employees; use the chapter's 1-3% band and defend where in the band you are landing.
2. A dollar envelope at current 409A FMV. Flag the 409A FMV and the fully-diluted share count with `<!-- needs-research -->` markers but structure the calculation so the comp committee sees how dollars and share count move together.
3. The quartile-weighted distribution shape (the chapter's 50-30-20 or your defended alternative), and the policy on bottom-quartile and PIP employees.
4. The interaction with the option pool — how much headroom is left after refresh, promotion, and retention commitments and new-hire grants against Marbletop's existing option-pool size. Flag the current available-pool number with `<!-- needs-research -->`.
5. A sensitivity note — how the pool changes if the critical-IC population is 50% larger than the current estimate, or if a performance-quartile distribution assumption shifts.

### Part D — Design the promotion-grant structure

Design the promotion-grant policy. Cover:

1. The delta table for level transitions Marbletop will support in the next 12 months — at minimum L3→L4, L4→L5, L5→L6, and M1→M2 (or Marbletop's management-track equivalents). Express each delta as a dollar value at current 409A, flagged with `<!-- needs-research -->` for the current band data (Pave / Carta Total Comp / Option Impact).
2. The interaction policy with the annual refresh — blended or reset (chapter's two options). Pick one and justify. Document the specific rule the comp committee will see: "an employee promoted in month X of the fiscal year receives Y in the same year's annual refresh."
3. The vesting structure on promotion grants — new 4-year schedule from the promotion effective date, with or without a fresh 1-year cliff.
4. The approval workflow — which grants are approved by the CEO and CFO versus which come to the comp committee, with the specific dollar threshold stated. Cross-reference [../05-compensation-committee.md](../05-compensation-committee.md).
5. The remediation plan for the two L4-to-L5 engineering promotions already made at inconsistent share counts — do you true-up the lower grant, document the inconsistency, or absorb it. Pick one.

### Part E — Design the retention-grant policy

Design the retention-grant policy, including explicit approval mechanics. Cover:

1. The three legitimate triggers (credible competing offer, mission-critical status for an upcoming event, one-time role-specific retention risk) stated as the policy will read. For each, specify what evidence the Head of People must produce in the pre-read packet (verifiable offer letter, written mission-critical designation, specific role / territory / reorganisation documentation).
2. The specific comp-committee approval threshold — a dollar amount at current 409A FMV **or** a share-count figure. Pick one and state it. Grants above the threshold come to the committee; grants at or below are approved by CEO and CFO jointly. Justify where the threshold is set relative to the L5 initial-grant band.
3. The documentation requirement — who signs, where the record lives, how it is reconciled against the quarterly comp-committee pack. Address the recent out-of-process staff perception engineer grant explicitly: either formally ratify it, re-process it, or document the policy violation.
4. The sizing conventions for retention grants — the chapter calls out 1-2 year forward-vest focus and event-cliff vesting for mission-critical designations. Specify defaults and the exception workflow.
5. The reporting cadence — how retention-grant activity is surfaced to the comp committee between approvals (quarterly roll-up, trailing-twelve-month retention-grant dilution against pool, etc.).

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 03 ([../03-refresh-promotion-and-retention-grants.md](../03-refresh-promotion-and-retention-grants.md)) is the primary reference — the forward-vest curve, the three cadence options, the sizing conventions, and the retention-grant triggers all come from it. Do not invent frameworks the chapter does not provide.
- The comp-committee workflow — pre-read package, threshold mechanics, meeting cadence — is in [../05-compensation-committee.md](../05-compensation-committee.md). The annual-cycle calendar and refresh-cycle operating workflow are in [../06-annual-comp-cycle.md](../06-annual-comp-cycle.md). Cross-reference both when you specify the operating details.
- Do not manufacture Marbletop's 409A FMV, fully-diluted share count, or the Pave / Carta / Option Impact band numbers. Where you cite them, flag with `<!-- needs-research -->` and name the source the corporation will refresh from.
- Marbletop is clearly past the stage where milestone-based refresh is defensible. If you nevertheless pick it, carry the burden of defending it against the chapter's "why it does not scale" critique.
- The retention-grant threshold is where you will feel the most pressure to leave a range. Pick a number. The comp committee cannot approve "approximately $150-250k."
- The recent out-of-process retention grant is a governance-posture question. The committee will read whatever you propose as a signal about how strictly the new policy will be enforced going forward.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. The cohort diagnosis names a specific headcount in the month-30-to-month-42 pre-cliff window at 6 months and 12 months, with the assumptions stated.
2. The learner has **picked one specific refresh cadence** (annual top-up, milestone-based, or cliff-based) with a named anchor date — not left as a choice between options.
3. The refresh pool is sized with a specific percent-of-fully-diluted figure *and* a dollar envelope at current 409A FMV, with the inputs flagged `<!-- needs-research -->` where the exercise brief does not supply them.
4. The quartile-distribution shape is a specific split (50-30-20 or an alternative with the splits numbered) rather than "differentiated by performance."
5. The promotion-grant policy includes a delta table with at least four level transitions and a specific blended-or-reset interaction rule with the annual cycle.
6. The retention-grant policy states **a single specific comp-committee approval threshold** — a dollar figure at current 409A FMV or a share-count figure — not a range.
7. The retention-grant policy addresses the recent out-of-process staff perception engineer grant explicitly, with a named remediation (ratification, re-processing, or documented violation).
8. The approval workflow for each instrument (refresh, promotion, retention) names the specific sign-offs (CEO, CFO, comp-committee chair, full committee) and cross-references [../05-compensation-committee.md](../05-compensation-committee.md).
9. Every market number cited (409A FMV, fully-diluted share count, level-band dollar values, pool-benchmark percents, quartile distributions) is either taken from chapter 03's ranges or flagged with a `<!-- needs-research -->` marker that names the source Marbletop will refresh from.
10. Nothing left as `[TBD]` or `[FILL IN]`.
