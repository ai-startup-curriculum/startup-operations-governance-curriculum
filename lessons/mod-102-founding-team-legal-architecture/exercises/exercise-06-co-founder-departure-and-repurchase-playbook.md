# Exercise 06 — Co-founder departure and repurchase playbook

> Estimated time: **~3 hours** · Related chapters: [07 — The founder-employment relationship and the departure playbook](../07-founder-employment-relationship-and-departure.md) · [02 — Founders' restricted stock, vesting, and the repurchase right](../02-founders-restricted-stock-and-repurchase.md) · [06 — Founder conflict-of-interest baseline](../06-founder-conflict-of-interest.md)

## Problem statement

Continue the three-founder corporation from the earlier exercises. It is now month 22. The corporation has closed a seed round; a Series-A is anticipated in about four months. **Priya (CTO) has decided to leave** to found a different company in an adjacent-but-non-competitive space. The remaining founders (Alex and Marcus) are supportive; the outside director is neutral; Priya is willing to sign a clean separation package.

You are the COO / GC. Author the **founder-departure playbook** end-to-end for this scenario, per chapter 07's playbook and the SPA / § 144 mechanics from chapters 02 and 06.

## Facts

- Priya's SPA (exercise 02): 3,500,000 shares of common stock at $0.0001 par per share; 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff, vesting-start date at formation (no prior-service credit was granted — but see exercise 01 / 02 for whether you did credit her three months; use whichever you have on record).
- Priya's PIIA (exercise 03): current-form, present-assignment language, complete Prior Inventions schedule, DTSA notice included after exercise 04.
- Priya's employment agreement (assume): at-will W-2 employee, base salary $150k, no separate severance provision. Standard confidentiality and post-termination obligations cross-referenced to the PIIA.
- Priya's tenure at departure: 22 months from formation. Vested per SPA schedule = 22/48ths = 45.83% of 3,500,000 = ~1,604,167 shares (round per your vesting-schedule mechanic — monthly vs. daily accrual matters).
- Priya is 42 years old.
- The corporation has adopted the trade-secret baseline from exercise 04.
- No pending disputes; Priya is departing amicably.
- The Series-A term sheet is expected in 4 months; the anticipated lead investor is a Tier-1 fund.

Assume the corporation's outside counsel is engaged. You are the operating owner.

## Requirements

### Part A — The separation matrix

Restate the SPA's separation-trigger matrix for Priya's specific SPA (from exercise 02). For each trigger type (voluntary resignation, termination for cause, termination without cause, resignation for good reason, death or disability, change-of-control), state:

- What vests / stays with Priya.
- What the corporation repurchases and at what price.
- Any acceleration.
- Any severance.
- Ongoing obligations (PIIA / DTSA / trade-secret).

Then identify which trigger applies to this specific departure — chapter 07 distinguishes "voluntary resignation" from "termination without cause" from "resignation for good reason." The amicable-mutual case does not fit any bucket cleanly; explain how the board should characterise it, and why that characterisation matters for the equity and severance treatment.

### Part B — The board convening and § 144 cleansing

Draft the board consent authorising the separation:

1. Priya (as a director) is the interested director. She recuses.
2. Alex and the outside director are the disinterested directors. They deliberate and vote.
3. The consent recites the disclosure of the material facts, Priya's recusal, and the disinterested-director vote.
4. The material terms:
   - Departure date (30 days out for transition).
   - Severance: propose a specific number of months of base salary (chapter 07 gives 3–12 months as a common range; justify your number) plus continued benefits under COBRA at corporation expense.
   - Acceleration: propose a specific number of months of accelerated vesting (chapter 07 notes 3–12 months on termination-without-cause is common; this is amicable-mutual — justify).
   - Repurchase of remaining unvested shares (post-acceleration) at par.
   - Continued PIIA and DTSA obligations.
   - Non-solicit of the corporation's employees for a defined period (chapter 07 notes 12 months is common; verify against state law).
   - Full mutual release of claims with OWBPA-compliant formalities (Priya is 42).
   - Non-disparagement (mutual).
   - Board and officer resignations effective on the departure date.
5. Cleanse under DGCL § 144(a)(1).

### Part C — The separation agreement

Author the separation agreement itself. It must include, at minimum:

1. Recitals, effective date, and departure date.
2. Compensation through departure date.
3. Severance treatment (matching the board consent).
4. Vesting treatment on the departure date (including any acceleration).
5. Repurchase of unvested shares (mechanics referenced to the SPA).
6. Continuing PIIA / DTSA / trade-secret obligations.
7. Non-solicit and (if you include one) narrow non-compete. If you include a non-compete, address enforceability under Priya's state of residence (from exercise 03: Washington — RCW 49.62 restricts non-competes below defined earnings thresholds; verify current numbers as of drafting date).
8. Mutual release of claims. For Priya's age-40+ status, include the OWBPA 21-day consideration / 7-day revocation / written eligibility-factors formalities per 29 U.S.C. § 626(f) and 29 C.F.R. Part 1625.
9. Non-disparagement (mutual).
10. Return of property.
11. Cooperation with pending matters (e.g., patent-perfection documents from the PIIA's further-assurances clause).
12. Board and officer resignation letters as attached exhibits.
13. Governing law and dispute resolution.

### Part D — The share-repurchase mechanic

Author the share-repurchase execution as a separate board consent (chapter 07 recommends the repurchase be its own consent so it is legible as an ordinary-course board action):

1. Recital: the departure and vesting math.
2. Vested count at departure date = X shares (compute against Priya's SPA and any acceleration).
3. Unvested count = 3,500,000 − X.
4. Repurchase price = unvested count × $0.0001.
5. Payment method (check or wire) and delivery mechanic.
6. Cancellation on the share ledger and return to the authorised-but-unissued pool.
7. Cap-table platform (Carta / Pulley) update instructions.
8. Confirmation that the vested shares remain with Priya subject to SPA transfer restrictions (ROFR, permitted-transferee carveouts).

Show the math for the vesting calculation explicitly. If you granted acceleration in Part B, apply it here.

### Part E — Cap-table snapshot before and after

Produce a two-column cap-table view: pre-departure vs. post-repurchase. Include every founder, each employee-scale grant (assume ~800,000 shares outstanding to non-founder employees), and the option pool. The post-departure snapshot must reflect:

- Priya's vested shares held as passive common stock with SPA transfer restrictions.
- The repurchased unvested shares back in the authorised-but-unissued pool.
- Updated fully-diluted percentages.

### Part F — Communication plan

Draft the communication package:

1. An internal all-hands note (from Alex, ~200 words) announcing Priya's departure.
2. A customer / partner note (~150 words) for any counterparty that has been in direct contact with Priya.
3. A brief external note (~100 words) if warranted — chapter 07 says truthful, respectful, tightly-scoped.
4. A talking-points doc for Alex, Marcus, and the outside director in case investors, recruits, or press ask.

Chapter 07 flags that over-elaboration creates defamation exposure; keep the communications tight.

### Part G — Off-boarding and property-return checklist

A checklist covering:

- Laptop and hardware return.
- Credentials revocation (email, SSO, GitHub, cloud consoles, cap-table platform).
- Confirmation of return of confidential information (a signed certification).
- Access-log preservation for post-departure audit (relevant to trade-secret enforcement per chapter 05).
- COBRA election paperwork.
- Final-pay and expense-reimbursement resolution.

### Part H — Diligence-ready file summary

A one-page inventory of what the Series-A diligence request will want, and what is in the file (per chapter 07's "The paperwork trail Series-A will read" section). Confirm each artefact is present:

- Original SPA + 83(b) receipt.
- Original PIIA (with DTSA notice).
- Original offer letter / employment agreement.
- Separation agreement (with OWBPA compliance).
- Board consent approving separation (§ 144 cleansed).
- Board consent authorising repurchase.
- Share-ledger entries.
- Updated cap-table snapshot.
- Resignation letter (board seat + officer positions).
- Any side letters or amendments (there should be none for a clean separation).

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 07's "The departure conversation" section is the primary reference — walk each of its nine steps.
- Chapter 07's "concrete example" is a departure playbook for a similar scenario (a CTO departure at month 22 ahead of Series-A). Use it as a structural guide, not as boilerplate to copy.
- The vesting math depends on how you defined vesting in exercise 02 — monthly (1/48 per month) is the market default. If you did quarterly, apply consistently.
- The OWBPA 21-day / 7-day formalities are statutory. Do not shorten the 21-day consideration period below 21 days for an individual (or below 45 days for a group release).
- Non-competes are heavily restricted; Priya is in Washington which has an earnings-threshold framework (RCW 49.62). If you include a non-compete, address enforceability explicitly. Chapter 07 defers non-compete depth to mod-103, but the separation-agreement drafter has to make a call.
- Do not treat "amicable mutual" as a legal characterisation — the board still needs to characterise the trigger (voluntary vs. termination without cause vs. resignation for good reason) because vesting, acceleration, and severance depend on it.
- The § 144 cleansing consent should recite the disinterested-director vote — the outside director's role here is essential.
- Reference the `<!-- needs-research -->` markers in chapter 07 on the Carta ~30% co-founder-departure statistic (do not cite a specific figure without verification) and on the current FLSA exempt-executive salary threshold if you address any exempt-status implications.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. The separation-trigger characterisation is defensible and consistent with the SPA / employment agreement.
2. The § 144 cleansing consent recites disclosure, recusal, and disinterested-director approval.
3. The vesting math is arithmetically correct against the SPA and any acceleration.
4. The share repurchase is executed via a separate board consent with correct price ($0.0001 × unvested count), payment mechanic, and ledger cancellation.
5. The separation agreement's OWBPA formalities are present (21-day consideration, 7-day revocation, written eligibility factors) because Priya is over 40.
6. Non-solicit / non-compete drafting acknowledges Washington-state enforceability constraints if applicable.
7. Continuing PIIA / DTSA / trade-secret obligations are cross-referenced to the original PIIA (not restated in full).
8. The board and officer resignations are documented as effective on the departure date.
9. The cap-table snapshot's math reconciles pre- and post-departure.
10. The communication plan is tight and does not over-elaborate.
11. The diligence-file summary confirms every artefact chapter 07 lists is present.
12. Statutory / regulatory citations (DGCL § 144, 29 U.S.C. § 626(f), 29 C.F.R. Part 1625, RCW 49.62) are correct.
