# 2. Board cadence and materials

> How often the board meets, what lands in their inbox, when it lands, and where it is stored — the governance machinery around the pack, not the pack itself.

## Motivation

A board meeting is not a status update. It is a legally-constituted decision-making event that produces (a) minutes admissible as evidence of the board's deliberation and (b) resolutions that authorize the corporation to act. The COO / GC / corporate secretary owns the *machinery* — cadence, notice, delivery, session structure, portal, archive — so that directors arrive prepared, deliberate on the right topics, vote on the right resolutions, and leave a defensible record.

The *content* of the pack — KPI dashboard, cash / runway, financial model, investor update — is the CFO's product. ## Meeting cadence

Cadence is a function of stage, burn, and information asymmetry between management and the board.

- **Seed / pre-seed.** Quarterly minimum. Delaware law (DGCL § 141) does not mandate a specific meeting frequency; the bylaws set it and the market sets the expectation. Investor-side directors on a seed board expect at least quarterly meetings; the term sheet or Voting Agreement often codifies this.
- **Series A.** Quarterly is standard, with a monthly written CEO update between meetings. Some Series-A boards move to every-6-weeks when the company is scaling headcount fast or burn is elevated.
- **Series B and growth stage.** Monthly or every-6-weeks. At this stage the board is making capital-allocation and executive-comp decisions that cannot wait a full quarter.
- **Pre-IPO.** Monthly, with committee meetings (audit, compensation, nominating/governance) on their own cadences interleaved. Audit committee typically meets at least quarterly to review financial statements before filing; comp committee meets around each grant cycle and each annual comp review.
- **Special meetings.** Called on the notice period the bylaws prescribe (commonly 24–48 hours for special meetings; check the specific bylaws). Special meetings are the mechanism for time-sensitive approvals — a term sheet countersignature, an emergency bridge, a departure of a named executive officer.

A written CEO update between meetings is not a substitute for a meeting. Boards act by (a) meeting and voting or (b) unanimous written consent under DGCL § 141(f). A "monthly update" that is neither is management communication, not board action.

## Pre-read discipline

The dominant failure mode of a startup board meeting is the CEO walking directors through the deck live, page by page, for the first ninety minutes — leaving no time for the discussion the deck was supposed to enable. Fix this with a pre-read rule and enforce it.

- **Delivery deadline.** Materials in the portal **72 hours before the meeting.** Some boards (particularly with international directors or heavy audit-committee agendas) tighten this to 96 hours or 5 business days. Pick one, put it in a board charter or board operating norms document, and hold to it.
- **Content of the pre-read.** Full CFO pack, prior-meeting minutes for approval, any resolutions to be voted on, any consents circulating, and a one-page cover memo from the CEO framing the decisions on the table and the deep-dive topic.
- **Meeting expectation.** Directors arrive read. The agenda does *not* include "CFO walks through the deck." It includes "open questions on materials" — meaning the CFO fields questions, does not narrate slides. If a director shows up unread, that is a director-management issue for the chair, not a reason to re-narrate the pack.
- **Late-add rule.** Anything not in the pre-read is either (a) tabled to the next meeting, (b) added only with unanimous director consent to a walk-in item, or (c) handled as a post-meeting written consent. The chair enforces this.

## Standard agenda structure with time budgets

A well-run 3–4 hour startup board meeting runs on a repeatable skeleton:

1. **CEO pre-read check-in / open questions on materials — 20–30 minutes.** Not a walk-through. The CEO opens, confirms directors have read, and takes questions on the pack. Anything that is *not* the strategic topic gets answered here and closed.
2. **Strategic deep-dive on ONE topic — 60–90 minutes.** One topic per meeting. Pipeline health. International expansion. Pricing model change. Platform bet. The CEO or the topic owner presents a decision memo (already in the pre-read), states the recommendation, and opens discussion. The board debates. Where a decision is needed, the resolution goes to a vote in the next block.
3. **Risks / decisions / consents / approvals — 30–45 minutes.** Formal board actions: approving prior minutes, ratifying option grants (comp committee recommendation), approving equity issuances, approving material contracts above a threshold, ratifying bank/signatory changes, adopting policies. Each action item has a pre-drafted resolution the secretary reads and puts to a vote. The secretary records the vote by director.
4. **Executive session without management — 15–30 minutes.** Directors only. CEO, CFO, GC, corporate secretary all leave the room. The chair runs. This is where directors discuss CEO performance, management-team concerns, and anything they will not say in front of the executives. The corporate secretary re-enters at the end to record the resolutions and any action items that came out; the *content* of the executive-session discussion is not minuted, but the *fact* of the session and any resolutions passed are.
5. **Independent-directors-only session — as needed.** A subset of the executive session: only directors who are not investor-affiliated and not management. Required when the board is discussing a conflicted transaction — an investor-led bridge, a co-investment by an existing investor, a related-party contract. NYSE and Nasdaq listing rules will require regular independent-only sessions post-IPO; adopting the habit early is cheap.

## Concrete example: Series-B quarterly meeting, deep-dive on international expansion

**Company:** post-Series-B SaaS, ~$18M ARR, US-only, 120 employees. **Meeting length:** 3 hours 45 minutes, in person. **Attendees:** 5 directors (2 founder-directors, 2 investor-directors, 1 independent), CEO, CFO, GC/corporate secretary (secretary). **Pre-read delivered:** Tuesday 9:00 AM (72 hours before Friday 9:00 AM start), including a 12-page international-expansion decision memo authored by the CEO and CRO.

| Time | Block | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00 – 9:05 | Call to order, confirm quorum, approve prior minutes | Chair / Secretary |
| 9:05 – 9:30 | CEO check-in and open questions on the pack (financials, KPIs, hiring, pipeline) | CEO / CFO |
| 9:30 – 11:00 | **Strategic deep-dive: UK + EU expansion — go / no-go and structure** — CEO/CRO recommendation memo; discussion of market sizing, entity structure (UK Ltd + Irish DAC vs. UK-only branch), first-hire profile, transfer-pricing implications, GDPR posture (cross-reference [mod-110 privacy](../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/)) | CEO / CRO |
| 11:00 – 11:15 | Break | — |
| 11:15 – 12:00 | Risks / decisions / consents / approvals: (a) resolution to authorize formation of UK subsidiary and appointment of UK counsel, (b) approval of Q3 option grants per comp committee recommendation, (c) ratification of D&O renewal, (d) approval of $2M ARR customer master agreement deviation from standard MSA | Secretary / GC |
| 12:00 – 12:30 | Executive session — directors only; CEO/CFO/GC leave | Chair |
| 12:30 – 12:45 | Reconvene, secretary records any resolutions from executive session; confirm action items and next-meeting date | Secretary |

The secretary drafts minutes from a running outline kept during the meeting, circulates for chair review within 5 business days, and posts to the portal for approval at the next meeting.

## Board portal selection

Email + Dropbox is not a board portal. It fails on access control, mobile experience, resolution workflow, and archive. The verified portals in the market are **Diligent Boards, Nasdaq Boardvantage, OnBoard (Passageways), BoardEffect (a Diligent product), Boardable, and Board Intelligence.** Evaluate on:

- **(a) Security posture.** SOC 2 Type II report on file and reviewable by the buyer; ISO/IEC 27001 certification; encryption at rest and in transit; SSO/SAML integration; per-tenant data isolation. Ask for the current SOC 2 report under NDA. <!-- needs-research: current SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 posture for each of Diligent Boards, Boardvantage, OnBoard, BoardEffect, Boardable, Board Intelligence -->
- **(b) Granular per-committee access control.** Committee books (audit, comp, nominating) restricted to committee members. Directors on one committee should not see another committee's materials by default. Guests (auditors, outside counsel) get scoped read-only access to specific books.
- **(c) Mobile app quality.** Directors read on iPads and phones. Native iOS/Android apps with offline read, annotation sync, and page-jump navigation are table stakes. Test with two directors before you buy.
- **(d) Resolutions / consents workflow with countersignature.** Unanimous written consents (DGCL § 141(f)) should circulate inside the portal, capture each director's signature with timestamp, and produce a signed PDF for the minute book. Same for stockholder consents.
- **(e) Minute-drafting workflow with version control.** Secretary drafts, chair reviews with tracked changes, version history preserved, final approved minutes locked and dated.
- **(f) Permanent annotations vs. ephemeral.** Some portals make director annotations permanent and discoverable; others make them ephemeral (per-user, cleared on close). Ephemeral is the safer default — annotations by individual directors during pre-read are personal preparation notes, not corporate records, and preserving them creates unnecessary discovery surface.
- **(g) Auditor-view access for the audit committee.** Read-only access for external auditors to the audit committee book, scoped to specific meetings and materials, with an audit trail of every view.
- **(h) Archive / records-retention policy.** The corporation controls retention. Exported minute-book PDFs should be periodically archived to the corporation's own document management system so that portal termination does not create a records gap. Understand the vendor's data-return-on-termination terms before signing.

The portal is a records system as much as a distribution system. Treat the selection with the same rigor as any other SOC-2-touching vendor.

## Coordination boundary with the CFO

The CFO owns the *content* of the board pack: KPI dashboard, cash / runway, financial model, investor update, department reviews. That authoring, review, and finance-narrative work lives in the startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum. <!-- needs-research: confirm the exact chapter path in startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum mod-110 for board-pack authoring -->

This chapter — and the corporate-secretary function generally — owns:

- The **cadence** the pack is delivered on (72-hour rule, calendared meeting dates).
- The **delivery mechanism** (the board portal and its access controls).
- The **agenda** the pack is presented against and the time budgets for each block.
- The **resolutions and consents** the pack triggers (drafted by GC / corporate secretary in advance of the meeting).
- The **minutes** that record what the board did with the pack.
- The **archive** — where minutes, resolutions, consents, and the pack itself live after the meeting, and for how long.

Draw this line explicitly with the CFO in the first month on the job. The CFO owns the narrative; the corporate secretary owns the machinery. Both must run cleanly for the board to function.

## Summary

- Cadence scales with stage: quarterly at seed / Series A, monthly or every-6-weeks at growth / high-burn / pre-IPO, special meetings on defined notice.
- Materials in the portal 72 hours before the meeting (some boards use 96 hours or 5 business days). Directors arrive read; the agenda is a discussion agenda, not a walk-through.
- Standard skeleton: CEO check-in (20–30 min) → one strategic deep-dive (60–90 min) → decisions / consents / approvals (30–45 min) → executive session (15–30 min) → independent-directors-only as needed.
- One strategic topic per meeting. The pre-read includes a written decision memo; the meeting is for debate and vote.
- Evaluate board portals — Diligent Boards, Nasdaq Boardvantage, OnBoard, BoardEffect, Boardable, Board Intelligence — on SOC 2 / ISO 27001, per-committee access control, mobile, resolutions / consents workflow, minute-drafting version control, annotation model, auditor access, and records retention.
- The CFO owns the *content* of the pack; the corporate secretary owns the *machinery* around it — delivery, agenda, resolutions, minutes, archive.
