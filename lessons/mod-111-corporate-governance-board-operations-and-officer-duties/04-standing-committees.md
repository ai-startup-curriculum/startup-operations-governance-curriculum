# 4. Standing committees

> Committees are how a working board scales — audit, compensation, nominating & governance, plus the ad-hoc special committee and the emerging AI-risk sub-committee. Each has a lifecycle trigger, an independence bar, a charter, a cadence, and a reporting line back to the full board.

## Motivation

A five-person early-stage board can transact all of its business in a two-hour meeting. A nine-person pre-IPO board cannot. Long before the S-1 goes on file, the board needs to disaggregate its work into standing committees — bodies that meet on their own cadence, retain their own advisors, and deliver pre-digested recommendations to the full board. The listing standards (NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A; Nasdaq Rule 5605) and the SEC's independence rules (Rule 10A-3 for audit; Rule 10C-1 for compensation) impose hard requirements at the moment the company lists — but the disciplined pattern is to stand each committee up 12–18 months earlier. Delaware law permits the board to delegate authority to committees under DGCL § 141(c), subject to statutory carve-outs, which is what makes special-committee governance a real cleansing device under DGCL § 144 and *Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014).

### Audit committee

**Lifecycle.** Typically stood up at Series B, and non-negotiable by the pre-IPO organizational meeting. Once the company begins issuing audited GAAP financial statements, the audit committee becomes the body that owns the audit relationship.

**Composition & independence.** Under NYSE 303A.06 / 303A.07 (or Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)) and SEC Rule 10A-3, every member must be independent — no compensatory fees from the issuer other than board fees, and no affiliation with the issuer or any subsidiary. The chair should be financially literate; at least one member must qualify as an "audit committee financial expert" under Reg S-K Item 407(d)(5), disclosed in the proxy statement. In practice this is the ex-CFO or ex-audit-partner director recruited for the seat.

**Charter contents.** The audit committee charter must, at minimum:

- Grant the committee **sole authority to appoint, compensate, retain, and oversee the independent auditor** (SOX § 301; Exchange Act § 10A(m)). The Big Four (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) are the typical picks for an IPO-track company; a second-tier firm (BDO, Grant Thornton, RSM) is common at Series B before the audit firm is upgraded pre-IPO.
- Establish a **direct reporting line from the auditor to the committee**, not to management. The engagement partner reports to the audit committee chair; management is a participant, not a gatekeeper.
- Require **quarterly review of the financial statements** (10-Q pre-filing) and annual review of the 10-K.
- Require **pre-approval of all non-audit services** under SOX § 202 (Exchange Act § 10A(i)).
- Establish **whistleblower complaint procedures** under SOX § 301 — a confidential, anonymous channel for accounting or auditing concerns, with the committee receiving the log and disposition.
- Oversee **internal control over financial reporting (ICFR)** — the SOX § 404 program, management's assessment and (once an accelerated filer) the auditor's attestation.
- Review **related-party transactions** for accounting and disclosure treatment (Item 404 of Reg S-K), coordinated with the nominating & governance committee's DGCL § 144 review.
- Require an **executive session with the auditor** at every meeting, no member of management present.

**Cadence.** Quarterly at minimum, timed to the earnings cycle. Additional meetings for the annual audit plan (Q4), the ICFR walkthrough (Q3), and any restatement or material-weakness event.

**Deliverables to the full board.** Recommendation on filing the 10-Q/10-K; annual recommendation on ratifying the auditor; the annual audit committee report for the proxy statement (Item 407(d)(3)); escalation of whistleblower matters reaching a materiality threshold.

### Compensation committee

**Lifecycle & scope.** Formed on the same Series-B-through-pre-IPO arc as the audit committee. This chapter is a pointer only — the substance of the compensation committee's charter, the equity-comp philosophy, the § 162(m) / § 409A / ISO mechanics, and the say-on-pay disclosure regime are owned by [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).

**Independence heightening.** Dodd-Frank added SEC Rule 10C-1, which directs the exchanges to impose heightened independence tests on compensation-committee members beyond the baseline — considering the source of the member's compensation (including consulting or advisory fees) and any affiliation with the issuer. NYSE 303A.05 and Nasdaq Rule 5605(d) implement Rule 10C-1 and set the charter requirements: authority to retain independent compensation consultants, funding for advisors, and an independence assessment for those advisors.

Everything else — CEO pay-setting, equity-refresh, clawbacks under Rule 10D-1, CD&A drafting — lives in mod-105.

### Nominating & governance committee

**Lifecycle.** Typically pre-IPO, formed 12–18 months before the S-1. Some boards fold this into the compensation committee at Series B and split it out at Series D; the pattern to prefer is a separate committee once the board reaches seven directors.

**Composition.** Majority-independent under NYSE 303A.04 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(e); market practice is fully independent.

**Charter contents.** The committee owns:

- **Board-composition planning** — the skills matrix, tenure planning, diversity commitments, and refreshment cadence covered in [chapter 01](./01-board-architecture-and-composition-trajectory.md).
- **Director search, nomination, onboarding, and continuing education.**
- **Board and committee evaluations** — the annual self-assessment and periodic third-party evaluations, covered in [chapter 08](./08-annual-governance-operating-rhythm.md).
- **Corporate-governance guidelines** — meeting cadence, executive sessions, director stock-ownership guidelines, over-boarding limits, retirement age if any.
- **Code of business conduct and ethics** — annual review and any waiver approvals (waivers for executive officers or directors must be promptly disclosed under Item 5.05 of Form 8-K).
- **ESG / sustainability disclosure oversight** — climate, human-capital, and governance disclosures. <!-- needs-research: post-2024 status of SEC climate-disclosure rules and any final Reg S-K amendments after the Fifth Circuit litigation -->
- **Related-party transactions** — the DGCL § 144 review process for interested-director transactions, running in parallel with the audit committee's Item 404 disclosure review.

**Cadence.** Quarterly, with an intense Q1 around the annual meeting and a Q3 composition review that feeds the following year's search plan.

**Deliverables to the full board.** Director-nominee recommendations for the proxy; evaluation results; proposed amendments to governance guidelines and code of ethics; related-party-transaction determinations.

### Transaction / special committee

**Lifecycle.** Ad hoc. Formed by board resolution whenever a transaction implicates a conflict — the CEO or a controlling stockholder is on both sides, a director's fund is participating in a financing, a management-led buyout is on the table, or the board needs an untainted body to evaluate M&A interest.

**Composition.** Disinterested and independent directors only. "Disinterested" in the DGCL § 144 sense — no personal financial interest in the transaction — and "independent" in the *MFW* sense: no dominating relationship with the conflicted party. Two or three directors is the working minimum.

**Charter contents.** The board resolution that forms the committee is the charter, and it must grant:

- **Full authority to negotiate, evaluate, and reject** the transaction — including the express power to "just say no." A committee empowered only to negotiate, without authority to walk away, does not cleanse the transaction. Non-negotiable.
- **Authority to retain independent legal counsel and an independent financial advisor**, funded by the corporation, without management's approval.
- **A defined scope** — the specific transaction or category of transactions.
- **A reporting obligation** back to the full board.

**The MFW framework.** For a transaction with a controlling stockholder, *Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014), holds that the deal earns business-judgment-rule review (rather than entire-fairness review) only if the controller conditions the transaction, **ab initio**, on both (a) approval by a fully-empowered special committee of independent directors, and (b) an uncoerced, informed vote of a majority of the minority stockholders. Both conditions must be present from the outset; a committee bolted on mid-process does not qualify. See [chapter 05](./05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md) for the duty-of-loyalty framework the committee operates inside.

**Cadence.** Whatever the deal timeline requires — often weekly through active negotiation, with full contemporaneous minutes.

**Deliverables to the full board.** Recommendation to approve, reject, or continue negotiating; the fairness opinion (if obtained); the complete record of the committee's process, which becomes the litigation file if the deal is later challenged.

### Emerging AI-risk / model-risk sub-committee

**Lifecycle.** Increasingly common at companies where AI systems are a material driver of revenue or risk — the arc mirrors where cybersecurity sub-committees were around 2018. Often stood up as a sub-committee of the audit committee, or at AI-native companies as a standalone technology & risk committee.

**Composition.** At least one director with genuine technical fluency in modern ML systems, not a generalist "tech" director. Independence expectations follow the parent committee (an audit-committee sub-committee inherits Rule 10A-3 independence).

**Charter contents.** The sub-committee owns:

- **Model-risk oversight** — inventory of production models, risk tiers, and governance controls (evals, red-teams, human-in-the-loop) at each tier.
- **Deployment approval for high-stakes systems** — a gating role for any model whose deployment crosses a defined materiality threshold (regulated-decision use, customer-facing autonomous agents, safety-critical systems).
- **Incident review** — post-incident review of model failures and near-misses; the log feeds the enterprise risk register.
- **Alignment with the enterprise AI-risk framework** — the sub-committee is the board-level owner of the AI-risk policy (authored elsewhere).
- **Coordination with the audit committee** on AI-related financial-reporting risk — where a model materially affects revenue recognition, valuation of intangibles, or ICFR, the audit committee remains primary and the AI sub-committee is a technical advisor.

**Caremark linkage.** A board that ignores AI risk at a company where AI is mission-critical is exposed to a *Caremark* claim for failure of oversight. The AI sub-committee is the board's principal answer to the *Marchand v. Barnhill* / *Boeing* line of cases — see [chapter 05](./05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md).

**Cadence.** Quarterly, with additional meetings triggered by incidents or deployment decisions.

**Deliverables to the full board.** Quarterly AI-risk dashboard; deployment approvals; incident summaries with root cause and remediation; annual review of the AI-risk framework.

**Scope handoff.** This chapter defines the governance-machinery pattern only. AI-safety engineering, evals design, red-team protocols, and the enterprise AI-risk framework itself are owned by the head-of-ai-governance-learning curriculum.

## Summary

- Committees are how a working board scales; the audit and compensation committees are non-negotiable by the pre-IPO organizational meeting, and the disciplined pattern stands them up 12–18 months earlier at Series B.
- The audit committee owns the auditor relationship end-to-end under SOX § 301 (retention), § 202 (non-audit-service pre-approval), and SEC Rule 10A-3 (independence), with at least one Item 407(d)(5) financial expert and a direct reporting line from the auditor to the committee.
- The compensation committee's substance lives in [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); this module records only that Rule 10C-1 and NYSE 303A.05 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(d) heighten independence and set charter requirements for advisor retention.
- The nominating & governance committee owns board composition, director search and onboarding, evaluations, governance guidelines, the code of ethics, ESG disclosure oversight, and the DGCL § 144 related-party review.
- The special committee is ad hoc, disinterested, independent, and — critically — empowered to say no; the *Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.* MFW framework requires both an ab-initio special committee and a majority-of-the-minority vote to earn business-judgment-rule review for controlling-stockholder transactions.
- The AI-risk sub-committee is the emerging pattern for AI-heavy companies and the board's answer to a modern *Caremark* claim; it defines the governance machinery, while the technical AI-safety substance lives in the AI-governance curriculum.
- Every committee has a written charter, a cadence, a reporting line to the full board, and independent-advisor authority — all delegated under DGCL § 141(c) subject to the statutory carve-outs on non-delegable matters.
