## Why this module exists

Once a company takes institutional capital, the board stops being a founder-and-friend formality and becomes the legal and operational control surface of the business. The quality of that surface — the composition, the cadence, the materials, the minute book, the committee charters, the insurance tower, the insider-trading plumbing — is what the business-judgment rule, a Caremark plaintiff, a D&O carrier, and an IPO underwriter will each inspect. The board operating layer is where governance either compounds into protection or quietly rots into exposure.

The failure modes are depressingly consistent. Minutes that read as narrative transcripts rather than records of informed deliberation forfeit the Van Gorkom-style business-judgment presumption. Equity issuances approved by email instead of a §141(f) unanimous written consent are invalid at the moment Series-A counsel audits the cap table. Indemnification agreements that were never actually signed leave officers personally exposed in the first derivative suit. A Caremark compliance system that formally exists on paper but has never produced a board-level report on a mission-critical risk is the exact pattern the Delaware Supreme Court identified in *Marchand* as liability.

This module owns the **board operating layer**. It teaches the incoming General Counsel, Chief of Staff, Corporate Secretary, or COO how to compose a board on an institutional trajectory, run it on a disciplined cadence, document it defensibly, insure it correctly, and prepare it for the governance regime that attaches the day the S-1 goes effective.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical venture-backed company moving from Series-A through pre-IPO, produce and defend:

1. A dated Series-A → Series-B → Series-C → pre-IPO board composition trajectory with the independence math worked under NYSE §303A / Nasdaq 5605 and an explicit independent-director recruitment plan.
2. A full year of adoption-ready board materials: cadence, 72-hour pre-read norm, standard agenda template, executive-session protocol, and a board-portal RFP matrix.
3. A corporate-secretary artefact set: action-and-decision-style minutes, a §141(f) unanimous written consent, a §228 stockholder written consent with the §228(e) notice, an NVCA-form indemnification agreement, and a 12-month cadence calendar.
4. Audit, compensation, nominating & governance, transaction / special, and AI-risk sub-committee charters that satisfy Rule 10A-3, Rule 10C-1, DGCL §141(c), and the *MFW* conditions where applicable.
5. A fiduciary-duty case-teardown library covering *Van Gorkom*, *Caremark*, *Marchand*, *Revlon*, *Unocal*, *Blasius*, *Corwin*, *MFW*, and *Zuckerberg*, plus a Caremark / Revlon defense memo applied to a modern hypothetical.
6. A D&O tower specification with primary ABC, excess layers, a Side-A DIC layer, exclusion carve-backs, and a 6-year IPO run-off tail — defended against a benchmark broker quote.
7. A full insider-trading policy stack: policy, blackout and pre-clearance SOP, a 10b5-1 plan template compliant with the December 2022 SEC amendments (cooling-off periods, certifications, single-plan limit, good-faith representation), and a Section 16 / 13D / 13G filing protocol.
8. A T-24-to-T-0 pre-IPO governance-readiness roadmap integrated with the CFO-side finance / controls track, plus an EGC-election memo under the JOBS Act.
9. A crisis-governance role-play response across cyber incident, executive misconduct, and hostile-acquisition-approach scenarios, with the board / committee / officer decision rights and disclosure triggers pre-wired.
