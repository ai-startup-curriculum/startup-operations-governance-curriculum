## Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL)

- **DGCL** — Title 8, Chapter 1 of the Delaware Code. https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/
  - § 102(b)(7) — director exculpation for duty-of-care breaches; the 2022 amendment extended optional exculpation to specified senior officers for direct (non-derivative) duty-of-care claims.
  - § 109 — bylaws; power to adopt, amend, repeal.
  - § 141(a) — board authority to manage the business and affairs of the corporation.
  - § 141(b) — board size, quorum, and the ≥⅓-of-authorized-directors quorum floor.
  - § 141(c) — committee delegation; enumerated non-delegable matters (e.g., amending bylaws, approving matters requiring stockholder approval, certain dividend/stock-issuance actions absent specific authorization).
  - § 141(e) — good-faith reliance on records, officers, experts, and board committees.
  - § 141(f) — action by unanimous written consent of directors.
  - § 141(i) — remote participation at board meetings by conference telephone or similar communications equipment.
  - § 142 — officers; required and permitted officer roles; appointment and removal.
  - § 144 — interested-director transactions; safe harbors via disinterested-director approval, disinterested-stockholder approval, or substantive fairness.
  - § 145 — indemnification and advancement of expenses to directors, officers, employees, and agents.
  - § 174 — directors' joint-and-several liability for unlawful dividends and stock repurchases/redemptions.
  - § 211 — annual meeting of stockholders; § 211(b) annual-meeting requirement; § 211(c) remedy to compel a meeting.
  - § 220 — stockholder books-and-records inspection rights.
  - § 228 — stockholder action by written consent; § 228(e) prompt-notice requirement to non-consenting stockholders.
  - § 262 — appraisal rights.
- **Delaware Court of Chancery** — https://courts.delaware.gov/chancery/

## Delaware case law

### Duty of care

- **Smith v. Van Gorkom**, 488 A.2d 858 (Del. 1985) — gross negligence in approving a merger without adequate deliberation; catalyst for § 102(b)(7).

### Interested-director transactions and entire fairness

- **Weinberger v. UOP, Inc.**, 457 A.2d 701 (Del. 1983) — entire-fairness standard (fair dealing and fair price).
- **Kahn v. Lynch Communication Systems, Inc.**, 638 A.2d 1110 (Del. 1994) — controller transactions and burden-shifting.

### Duty of oversight (Caremark line)

- **In re Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation**, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996) — oversight duty and information-and-reporting systems.
- **Stone v. Ritter**, 911 A.2d 362 (Del. 2006) — anchoring Caremark in the duty of loyalty; bad-faith standard.
- **Marchand v. Barnhill**, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019) — mission-critical-risk oversight (Blue Bell listeria).
- **In re Boeing Co. Derivative Litigation** <!-- needs-research: confirm reporter cite, likely 2021 WL 4059934 (Del. Ch. Sept. 7, 2021) — verify before quoting --> — safety-oversight failure as a Caremark claim.
- **In re McDonald's Corp. Stockholder Derivative Litigation**, 289 A.3d 343 (Del. Ch. 2023) — officers owe Caremark-style oversight duties within their areas of responsibility.

### Business-judgment rule and burden-shifting

- **Aronson v. Lewis**, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984) — business-judgment rule and demand-futility test (subsequently refined by *Zuckerberg*).
- **Cede & Co. v. Technicolor, Inc.**, 634 A.2d 345 (Del. 1993) — rebuttal of the business-judgment presumption shifts the burden to entire fairness.

### Revlon / change-of-control

- **Revlon, Inc. v. MacAndrews & Forbes Holdings, Inc.**, 506 A.2d 173 (Del. 1986) — reasonable efforts to obtain the best price when a sale is inevitable.
- **Paramount Communications, Inc. v. QVC Network, Inc.**, 637 A.2d 34 (Del. 1994) — Revlon duties triggered by cash/controller transactions.
- **Paramount Communications, Inc. v. Time Inc.**, 571 A.2d 1140 (Del. 1990) — strategic combinations and when Revlon is *not* triggered.

### Takeover defense

- **Unocal Corp. v. Mesa Petroleum Co.**, 493 A.2d 946 (Del. 1985) — intermediate scrutiny for defensive measures.

### Stockholder-franchise interference

- **Blasius Industries, Inc. v. Atlas Corp.**, 564 A.2d 651 (Del. Ch. 1988) — compelling-justification standard for board action primarily intended to impede the vote.

### Cleansing devices

- **Corwin v. KKR Financial Holdings LLC**, 125 A.3d 304 (Del. 2015) — fully informed, uncoerced, disinterested stockholder vote invokes business-judgment review.
- **Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.**, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014) — MFW framework for business-judgment review of controller transactions.

### Demand futility and special litigation committees

- **Aronson v. Lewis**, 473 A.2d 805 (Del. 1984) — original demand-futility test (above).
- **United Food & Commercial Workers Union v. Zuckerberg**, 262 A.3d 1034 (Del. 2021) — three-part director-by-director demand-futility test replacing the *Aronson/Rales* bifurcation.
- **Zapata Corp. v. Maldonado**, 430 A.2d 779 (Del. 1981) — special-litigation-committee two-step inquiry.

## Federal securities law — statutes, rules, releases

### Securities Act of 1933

- **§ 2(a)(19)** — "emerging growth company" (EGC) definition (added by the JOBS Act).
- **§ 5** — gun-jumping / quiet-period framework (pre-filing, waiting, post-effective).
- **§ 5(d)** — EGC "testing-the-waters" communications with QIBs and institutional accredited investors.
- **§ 6(e)** — confidential S-1 submission by EGCs.

### Exchange Act of 1934

- **§ 10(b)** — anti-fraud; implemented by Rule 10b-5.
- **§ 10A(m)** — audit-committee independence requirement.
- **§ 13(d)** — beneficial-ownership reporting (Schedule 13D/13G).
- **§ 14** — proxy rules.
- **§ 16(a)** — insider reporting (Forms 3, 4, 5).
- **§ 16(b)** — short-swing profit disgorgement.
- **§ 16(c)** — short-sale prohibition for insiders.

### SEC rules under the Exchange Act

- **Rule 10b-5** — 17 C.F.R. § 240.10b-5 (anti-fraud).
- **Rule 10b5-1** — affirmative defense for pre-planned insider trading plans (as amended by the December 2022 release).
- **Rule 10b5-2** — duties of trust and confidence in misappropriation cases.
- **Rule 10A-3** — audit-committee independence and responsibilities.
- **Rule 10C-1** — compensation-committee independence and adviser-independence factors.
- **Rule 10D-1** — issuer clawback-policy requirement.
- **Rule 12b-2** — filer categories (large accelerated, accelerated, non-accelerated, smaller reporting company).
- **Rule 13d-1 / 13d-2** — Schedule 13D/13G filing and amendment requirements. <!-- needs-research: confirm effective dates for the October 2023 amendments accelerating the initial 13D deadline to 5 business days and compressing 13G windows -->
- **Rule 14a-8** — stockholder-proposal rule.
- **Rule 14a-16** — notice-and-access proxy delivery.
- **Rule 14a-19** — universal proxy card in contested director elections.

### Regulation FD

- **Regulation FD** — 17 C.F.R. §§ 243.100–243.103 (selective-disclosure prohibition).

### Regulation S-K

- **Item 401** — directors, executive officers, promoters, and control persons.
- **Item 402** — executive-compensation disclosure (including CD&A and pay-ratio disclosure).

### Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)

- **§ 202** — audit-committee pre-approval of non-audit services.
- **§ 301** — audit-committee independence and whistleblower complaint channels.
- **§ 302** — CEO/CFO certifications of periodic reports.
- **§ 304** — clawback of CEO/CFO bonuses and equity gains following a restatement caused by misconduct.
- **§ 306** — pension-blackout trading prohibition (and Regulation BTR).
- **§ 404** — management ICFR assessment (§ 404(a)) and auditor attestation (§ 404(b); EGCs and SRC/non-accelerated filers are exempt from § 404(b)).
