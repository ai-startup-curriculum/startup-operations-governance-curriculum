# Exercise 09 — Pre-IPO governance readiness dual-track plan

> Estimated time: **~12 hours** · Related chapter: [09 — IPO readiness and the transition to public-company governance](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp operating an AI-infrastructure / GPU-fabric platform selling capacity and orchestration to model-training customers and inference tenants across the US, EU, and UK. The board met two weeks ago, approved a T-24-to-T-0 dual-track IPO-readiness workstream, and set a targeted S-1 filing date 24 months from today. Today is T-24. The general counsel owns the governance track; the CFO owns the finance / controls / audit-firm-upgrade track; the head of investor-relations role has not been filled; the lead underwriter and the IPO counsel have not yet been selected; and the board has further agreed the corporation will elect emerging-growth-company (EGC) status under the JOBS Act at filing and will evaluate listing on NYSE versus Nasdaq with a formal recommendation at T-18.

The governance footprint is material. The current board is seven seats — three founder-affiliated, three preferred-stock investor-designees, and one legacy independent carried over from Series-B — and no committees are composed to NYSE § 303A or Nasdaq Rule 5605-5615 independence. The incumbent auditor is a regional firm engaged five years ago; the CFO's track will drive the Big Four upgrade, but the governance track owns the audit-committee stand-up that will interview and recommend audit-firm selection. The corporation carries a Series-C D&O tower (primary ABC $15M, one $10M excess layer) sized for a private company; the pre-IPO re-tower, the six-year run-off tail, and the Section 11 / 12(a)(2) / 15 Securities Act exposure overlay were scoped in exercise-06 and will be re-executed against the filing calendar here. The pre-IPO stockholder-agreement information rights, board-observer rights, and protective provisions will sunset at listing and are flagged by preferred counsel as needing restatement before S-1 filing.

Three operating realities force the governance-readiness roadmap onto the GC's desk at T-24 rather than T-12. **(i)** The two-year audit history under Reg S-X requires the Big Four audit firm to be engaged no later than T-24 so that both years of audited financials in the S-1 are produced by the firm the market will see at listing; the audit-committee stand-up (two independent directors meeting Rule 10A-3 at T-18, three at T-12) is on the governance critical path. **(ii)** The independent-director recruiting cycle for an audit-committee financial expert under Reg S-K Item 407(d)(5) and a compensation-committee chair meeting the Rule 10C-1 heightened independence test runs 6-12 months per seat; starting at T-12 forces unqualified recruits onto the roster. **(iii)** The EGC-election analysis is not a filing-day decision — the §102(b)(1) phase-in-of-accounting-standards opt-in is a one-time, irrevocable election at first filing, and the CFO track needs the governance track's recommendation to lock the audit workpapers against the elected accounting-standards posture by T-18.

The GC has eight weeks to walk into the board governance subcommittee with the dual-track plan the corporation will actually execute. She owns the governance track end-to-end; the CFO owns the finance / controls / audit-firm-upgrade track with jointly-owned interfaces into the audit committee; the compensation-committee chair (once seated) owns the Rule 10D-1 clawback adoption and the Item 402 disclosure pre-work; the lead independent director (once seated) owns the committee-charter restatements. Author the full package.
