# Exercise 01 — Board composition trajectory and independent-recruitment drill

> Estimated time: **~8 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — Board architecture and the composition trajectory](../01-board-architecture-and-composition-trajectory.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp operating a GPU-fabric platform that leases reserved and on-demand accelerator capacity to AI-model-training customers under multi-month and multi-year commit contracts. The corporation is 112 FTE, has booked ARR in the high-single-digit millions on a committed-contract run-rate well into eight figures, and is currently closing a Series-A round led by a top-tier infrastructure-focused venture fund with co-investment from a strategic silicon partner. Pre-Series-A the board has two seats, both held by the two co-founders (the CEO and the CTO); the incoming financing documents will drop an NVCA-form Voting Agreement onto the closing deck. The corporation has never adopted anything beyond a stock-form set of incorporation-service bylaws, and the general counsel started three weeks before signing.

Three operating realities force the board-composition-and-architecture question onto the general counsel's agenda before the closing-day signing sprint. **(i)** The lead Series-A investor has circulated a term sheet calling for a five-seat board on the NVCA 2+1+2 pattern (two common, one Series-A preferred, two independents designated jointly), and has asked whether the corporation's bylaws permit the board to seat a new director by resolution on closing day — which the current fixed-at-two bylaws do not. **(ii)** The strategic co-investor has requested an observer seat and the lead has pushed back, asking the CEO to produce a one-page memo on where the bylaws end and where the Voting Agreement begins so the two seat-allocation surfaces do not drift. **(iii)** The compensation-committee-and-audit-committee chairs that the corporation will need at Series-B and pre-IPO do not exist yet, and no independent-director recruitment work has begun; the lead investor has conditioned post-closing cooperation on a named independent-recruitment plan with a sourcing split, a selection-criteria slate, and a dated trajectory through Series-C and pre-IPO.

The general counsel has eight weeks — from signing through the first post-closing board meeting — to walk into the inaugural board with a composition-and-architecture package the corporation will actually run against. She owns the end-to-end package; the CEO owns the independent-candidate sourcing and the reference-check conversations; the CFO owns the audit-committee-financial-expert interface under Regulation S-K Item 407(d)(5) and the retainer-benchmarking spine; outside corporate counsel owns the bylaws restatement for Series-A closing; the corporate secretary (the general counsel at this stage) owns the new-director onboarding package assembled on closing day. Author the full package.

1. **The Series-A closing-day composition.** The five-seat NVCA 2+1+2 pattern against Maridian's specific facts: the two common seats (CEO and CTO today, with explicit flag that the CTO common seat may rotate to an outside common-designated independent at Series-B per the lead's preferences), the one Series-A preferred seat (the lead investor's designee), the two joint-independent seats (common and preferred acting together). Name whether the strategic co-investor takes an observer seat or a full director seat, and if an observer, defer the observer-rights cap and confidentiality undertaking to the Voting Agreement memo in Part E. Name the timing sequence on closing day: the bylaws restatement from Part B is adopted first (expanding the director-count range under DGCL § 141(b)); the board then by resolution fixes the exact number at five; the stockholders act under DGCL § 228 to elect the three incremental directors to the newly-created seats; the three new directors each counter-sign their indemnification agreement before taking their seat.
2. **The Series-B composition.** The incremental seat the Series-B lead typically receives, the common-side seat that most frequently shifts to an independent at Series-B, the resulting six- or seven-seat board, and the explicit independence math under NYSE §303A.02 and Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2) (name which seats count as independent, which are categorically non-independent, and which require a case-by-case §303A.02(b) / Rule 5605(a)(2) analysis). Name the standing-committee trigger: at Series-B the corporation stands up a compensation committee under Rule 10C-1 and a nominating & governance committee, deferring the charter drafting to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md). Name the specific §303A.02(b) and Rule 5605(a)(2) disqualifying affiliations that will apply to the Series-A and Series-B preferred designees (employee or partner of the fund, $120,000-threshold compensation relationship, immediate-family-member relationship) so the independence characterisation is not a surprise at Series-C.
3. **The Series-C composition.** The seven-seat board with a non-founder, non-lead-investor majority; the audit-committee-financial-expert seat filled by a named Series-C independent (defer the Item 407(d)(5) analysis to Part C's selection criteria); the progressive founder-seat dilution and the explicit retention of a founder-CEO seat through pre-IPO. Name the observer-seat rationalisation step: observer counts are capped and observers with Voting-Agreement termination triggers are sunset at Series-C. Name the AI-risk sub-committee stand-up trigger at Series-C per [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md) — Maridian's GPU-fabric platform carries AI-adjacent Caremark exposure that a formed sub-committee will oversee, deferring the charter authoring itself to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md).
4. **The pre-IPO composition.** Seven to nine seats with an independent majority sized for NYSE §303A.01 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(b)(1) listing-standard independent-majority compliance and Rule 10A-3 audit-committee-independence compliance. Name the lead-independent-director designation if the CEO is also chair (per chapter 01), the fully-independent audit / compensation / nominating-and-governance committee composition, and the §303A.07 / Rule 5605(c) audit-committee size and financial-expert posture. Name the §303A.00 / Rule 5615(b) controlled-company exemption posture explicitly: if a single stockholder or a group of stockholders acting together continues to hold more than 50% of the voting power, the corporation may elect the controlled-company exemption from the majority-independent board requirement and from the fully-independent compensation and nominating-committee requirements — defer the election decision to the Series-C-to-pre-IPO window where the cap-table posture is known.
5. **The independence math, worked.** For each stage, a one-row-per-seat table listing (seat number, designating party under the Voting Agreement or the bylaws, independence characterisation under NYSE §303A.02 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2), §303A.07 / Rule 5605(c)(2)(A) audit-committee-eligibility flag, Rule 10C-1 compensation-committee-eligibility flag). Any benchmark on typical Series-B or Series-C seat counts for a comparable AI-infrastructure company carries a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker because the comparable set changes with each cohort. Name the transitional-period reliance Rule 10A-3(b)(1)(iv)(A) and the §303A.00 phase-in schedule the corporation will rely on at IPO (one independent audit-committee member on the listing date, a majority within 90 days, fully independent within one year).

### Part B — Bylaws architecture memo

Author `bylaws-architecture-memo.md` — the memo outside corporate counsel will work against when restating the bylaws at Series-A closing. Cover:

1. **Director-count architecture under DGCL § 141(b).** Move from the current fixed-at-two bylaws to a range (one to nine is the practitioner default; defend the maximum against the Part A pre-IPO target). Name the board-resolution mechanic that seats a new director inside the range without a bylaw amendment, and name the §141(k) removal-with-or-without-cause posture the restatement adopts.
2. **Quorum.** Name the quorum (majority of the whole board is the practitioner default); state explicitly that the restatement does not drop quorum to the § 141(b) one-third floor, and explain the reason in two sentences (a quorum below majority invites single-interested-director action and complicates the business-judgment-rule record in a Van Gorkom-style challenge — defer the fiduciary-duty substance to [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md)).
3. **Remote participation under DGCL § 141(i) and action by unanimous written consent under § 141(f).** Name the restatement's § 141(i) remote-participation clause and the § 141(f) UWC clause including electronic-signature acceptance. Name the practical cadence: § 141(f) UWCs carry routine option grants, form-of-agreement approvals, and banking-authority updates between quarterly meetings — defer the §141(f) consent-package authoring to [chapter 03](../03-corporate-secretary-function.md) and the corporate-secretary exercise.
4. **Officer architecture under DGCL § 142.** Name the officer slate the restatement adopts (CEO, President, Secretary, Treasurer or CFO at minimum; flag whether the CEO also holds President at Series-A) and the signing-authority delegation per officer — contracts, banking, stock issuances. Defer officer-duty substance to [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md).
5. **Notice-and-waiver, special-meeting-calling-authority, and advance-notice posture.** The DGCL § 229 waiver mechanic, the special-meeting-calling-authority set (chair, CEO, any two directors is the practitioner default), and the explicit deferral of advance-notice bylaw provisions and the prohibition-on-stockholder-written-consent restatement to the pre-IPO bylaws cycle covered in [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md). State explicitly that the Series-A restatement *preserves* DGCL § 228 less-than-unanimous stockholder written consent because the corporation remains private.
6. **Charter-vs-bylaws placement and amendment-authority allocation.** For each architectural decision above, name whether it sits in the restated certificate of incorporation (amendable only by board-plus-stockholder action under DGCL § 242) or in the restated bylaws (amendable by the board alone under DGCL § 109, unless the charter reserves the power to stockholders). The director-count range and the officer slate sit in the bylaws; the preferred-director designation rights and the protective-provisions list sit in the charter and are owned by the fundraising curriculum. Flag any charter amendment the lead investor requires at closing (Series-A protective provisions, authorised-share increase, charter restatement to adopt the certificate of designation) as a cross-reference to the fundraising curriculum rather than a drafting item for this memo.

### Part C — Independent-director recruitment plan

The audit-committee-financial-expert criterion specifically names the Item 407(d)(5)(ii) qualifying-experience set.
2. **The sourcing split.** Board-network platforms for the first independent seat at Series-A and the incremental Series-B independent (**Bolster**, **theBoardlist**, **Extraordinary Women on Boards**, **Him for Her**, **Ascend Pinnacle**, **Nurole**); executive-search firms for the Series-C independent and the pre-IPO audit chair and compensation chair (**Spencer Stuart**, **Heidrick & Struggles**, **Russell Reynolds**, **Egon Zehnder**). Name the rationale for the split (chapter 01: working directors come off the platforms; marquee pre-IPO audit chairs come off the search firms with a formal process the S-1 governance narrative can reference).
3. **The lead-time and process discipline.** Name the lead time from search kickoff to board election per sourcing path (platform-sourced searches are shorter; search-firm-led searches longer). Flag every specific week-count and every retainer figure with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` because lead times and retainer structures move with the market cycle. Name the process gates: longlist review, slate shortlist, first-round CEO and lead-investor conversations, formal interview with full board, reference checks, final board vote.
4. **The retainer approach.** Name the retainer components (annual cash retainer, committee-chair premium, equity grant in options or RSUs vesting over a service period, expense reimbursement, D&O coverage per the tower spec in [chapter 06](../06-do-insurance-program.md), indemnification agreement signed on or before day one). 5. **The pipeline and bench.** Name the operating discipline that keeps an active bench of two to three candidates per upcoming seat so the corporation is not sourcing from a cold start when a seat opens; the quarterly pipeline review cadence; the DEI-composition reporting into the nominating & governance committee once that committee is stood up at Series-B.

### Part D — New-director onboarding package

Author `new-director-onboarding-package.md` — the standing onboarding deliverable set the corporate secretary assembles on or before a new director's first day. Cover:

1. **The ten-item onboarding set from chapter 01.** Name each of the ten items explicitly: (i) charter and bylaws (as currently in effect, with a redline against the prior restatement); (ii) all executed board consents from the trailing 24 months, indexed by date and subject; (iii) D&O policy summary and the director's executed indemnification agreement (signed on or before day one); (iv) organisational chart and current headcount; (v) cap table (current, fully-diluted and outstanding views) and the most recent 409A valuation; (vi) the last three board packs in full; (vii) standing-committee charters at the current stage (deferred to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md) for substance); (viii) code of ethics and insider-trading policy (insider-trading policy signed and returned, policy substance deferred to [chapter 07](../07-insider-trading-policy-and-10b5-1-programme.md)); (ix) calendar of upcoming meetings for the next twelve months, including committee meetings, plus the annual planning cycle; (x) a formal orientation session with the CEO, CFO, GC, and the audit-committee chair (one to two hours, scheduled within two weeks of election).
2. **Series-specific overlays.** Name the overlay items that attach at specific stages: a Caremark compliance-system orientation at Series-C per [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md); a public-company-director orientation at pre-IPO covering Regulation FD, Rule 10b5-1 affiliate obligations, Section 16, and Schedule 13D / 13G mechanics per [chapter 07](../07-insider-trading-policy-and-10b5-1-programme.md) and [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md).
3. **Assembly ownership and the audit trail.** The corporate-secretary function owns assembly and the audit trail (defer the corporate-secretary operating substance to [chapter 03](../03-corporate-secretary-function.md)). Name the delivery mechanic (board-portal upload with acknowledged-receipt tracking), the retention of the onboarding-package manifest in the minute book, and the annual refresh cadence for sitting directors.
4. **The indemnification agreement execution gate.** State explicitly that a new director does not attend their first meeting without a counter-signed indemnification agreement on file and D&O coverage confirmed. Reference the NVCA-form indemnification agreement that [chapter 03](../03-corporate-secretary-function.md) names and the tower spec in [chapter 06](../06-do-insurance-program.md).

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 01 and the module [README](../README.md) are the primary references for the composition-trajectory substance. Chapter 01 is the arc; [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md) is the committee-charter substance Part A defers to at Series-B; [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md) is the pre-IPO bylaws-restatement surface Part B defers to.
- Do not duck the independence math in Part A. Walk NYSE §303A.02 and Nasdaq Rule 5605(a)(2) seat-by-seat; identify which seats are categorically non-independent (the CEO, an investor-fund partner holding a §303A.02(b)(iv) or Rule 5605(a)(2)(E) disqualifying affiliation), which are presumptively independent (a joint-designated outside director with no business relationship to the corporation), and which require a case-by-case affirmative-board-determination under §303A.02(a)(ii) and Rule 5605(a)(2). The §303A.02(b)(ii) three-year lookback on prior employment and the §303A.02(b)(iii) $120,000-threshold compensation lookback are both operative; a candidate with a past consulting arrangement inside the lookback window is not categorically independent and the board-determination record must reflect the analysis.
- Do not invent specific dollar numbers for director retainers, committee-chair premiums, equity-grant sizes, or vendor retainer fees in Part C. The corporation's compensation consultant and the CFO verify every figure against a named benchmark (chapter 01 names the practitioner-default sources; the specific benchmark survey carries `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`). Note that the retainer benchmarks for a private-company independent director and for a public-company director differ materially; the retainer approach Part C authors should name the pre-IPO step-up explicitly.
- Do not invent specific lead-time week-counts for search-firm or platform searches in Part C. Lead times move with the market cycle; name the sequence of gates and flag the week-count against each gate with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`. The audit-chair search in particular carries a longer gate because the Item 407(d)(5) qualifying-experience set is narrow and the slate is short.
- Do not re-author adjacent-module substance. Founder-side seat mechanics belong to [mod-102](../../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/); equity-grant sizing for independent directors belongs to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); the Voting-Agreement draft itself belongs to the fundraising curriculum. Reference the deferrals; do not re-decide.
- No real peer-company names. "Maridian Compute, Inc." is the fictional corporation; the lead Series-A investor, the strategic co-investor, the search firms, and the board-network platforms are referenced by name only where chapter 01 has already named them.
- The Series-A restatement *preserves* DGCL § 228 less-than-unanimous stockholder written consent because the corporation remains private; the stockholder-written-consent prohibition is a pre-IPO-cycle decision (chapter 01 and [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md)). Do not prematurely prohibit § 228 action at Series-A. Similarly, do not add advance-notice stockholder-nomination bylaw provisions at Series-A; they are a pre-IPO restatement item.

The AI-risk sub-committee is a Series-C formation driven by Maridian's GPU-fabric business model — defer the charter to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md) and the fiduciary-oversight rationale to [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md).
