# Exercise 03 — Corporate-secretary minutes and consents drill

> Estimated time: **~7 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — The corporate-secretary function](../03-corporate-secretary-function.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corporation building a GPU-fabric platform for AI-infrastructure customers — a managed accelerated-compute service that abstracts multi-cloud GPU inventory, orchestrates workload placement across vendor pools, and bills on effective-token throughput. The corporation is 260 FTE, closed its Series B eighteen months ago, and is now papering a $120M Series C led by a growth fund with a strategic co-lead; signing is targeted for Friday, closing the following Tuesday. The board will expand from five seats to seven on closing: the existing three preferred-director seats (one Series A, one Series B, one from the Series C lead) plus two common directors (the CEO-founder and the CTO-founder) are joined by a second Series C director designated by the co-lead and a new independent director — a former public-company audit-committee chair who has agreed to join subject to receipt of her executed indemnification agreement and D&O questionnaire.

The incoming corporate secretary is the deputy general counsel for corporate and securities, promoted into the office this quarter and inheriting a minute book that has been populated by rotating paralegals for three years. The known inherited gaps are specific and non-trivial. First, a May 2023 unanimous written consent approving a 420,000-share option grant to the then-VP of Engineering shows signatures from all four directors but was never counter-signed by the then-secretary, was never filed in the minute book, and surfaces now only because a Series-C diligence request for the consent file turned up the orphan DocuSign envelope. Second, the Series-A investor director who joined in 2021 was never issued an indemnification agreement — the form was drafted, the director countersigned a page that was never returned, and the D&O policy schedule names him but the contractual indemnity does not exist. Third, the 2022–2024 minutes are a mix of narrative-style transcripts (capturing director quotes verbatim) and sparse action-and-decision entries, with no consistent exhibit numbering and no index.

The incoming independent director, the Series-C lead counsel, and the company's outside counsel all want a clean minute-book posture at closing. The incoming secretary has sixty days from the Series-C closing to ship a corporate-secretary artefact set that supports the closing itself, remediates the three inherited gaps, and stands up the recurring cadence the function will run on. She owns the end-to-end package; the general counsel countersigns the remediation memo; the CFO owns the exhibit stack for the Series-C resolutions; outside counsel owns the §228(e) prompt-notice form.

## Requirements

### Part A — Series-C closing board-meeting minutes

Author `board-meeting-minutes-series-c-closing.md` — action-and-decision-style minutes for the special board meeting at which the Series-C financing, the amended and restated certificate of incorporation, the option-pool expansion, and the appointment of the new independent director are approved. Cover:

1. **The meeting mechanics.** Caption, date, time, location (virtual with a Delaware-registered-office reference), notice-and-waiver recital, attendance (directors present, directors absent, management and advisors present, counsel from the lead firm and from the co-lead's firm), quorum attestation under the bylaws' DGCL §141(b) quorum provision, and chair/secretary designations.
2. **The materials pre-read and exhibit stack.** Named exhibits with a consistent numbering convention (Exhibit 1 through Exhibit N) covering the term sheet, pro-forma cap table, A&R Certificate of Incorporation, Series C Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement, Investors' Rights Agreement, Right of First Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement, Voting Agreement, option-pool expansion resolution, D&O questionnaire and indemnification agreement for the incoming independent director, and the officer's closing certificate.
3. **The resolutions, in action-and-decision style.** The substantive approvals written as standalone resolutions (financing approval, charter approval, option-pool expansion subject to stockholder approval, appointment of the new independent director effective at closing subject to receipt of her executed indemnification agreement, delegation of further-actions authority to the officers). Record who moved, who seconded, the vote count including abstentions and recusals, and any director who did not participate because of an interested-director posture under DGCL §144.
4. **The executive-session entry and exit.** The timestamp the board entered executive session (independent directors only, with management excused), the single-sentence subject-matter description, the timestamp of exit, and a note that no formal action was taken in executive session.
5. **Minutes-approval mechanics.** The scheduled next-meeting ratification of these minutes, the circulation path to the CEO and general counsel for accuracy review before the next meeting, and the exhibit-index entry in the minute book.

### Part B — §141(f) unanimous written consent

Author `141f-unanimous-written-consent.md` — a UWC for the between-meeting approval scenario the Series-C financing will produce (a between-signing-and-closing waiver of a protective-provision condition, or a between-closing-and-first-post-closing-meeting approval of a commercial contract the board must approve under the IRA's protective provisions). Cover:

1. **The caption and preamble.** The DGCL §141(f) caption, the recital that the undersigned constitute all of the directors of the corporation and are acting by written consent in lieu of a meeting, and a reference to the bylaws provision authorizing §141(f) action.
2. **The recital stack.** One "WHEREAS" per material fact the board is relying on — the negotiation history, any committee recommendation (compensation committee or audit committee where relevant), management's recommendation, and the fairness-to-stockholders finding where the matter is one the directors must find fair.
3. **The operative resolutions.** Standalone RESOLVED clauses drafted so each can be extracted as a certified excerpt, including a final omnibus further-actions resolution authorizing the officers to execute all documents necessary or advisable to effect the foregoing.
4. **The exhibit stack and signature-and-countersignature mechanics.** The numbered exhibit list, the e-signature blocks with date lines (one per director), the DGCL §116 electronic-signature recital, the effective-time clause, and the corporate-secretary countersignature-and-attestation block.
5. **The circulation-and-filing SLA.** The 48-hour or 72-hour signature SLA appropriate to the matter (same-day for closings), the board-portal workflow Maridian will use (one of Diligent, BoardEffect, Nasdaq Boardvantage, or Athennian — do not fabricate pricing; flag any vendor-feature claim with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`), and the minute-book filing step with exhibit-numbering.

### Part C — §228 majority stockholder consent and §228(e) notice

Author `228-stockholder-consent-and-notice.md` — the majority written consent of stockholders approving the A&R Certificate of Incorporation, the increase in authorized common and preferred, the increase in shares reserved under the 2022 Equity Incentive Plan, and (if required by the charter) the Series C financing itself, together with the §228(e) prompt-notice form to non-consenting stockholders. Cover:

1. **The §228 consent itself.** Caption, recital that the undersigned stockholders hold at least the minimum number of votes that would be required to authorize the action at a meeting under DGCL §228(a), the recital stack identifying each matter being approved, the operative resolutions, and the signature blocks for each consenting holder (founders' common, preferred holders voting on an as-converted basis as the charter requires, any class-vote signature blocks required by the existing charter's protective provisions).
2. **The vote-count worked-math.** The total shares outstanding on an as-converted basis as of the record date; the threshold the matter requires (majority of outstanding common and preferred voting together as a single class, plus any class-vote thresholds the existing charter imposes on charter amendments under DGCL §242(b)(2)); the actual consenting-holder share count; a short paragraph attesting that the threshold is met.
3. **The §228(e) prompt-notice form.** A separate notice to every non-consenting stockholder of record, identifying the actions taken, the effective date, the fact that the actions were taken by written consent of the holders of the minimum number of votes required, and (for any action that triggers DGCL §262 appraisal rights) the appraisal-notice language and the statutory deadline. Address delivery mechanics (notice-of-internet-availability style vs. direct mail, the record-date mechanics, and the retention of proof-of-mailing).
4. **Permissibility check.** A short analysis confirming that the current bylaws and charter permit stockholder action by written consent for private-company matters, with a forward-looking note that the IPO-readiness bylaws amendment will remove §228 action for post-IPO stockholder business (cross-referenced to [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md)).
5. **Filing discipline.** The minute-book entry for the executed consent, the §228(e) notice-sent log, and the corporate-secretary attestation that both the consent and the notice are on file.

### Part D — Indemnification agreement and priority-of-payments stack

Author `indemnification-agreement-and-priority-stack.md` — an NVCA-form indemnification agreement for the incoming independent director, with the priority-of-payments stack worked against the actual Maridian D&O program and the Series-C lead fund's investor-indemnity. Do not quote NVCA contract terms; reference the form by name and annotate the deal-specific decisions. Cover:

1. **The agreement anatomy.** The named sections the NVCA form contemplates (mandatory indemnification to the fullest extent permitted by law under DGCL §145(a)(b), mandatory advancement under DGCL §145(e) with the §145(e) undertaking, the §145(c) mandatory-indemnification-on-success restatement, the presumption-in-favor-of-indemnitee and burden-of-proof provisions, the independent-counsel determination mechanic, the no-waiver and successors-and-assigns clauses) — annotated with Maridian's chosen advancement turnaround (20 or 30 days) flagged with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current practitioner-default.
2. **The §141(i) committee-reliance recital.** A short recital in the agreement's interpretive section acknowledging that the directors' good-faith reliance on committees and on officer / employee / advisor reports is protected by DGCL §141(i) for purposes of the §145 standard-of-conduct determination.
3. **The priority-of-payments stack.** Named sources of indemnity in priority order: (i) the corporation as primary indemnitor; (ii) the Side-A / B / C D&O policy stack as secondary (deferring the tower architecture itself to [chapter 06](../06-do-insurance-program.md)); (iii) the Series-C lead fund's investor-affiliated indemnity as tertiary and expressly excess-and-non-contributing. Name the subrogation-and-contribution mechanics, the "no circularity" discipline (the fund indemnity does not cycle through the corporation), and the carrier-notification path when a claim first hits.
4. **The intake SOP.** The secretary's day-one discipline for every new director and every new Section 16 officer — form execution, signed-original delivery to the indemnitee, minute-book filing, D&O policy-schedule update (add-the-name notice to the broker), and quarterly re-attestation per the cadence calendar in Part E.
5. **The Series-A investor director remediation.** A short sub-section addressing the 2021 Series-A investor director who was never issued the agreement — the clean-paper discipline (execute the current NVCA form effective the original director start date where the agreement expressly contemplates retroactive effective-date, or execute effective today with an acknowledgment of the §145(a) and (e) coverage during the gap period) and the general-counsel memo-to-file documenting the remediation.

### Part E — Corporate-secretary cadence calendar

Author `corporate-secretary-cadence-calendar.md` — a 12-month cadence the incoming secretary will run the function on. Cover:

1. **The annual obligations.** Named months and owners for: bylaws review (Q4, general counsel and secretary); committee-charter review (each committee at a scheduled meeting; full board ratifies); board self-evaluation kickoff (Q3, chaired by the nominating/governance committee chair — defer committee architecture to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md)); minute-book close-out (fiscal year-end or immediately after the year-end board meeting), producing the index of meetings and consents, the exhibit index, the share-ledger reconciliation, and the written attestation that the record is complete.
2. **The quarterly obligations.** Named quarterly cadence for: consent-tracking audit (reconcile consents circulated against consents executed and filed, resolve stragglers, flag resolutions authorizing further-actions never taken); indemnification-agreement re-attestation for any new directors or Section 16 officers appointed during the quarter, including the D&O policy-schedule update confirmation; subsidiary-maintenance audit where Maridian has operating subsidiaries.
3. **The meeting-driven obligations.** Each regular board meeting produces action-and-decision minutes circulated to the CEO and general counsel within five business days, presented for approval at the next meeting, filed with exhibits in the minute book within ten business days of approval. Each §141(f) UWC follows the circulation-signature-filing SLA in Part B.
4. **The tooling stack.** Named vendor categories (board-portal, entity-management system, e-signature) with a vendor list (Diligent / Diligent Entities, Athennian, BoardEffect, Nasdaq Boardvantage; DocuSign / Adobe Acrobat Sign) — vendor-feature claims and any pricing or retainer flagged with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.
5. **The reporting line into the board.** The quarterly corporate-secretary report to the nominating/governance committee covering the consent-audit results, the indemnification-coverage status, the minute-book close-out status, and any open remediation items — anchored against the DGCL §141(i) reliance posture directors are entitled to on secretary-produced reports.

### Part F — Minute-book remediation memo

Author `minute-book-remediation-memo.md` — a memo from the incoming corporate secretary to the general counsel and the audit committee addressing the three inherited gaps. Cover:

1. **The 2023 option-grant consent.** The orphan-envelope analysis (signatures were collected but the consent was never counter-signed by the then-secretary and never filed), the DGCL §141(f) effectiveness analysis (the consent was effective when the last director signed if the signatures themselves were valid, independent of counter-signature or filing — the counter-signature-and-filing step is the secretary's administrative discipline, not a statutory effectiveness condition under §141(f)), and the proposed remediation (counter-sign and file now with an attached memo-to-file explaining the delay; confirm with outside counsel that the underlying option grant is valid and that the equity records reflect the grant; no re-approval required unless outside counsel determines the original signatures were themselves defective).
2. **The 2021 Series-A investor-director indemnification agreement.** Cross-reference Part D's remediation subsection; summarize for the audit committee the gap exposure (the director had §145 statutory coverage but no contractual mandatory-indemnification-and-advancement) and the clean-paper disposition.
3. **The mixed-style minutes 2022–2024.** The go-forward posture (action-and-decision as the default effective immediately; narrative style only for specific agenda items where the record-of-factors-considered protects the directors — e.g., a Revlon-mode sale-process or a going-concern determination) and the no-rewriting posture for historical minutes (the record is what the record is; a rewriting attempt would itself be a litigation exposure). Author a short practitioner note on the exhibit-numbering convention going forward and the one-time minute-book re-indexing project the secretary will scope in Q1.
4. **The diligence-posture recommendation.** For the Series-C data-room diligence question list (counsel-prepared), the recommended disclosure posture on each of the three gaps — what to disclose, how to characterize the remediation, and the memo-to-file that will accompany the data-room upload.
5. **The audit-committee briefing.** A one-paragraph briefing the audit-committee chair will read at the next committee meeting summarizing the gaps, the remediation posture, and the cadence discipline in Part E that prevents recurrence.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 03 is the primary reference. The §141(f) UWC template outline, the action-and-decision-style minutes excerpt, and the cadence-calendar substance in chapter 03 are the authoritative anchors; do not re-author the substance, apply it to the Maridian fact pattern.
- Cite the DGCL sections by number the first time they appear in each deliverable file: §141(b) for quorum, §141(c) for committee authority, §141(f) for board UWCs, §141(i) for the reliance-on-reports protection, §142 for the secretary office, §144 for interested-director transactions, §145(a)(b)(c)(e) for indemnification and advancement, §228 and §228(e) for stockholder consents and prompt notice, and §116 for electronic signatures.
- Reference the NVCA-form indemnification agreement by name in Part D; do not quote specific contract terms. The deal-specific annotations (advancement turnaround, priority-of-payments stack, Series-C-fund-indemnity interaction) are what the exercise tests.
- Vendors named in the cadence calendar and the UWC circulation SOP (Diligent, Diligent Entities, Athennian, BoardEffect, Nasdaq Boardvantage, DocuSign, Adobe Acrobat Sign) are referenced by name only. Do not fabricate pricing, retainers, or feature claims; flag any such figure or claim with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` and name the verification step.
- Do not invent real peer-company names. "Maridian Compute, Inc." is the fictional corporation; the Series-C lead fund is referenced as "the lead" or by role.
- Defer committee substance to [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md), D&O tower architecture to [chapter 06](../06-do-insurance-program.md), and the IPO-readiness bylaws amendment removing §228 action to [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md). Reference the deferrals; do not re-author.
- Nothing in this exercise is a legal determination. The DGCL §141(f) effectiveness analysis for the orphan option-grant consent, the remediation disposition for the Series-A investor-director indemnification gap, and the §228(e) notice contents are all drafts the general counsel and outside counsel finalize.
