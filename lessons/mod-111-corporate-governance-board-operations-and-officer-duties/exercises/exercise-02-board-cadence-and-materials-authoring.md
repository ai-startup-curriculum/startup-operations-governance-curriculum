# Exercise 02 — Board cadence and materials authoring

> Estimated time: **~8 hours** · Related chapter: [02 — Board cadence and materials](../02-board-cadence-and-materials.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a post-Series-B Delaware C-corp operating an AI-infrastructure platform — a GPU-fabric orchestration layer sold to foundation-model labs and mid-market enterprises running their own training and fine-tuning workloads. The corporation is 180 FTE across a San Francisco headquarters and a Zurich engineering outpost, booked mid-tens-of-millions of ARR in the trailing four quarters, closed a Series B eleven months ago led by a growth-stage infrastructure fund, and sits on a declared 24-month IPO horizon in the most recent board-approved operating plan. The board is a five-director body under the restated bylaws: two founder-directors (the CEO and the CTO), two investor-directors (one from the Series-A lead and one from the Series-B lead), and one independent director recruited eight months ago with public-company audit-committee experience. The company has not yet stood up formal standing committees beyond an informal audit-and-comp cadence; that work is the subject of Exercise 04 and is not re-executed here. Three of the five directors sit in Pacific time; one investor-director sits in Eastern time; the independent director is Chicago-based; the standing meeting rhythm will mix in-person and video sessions and must contemplate DGCL §141(i) remote-participation mechanics for every video meeting on the calendar.

The governance-machinery failure modes are already visible and the general counsel inherited them on week one. Pre-reads currently land on a shared Dropbox folder on a cadence that drifted to "the night before" over the last three meetings. The CEO narrates the deck for the first ninety minutes of every session and the strategic topic — the one thing the board was supposed to debate — gets twenty rushed minutes at the end. There is no written executive-session protocol; the chair clears the room when he remembers to. Two material actions in the last quarter — a signatory-authority update at the operating bank and the Q2 option-grant cohort — were "approved by email" and have no DGCL §141(f) unanimous written consent on file. The Series-B lead has asked, in writing, for a board-portal decision inside ninety days; the Series-A lead has countersigned the request. The independent director has separately asked, through the chair, for an independent-directors-only standing block on every agenda and a 72-hour pre-read rule written into a board-operating-norms document the board can actually adopt. The CFO joined the company four months ago from a public-company controller seat and has signalled, informally, that the pack cadence she is accustomed to running is not compatible with "the night before" distribution and that she will push back if the operating norms do not land in the next two cycles.

The general counsel has six weeks to walk into the next regularly-scheduled meeting with a full-year adoption-ready board-operations package the directors will vote to adopt as the standing operating norms of the board. She owns the end-to-end deliverable set; the CFO owns the *content* of the board pack (KPI dashboard, cash and runway, financial model, investor update) and is a coordination counterparty, not a co-author on this package. The corporate-secretary minute-book machinery itself — the minutes template, the §141(f) UWC form, the §228 stockholder-consent mechanic, the NVCA-form indemnification agreement — is the subject of Exercise 03 and is referenced here rather than re-authored. The standing-committee charters (audit, comp, nominating and governance, transaction, AI-risk) are the subject of Exercise 04 and are referenced here as forward dependencies that this package must be compatible with. Author the full package below.

## Requirements

### Part A — Board cadence and operating norms

Author `board-cadence-and-operating-norms.md` — the board-operating-norms document the directors will adopt by resolution at the next meeting. Cover:

1. **The cadence rule for Maridian's current stage.** Series-B, mid-tens-of-millions ARR, 24-month IPO horizon on the operating plan. Name the cadence explicitly — monthly or every-six-weeks for the full board — and defend it against the chapter 02 stage-to-cadence framework. Name the audit-committee and comp-committee interleave cadence by reference to Exercise 04 without re-authoring the committee charters. Reference DGCL §141(b) for the quorum and voting rule the bylaws read into.
2. **The twelve-month meeting calendar.** A dated calendar of regular meetings for the next twelve months, naming the meeting date, the city or format (in-person vs. video), the standing strategic-deep-dive topic slot (one per meeting), the committee meetings running in the same week, and the pre-read-delivery date computed off the 72-hour rule in Part B. The strategic-deep-dive slots are named thematically (pipeline health, pricing-model revisit, international-expansion go/no-go, platform-architecture bet, executive-comp philosophy, security-and-AI-risk posture, etc.) rather than scheduled with specific business content.
3. **Special-meeting notice.** The bylaws-driven notice period (commonly 24–48 hours) for a special meeting, the mechanism (chair or two directors may call), the quorum requirement reading back to DGCL §141(b), the DGCL §141(i) remote-participation posture for a special meeting called on short notice, and the specific triggers Maridian will anchor against — term-sheet countersignature, emergency bridge, departure of a named executive officer, material-contract approval above the delegated threshold, cyber-incident disclosure trigger. Name the notice-waiver mechanic under DGCL §229 and the posture that waivers are the exception, not the operating pattern; name the waiver-record discipline the secretary enforces when §229 is used.
4. **The between-meeting CEO update discipline.** The written monthly CEO update between regular meetings — distribution surface (the board portal selected in Part E), standing content (KPI delta vs. plan, cash position and runway, pipeline movement, headcount delta, open risks), and the explicit disclaimer that a written update is management communication, not board action, and that board action occurs only by (a) a duly-called and quorate meeting under DGCL §141(b) or (b) a unanimous written consent under DGCL §141(f).
5. **The adoption resolution.** A short-form resolution the board will vote on to adopt the operating-norms document as the standing operating discipline of the board, to take effect on the first full meeting cycle after adoption, and to be reviewed annually at the first meeting of the fiscal year.

### Part B — Pre-read and materials standard

Author `pre-read-and-materials-standard.md` — the pre-read discipline the chair and the corporate secretary will enforce. Cover:

1. **The 72-hour rule.** Materials in the portal no later than 72 hours before the meeting start time, measured in the time zone of the meeting (not the time zone of each director). Name the business-hours convention for weekend cut-offs. Name the rationale (directors arrive read; the agenda is a discussion agenda, not a walk-through) and the escalation path when the CFO or the CEO cannot meet the deadline (chair notification plus either a late-materials disclosure at call-to-order or a meeting reschedule).
2. **Required pre-read contents.** The named artefacts every pre-read carries — (a) the CFO board pack, (b) the prior-meeting minutes for approval (authored under Exercise 03), (c) every resolution scheduled for a vote, in final form, (d) every unanimous written consent currently circulating under DGCL §141(f), (e) a one-page CEO cover memo framing the decisions on the table and the strategic-deep-dive question, (f) any committee pre-reads (audit, comp) running the same week. Name the pre-read-table-of-contents convention the secretary will enforce.
3. **The CEO cover-memo template.** The one-page framing memo the CEO authors for every meeting — the two or three decisions on the table, the strategic-deep-dive question framed as a specific go/no-go or design choice, the top-three open risks since the last meeting, and the specific questions the CEO is asking the board to opine on. The cover memo is not the board pack; it is the index into the board pack.
4. **The late-add rule.** Anything not in the pre-read is handled in one of three ways — (a) tabled to the next regular meeting, (b) added as a walk-in only with unanimous director consent at call-to-order, (c) handled after the meeting as a DGCL §141(f) unanimous written consent. The chair enforces; the corporate secretary records the late-add disposition in the minutes.
5. **The pre-read-compliance metric.** The named metric the secretary tracks for the board governance review — pre-read-on-time rate (pre-reads delivered 72+ hours ahead as a share of regular meetings in the trailing four quarters), the target the chair will defend, and the escalation path if the metric breaches.

### Part C — Standard agenda template

Author `standard-agenda-template.md` — the five-block skeleton every regular meeting runs on, with time budgets, plus a fully-worked example meeting. Cover:

1. **The five-block skeleton.** Block 1 — call to order, confirm quorum under DGCL §141(b), approve prior minutes, approve the agenda (5–10 minutes). Block 2 — CEO pre-read check-in and open questions on the pack (20–30 minutes, not a walk-through). Block 3 — strategic deep-dive on one topic (60–90 minutes) with a written decision memo in the pre-read. Block 4 — risks, decisions, consents, approvals (30–45 minutes) where each action item has a pre-drafted resolution the secretary reads and puts to a vote and the vote is recorded by director. Block 5 — executive session (15–30 minutes) and independent-directors-only session as needed. Name the reconvene-to-close block at the end for recording any resolutions that emerged from executive session.
2. **The one-topic-per-meeting discipline.** Only one strategic deep-dive per meeting. The pre-read carries a written decision memo authored by the topic owner — pipeline health by the CRO, pricing by the CFO and head of product, international expansion by the CEO and CRO, platform-architecture bets by the CTO, executive-comp by the comp-committee chair (once seated per Exercise 04). Name the forcing-function — a strategic topic without a written decision memo in the pre-read does not clear the chair's agenda review and is deferred.
3. **A worked quarterly meeting.** Author a full agenda for a worked quarterly meeting with an in-person session in San Francisco, a 3-hour-45-minute session running 9:00 AM to 12:45 PM Pacific, a strategic deep-dive on the platform-architecture bet (consolidated single-tenant vs. multi-tenant GPU-fabric for the next-generation product line) with the CTO as topic owner, and a Block-4 action list including (a) prior-minutes approval, (b) ratification of a Q-cohort option grant batch, (c) approval of a specific above-threshold master-services-agreement deviation for a named foundation-model-lab customer, (d) approval of the D&O renewal structure (the substance of which is deferred to Exercise 06), (e) ratification of a bank-signatory update reflecting a treasurer change. Render the agenda as a timed table with time ranges, blocks, topic owners, and the decision or discussion disposition for each row; mark any director attending by video and name the DGCL §141(i) remote-participation posture for that director.
4. **The resolutions-pack convention.** Every action item in Block 4 has a pre-drafted resolution in the pre-read. Name the resolution-numbering convention, the secretary's read-before-vote discipline, and the vote-recording convention (named vote by director rather than a bare "unanimous" where any abstention or conflict is present, including conflicts handled under DGCL §144).
5. **The time-overrun discipline.** When a block runs over, the chair decides in the moment — extend the block and compress the next, defer the strategic topic to a special meeting, or table a non-time-critical action. Name the default (the strategic topic does not get compressed; the Block-4 action list defers before the Block-3 debate does).

### Part D — Executive-session protocol

Author `executive-session-protocol.md` — the written protocol for the Block-5 executive and independent-directors-only sessions. Cover:

1. **The executive-session default.** Every regular meeting ends with an executive session without management present. The chair runs. The CEO, CFO, GC, corporate secretary, and every other non-director attendee leaves the room (or the video call). The secretary re-enters at the end to record any resolutions passed and any action items that came out of the session. Name the standing content — CEO performance, management-team concerns, succession readiness, chair-director one-on-one surfacing, and anything the directors will not say in front of the executives.
2. **The independent-directors-only session.** A subset of the executive session attended only by directors who are not management-affiliated and not investor-affiliated. For Maridian this is currently the single independent director plus any director the board formally determines is independent for a specific matter under DGCL §144 (interested-director transaction). Name the triggers — any conflicted-transaction discussion (an investor-led bridge, a co-investment by an existing investor, a related-party contract), the standing block the independent director has requested. Name the forward trigger — NYSE and Nasdaq listing rules will require regular independent-only sessions post-IPO (the listing-standard substance is deferred to Exercise 09) and adopting the habit in the Series-B board is cheap.
3. **What gets minuted vs. not.** The *fact* of the executive session is minuted (date, time, duration, directors present, chair). The *content* of the deliberation is not minuted. Any *resolution* passed in executive session is minuted in full with the vote by director. Any *action item* assigned in executive session is minuted as an action item with owner and due date. Name the minute-template convention — a single "Executive session" entry with the fact, duration, and any resolutions or actions, and no narrative of the discussion.
4. **The CEO-performance review cadence.** The named cadence for the executive session's CEO-performance review — at minimum the annual review tied to the compensation cycle (deferred to Exercise 04 and the comp-committee charter) and a mid-year check-in. The independent director leads the review conversation; the chair coordinates with the comp-committee chair once that committee is seated.
5. **The re-entry and record-of-resolutions discipline.** The secretary re-enters to record resolutions and action items, confirms the minute-book entry language with the chair before the meeting closes, and routes the executive-session minute entry through the chair-review cycle (not the full-management-review cycle) before posting to the portal for approval at the next meeting.

### Part E — Board-portal RFP matrix

Author `board-portal-rfp-matrix.md` — the RFP evaluation matrix the general counsel will run to replace the Dropbox-and-email workflow. The vendors in scope are the named set: Diligent Boards, Nasdaq Boardvantage, OnBoard (Passageways), BoardEffect (a Diligent product), Boardable, and Board Intelligence. Cover:

1. **The eight evaluation dimensions.** The chapter 02 evaluation set — (a) security posture (SOC 2 Type II report on file and reviewable under NDA, ISO/IEC 27001 certification, encryption at rest and in transit, SSO/SAML integration, per-tenant data isolation); (b) granular per-committee access control (committee books restricted to members, scoped read-only guest access for auditors and outside counsel); (c) mobile app quality (native iOS/Android, offline read, annotation sync, page-jump navigation); (d) resolutions and consents workflow with countersignature (DGCL §141(f) UWC circulation inside the portal with timestamped per-director signature and a signed-PDF output for the minute book); (e) minute-drafting workflow with version control (draft, tracked-changes chair review, version history, locked-and-dated approved minutes); (f) permanent vs. ephemeral annotation posture (ephemeral is the safer default — annotations are personal preparation, not corporate records); (g) auditor-view access scoped to the audit-committee book with per-meeting read-only audit trail; (h) archive and records-retention posture (corporation controls retention, periodic export to the corporation's own document-management system, data-return-on-termination terms).
2. **The matrix in operational form.** A table with one row per vendor and one column per evaluation dimension, plus columns for commercial posture (pricing tier, contract term, data-return terms) and implementation posture (onboarding duration, director-training support). Every substantive cell carries a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker on current SOC-2 status, current pricing, current feature parity, and current data-return-on-termination terms, because those move and the RFP author must verify against the vendor's current response under NDA rather than a stale public claim.
3. **The scoring rubric.** A named weighting across the eight dimensions — security posture and resolutions/consents workflow weighted highest (the portal is a records system and a §141(f) circulation surface); mobile and annotation posture weighted mid; archive and records-retention weighted high (the portal termination case is the one that produces a records gap). Name the scoring scale (0–3 per dimension with a defined rubric per level) and the composite-score computation.
4. **The decision framework.** The named decision — a short-list of two vendors advances to director demo; two directors (the independent director plus one other) test the mobile app on their own devices across a full dry-run pre-read cycle; the corporate secretary tests the resolutions and minute-drafting workflows against a dry-run DGCL §141(f) UWC and a dry-run minutes draft derived from the Exercise-03 template; the audit-trail and data-return-on-termination terms go to outside counsel for review. The decision is recommended to the full board by the chair plus the general counsel; the board approves by resolution at the first regular meeting after the demo cycle. Name the fallback if both short-listed vendors fail the dry-run — a second short-list runs on a named timeline rather than a silent regression to Dropbox-and-email.
5. **The implementation-and-cutover plan.** The named cutover from the Dropbox-and-email workflow — the historical-minute-book migration (the records under Exercise 03 are the forcing artefact), the director-training session, the parallel-run window where the first full meeting cycle runs on the portal with Dropbox kept as a read-only archive, and the Dropbox decommissioning milestone. Name the records-retention bridge so no minute, resolution, or consent falls out of the record during cutover.

### Part F — CFO coordination memo

Author `cfo-coordination-memo.md` — the one-page memo the general counsel sends to the CFO on week one to draw the ownership boundary between corporate-secretary machinery and CFO board-pack content. Cover:

1. **The ownership line.** The CFO owns the *content* of the board pack — KPI dashboard, cash and runway, financial model, investor update, department reviews. That authoring, review, and finance-narrative discipline lives in the finance curriculum and is not re-authored here. The corporate secretary (reporting line to the general counsel) owns the *machinery* around the pack — cadence, pre-read delivery, agenda, resolutions, minutes, archive, and the board-portal selection and operation.
2. **The named handoffs.** The pre-read-delivery handoff (CFO draft to secretary 96 hours before the meeting; secretary posts final to the portal 72 hours before); the agenda handoff (secretary drafts from the operating-norms template; CFO confirms Block-2 and Block-4 time budgets against the pack content); the resolutions handoff (CFO flags any action item from the pack that requires a vote; secretary drafts the resolution in advance); the minutes handoff (secretary drafts action-and-decision-style minutes; CFO has read-rights but no edit-rights on the minute draft, per the Exercise-03 minute-review discipline).
3. **The audit-committee preview.** Once the audit committee stands up (Exercise 04), the audit-committee preview of the CFO pack runs ahead of the full-board meeting on a cadence named in the audit-committee charter. Name the forward cross-reference and the implication for the CFO's pack-authoring lead time (the pack cycle gets longer, not shorter, when the audit committee is seated).
4. **The escalation path for a pack-cadence breach.** When the CFO cannot meet the 96-hour handoff to the secretary, the escalation goes general-counsel → chair → consideration of either a late-materials disclosure at call-to-order or a meeting reschedule. The default is not to compress the pre-read window.
5. **The adoption confirmation.** The named confirmation step — the CFO countersigns the memo; the general counsel files it in the board-operating-norms folder of the portal; the memo is reviewed annually at the first meeting of the fiscal year alongside the operating-norms document from Part A.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 02 is the primary reference. The cadence framework, the 72-hour rule, the five-block agenda, the executive-session protocol, and the eight portal-evaluation dimensions are all drawn from it. Do not re-author its substance; adopt it to Maridian's facts.
- Do not duck the stage-to-cadence pick in Part A. Maridian is post-Series-B with a declared 24-month IPO horizon and inherited dysfunction; the cadence picks itself. Name it and defend it against the chapter 02 framework rather than hedging with a range.
- Do not fabricate current SOC-2 posture, current pricing, or current feature parity for any of the named portal vendors in Part E. Every substantive cell in the RFP matrix carries a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker; the general counsel will verify against the vendor's current response under NDA. The same discipline applies to any dollar number, vendor fee, security-posture claim, or market statistic anywhere else in the package.
- The minutes template, the §141(f) UWC form, the §228 stockholder-consent mechanic, and the NVCA-form indemnification agreement are the subject of Exercise 03 and are referenced here (particularly in Part D and the Part E resolutions workflow) rather than re-authored.
- The standing-committee charters (audit, comp, nominating and governance, transaction, AI-risk) are the subject of Exercise 04 and are referenced in Parts A, D, and F rather than re-authored. Where the package must be compatible with a committee that is not yet seated — the audit-committee pre-view of the CFO pack is the clearest example — write forward against the committee that will be seated, not against the current informal cadence.
- The D&O renewal substance referenced in the worked Block-4 action list in Part C is the subject of Exercise 06; name it as an action item and do not re-author the tower, the exclusions, or the Side-A DIC layer.
- The listing-standard independent-session requirement is deferred to Exercise 09 and the chapter-09 IPO-readiness track; name the forward trigger in Part D without citing specific NYSE §303A or Nasdaq 5605 provisions here.
- No real peer-company names. "Maridian Compute, Inc." is the fictional corporation; the Series-A lead, the Series-B lead, the independent director, the operating-bank counterparty, and every customer are referenced by role only. The board portal vendors are referenced only as the chapter 02 named list — Diligent Boards, Nasdaq Boardvantage, OnBoard, BoardEffect, Boardable, Board Intelligence — and no vendor's current commercial or security posture is quoted without a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker.
- Nothing in this exercise is a legal determination. The cadence resolution, the §141(f) UWC-circulation posture, the special-meeting notice interpretation, the DGCL §141(i) remote-participation posture, the DGCL §229 waiver discipline, and the portal selection are all drafts the general counsel and outside counsel finalise before adoption.

The package is acceptable if:

1. Part A names the cadence pick explicitly for Maridian's stage (monthly or every-six-weeks for the full board, with a defended reason), publishes a dated twelve-month calendar with a named strategic-deep-dive slot per meeting, addresses special-meeting notice under the bylaws with the DGCL §229 waiver mechanic flagged, names the between-meeting CEO update discipline with the DGCL §141(b) / §141(f) action-vs.-communication distinction, and closes with an adoption resolution.
2. Part B names the 72-hour pre-read rule with the time-zone convention and the weekend cut-off, enumerates the required pre-read contents including resolutions and any §141(f) consents circulating, authors a CEO cover-memo template, names the late-add rule's three dispositions (table, walk-in with unanimous consent, post-meeting §141(f) UWC), and names a pre-read-on-time metric with target and escalation.
3. Part C renders the five-block skeleton with time budgets, names the one-strategic-topic-per-meeting discipline with the written-decision-memo forcing function, authors a fully-worked quarterly agenda in timed-table form with a strategic deep-dive and a Block-4 action list of at least five items, and names the resolutions-numbering, read-before-vote, and vote-by-director conventions including the DGCL §144 interested-director handling.
