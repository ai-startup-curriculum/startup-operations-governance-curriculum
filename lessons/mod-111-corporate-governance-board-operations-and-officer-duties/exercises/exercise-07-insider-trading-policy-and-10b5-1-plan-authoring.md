# Exercise 07 — Insider-trading policy and 10b5-1 plan authoring

> Estimated time: **~8 hours** · Related chapter: [07 — Insider-trading policy and 10b5-1 programme](../07-insider-trading-policy-and-10b5-1-programme.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp operating an AI-infrastructure / GPU-fabric platform that sells reserved-capacity contracts, on-demand compute, and a managed inference tier to AI-native enterprise customers. The corporation listed on a US national exchange eight weeks ago through a traditional IPO, sold 11% of post-offering outstanding shares in the primary, and priced after a one-week roadshow. The pre-IPO insider-trading policy was adopted by board resolution five months before pricing — a short-form policy approved on counsel's template — but has never been operated inside the Section 16 / Rule 144 / Rule 10b5-1 regime that attaches at a public company. The corporate secretary is twelve weeks into the role; the general counsel is six months into the role; the prior general counsel exited at Series-C close.

The forcing-function surface is already live. Two of the three founder-executives — the CEO and the CTO, both Section 16 officers — want to adopt Rule 10b5-1 trading plans to begin diversifying after the 180-day post-IPO lockup expires; the CFO has indicated she will adopt a sell-to-cover-only plan on her RSU vesting and no open-market-sale plan. Two of the eight directors are investor-fund-affiliated partners at pre-IPO growth funds whose aggregate fund-family positions passed the Schedule 13G 5% threshold at the moment the IPO closed; neither fund has filed its initial Schedule 13G and both filing clocks are running under the October 10, 2023 SEC amendments that accelerated beneficial-ownership deadlines. The next quarterly earnings release — the first as a public company — is nine weeks away, which anchors the entire blackout calendar and gates the first Form 10-Q disclosure under Item 408(a) of Reg S-K of any Rule 10b5-1 arrangement adopted during Q1-as-a-public-company. The audit-committee chair asked at the last meeting whether Maridian has a defensible blackout-and-pre-clearance SOP and a 10b5-1 template compliant with SEC Final Rule Release No. 33-11138; the honest answer today is "the policy exists, the operating stack does not."

The general counsel has nine weeks to put the full insider-trading stack into operation before the first public-company earnings release. She owns the end-to-end package; the corporate secretary owns the Section 16 roster, the EDGAR filing-codes inventory, and the powers-of-attorney file; the CFO owns the earnings calendar that anchors the blackout schedule; investor relations owns the window-opening / window-closing communications surface; outside securities counsel finalises the 10b5-1 template and signs off on the Section 13 analysis for the two investor-fund directors. Author the full package.

The tail-period rule for former employees and directors (through the end of the first open window after departure, or until any MNPI known at departure becomes public).
2. **MNPI definition.** The materiality standard a reasonable investor would consider important, or that would significantly alter the total mix of information available; the non-public standard that holds until broad dissemination plus one full trading day; and the illustrative categories (unannounced earnings, M&A activity, major customer wins / losses, financing activity, senior-personnel transitions, material litigation, material cybersecurity incidents under Item 1.05 of Form 8-K, product-launch and regulatory events). Name the "when in doubt, do not trade and consult the general counsel" rule and name the illustrative list as non-exhaustive.
3. **The Rule 10b-5 liability theories the policy is designed against.** The classical theory (fiduciary-duty breach by a corporate insider trading on MNPI against the issuer's stockholders), the misappropriation theory under *United States v. Reference Rule 10b5-2 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10b5-2) for the duties of trust or confidence that extend the policy's reach to spouses, parents, children, siblings, and parties in a documented confidentiality relationship. Reference the Rule 10b5-1(b) "aware of" standard as the trigger the affirmative defense in Part C is designed against.
4. **Prohibited transactions.** Short sales (prohibited by Exchange Act § 16(c) for Section 16 insiders and extended to all covered persons by policy); puts, calls, and other derivative securities on Maridian stock outside employer-granted equity awards; hedging transactions (collars, prepaid variable forwards, equity swaps, zero-cost collars) with the Item 407(i) of Reg S-K proxy-disclosure consequence named; pledging and margin-account holding of Maridian shares with the proxy-advisor (ISS, Glass Lewis) scrutiny named; standing and limit orders other than inside a validly adopted Rule 10b5-1 plan; front-running family and friends; and the Sarbanes-Oxley § 306(a) pension-blackout prohibition during 401(k) plan blackouts.

6. **Whistleblower and reporting channel.** The named reporting path (direct line to the general counsel; alternative anonymous channel through the audit-committee chair under the Rule 10A-3 whistleblower procedure named in [chapter 04](../04-standing-committees.md)), the no-retaliation commitment, and the interaction with the Dodd-Frank § 922 SEC whistleblower-award regime. Name the duty to report suspected violations by other covered persons and the policy-side disciplinary consequence for a knowing violation (termination, clawback of equity under the compensation-committee clawback policy, referral to law enforcement where criminal exposure under 18 U.S.C. § 1348 or Exchange Act § 10(b) is present).
7. **Policy administration.** The named administrator (the general counsel), the named compliance officer (the corporate secretary or a designated deputy), the policy-amendment procedure (board resolution on recommendation of the audit committee), and the annual policy-refresh cadence tied to the one-page annual governance calendar in [chapter 08](../08-annual-governance-operating-rhythm.md).
8. **Public-company disclosure obligations.** Name the Item 408(b) of Reg S-K annual 10-K disclosure that the issuer has adopted insider-trading policies and procedures reasonably designed to promote compliance; the Exhibit 19 filing of the policy itself under Item 601(b)(19) of Reg S-K; the Item 408(a) quarterly 10-Q / 10-K disclosure of adoption, modification, or termination during the quarter of a Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangement or non-Rule 10b5-1 trading arrangement by a director or Section 16 officer.

### Part C — Rule 10b5-1 plan template

### Part F — Rule 144 affiliate-sale protocol

Author `rule-144-sale-protocol.md` — the Rule 144 safe-harbor sale protocol for affiliates under 17 C.F.R. § 230.144. Cover:

1. **Affiliate determination.** The Rule 144 affiliate definition — directors, Section 16 officers, and 10%-plus holders in substantially all cases — and the named Maridian population subject to Rule 144 at this stage.
2. **Holding-period requirement.** The Rule 144(d) holding-period requirement for restricted securities (not generally implicated for affiliates selling previously-registered shares on the open market, but named for completeness in case of secondary-placement or restricted-stock-award sale scenarios).
3. **Current-information requirement.** The Rule 144(c) requirement that adequate current information about Maridian be available — satisfied for a reporting issuer by compliance with the Exchange Act § 13 reporting obligations (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K filings current). The pre-clearance reviewer confirms current-information status at approval.
4. **Volume limitation.** The Rule 144(e) volume cap for any three-month period — the greater of (i) 1% of the outstanding shares of the class or (ii) the average weekly trading volume reported on the exchange during the four calendar weeks preceding the Form 144 filing. The pre-clearance reviewer calculates the cap at approval and the broker-side execution instruction enforces it.
5. **Manner of sale.** The Rule 144(f) manner-of-sale requirements for equity securities — brokers' transactions, transactions directly with a market maker, or riskless principal transactions — and the broker-instruction discipline that routes affiliate sales to a single designated broker (named as the executive-services broker, flagged `<!-- needs-research -->` on vendor).
6. **Form 144 filing.** The Rule 144(h) Form 144 filing requirement for affiliates whose sales exceed the de minimis thresholds; the three-month-coverage window of a Form 144; the electronic-filing requirement on EDGAR <!-- needs-research: confirm current Form 144 electronic-filing effective date and de minimis threshold figures -->; the signature-and-filing responsibility (the affiliate signs, the filing agent files).
7. **Interaction with Section 16, Rule 10b5-1, and the pre-clearance workflow.** The coordination of the Form 144 filing with the Form 4 filing (Form 144 is filed concurrently with or immediately before the sale; Form 4 is filed within two business days after); the Rule 10b5-1 plan as the affirmative-defense backing for the sale; the pre-clearance approval that gates the entire sequence.
8. **Non-affiliate sale path.** The Rule 144 sale protocol for a former Section 16 officer or director whose Rule 144 affiliate status has terminated — the three-month lookback discipline (a person who has not been an affiliate in the three months preceding the sale is no longer bound by Rule 144(c), (e), (f), or (h) for sales of previously-registered or holding-period-satisfied shares); the audit trail the corporate secretary maintains to evidence affiliate-status change on director resignation or officer separation.
9. **Worked sale sequence.** A worked illustration for a Section 16 officer selling 25,000 shares under an adopted Rule 10b5-1 plan during an open window — the pre-clearance request confirmation (even inside a plan, the broker-side execution instruction reads to the plan, not a fresh pre-clearance), the Rule 144(e) volume cap calculation against the trailing four-week average weekly trading volume, the Form 144 filing concurrent with the sale, the Form 4 filed by 10:00 p.m. Eastern on the second business day after execution with the Rule 10b5-1(c) checkbox selected and the plan adoption date disclosed in the footnote.
10. **Aggregation rules.** Named aggregation across a three-month window of sales by the affiliate and by any person or entity whose sales must be aggregated with the affiliate's under Rule 144(a)(2) and 144(e)(3); the pre-clearance reviewer's spreadsheet that tracks the trailing three-month aggregate per affiliate and refuses pre-clearance where the cap would be breached; the operational implication for spouses, household family members, and controlled entities whose sales aggregate with the affiliate's for Rule 144 purposes.
11. **Broker-side execution controls.** Named broker-side controls that enforce the Rule 144 safe harbor at execution — the affiliate-flag on the brokerage account, the Rule 144-compliant order type (brokers' transaction), the trailing-four-week average-weekly-trading-volume calculation the broker runs at order entry, and the Form 144 filing routine triggered by any sale above the de minimis threshold; the pre-clearance-to-execution linkage that ensures the designated executive-services broker is the only route for affiliate sales.
12. **Worked volume-cap calculation.** A worked illustration against Maridian's post-IPO float — assume 115 million shares outstanding and a trailing-four-week average weekly trading volume of 3.2 million shares; compute the Rule 144(e) volume cap as the greater of 1% of outstanding (1.15 million shares) and the four-week average (3.2 million shares), yielding a 3.2-million-share cap per three-month window; name the pre-clearance implications when two affiliates request sales in the same window.
13. **Record-retention discipline.** Named record-retention cadence for Form 144 filings, broker execution confirmations, pre-clearance approvals, and the Rule 10b5-1 plan documents — retained for at least seven years in the corporate-secretary file; indexed to the Section 16 roster; produced on request to the SEC enforcement staff, the audit-committee chair, and outside securities counsel.
14. **Secondary-sale and tender-offer coordination.** Named coordination with the IR and finance teams if a company-led secondary offering, issuer tender offer, or accelerated-share-repurchase programme is contemplated within the pre-clearance window — the Rule 10b-18 safe-harbor interaction for issuer repurchases, the Regulation M trading restrictions during a distribution, and the pre-clearance implications for affiliate participation.
