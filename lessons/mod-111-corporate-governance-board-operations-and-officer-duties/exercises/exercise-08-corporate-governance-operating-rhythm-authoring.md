# Exercise 08 — Corporate-governance operating-rhythm authoring

> Estimated time: **~7 hours** · Related chapter: [08 — Annual governance operating rhythm](../08-annual-governance-operating-rhythm.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a newly public Delaware C-corp operating an AI-infrastructure / GPU-fabric platform that sells reserved-capacity GPU clusters, inference-serving infrastructure, and a model-training orchestrator into AI-native and AI-adjacent enterprise customers. The corporation listed on Nasdaq Global Select Market three months ago at a $2.4B offering-day market cap, closed the fiscal year just before the IPO on a short-period basis, and is now in Q1 of its first full fiscal year post-IPO on a December 31 calendar fiscal year. Common-stock float, insider lock-up, and the underwriters' 180-day lock-up all sit on the corporate-secretary desk alongside the first post-IPO annual-meeting cycle. The corporation is 410 FTE across a Delaware-of-record / California-headquartered / Washington-and-Texas-co-located footprint, carries foreign qualifications in nine states, and runs on a June-selected large-accelerated-filer trajectory (currently an accelerated filer, with the large-accelerated test running against the June 30 measurement date later this fiscal year).

The governance machinery is already the heavy load on the corporate-secretary chair. The incoming corporate secretary onboarded two weeks ago from a mid-cap public issuer and inherited a governance calendar that was assembled piecemeal during the S-1 drive-to-effective sprint: the audit-committee charter was ratified but the audit-committee annual cycle was never written against the 10-Q / 10-K cadence; the compensation-committee charter was adopted on a NYSE template and then the corporation listed on Nasdaq, so the committee's annual cycle is anchored to the wrong listing-standard reference; the nominating-&-governance-committee Q1 slate-building window is already compressed because the first annual meeting is 18 weeks out; the AI-risk sub-committee has met once and has no written annual cycle at all. The CEO has asked for a one-page annual governance operating rhythm on the wall of the corporate-secretary office by the end of Q1, and the audit-committee chair has asked for the filing-and-disclosure calendar on the same deadline so the audit committee can mesh its earnings-cycle cadence against it.

Three operating realities force the operating-rhythm-authoring question. **(i)** The first post-IPO 10-K is due 75 days after FYE under the accelerated-filer deadline in Exchange Act Rule 13a-1 (General Instruction A.(2) of Form 10-K), the proxy statement (DEF 14A) must mail no later than 120 days after FYE to qualify for the Item 10(a)(3) incorporation shortcut, and the annual meeting is being targeted for mid-May — the three deadlines interlock in a way the inherited calendar does not model. **(ii)** The Delaware franchise-tax and annual-report deadline under 8 Del. C. §§ 501-503 falls on March 1, inside the 10-K drafting window, and the nine foreign-qualification states each have their own anniversary-based annual-report cycles that have never been consolidated against a single master calendar. **(iii)** The Rule 10D-1 clawback policy, the Reg FD policy, the insider-trading policy, and the 10b5-1 compliance-refresh cycle all need an annual-review owner and an approval path that reads to the right committee, and the inherited policy library has none of them scheduled against the committee cadence.

The corporate secretary has six weeks to walk into the first Q1 full-board meeting with a complete operating-rhythm package. The corporate secretary owns the end-to-end package; the general counsel owns policy substance and the SEC filing-agent interface; the CFO owns the 10-Q / 10-K / earnings-release cadence; the audit-committee chair owns the audit-committee calendar sign-off; the compensation-committee chair owns the comp-cycle sign-off; the nominating-&-governance-committee chair owns the slate-building and board-evaluation sign-off. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Annual governance calendar

Author `annual-governance-calendar.md` — the one-page year-at-a-glance the corporate secretary pins to the office wall and circulates to every director, officer, and committee chair. Cover:

1. **The twelve-month table.** One row per month (January through December), with columns for:
   - Full-board meeting.
   - Audit-committee meeting.
   - Compensation-committee meeting.
   - Nominating-&-governance-committee meeting.
   - AI-risk sub-committee meeting.
   - Major filing or disclosure event.
   - Policy refresh or attestation.
   - Every cell is populated; no cell is `—` unless the month genuinely has no committee or filing activity in that column.
2. **The anchor meetings.** The four quarterly full-board meetings anchored to the 10-Q / 10-K-approval windows; the annual stockholder meeting (DGCL § 211) in May; the Q1 board meeting positioned to approve the 10-K and the DEF 14A; the Q4 board meeting positioned to approve next-year budget, comp grants, and the policy-refresh cycle. Name the DGCL § 211(d) special-meeting standby pattern (the mechanic the corporate secretary keeps in the drawer for a mid-year governance event that cannot wait for the next quarterly).
3. **The stockholder-meeting anchor.** The annual-meeting date targeted for mid-May, the record-date determination under DGCL § 213, the stockholder-list availability under DGCL § 219 and DGCL § 220, the proxy-solicitation window driven by the DEF 14A mailing, and the Rule 14a-8 stockholder-proposal deadline (120 days before the anniversary of the prior-year proxy, which for a first-year-post-IPO issuer the corporate secretary must set and disclose). <!-- needs-research: confirm the SEC's current no-action-letter guidance on the Rule 14a-8 deadline for a first-year-post-IPO issuer with no prior-year proxy anniversary before publishing the deadline in the DEF 14A. -->
4. **The policy-refresh anchors on the one-page view.** The bylaws review (Q1), the committee-charter reviews (Q4), the code-of-ethics attestation (Q4), the insider-trading-policy re-attestation (Q4), the clawback-policy review under Rule 10D-1 (Q4), the Reg FD policy refresh (Q3), the 10b5-1 compliance refresh (Q3) — each named on the calendar in the month the review lands, with the owning committee in the cell.
5. **The listing-maintenance anchors.** The Nasdaq 5600-series annual certifications (Rule 5625 / 5610 series), specifically the Nasdaq Rule 5605 independent-director majority annual affirmation, the Rule 5605(c) audit-committee composition affirmation, and the Rule 5615 controlled-company / foreign-private-issuer exemption posture review (Maridian is neither controlled nor an FPI, but the annual review records the posture). Note the NYSE § 303A.12(a) annual written affirmation as the equivalent that would apply if the corporation were NYSE-listed and flag the Nasdaq-specific posture for Maridian.

### Part B — Filing-and-disclosure calendar

Author `filing-and-disclosure-calendar.md`. The filing calendar is the SEC-and-state-filing operating-rhythm artefact the general counsel and the CFO work from in parallel. Cover:

1. **The Form 10-Q filing calendar.** The three quarterly 10-Q filings (Q1, Q2, Q3), the filing deadline under Exchange Act Rule 13a-13 and General Instruction A.(1) of Form 10-Q (40 days after quarter-end for accelerated filers, 45 days for non-accelerated filers), the quiet-period mechanics preceding each filing, the audit-committee pre-filing review, and the sub-certifications under Exchange Act Rule 13a-14 / Rule 15d-14 (SOX § 302 certifications) that feed the CEO and CFO signatures.
2. **The Form 10-K filing calendar.** The annual filing deadline under Exchange Act Rule 13a-1 and General Instruction A.(2) of Form 10-K (60 days after FYE for large accelerated filers; 75 days for accelerated filers; 90 days for non-accelerated filers), the accelerated-filer-category test under Rule 12b-2 and the dollar-threshold public-float measurements that drive the category, the ICFR management-assessment attestation under SOX § 404(a) and Exchange Act Rule 13a-15, the auditor ICFR attestation under SOX § 404(b) (applicable to accelerated and large-accelerated filers), and the Part III incorporation-by-reference shortcut that ties the 10-K to the DEF 14A 120-day deadline.
3. **The Form 8-K triggering-event calendar.** The four-business-day disclosure clock under Exchange Act Rule 13a-11 and General Instruction B.1 of Form 8-K, and the Item inventory the corporate secretary pre-maps:
   - Item 1.01 / 1.02 — material definitive agreements (entry into / termination).
   - Item 1.05 — cybersecurity incidents (four-business-day clock added by the July 2023 SEC cybersecurity rule).
   - Item 2.02 — earnings releases.
   - Item 2.03 — creation of a direct financial obligation / off-balance-sheet arrangement.
   - Item 2.04 — triggering events that accelerate or increase a financial obligation.
   - Item 3.02 / 3.03 — unregistered sales / material modifications of security-holder rights.
   - Item 4.01 / 4.02 — auditor changes / non-reliance on prior financials.
   - Item 5.02 — departure or appointment of directors / principal officers.
   - Item 5.07 — submission of matters to a vote of security holders.
   - Item 8.01 / 9.01 — other events / financial statements and exhibits.
4. **The DEF 14A proxy-statement calendar.** The Schedule 14A proxy-rule content under Exchange Act Rules 14a-1 through 14a-21, the Item 10(a)(3) incorporation shortcut requiring the DEF 14A to be filed within 120 days after FYE, the Reg S-K Item 402 executive-compensation disclosure, the Item 404 related-party-transaction disclosure, the Item 406 code-of-ethics disclosure, the Item 407 corporate-governance disclosure (including Item 407(h) board leadership and risk oversight), and the Item 408 insider-trading-policy and 10b5-1-plan disclosure added by the December 2022 SEC amendments.
5. **The Section 16 Form 3 / 4 / 5 filing calendar.** The initial Form 3 ten-day clock on becoming a Section 16 insider under Exchange Act Rule 16a-3, the Form 4 two-business-day clock for reportable transactions under Rule 16a-3(g), the Form 5 annual report (within 45 days of FYE) for any transactions exempt from Form 4 reporting, the Form 144 filing for affiliate Rule 144 sales (concurrent with the Rule 144 sale), and the Schedule 13D / 13G filing calendar for stockholders crossing 5% beneficial ownership under Exchange Act Rules 13d-1 and 13d-2 (and the February 2024 SEC amendments compressing certain 13G timelines). Name the brokers-and-insiders filing-coordination mechanic the corporate secretary runs.
6. **The Reg FD touchpoint calendar.** The Reg FD disclosure obligation under Rule 100 of Reg FD (17 C.F.R. § 243.100), the standard disclosure vehicles (press release, Form 8-K Item 7.01, properly-noticed webcast), the quiet-period posture relative to each quarterly earnings release, and the Reg FD-policy refresh tied to the policy-refresh cycle in Part C.

### Part C — Policy-refresh calendar

Author `policy-refresh-calendar.md`. The policy library is the compliance surface the Caremark plaintiff reads first; stale policies are a compliance-system gap on their face. Cover:

1. **The policy inventory.** Name every governance-adjacent policy with an annual-refresh obligation at Maridian's stage:
   - Bylaws.
   - Audit-committee charter.
   - Compensation-committee charter.
   - Nominating-&-governance-committee charter.
   - AI-risk sub-committee charter.
   - Code of business conduct and ethics (required by Item 406 of Reg S-K and Nasdaq Rule 5610).
   - Related-party-transactions policy (Item 404 of Reg S-K and Nasdaq Rule 5630).
   - Insider-trading policy (Item 408 of Reg S-K).
   - 10b5-1 trading-plan policy under the December 2022 SEC amendments.
   - Clawback policy under Rule 10D-1.
   - Whistleblower policy (SOX § 301 and Exchange Act Rule 10A-3(b)(3)).
   - Reg FD policy.
   - Corporate communications and social-media policy.
   - Corporate-opportunity and conflicts-of-interest policy.
2. **The owner column.** Name the policy owner for each policy — typically the general counsel for the bylaws and committee-independent-of-management policies, the corporate secretary for the committee charters, the CFO for the clawback and the insider-trading policy on the finance side, the chief compliance officer (if named; otherwise the general counsel) for the whistleblower policy, the head of IR for the Reg FD policy, the head of communications for the social-media policy.
3. **The approval-path column.** The named approval route for each policy — audit-committee review then full-board ratification for the audit-committee charter, the whistleblower policy, the Rule 10A-3(b)(3) procedures, and the auditor-independence framework; comp-committee review then full-board ratification for the comp-committee charter and the clawback policy; nom-gov review then full-board ratification for the nom-gov charter, the director-independence standards, and the code of ethics; full-board review and ratification for the bylaws and the insider-trading policy; AI-risk sub-committee review then full-board ratification for the AI-risk sub-committee charter.
4. **The refresh-month column.** The month the refresh lands each year — bylaws in Q1 (February) after the Delaware-amendment review window closes; charters and the AI-risk charter in Q4 (November) before the Q1 attestation cycle; code of ethics in Q4 (November / December) with employee attestation in December; insider-trading policy in Q3 (September) before the Q3 trading-window planning cycle; 10b5-1 compliance refresh in Q3 (September) tied to the insider-trading-policy cycle; clawback-policy review in Q4 (November) as the comp-committee annual review item; whistleblower policy in Q1 (February) as the audit-committee annual review item; Reg FD in Q3 (September) before the Q3 earnings quiet period; related-party policy in Q1 (February) tied to the D&O-questionnaire cycle.
5. **The attestation-and-training mechanic.** The annual code-of-ethics attestation cycle (Q4, every director / officer / employee), the annual insider-trading-policy attestation cycle (Q3 for the covered population, Q4 for the extended population), the annual D&O questionnaire (Q4 for calendar-year filers), and the training cadence for insider-trading-policy refresh (annual, with onboarding for new insiders). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current Nasdaq Rule 5605 / 5610 training expectations and whether any attestation-tooling vendor (Diligent Entities, Governance Solutions Group, OnBoard, Nasdaq Boardvantage) offers an annual-attestation module that aligns to Item 408 Reg S-K before selecting. -->

### Part D — Standing-committee interlock calendar

Author `standing-committee-interlock-calendar.md`. The four standing committees and the AI-risk sub-committee feed the full board; the full board feeds the stockholders through the proxy. The interlock is the artefact that makes the handoffs legible. Cover:

1. **The quarterly interlock map.** For each quarter, name the sequence: audit-committee meets first (two weeks before the full-board meeting) to clear the 10-Q or 10-K and the ICFR posture; comp-committee meets second (one week before the full-board meeting) on the quarter's comp agenda; nom-gov meets third (one week before) on composition, independence, and policy-refresh; AI-risk sub-committee meets fourth (one week before) on the quarter's AI-risk agenda; the full board meets with each committee chair delivering a written report and the corporate secretary capturing the full-board pack.
2. **The pre-read window.** The 72-hour pre-read norm for the full board (from [chapter 02](../02-board-cadence-and-materials.md)), the 5-business-day pre-read for the audit committee driven by the 10-Q / 10-K materials, the 5-business-day pre-read for the comp committee driven by the comp-consultant materials, the 3-business-day pre-read for nom-gov, the 3-business-day pre-read for the AI-risk sub-committee. Every committee chair's report lands in the full-board pack 72 hours before the full-board meeting.
3. **The full-board-pack contents.** Name the standing sections of every full-board pack:
   - CEO check-in.
   - CFO financial pack.
   - Audit-committee chair's written report.
   - Comp-committee chair's written report.
   - Nom-gov chair's written report.
   - AI-risk sub-committee chair's written report.
   - GC / corporate-secretary report (open UWCs, filings pending, open regulator inquiries, policy-refresh status).
   - Executive-session agenda.
   - Decisions / consents / approvals block.
   - Every committee report follows a standard template so the directors can read across quarters.
4. **The committee-to-committee handoffs.** The audit-committee-to-comp-committee handoff on clawback-triggering accounting restatement determinations; the comp-committee-to-nom-gov handoff on CEO-evaluation inputs (comp committee runs the metrics, nom-gov runs the governance review); the nom-gov-to-AI-risk handoff on AI-risk sub-committee composition and independence; the audit-committee-to-AI-risk handoff on the AI-risk sub-committee's quarterly cyber / AI-incident log review (incidents that trigger Form 8-K Item 1.05 are audited by the audit committee for disclosure and by the AI-risk sub-committee for substantive AI-risk posture).
5. **The special-committee standby pattern.** The DGCL § 141(c) standing-committee delegation posture, the DGCL § 141(f) unanimous-written-consent mechanic for between-meeting action, and the transaction-or-special-committee standby pattern (independence under *MFW* and *Trulia*, charter template on-the-shelf) the corporate secretary keeps ready for a mid-year related-party transaction, a M&A approach, or an SEC-inquiry governance response.

### Part E — State-maintenance and foreign-qualification calendar

Author `state-maintenance-and-foreign-qualification-calendar.md`. The state-maintenance calendar is the cross-reference the corporate secretary runs against [mod-101](../../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) and against the SEC filing calendar in Part B. Cover:

1. **The Delaware annual-report and franchise-tax obligation.** The March 1 annual-report filing deadline under 8 Del. C. § 502(a), the franchise-tax deadline the same day under 8 Del. C. §§ 501-503 (and §§ 601-607 for the related large-corporate-filer provisions), the authorized-shares vs. assumed-par-value computation decision under § 503, the Secretary-of-State electronic-filing mechanic, the registered-agent certification refresh under DGCL § 132, and the fiscal-year-end data the report carries (directors of record as of the filing date).
2. **The foreign-qualification state calendar.** For each of the nine foreign-qualification states, the annual-report filing cycle (anniversary vs. calendar-year), the franchise-tax or filing-fee amount if applicable, and the Secretary-of-State-of-record contact. Name the practitioner default that the calendar lives in a single master spreadsheet the corporate secretary and the registered-agent vendor both reference, with a 30-day lead-time trigger before each state's deadline.
3. **The registered-agent contract and refresh cadence.** The annual registered-agent-service contract renewal (CSC, CT Corporation, Cogency Global, Harbor Compliance, Northwest Registered Agent), the registered-agent-service-of-process intake protocol, the registered-agent address-update mechanic when the corporation changes headquarters, and the annual confirmation that the registered-agent on file with Delaware matches the registered-agent on file with each foreign-qualification state. <!-- needs-research: confirm current registered-agent pricing (per-state annual fee and master-agreement discount structure) across CSC, CT Corporation, Cogency Global, and Harbor Compliance before scoring in the state-maintenance calendar; vendor pricing moves annually. -->
4. **The required-business-license refresh cadence.** The city / county business-license refresh for each operating location (California head-office, Washington co-location facility, Texas co-location facility), the state-level sales-and-use-tax registration refresh where applicable, and the specialty-licensing posture (export-control registration under the EAR for the GPU-fabric export posture — flagged for deferral to the commercial-contracts / export-controls workstream rather than the corporate-secretary calendar).
5. **The cross-reference to mod-101.** The entity-maintenance pillar lives in [mod-101](../../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/); this calendar is the operating-rhythm projection of that pillar onto the corporate-secretary chair. Reference the mod-101 artefact (the state-of-good-standing certificate inventory, the registered-agent master file, the Secretary-of-State annual-report filing pack) and do not re-author the formation-side mechanics here. Name the quarterly state-good-standing-certificate refresh the corporate secretary runs for the financing / M&A diligence room.
