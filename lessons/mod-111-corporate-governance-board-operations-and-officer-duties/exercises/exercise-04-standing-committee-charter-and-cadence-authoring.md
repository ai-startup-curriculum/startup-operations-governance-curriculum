# Exercise 04 — Standing committee charter and cadence authoring

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — Standing committees](../04-standing-committees.md)

## Problem statement

Maridian Compute, Inc. is a Delaware C-corp operating a GPU-fabric platform for frontier-model training and inference. The corporation is approximately 18 months from its target S-1 filing, carries a seven-director board, and has just agreed at its last full-board meeting to complete the standing-committee architecture that a listed company will have to point to on day one. The audit committee was stood up at Series B with the departing investor-director as chair and two outside directors as members; the new independent audit chair — an ex-Big-Four audit partner recruited specifically for the Reg S-K Item 407(d)(5) financial-expert seat — joined the board last quarter and has taken the chair, but one investor-director still sits on the committee and will have to rotate off before the SEC Rule 10A-3 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10A-3) one-year post-listing cure period lapses. The compensation committee exists on paper from Series C but has never been finalised to the Rule 10C-1 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10C-1) posture; the nominating & governance committee has never been formally stood up at all; and the board has agreed — in the same meeting — to stand up an AI-risk sub-committee of the audit committee given that production ML models now drive a material share of revenue and a material share of enterprise risk.

The forcing functions are three. **(i)** The pre-IPO organisational-meeting checklist underwriters will walk through at T-6 requires live, signed charters for audit, compensation, and nominating & governance; a committee with a draft charter and no approved charter will not clear the SEC-staff review of the S-1. **(ii)** The current audit-committee composition — one investor-director — cannot be the committee's steady state; it is only tolerable under the Rule 10A-3 cure-period transition the company must plan now rather than discover at T-0. **(iii)** The AI-risk sub-committee is the board's answer to a *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019) *Caremark* claim at a company where ML models are a mission-critical risk; a sub-committee that exists only after the first production incident will not carry the "good-faith effort to implement an oversight system" the Delaware Supreme Court is now looking for.

The general counsel has agreed with the board chair to walk into the next regular board meeting with a complete standing-committee package: five charters, a special-committee board-resolution template for the next controlling-stockholder transaction, and a one-page sequencing memo that walks the full board through the stand-up order, the 10A-3 cure-period mechanics, and the second-independent-director search the Rule 10C-1 posture still depends on. The GC owns the end-to-end package; the audit-committee chair owns the audit-committee charter sign-off; the compensation-committee chair owns the compensation-committee charter sign-off and the Rule 10C-1(b)(4) advisor-independence framework (defer substance to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)); the nom-&-gov chair will be named at the same meeting the charter is approved; the head-of-ai-governance-learning programme owns the technical AI-safety substance the AI-risk sub-committee reports against. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Audit committee charter

Author `audit-committee-charter.md` — the signed charter the audit committee will adopt by resolution at its next meeting. Cover:

1. **Composition and independence.** Three members, each independent under SEC Rule 10A-3 (17 C.F.R. § 240.10A-3(b)(1)) — no compensatory fees from the issuer other than board fees and no affiliation with the issuer or any subsidiary — and under NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.06-.07 or Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)(2). Name the Reg S-K Item 407(d)(5) financial-expert designee; the chair is the ex-audit-partner recruited for that seat. Name the Exchange Act § 10A(m)(3) and Rule 10A-3(b)(1)(iv) exemptions the committee will not rely on.
2. **The Rule 10A-3 cure-period transition plan.** The investor-director member who sits on the committee today cannot sit on it at listing. Run the Rule 10A-3(b)(1)(iv)(A) new-listing transition cure period — up to one year after the effective date of the Form S-1 — and name (a) the date by which the investor-director must rotate off, (b) the second independent director the nom-&-gov committee must recruit to backfill, (c) the Item 407(a) disclosure the proxy will carry if the cure period is being relied on, and (d) the audit-committee composition at every intermediate milestone. Do not depend on the cure period as a planning tool; use it as a backstop against a slipped recruitment timeline.
3. **Auditor-relationship authority.** Sole authority to appoint, compensate, retain, and oversee the independent auditor under Sarbanes-Oxley § 301 (codified at Exchange Act § 10A(m)); the direct reporting line from the engagement partner to the committee chair; the annual auditor-independence confirmation under PCAOB Rule 3526; the annual recommendation to ratify the auditor for the proxy.
4. **Non-audit-service pre-approval regime.** SOX § 202 (Exchange Act § 10A(i)) pre-approval policy for all permitted non-audit services; the de minimis exception's numeric gate (flag with `<!-- needs-research: current 17 C.F.R. § 210.2-01(c)(7)(i)(C) de minimis threshold and the auditor's internal cap -->`); the list of § 201 prohibited non-audit services referenced by citation, not re-enumerated.
5. **ICFR oversight (SOX § 404).** Management's quarterly certification cycle, the annual management assessment under SOX § 404(a), and the auditor's attestation under SOX § 404(b) at the moment the corporation ceases to be an EGC or large-accelerated-filer status attaches — defer the EGC-election analysis to [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md).
6. **SOX § 301 whistleblower channel.** The confidential, anonymous intake channel for accounting or auditing concerns; the committee's direct line into the intake log; the quarterly disposition report from the general counsel; the carve-out for matters implicating the general counsel (routed to the audit chair direct).
7. **Related-party transactions.** The Reg S-K Item 404 disclosure-screen the audit committee runs in parallel with the nom-&-gov committee's DGCL § 144 approval process; the dollar-threshold for committee pre-approval flagged with `<!-- needs-research: market practice for pre-approval threshold at pre-IPO issuer -->`.
8. **Cadence and deliverables.** Quarterly meetings timed to the 10-Q cycle, an annual audit-plan meeting (Q4), an ICFR walkthrough (Q3), an executive session with the auditor at every meeting; the annual audit-committee report for the proxy under Item 407(d)(3); the full-board report-out at the meeting following each committee meeting.
9. **Charter review and amendment.** The annual charter-review requirement under NYSE § 303A.06 / Nasdaq Rule 5605(c)(1); the public-posting obligation; the amendment process running through the nom-&-gov committee to the full board.

### Part B — Compensation committee charter

Author `compensation-committee-charter.md`. The charter is Rule 10C-1-compliant and leaves the substantive compensation work to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/). Cover:

1. **Composition and heightened independence.** Three members, each independent under NYSE § 303A.05 or Nasdaq Rule 5605(d)(2) implementing SEC Rule 10C-1(b)(1). Run the Rule 10C-1(b)(1)(ii) source-of-compensation and affiliation factors against each proposed member; name the current gap — the committee today carries only one qualifying independent director, and the second-independent-director recruit from Part F's sequencing memo is the forcing function for the compensation committee's final posture.
2. **Charter authority.** Sole authority to retain compensation consultants, independent legal counsel, and other advisors under Rule 10C-1(b)(2); funding from the corporation for those advisors without management approval; direct reporting line from each advisor to the committee chair.
3. **Rule 10C-1(b)(4) advisor-independence framework.** State the six-factor advisor-independence assessment the committee will perform before retaining any advisor (provision of other services; fees as a percentage of total revenue; conflict-of-interest policies; business or personal relationships with committee members; stock ownership; business or personal relationships with executive officers). Name it as the operative obligation; defer the substantive framework and the committee's running independence log to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/). Do not author the substantive finding here.
4. **Scope handoff to mod-105.** Name the substance that lives in [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/): CEO pay-setting, the equity-refresh grid, Rule 10D-1 clawbacks, Section 162(m) / 409A / ISO mechanics, CD&A drafting, say-on-pay. This charter governs the machinery only.
5. **Delegation posture under DGCL § 141(c).** State the matters the committee may finally act on and the matters it may only recommend to the full board; name the non-delegable carve-outs (matters requiring stockholder approval, bylaw amendment, dividend declaration) that remain with the full board under § 141(c)(2); reference the officer-grant subdelegation regime the board may authorise for below-threshold equity grants under DGCL § 157(c) / § 152 (named here, defer substance to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)).
6. **Cadence and deliverables.** Quarterly meetings; off-cycle meetings for each annual grant cycle and each new-hire executive offer requiring committee approval; an executive session at every meeting; the annual compensation-committee report for the proxy under Item 407(e)(5); the full-board report-out at the meeting following each committee meeting.

### Part C — Nominating & governance committee charter

Author `nominating-governance-committee-charter.md`. This committee has never been formally stood up at Maridian; the charter is the stand-up instrument. Cover:

1. **Composition and independence.** Three members, each independent under NYSE § 303A.04 or Nasdaq Rule 5605(e); market practice at the pre-IPO issuer is fully independent and this charter adopts that posture.
2. **Board-composition planning.** The skills matrix, the tenure-and-refreshment plan, the diversity commitments, and the search protocol for the second independent director that Part F's sequencing memo flags as the Rule 10C-1 forcing function. Defer the substantive composition trajectory to [chapter 01](../01-board-architecture-and-composition-trajectory.md).
3. **Director search, nomination, onboarding, and continuing education.** The annual nomination process feeding the proxy; the onboarding packet for a new director; the continuing-education budget.
4. **Board and committee evaluations.** The annual self-assessment and the periodic third-party evaluation — defer cadence to [chapter 08](../08-annual-governance-operating-rhythm.md).
5. **Corporate-governance guidelines.** Meeting cadence, executive-session protocol, director stock-ownership guidelines, over-boarding limits, and retirement age if adopted. Defer meeting-materials substance to [chapter 02](../02-board-cadence-and-materials.md).
6. **Code of business conduct and ethics.** Annual review; the Item 5.05 of Form 8-K disclosure obligation for any waiver granted to an executive officer or director, with the four-business-day filing clock.
7. **Related-party transactions.** The DGCL § 144 interested-director transaction review process, coordinated with the audit committee's Reg S-K Item 404 disclosure screen; the standing conflicts-and-related-party questionnaire the director-and-officer population completes annually.
8. **Stockholder-engagement and nominee-communication protocol.** The written process for stockholders to recommend director candidates (an Item 407(c)(2)(vi) disclosure in the proxy); the posture on advance-notice bylaws and proxy-access thresholds carried forward into the public-company bylaw package.
9. **Cadence and deliverables.** Quarterly meetings, intense Q1 pre-annual-meeting cycle, Q3 composition review feeding the following-year search plan; the annual director-nominee slate; evaluation results; proposed amendments to governance guidelines and code of ethics; related-party-transaction determinations.

### Part D — Special-committee board-resolution template

Author `special-committee-board-resolution-template.md`. This is the form-of-resolution the full board will adopt to form a special committee when a conflict transaction arises; the next foreseeable use is a controlling-stockholder transaction with the lead-Series-C investor-group. Cover:

1. **The recitals.** The specific transaction or category of transactions; the identified conflict (CEO on both sides, controlling-stockholder on both sides, director's fund participating, management-led buyout); the recusal of each conflicted director; the basis in DGCL § 141(c) for delegation and DGCL § 144 for cleansing.
2. **The *MFW* ab-initio dual conditions.** For a controlling-stockholder transaction, name explicitly that the transaction is being conditioned, **ab initio**, on (a) approval by a fully-empowered special committee of independent directors and (b) an uncoerced, informed vote of a majority of the minority stockholders, each as required under *Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014) to earn business-judgment-rule review in lieu of entire-fairness review. Include the operative "ab initio" recital and the controller's parallel acknowledgment.
3. **Composition.** Disinterested under DGCL § 144 (no personal financial interest in the transaction) and independent under *MFW* (no dominating relationship with the conflicted party); two or three directors minimum; the specific directors named; the disinterestedness-and-independence findings the full board is making at the time of formation.
4. **The "authority to say no."** The express grant of full power to negotiate, to evaluate, to approve, **and to reject** the transaction — including the power to walk away. The resolution language must be unambiguous that the committee is not a mere negotiating body; a committee that cannot say no does not cleanse the transaction.
5. **Independent advisors.** Authority to retain independent legal counsel and an independent financial advisor at the corporation's expense without management or controller approval; the engagement letters flow to the committee chair, not to the CFO.
6. **Scope, reporting line, and record.** The defined scope of the committee's mandate; the reporting obligation to the full board; the contemporaneous-minutes discipline against the record *MFW* and its progeny will reconstruct in any subsequent litigation.
7. **Process-cleansing cross-reference.** Cross-reference to DGCL § 144(a)(1) disinterested-director approval and § 144(a)(2) stockholder approval; name the alternative *Corwin* cleansing path for non-controller transactions (deferred to [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md)) and the entire-fairness fallback if *MFW* conditions are not met in full.

### Part E — AI-risk sub-committee charter

Author `ai-risk-subcommittee-charter.md` — the sub-committee of the audit committee the board has agreed to stand up. Cover:

1. **Composition and independence.** At least one director with genuine technical fluency in modern ML systems (not a generalist "tech" director); independence inherited from the parent audit committee under Rule 10A-3; at least one member sits on both the parent audit committee and the sub-committee to carry the coordination line.
2. **Model-risk inventory.** The sub-committee's annual review of the production-model inventory, the risk-tier assignment per model, the controls each tier carries (evals cadence, red-team cadence, human-in-the-loop requirement, deployment constraints). Defer the technical substance to the head-of-ai-governance-learning curriculum and to [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) for data-governance substance.
3. **Deployment-approval gating.** A gating role for any production deployment crossing a defined materiality threshold — regulated-decision use, customer-facing autonomous-agent systems, safety-critical systems, systems materially affecting revenue recognition. The threshold is flagged with `<!-- needs-research: deployment-materiality threshold calibrated to Maridian's revenue concentration and model-portfolio composition -->`.
4. **Incident-review cadence.** Post-incident review of model failures and near-misses; the standing incident log that feeds the enterprise risk register; the escalation path to the parent audit committee and to the full board on a *Caremark* "red flag" materiality trigger.
5. **Caremark / *Marchand* framing.** State the *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019) frame explicitly — the sub-committee is the board's mission-critical-risk oversight system for AI-model risk at a company where AI is mission-critical, and the sub-committee's charter, cadence, minutes, and escalation log are the board's defence against a failure-of-oversight *Caremark* claim. Cross-reference [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md) for the full duty-of-oversight doctrine.
6. **Coordination with audit committee.** The AI-risk sub-committee is a technical advisor to the parent audit committee where a model materially affects ICFR, revenue recognition, or intangibles valuation; the parent audit committee remains primary on financial-reporting matters.
7. **Handoff to the head-of-ai-governance-learning curriculum.** AI-safety engineering, evals design, red-team protocols, and the enterprise AI-risk framework substance are owned by the head-of-ai-governance-learning programme; this charter defines the governance machinery only.
8. **Information rights and management reporting line.** The reporting line from the head-of-AI-governance into the sub-committee chair; the standing data pack (model inventory, eval-result summary, incident log, red-team findings, deployment pipeline); the right to retain independent technical advisors at the corporation's expense; the privilege posture when technical findings intersect with general-counsel legal advice.
9. **Cadence and deliverables.** Quarterly meetings, additional meetings triggered by incidents or deployment decisions; the quarterly AI-risk dashboard to the full board; deployment-approval log; incident summaries with root cause and remediation; the annual review of the AI-risk framework.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 04 is the primary reference for every charter. Read the audit, compensation, nom-&-gov, special, and AI-risk sub-committee sections before drafting; the chapter names the charter contents, the cadence, and the full-board reporting line each charter has to carry.
- Write the charters as adoption-ready documents, not as outlines. Each charter opens with a purpose clause, defines composition and quorum, enumerates authority and responsibilities, specifies the cadence, specifies the independent-advisor retention right, and closes with a charter-review clause. A reader should be able to lift the charter into a board resolution with minimal edits.
- Do not re-author compensation-committee substance. Rule 10C-1(b)(4)'s six-factor advisor-independence test is named here; the substantive framework, the running advisor log, and the CEO-pay-setting mechanics live in [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).
- Do not re-author fiduciary-duty substance. The *Marchand* / *Caremark* framing in the AI-risk charter and the *MFW* framing in the special-committee resolution are named here; the full doctrinal treatment lives in [chapter 05](../05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md).
- Do not re-author EGC-election mechanics or the Rule 10A-3 listed-company phase-in beyond the audit-committee cure period itself. Defer the broader IPO-readiness sequencing to [chapter 09](../09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md).
- Precise statutory citation. SEC Rule 10A-3 is 17 C.F.R. § 240.10A-3; SEC Rule 10C-1 is 17 C.F.R. § 240.10C-1; SOX §§ 301, 202, 404 are also Exchange Act §§ 10A(m), 10A(i), and no-citation-needed-in-the-statute; Reg S-K Items 407(d)(5), 407(d)(3), 407(e)(5), 404, and Item 5.05 of Form 8-K are cited by Item number; DGCL §§ 141(c) and 144 by section. The two Delaware cases are *Kahn v. M & F Worldwide Corp.*, 88 A.3d 635 (Del. 2014) and *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019).
- No fabricated advisor-independence findings in Part B. Name the Rule 10C-1(b)(4) six-factor assessment as an obligation; do not invent a finding against a named advisor.
- No real peer-company names. Maridian Compute is the fictional corporation; the lead Series-C investor-group, the departing investor-director, and the new independent audit chair are referenced by role.
- Flag every dollar number and every market-practice statistic with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` and a named verification step. No `[TBD]` placeholders.
