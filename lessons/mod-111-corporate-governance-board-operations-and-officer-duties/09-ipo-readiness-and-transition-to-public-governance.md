# 9. Pre-IPO governance readiness and the transition to public governance

> Going public is not a financing event with governance side effects; it is a governance rebuild that happens to be financed by a share issuance.

## Motivation

Going public converts the governance function from a private-company operating model — where the board serves the founders and lead investors, the cap table is negotiated, and disclosure is contractual — to a public-company disclosure-and-controls machine, where the board answers to a diffuse public float, disclosure is statutory, and controls are audited.

The transition takes 12 to 24 months. Companies that begin the governance-readiness sprint 18 months out enter registration with all standing committees composed and functioning, all listing-standard policies adopted and operating, and the independence math for the board and each committee already working under NYSE or Nasdaq counting rules. Companies that begin 6 months out negotiate every governance term under time pressure, retrofit committee charters against a filed S-1, chase independent directors while the bankers are already selling the deal, and expose the IPO to gating regulatory findings — an unremediated SOX-404 material weakness, an audit-committee independence failure, or a Reg S-K Item 404 related-party disclosure gap — that can push a transaction by a quarter or a year.

This chapter owns the governance dual-track of IPO readiness. It sequences committee formation, independent-director recruiting, policy adoption, S-1 governance-section authoring, and the transition into first-year public-company operating rhythm. The 2-year audit history and SOX-404 workstreams are referenced here but owned by the CFO-side dual-track in a sibling curriculum.

## The dual-track structure: governance, finance, and transaction

IPO readiness runs on three parallel tracks that share a single filing calendar.

| Track | Owner | Scope | Curriculum home |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governance | General counsel / corporate secretary / lead independent director | Board and committee composition, listing-standard compliance, policy adoption, S-1 governance sections, Section 16 program, insider-trading and Reg FD infrastructure | This chapter |
| Finance and controls | CFO / chief accounting officer / audit committee chair | 2-year audit history under Big Four, S-X compliance, MD&A drafting, quarterly-close controls, SOX-404 gap assessment and remediation | `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111 |
| Transaction execution | CEO / CFO / bankers / underwriters' counsel | Banker selection, S-1 drafting mechanics, testing-the-waters meetings, road show, listing-exchange application, first-day pricing and allocation | `startup-exit-curriculum` |

The three tracks converge at three checkpoints: the initial S-1 filing (public or confidential), the effectiveness of the registration statement, and the first 10-Q filed as a public company. A miss on any track at any checkpoint delays the other two.

Governance is the track most often underestimated because it produces no line item on the offering timeline. It is also the track most likely to force a re-file, because listing-standard independence and Reg S-K disclosure failures are not curable at the printer.

## The 2-year audit history requirement

SEC Regulation S-X governs the form and content of financial statements filed with the Commission. An emerging growth company (EGC), as defined in Securities Act Section 2(a)(19) as added by the JOBS Act, may include two years of audited financial statements in its S-1. A non-EGC must include three years.

The practical implication is a governance-adjacent one: the auditor upgrade must be timed against the S-1 filing calendar.

- Most venture-scale companies enter their growth phase with a regional or second-tier audit firm. The Big Four (Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC) are the practical standard for public-company audits — not because listing standards require it, but because the underwriters, the audit committee, and the market do.
- The auditor upgrade should be complete at least two full fiscal years before the S-1 filing so that both audit-history years are audited by the same firm the market will see. Retroactive re-audits by a new firm are expensive, time-consuming, and can surface restatement issues that would not have arisen if the incumbent firm had rolled forward.
- See [chapter 04](./04-standing-committees.md) for the audit-committee section on auditor selection and see [mod-112](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/) for the audit-firm relationship as it interacts with the D&O insurance dual-track.

The audit-committee chair is typically the executive sponsor of the auditor upgrade and is the interface into the CFO-side dual-track from the board.

## NYSE and Nasdaq listing-standard governance requirements

The exchange listing standards are the operational floor for public-company governance. Both exchanges adopted their core corporate-governance requirements in response to SOX and have since updated them for Rule 10C-1, Rule 10D-1, and Item 408(b). The requirements are close but not identical; policy language and committee charters should be drafted against the specific exchange the company plans to list on.

### Independent-board-majority

- NYSE Listed Company Manual Section 303A.01: a majority of the board must be independent.
- Nasdaq Rule 5605(b): a majority of the board must be independent.
- Both exchanges define independence in the negative — the board must affirmatively determine that a director has no material relationship with the company that would interfere with independent judgment.
- A phase-in accommodation is available for newly listed companies: one independent director at listing, a majority within one year of listing, and full committee independence within one year (see NYSE 303A.00 and Nasdaq Rule 5615(b) for the phase-in schedules).

### Audit committee

- SEC Rule 10A-3 sets the floor: audit-committee members must be independent under the Rule 10A-3 definition (no consulting or advisory fees from the issuer other than director fees; no affiliation with the issuer or a subsidiary).
- NYSE Sections 303A.06 and 303A.07: full independence at listing plus written charter with specific responsibilities enumerated.
- Nasdaq Rule 5605(c): parallel requirements; three-member minimum.
- At least one audit-committee financial expert must be disclosed in the proxy under Item 407(d)(5) of Reg S-K, or the company must disclose that it does not have one and explain why.

### Compensation committee

- SEC Rule 10C-1 requires listing standards for compensation-committee independence.
- NYSE Section 303A.05 and Nasdaq Rule 5605(d): heightened independence analysis considers (i) the source of the director's compensation, including consulting fees, and (ii) any affiliation with the issuer, a subsidiary, or an affiliate of a subsidiary.
- The heightened test is stricter than the general independence test. A director who is independent for board-majority purposes may not be independent for compensation-committee purposes.
- Compensation-committee independence extends to the retention of compensation consultants and independent legal counsel — the committee must have the authority to retain them directly and must consider six independence factors before engagement.

### Nominating and governance committee

- NYSE Section 303A.04: independent nominating / corporate governance committee with a written charter.
- Nasdaq Rule 5605(e): independent-director selection process, either by a majority of independent directors or by a nominations committee composed entirely of independent directors.

### Insider-trading policy

- Item 408(b) of Reg S-K requires public companies to disclose in the annual report whether they have adopted an insider-trading policy governing the purchase, sale, or other disposition of the company's securities by directors, officers, and employees, and to file the policy as Exhibit 19 to the 10-K if adopted.
- Adopted in the December 2022 SEC amendments; applicable to filings for periods beginning on or after April 1, 2023 <!-- needs-research: verify effective-date phasing for smaller reporting companies and non-calendar-year filers -->.
- The insider-trading policy authored under [chapter 07](./07-insider-trading-policy-and-10b5-1-programme.md) becomes an SEC-filed exhibit at the first 10-K.

### Code of ethics disclosure

- Item 406 of Reg S-K requires disclosure of whether the company has adopted a code of ethics that applies to the principal executive officer, principal financial officer, principal accounting officer or controller, and persons performing similar functions.
- If adopted, the code must be filed as an exhibit, posted on the company website, or made available on request. Amendments and waivers must be disclosed on Form 8-K under Item 5.05 within four business days.

### Corporate governance disclosure

- Item 407 of Reg S-K covers the annual proxy disclosures for director independence (407(a)), meetings and committees (407(b)), the nominating process (407(c)), the audit committee (407(d)), the compensation committee (407(e)), board leadership structure and the board's role in risk oversight (407(h)), and hedging policies (407(i)).

### Controlled-company exemptions

- NYSE Section 303A.00 and Nasdaq Rule 5615(c) provide exemptions for a company where more than 50% of the voting power is held by an individual, a group, or another company.
- Exempted requirements: independent-board-majority, independent nominating committee, independent compensation committee.
- Not exempted: audit-committee independence under Rule 10A-3 remains fully applicable.
- The controlled-company election must be disclosed in the S-1 and the annual proxy. Many dual-class founder-led IPOs elect controlled-company status at listing but design a sunset to phased-independence within three to five years.

## SOX Section 302 and 404 readiness

The Sarbanes-Oxley Act imposes the internal-controls architecture that distinguishes public-company financial reporting from private-company financial reporting.

### Section 302

- CEO and CFO personal certification, in each 10-Q and 10-K, of the financial statements, the design and operation of disclosure controls and procedures, and the design and operation of internal control over financial reporting (ICFR).
- Required from the first periodic report after the IPO. The certification is a personal certification with personal liability; both officers should have completed a dry run before the first live filing.

### Section 404(a)

- Management's annual assessment of the effectiveness of ICFR, disclosed in the 10-K.
- SEC accommodation for newly public companies defers the first 404(a) assessment to the second annual report on Form 10-K after the IPO.

### Section 404(b)

- Auditor attestation of management's ICFR assessment.
- Required once the company becomes an accelerated filer or large accelerated filer (public float thresholds under Rule 12b-2).
- EGCs are exempt from 404(b) for the duration of EGC status — up to five fiscal years from the IPO.

### Practical implication

- SOX-404 gap-assessment typically begins 18 to 24 months before the target IPO date.
- Remediation of material weaknesses takes 6 to 12 months. A material weakness disclosed in the S-1 or in the first 10-K is a stock-price event and, if severe, a delisting risk.
- The SOX-404 workstream is owned by the CFO in the finance dual-track. The audit committee chair is the board's interface into the workstream and should receive monthly readouts from month T-18 forward.

## CD&A and executive-compensation disclosure

Item 402 of Reg S-K governs executive-compensation disclosure. The full disclosure package is one of the most operationally intensive sections of the S-1 and the annual proxy.

- Item 402 components: Compensation Discussion and Analysis (CD&A), Summary Compensation Table, Grants of Plan-Based Awards, Outstanding Equity Awards at Fiscal Year-End, Option Exercises and Stock Vested, Pension Benefits, Non-Qualified Deferred Compensation, and Potential Payments upon Termination or Change-in-Control.
- Item 402(v) pay-versus-performance disclosure was added by SEC rulemaking in 2022 <!-- needs-research: verify Item 402(v) adopting release number and effective-date phasing for EGCs and smaller reporting companies -->.
- EGCs may provide reduced Item 402 disclosure: three named executive officers (rather than five), and no CD&A, no pay-versus-performance table, no compensation-committee report, and no pay-ratio disclosure during EGC status <!-- needs-research: verify EGC scaled-disclosure scope against latest Item 402 amendments -->.

Substance of executive-compensation policy is owned by [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/). This chapter owns the sequencing for IPO readiness:

- T-18 to T-12: engage a compensation consultant (typically Compensia, Aon Radford, F.W. Cook, Semler Brossy, or a Big Four executive-comp practice).
- T-12 to T-6: finalize the peer group. The peer group drives the benchmarking that will be defended in the CD&A and reviewed by ISS and Glass Lewis.
- T-6 to T-0: CD&A drafting begins in the year prior to IPO, with the first full-form CD&A landing in the S-1 (or a scaled version, if the company elects EGC accommodations) and the first-annual-proxy CD&A landing 120 days after the first full fiscal year-end as a public company.

## Regulation FD

Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure), codified at 17 C.F.R. Sections 243.100 through 243.103, prohibits selective disclosure of material non-public information (MNPI) by public-company executives to securities market professionals or holders of the company's securities under circumstances in which it is reasonably foreseeable that the holder will trade on the basis of the information.

### Pre-IPO comms lockdown

- From the initial S-1 filing (public or confidential) forward, and continuing through the quiet period after pricing, the company operates under gun-jumping restrictions and Section 5 of the Securities Act.
- The road-show quiet-period restrictions extend to 25 days after IPO pricing for road-show and prospectus-related communications <!-- needs-research: verify current SEC guidance on quiet-period duration for EGCs versus non-EGCs -->.
- Testing-the-waters communications by EGCs under Securities Act Section 5(d) are permitted before and after filing, but only with qualified institutional buyers and institutional accredited investors.

### Training and infrastructure

- CEO, CFO, IR head, and any executive who will interact with analysts and investors receives Reg FD training before the road show and repeats annually.
- Earnings-release script vetting: every earnings release, prepared remarks, and Q&A anticipation document is reviewed by counsel before the call.
- Analyst-communications policy: designated spokespersons only, no selective disclosure, prompt public disclosure of any inadvertent selective disclosure under Rule 100(a)(2).
- Employee-communications rules: internal all-hands, engineering blog posts, sales-team briefings, and social-media guidelines must all be structured to prevent selective disclosure of MNPI through channels the company does not intend to be public.

## S-1 governance-section authoring

The governance sections of the S-1 are the first public statement of the company's post-IPO governance model. Every claim in these sections becomes a proxy-statement anchor and an ISS / Glass Lewis input for the first annual meeting.

- Directors and Executive Officers: biographies, ages, committee memberships, independence status, and other public-company directorships.
- Corporate Governance: framework, committee structure, code of ethics, insider-trading policy, related-party transactions policy, board leadership structure, risk-oversight role.
- Certain Relationships and Related Party Transactions: the Item 404 inventory of every related-party transaction over the disclosure threshold ($120,000 under current Item 404(a) <!-- needs-research: verify current Item 404(a) threshold -->) since the beginning of the last three fiscal years, plus the related-party transactions policy adopted at IPO.
- Compensation Discussion and Analysis: the sequencing owned above; substance owned by [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).

### The D&O questionnaire

The director and officer (D&O) questionnaire is the intake instrument that seeds all four sections above and populates the Item 402 tables. It is circulated to each director and Section 16 officer three to four months before the S-1 filing and refreshed at each S-1 amendment and again before each annual proxy filing.

The D&O questionnaire covers:

- Biographical and background information for the Directors and Executive Officers section.
- Independence questions structured to the applicable NYSE or Nasdaq definition and the Rule 10A-3 / Rule 10C-1 heightened tests.
- Related-party transactions for Item 404 disclosure — the "list every business relationship you or any immediate family member has with the company" question that catches the disclosure gaps.
- Beneficial ownership for the Principal and Selling Stockholders table.
- Section 16 baseline for the initial Form 3.
- Directorships and other public-company affiliations.
- Litigation, regulatory, and disciplinary history for Item 401(f) disclosure.

The corporate secretary function (see [chapter 03](./03-corporate-secretary-function.md)) owns the D&O-questionnaire cycle from the first pre-IPO circulation forward.

## Pre-IPO governance policies to adopt or restate

The following table sequences the policy-adoption calendar against the T-24 to T-0 IPO readiness window. Every policy listed here is a listing-standard requirement, a Reg S-K disclosure requirement, or a market-expectation requirement that will be reviewed by underwriters' counsel in the S-1 drafting sessions.

| Policy | Standard / rule | Adoption target |
|---|---|---|
| Audit committee charter | Rule 10A-3, NYSE 303A.06-07 / Nasdaq 5605(c) | T-12 months |
| Compensation committee charter | Rule 10C-1, NYSE 303A.05 / Nasdaq 5605(d) | T-12 months |
| Nominating and governance charter | NYSE 303A.04 / Nasdaq 5605(e) | T-12 months |
| Code of business conduct and ethics | Reg S-K Item 406 | T-9 months |
| Insider-trading policy | Reg S-K Item 408(b) | T-9 months |
| Related-party transactions policy | Reg S-K Item 404 | T-9 months |
| Whistleblower policy | SOX Section 301 | T-12 months |
| Regulation FD policy | 17 C.F.R. Section 243 | T-6 months |
| Clawback policy | SEC Rule 10D-1; NYSE 303A.14 / Nasdaq 5608 | T-6 months (adopted at listing) |
| Director stock-ownership guidelines | Best practice / ISS-Glass Lewis expectation | T-3 months |
| Corporate governance guidelines | NYSE 303A.09 (NYSE-specific) | T-6 months |
| Anti-hedging / anti-pledging policy | Reg S-K Item 407(i) | T-9 months |

The T-12-months adoption target for committee charters is not aggressive — it is the point at which the committees themselves need to be functioning against those charters in order to have a track record for the S-1. Charters adopted the week before the S-1 filing are visible as such to underwriters' counsel and are a common source of underwriters'-diligence findings.

## The T-24 to T-0 governance-readiness roadmap

| Window | Governance workstream | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| T-24 to T-18 | Audit-firm upgrade to Big Four; comp-consultant retention; begin independent-director recruiting (audit chair, comp chair, domain independents); board-composition gap analysis; D&O policy repricing dry-run | Signed engagement letters; recruiting brief; gap-analysis memo |
| T-18 to T-12 | SOX-404 gap-assessment kickoff; code-of-ethics refresh; insider-trading-policy rewrite to Item 408(b) standard; related-party inventory scrub; committee-charter restatements; whistleblower policy adoption | Adopted policies; SOX remediation plan; restated committee charters |
| T-12 to T-6 | Dry-run 10-Q with public-company controls; board-charter restatements; sunset pre-IPO stockholder-agreement provisions (board-observer rights, protective provisions, information rights); comp-consultant peer-group finalization; independent-director onboarding | Dry-run 10-Q; restated charters; sunset agreements; peer-group memo |
| T-6 to T-0 | S-1 authoring (governance sections, related-party disclosures, executive-compensation tables); road-show materials review; listing-exchange application; first 10-Q playbook; adopt clawback policy at listing; Reg FD training | Filed S-1; approved listing application; effective registration statement |

The T-24 window is the point at which the audit-firm upgrade and independent-director recruiting must begin if the two-year audit history is to be delivered by the incumbent Big Four firm and the committees are to be composed of directors who have served together long enough to have a functioning working relationship.

The T-12 window is the point at which committee charters must be operating as adopted so that meeting minutes, committee reports, and board-approval trails have a record for the S-1 to reference.

## First-year-as-a-public-company operating rhythm

The 12 months after the IPO are the point at which the pre-IPO governance rebuild is stress-tested by the live public-company disclosure calendar. See also [chapter 08](./08-annual-governance-rhythm.md) for the full annual rhythm.

### Quarterly earnings-release process

- Prepared-remarks script and Q&A anticipation document: drafted by IR, reviewed by CFO, vetted by counsel for Reg FD and MD&A consistency.
- Earnings press release: reviewed and filed on Form 8-K under Item 2.02.
- Earnings call: held after market close on the day of release; simultaneously webcast; transcript retained.
- 10-Q filing: 40 days after quarter-end for accelerated filers, 45 days for non-accelerated filers, 40 days for large accelerated filers <!-- needs-research: verify current 10-Q filing deadlines under Rule 13a-13 -->.

### Annual proxy

- DEF 14A filed within 120 days of fiscal year-end for the annual meeting, or filed with the 10-K if not filed separately (permitted for smaller filers).
- CD&A and full Item 402 disclosure (or EGC-scaled disclosure during EGC status).
- Say-on-pay and say-on-frequency votes under Dodd-Frank Section 951 — first say-on-pay is required at the first annual meeting after the company ceases to be an EGC, but non-EGCs must include say-on-pay at the first annual meeting.
- Say-on-frequency: required at first annual meeting after ceasing to be an EGC and every six years thereafter.

### First annual stockholder meeting

- Notice-and-access mailing timeline under Rule 14a-16.
- ISS and Glass Lewis engagement: the first proxy will be reviewed against the ISS and Glass Lewis policy frameworks; governance-team engagement with both firms should begin the quarter before proxy filing.
- Broker-vote and beneficial-owner mechanics: coordinate with the transfer agent and Broadridge.

### First insider-trading window cycle

- Open- and closed-window schedule established at IPO; first blackout closes on the last day of the first fiscal quarter as a public company.
- First 10b5-1 plans typically adopted during the first open window after the IPO lockup expires (see [chapter 07](./07-insider-trading-policy-and-10b5-1-programme.md)).

### First Section 16 filings

- Form 3 for each director and Section 16 officer on the IPO effective date, disclosing beneficial ownership as of that date.
- Form 4 within two business days of any subsequent change in beneficial ownership.
- Form 5 annual reconciliation for any transactions not previously reported.
- Section 16(b) short-swing profit disgorgement infrastructure: pre-clearance process, quarterly reconciliation with the transfer agent, plaintiffs'-bar demand-letter response playbook.

## Emerging Growth Company (EGC) status

The JOBS Act (Jumpstart Our Business Startups Act), enacted in 2012, created the emerging growth company category and codified it at Securities Act Section 2(a)(19).

### Eligibility

- Total annual gross revenues under a threshold in the most recent fiscal year <!-- needs-research: verify current EGC revenue eligibility threshold as adjusted for inflation --> and the company completed its IPO after December 8, 2011.

### Duration

- EGC status lasts up to five fiscal years after the IPO, or until the earlier of:
  - Total annual gross revenues of $1.235 billion or more (as inflation-adjusted) <!-- needs-research: verify current inflation-adjusted revenue ceiling for EGC exit -->.
  - Issuance of more than $1 billion in non-convertible debt in the trailing three-year period.
  - Becoming a large accelerated filer under Rule 12b-2 (public float of $700 million or more).

### Accommodations available to EGCs

- Two-year (not three-year) audit history in the S-1 under Reg S-X.
- Auditor attestation exemption from SOX Section 404(b).
- Reduced executive-compensation disclosure: three named executive officers, no CD&A, no pay-versus-performance table, no compensation-committee report, no pay-ratio disclosure <!-- needs-research: verify scope of EGC scaled disclosure under Item 402 as most recently amended -->.
- Deferred say-on-pay: not required until the earlier of three years after IPO or exit from EGC status.
- Opt-in to extended transition periods for new or revised accounting standards (private-company transition timing).
- Confidential S-1 submission before public filing under Securities Act Section 6(e).

### Strategic considerations

- The confidential S-1 accommodation is used by almost all EGC issuers because it allows the company to complete two rounds of SEC comment before the S-1 becomes public.
- The reduced executive-compensation disclosure is a real relief in the first year but is a market-optics tradeoff — the market and ISS-Glass Lewis expect full disclosure even where the rules permit less. Many EGC issuers voluntarily include CD&A-equivalent disclosure to normalize expectations.
- The extended accounting-transition-period opt-in is a one-time election made at the first filing; opting in and then opting out later is disclosable and analyzed by investors.

## Summary

- Going public is a 12- to 24-month governance rebuild, not a financing event with governance side effects. The governance-readiness sprint should begin at T-24 to T-18, not at T-6.
- The IPO readiness workstream runs on three parallel tracks — governance (this chapter), finance and controls (`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` mod-111), and transaction execution (`startup-exit-curriculum`) — that converge at S-1 filing, effectiveness, and first 10-Q.
- Cite the specific standards. NYSE 303A and Nasdaq 5605-5615 govern committee independence. Rule 10A-3 (audit), Rule 10C-1 (compensation), Rule 10D-1 (clawback), Rule 12b-2 (filer categories), and Reg S-K Items 402, 404, 406, 407, and 408 govern disclosure. SOX Sections 301, 302, and 404 govern controls.
- The 2-year audit history is a governance-adjacent forcing function: the Big Four auditor must be engaged at least two full fiscal years before the S-1 filing.
- Committee charters must be adopted and operating at T-12 so that meeting minutes and approval trails have a record for the S-1 to reference.
- The D&O questionnaire is the intake instrument that seeds every governance section of the S-1 and every Item 402 and Item 404 table. It is circulated three to four months before filing and refreshed at each amendment.
- The pre-IPO policy checklist is not optional; every policy on the list is a listing-standard, Reg S-K, or market-expectation requirement that will be diligenced by underwriters' counsel.
- Reg FD training and infrastructure must be in place before the road show and must be operating continuously from the first pre-IPO investor meeting forward.
- EGC status provides real accommodations — two-year audit history, 404(b) exemption, scaled executive-comp disclosure, deferred say-on-pay, confidential S-1, extended accounting transitions — but market and proxy-advisor expectations often exceed the EGC floor.
- The first-year-as-a-public-company operating rhythm — quarterly earnings, annual proxy, first say-on-pay, first Section 16 cycle, first insider-trading window — stress-tests the pre-IPO rebuild. See [chapter 08](./08-annual-governance-rhythm.md) for the full annual calendar.
- Crisis governance is the next-order concern once the public-company machine is running (see [chapter 10](./10-crisis-governance.md)), and the ownership boundary map (see [chapter 11](./11-ownership-boundary-map.md)) captures the IPO-readiness handoffs across GC, CFO, corporate secretary, and audit-committee chair.
