# mod-109 — Commercial Contracts, IP & Legal Ops

> The six-document customer bundle (MSA / Order Form / SLA / DPA / Security Addendum / AUP), the fallback-position playbook that lets a deal desk clear 80% of redlines without counsel, the mirror-image vendor suite and tiered onboarding workflow, a stage-appropriate IP programme across patents / trademarks / copyrights / trade secrets, OSS-license hygiene and the SBOM programme, the two-sided AI contract pattern (inbound AI-DPA and outbound AI-transparency addendum), the CLM graduation at ~50 contracts a month, and the in-house-vs-outside-counsel decision framework — the commercial-legal infrastructure that lets the corporation sign enterprise paper, buy its stack cleanly, and come through diligence without surprises.

**Track:** Startup Operations & Governance (level 50) · **Stage:** SEED / SERIES-A / SERIES-B / GROWTH · **Pillar:** legal-ops · **Hours:** 32

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) stood up the Delaware C-corp, the charter and bylaws, and the officer-authority framework that gives signatures their force. [mod-102](../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/) wrote the founders' side: PIIAs, pre-formation IP assignments, founder stockholder agreements, vesting, 83(b). [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) authored the employment-contract substrate — at-will, arbitration, PIIA, DTSA whistleblower notice, non-compete / non-solicit variance across states. [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) wired the HRIS / PEO stack that administers those contracts. [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/) wrote the internal AI acceptable-use policy for the workforce. What none of those modules does is answer the question every enterprise sale, every vendor onboarding, every investor diligence Q&A, and every M&A buyer's counsel eventually asks: *where is the paper you sign and the paper you accept, and can you prove you own what you are licensing?*

This module owns that layer.

Commercial contracts, IP, and legal ops decompose into eight operating decisions the corporation has to make on purpose, not by drift:

1. **The customer contract suite.** The six-document bundle a Fortune-500 procurement team expects on first ask — Master Services Agreement, Order Form, Service Level Agreement, Data Processing Addendum, Security Addendum (often responsive to a Shared Assessments SIG-lite), and Acceptable Use Policy — the order-of-precedence clause that keeps them from contradicting each other, and the deal-specific carve-ins that make each new sale a short Order Form against a reused MSA rather than a bespoke 40-page redline.
2. **The contract playbook.** A clause-indexed fallback-position matrix (ideal / acceptable / walk-away) covering limitation of liability, indemnification, IP ownership, warranty, termination for convenience, SLA credits, MFN, audit rights, renewal, auto-renewal, and assignment; the internal-legal-turn SLA (typically 3–5 business days at Series-A, 24–48 hours at Series-B with a legal-ops team); and the escalation-to-outside-counsel trigger list that keeps the general counsel's billable hours proportional to actual risk.
3. **The vendor contract suite and onboarding workflow.** The mirror image — the corporation accepting someone else's paper and redlining only the handful of clauses that actually matter, the subscription / SOW / contractor / NDA template pack, and the tiered intake → DPA → security review → procurement approval → SSO-provisioning workflow that stops business-unit owners from signing on vendor paper under last-day-of-quarter pressure.
4. **The IP protection strategy.** The patent decision at Series-A (one narrowly-targeted provisional vs. no filing and trade-secret posture; portfolio build-out at growth); the trademark portfolio design (principal register for the company mark and hero product marks, intent-to-use for pipeline, EUIPO / CIPO / IP Australia for international); copyright registration for high-value creative works; and the trade-secret programme (identification, access control, marking, exit interview, DTSA-compliant audit trail) that makes the MSA's outbound IP indemnity underwritable.
5. **Open-source hygiene.** The OSI-license taxonomy (permissive MIT / BSD / Apache; weak copyleft LGPL / MPL; strong copyleft GPL / AGPL) and the go-to-market implications of each; the SBOM requirement in enterprise RFPs and under Executive Order 14028 § 4(k) / OMB M-22-18; the license-compliance tooling choice (FOSSA / Tidelift / Snyk / Black Duck); the outbound-contribution policy; and the enterprise OSS warranty in customer MSAs.
6. **The AI vendor / AI-DPA pattern.** The two-sided problem — the inbound model-provider addendum (input-data confidentiality, output-IP ownership, training-on-inputs prohibition, model-provenance disclosure, sub-processor list, EU AI Act general-purpose-AI-model addendum, data-residency, breach-notification), the customer-side AI-transparency addendum (disclosure of AI-in-the-loop, human-in-the-loop guarantees, model-provenance), and the AI-indemnification pattern (IP-indemnity for AI-generated output with practical cap / exception design) — kept consistent so a downstream commitment is not louder than the upstream vendor stack can satisfy.
7. **The CLM stack and the legal-ops graduation.** The shared-Drive-to-first-CLM transition that typically hits between Series-A and early Series-B when contract volume crosses ~50 per month; the CLM-tool choice (Ironclad / LinkSquares / DocuSign CLM / Concord / Contract Logix) along the sign-workflow / template-management / redline-tracking / clause-library / obligations-tracker axes; the CLM-onboarding programme; and the "CLM or first legal-ops hire first" decision at Series-B.
8. **The in-house vs. outside-counsel decision framework.** The outsourced-GC model at pre-seed / seed / early Series-A (Cooley / WSGR / Orrick / Fenwick / Latham / Gunderson / Goodwin / Perkins Coie); the fractional-GC bridge at ~$5–25M ARR; the first in-house Head of Legal / Senior Corporate Counsel hire at Series-A / B; the buy-vs-build calculus by specialty (commercial, corporate, employment, IP prosecution, litigation, privacy, tax); the outside-counsel management discipline (rate cards, matter management, e-billing, panel management); and the graduation to a full legal function at Series-C / pre-IPO.

A ninth problem-space closes the loop:

9. **The ownership-boundary map.** The reference that decides — for any contract / IP / legal-ops question — whether it belongs to mod-109, to a sibling module (mod-101 for entity-level corporate work, mod-102 for founder-side legal architecture, mod-103 for employment-facing contracts, mod-108 for the internal AI acceptable-use policy, mod-110 for privacy / DPA regulatory depth, mod-111 for corporate-governance / board-level contracts and NVCA documents, mod-112 for insurance / risk and the cyber tower), or to a non-role track (`startup-exit-curriculum` for M&A / IPO transaction-document work, `chief-ai-officer-learning` / `head-of-ai-governance-learning` / `ai-risk-engineer-learning` for AI model-risk and evaluation methodology).

Get this right and the corporation compounds — a six-document MSA bundle procurement routes cleanly to its commercial counsel, privacy office, and infosec team; a playbook that lets a deal-desk analyst close 80% of redlines on the published fallback positions; a vendor pipeline where every Tier-1 vendor arrives with an executed DPA and a current SOC 2 before SSO provisioning; a modest IP portfolio and a documented trade-secret programme that moves diligence from "please produce chain-of-title evidence for every material contribution" to "here is the manifest"; an SBOM that answers the Fortune-500 vendor questionnaire in a day; an AI paper stack where the downstream customer promise and the upstream vendor terms are actually consistent; a CLM that produces a contract manifest for a Series-C data room in an afternoon; and a legal function whose composition at every stage is a deliberate choice rather than a drift. Get it wrong and the corporation absorbs the predictable failure set — an uncapped data-breach cap that exceeds the cyber-tower limit, a $180k ARR customer that silently auto-renews because the 60-day non-renewal window was in one person's head, an AGPL dependency shipped into a proprietary binary that surfaces during Series-B technical diligence, a model-training commitment to a Fortune-500 customer whose free-tier upstream API retains the right to train, a shadow deal desk operated out of sales ops because legal cannot staff the volume, and the departed engineer with the source-code export who can neither be enjoined nor pursued because the trade-secret programme was never actually run.
