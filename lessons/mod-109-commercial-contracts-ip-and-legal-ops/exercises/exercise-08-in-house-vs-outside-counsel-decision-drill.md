# Exercise 08 — In-house vs. outside-counsel decision drill

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [08 — The in-house vs. outside-counsel decision framework](../08-in-house-vs-outside-counsel-decision-framework.md)

## Problem statement

Corvellis Health is a Series-B vertical-SaaS healthtech corporation selling a revenue-cycle-management product (claims scrubbing, denial-management workflow, prior-authorisation orchestration) into mid-market US provider groups. The corporation is 140 FTE, Delaware C-corp, closed a $42M Series B eleven months ago; trailing-twelve-month ARR is $19M growing to a $25M run-rate exit of the current quarter. The legal function today sits almost entirely outside: a single startup-focused Big Law firm has served as outsourced general counsel since formation, running the certificate of incorporation and the initial-board resolutions, the seed and Series-A and Series-B financings, the standard-form commercial contracts, the employment-agreement templates, and the HIPAA business-associate-agreement templates the corporation uses with its hospital-system customers. Specialty outside counsel is engaged ad-hoc — a patent-prosecution boutique on the one filed utility application and two provisionals and the one filed trademark; a labour-and-employment firm on two threatened claims surfaced in the last 18 months (one performance-related separation, one unresolved harassment complaint); a healthcare-regulatory firm on an active state-DOH licensure question that opened six months ago. Trailing-twelve-month outside-counsel spend is approximately $820k and the monthly run-rate is $65-78k and growing; the CFO has told the CEO at the last two staff meetings that the outside-counsel line "has to get under control before Series-C diligence." The CEO is asking the obvious question: does a first in-house legal hire reduce total legal spend, or just add to it?

Three forcing functions sit in-queue. First, a $14M-ARR renewal cycle with a hospital-system counterparty begins in four months; procurement at the counterparty has already signalled through the account-executive that it intends to push hard on the MSA — the uptime SLA, the data-protection-addendum terms, the uncapped-indemnification carve-outs — and the counterparty's outside counsel is a specialist health-system firm that the corporation's current firm has gone up against before. Second, an acquihire discussion is active with a 12-person adjacent startup whose founders Corvellis would retain; the current firm can run the transaction out of its M&A practice, but the initial scoping conversation came back at a seven-figure fees-run-rate for the deal if the counterparty pushes diligence hard. Third, the enterprise-sales motion is losing cycle-time to a known weakness in the privacy posture: the DPA has not been refreshed since Series A, lacks the current EU-US Data Privacy Framework schedule and the 2021 Standard Contractual Clauses modules, and is drawing redlines from every enterprise prospect with EU-processor obligations — the deal-desk is logging roughly 10–15 incremental days per deal because the DPA has to be bespoke-negotiated each time instead of signed off the shelf.

The CEO has asked the current firm's lead partner, the CFO, and the Head of People to walk into an exec-team meeting in six weeks with a recommendation: what does the next 24 months of legal-function build look like, which specialties move in-house and which stay outside, what does the first-hire scope look like, what does the outside-counsel panel discipline look like, and does Corvellis need a fractional GC bridge in the meantime. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Legal-function budget across three stages

Author the legal-function budget across three stages — current (Series-B, $19M ARR, 140 FTE), Series-B+12 months (projected $35M ARR, approximately 200 FTE), and Series-C (projected $65M ARR, approximately 320 FTE) — with the composition split across outside-counsel spend, in-house salary, and legal-ops tooling. Produce the budget under two scenarios: **(i) keep the current outsourced-GC model** (no in-house hire, outside spend grows with the business); **(ii) hire the first Head of Legal at Series-B** (the hire lands within the next two quarters, outside spend re-mixes against in-house capacity).

For each stage × scenario cell:

1. Named outside-counsel line items by specialty (commercial, corporate, employment, IP prosecution, IP litigation, healthcare regulatory, privacy, tax).
2. Named in-house roles and headcount by stage; defer the compensation-architecture mechanics (banding, mid-point-plus-range, benchmark sources, offer construction, geographic differentials) to [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/).
3. Named legal-ops tooling line items (CLM platform, matter-management / e-billing platform, IP-docketing tool); defer the CLM-selection mechanics to [chapter 07](../07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md).
4. The two-scenario delta on each line, in words — this scenario adds / displaces / preserves this specialty's outside spend.

Do not invent hourly rates, fully-loaded salary figures, or vendor pricing. Any dollar range not reproduced from chapter 08 is marked with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.

### Part B — Specialty-by-specialty buy-vs-build table

Produce the specialty-by-specialty buy-vs-build table for Corvellis Health. For each of the eight specialties — commercial, corporate, employment, IP prosecution, IP litigation, healthcare regulatory, privacy, tax — author:

1. A named posture: **in-house**, **outside-panel**, or **hybrid-with-fractional-support**.
2. The named rationale grounded in chapter 08 (the stage, the volume, the structural constraint, the vertical fit) and in the Corvellis-specific facts (healthtech vertical, HIPAA depth required, the three forcing functions in the problem statement).
3. The named sibling-module hand-off where the mechanic lives elsewhere — reference [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) for privacy depth and the DPA refresh, and reference [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) for the board-governance / corporate-secretary work the first in-house hire will not own at Series-B.
4. The specific trigger-event that moves the specialty from the current posture to the next posture (volume threshold, headcount threshold, revenue threshold, regulatory event).

Do not pre-decide the posture per specialty — author the drill as the first-time holder of the decision. Where chapter 08 states a structural constraint (IP prosecution requires a USPTO-registered attorney, litigation is always outside, tax is always outside for structural work), name the constraint explicitly rather than restating it as a judgement call.

### Part C — First in-house hire JD

Author the job description Corvellis Health will publish for the first in-house hire. Title the role Head of Legal or Senior Corporate Counsel per the chapter-08 title-selection discussion — pick one and defend the choice. Cover:

1. **Reporting line.** CEO vs. CFO vs. COO; name the trade-off and the choice.
2. **Scope the role will own on day one** — the specific slice of the specialty table in Part B that moves in-house with the hire.
3. **Scope the role will not own** — the specialty slice that remains outside, including the named ownership boundary with [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) (board operations, corporate-secretary function) and the healthcare-regulatory boundary that remains outside at Series-B.
4. **Vertical-fit requirement.** The chapter-08 discipline that healthtech corporations do not hire a generalist GC "who will learn HIPAA on the job." Author the HIPAA / healthcare-regulatory / health-system-contracting experience bar explicitly.
5. **Compensation band.** The band structure the offer will carry; defer the band mechanic (banding, benchmark sources, geographic differentials, equity-grant construction) to [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/). Do not quote specific base, equity, or bonus figures — mark any figure with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.
6. **Sourcing posture.** The first-GC search firms the corporation will approach, the in-network candidates from the current outsourced-GC firm, the second-in-house-move-vs-senior-Big-Law-associate profile preference, and the conflict-of-interest disclosure discipline if a candidate is sourced from the current firm (chapter 08 names this as a failure mode).
7. **First-six-months success criteria.** Five to seven named deliverables with named target dates — the DPA refresh, the CLM procurement decision, the outside-counsel-panel rate-card negotiation, the engagement-letter rewrite, the hospital-system renewal-cycle MSA preparation, and others as appropriate.

### Part D — Outside-counsel panel-firm selection

Author the outside-counsel panel composition and the engagement-letter architecture Corvellis Health will publish. Cover:

1. **Panel composition.** The specialty-firm roster per the Part B table — named by specialty, not by real firm. For each specialty retained outside, author the number of firms (chapter 08's two-firms-minimum-per-critical-specialty discipline), the primary-vs-backup posture, and the engagement scope.
2. **Engagement-letter architecture.** The scope of representation, the named day-to-day partner requirement, the hourly rate card vs. AFA (alternative fee arrangement) mix, the e-billing posture (LEDES format, UTBMS task-and-activity codes), the matter-budget-estimate discipline, and the named-attorney restriction. Defer specific hourly rate ranges to `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — chapter 08 names the market ranges but Corvellis Health's negotiated rates are not knowable in advance.
3. **Rate-card discipline.** The annual-escalation cap, the volume-rebate structure, the discount-off-standard-rates expectation, and the mechanism for annual rate-card renegotiation.
4. **Panel-review cadence.** The regular-cadence review the Head of Legal will run against each panel firm — the scorecard elements (cycle time, budget accuracy, quality of work product, responsiveness, cultural fit), the review frequency, the escalation path when a firm underperforms.

Do not invent real firm names. Reference specialty by name (patent-prosecution boutique, labour-and-employment firm, health-system-regulatory firm) rather than by brand.

### Part E — Outside-counsel-management discipline

Author the engagement-letter and outside-counsel-management discipline Corvellis Health will publish — the operational document the Head of Legal and the finance team will enforce against every panel firm. Cover:

1. **Matter-opening form.** The required fields (matter name, matter description, responsible attorney at the firm, responsible person at the corporation, pre-matter budget estimate, category / practice area, billing frequency and format).
2. **Pre-matter budget estimate discipline.** The requirement that no work commences without a matter number and a written budget; the exceptions (immediate litigation response, regulatory-inbound response) and how exceptions are handled.
3. **Scope-and-change-order discipline.** The threshold at which a budget overage requires prior written approval (chapter 08 names 10% as a common threshold — adopt or depart from it explicitly); the written change-order form; the escalation path when a firm overruns without approval.
4. **E-billing threshold.** The invoice-review discipline — line-item review, the ABA UTBMS task-code taxonomy, the LEDES 1998B billing format requirement, the counsel-side alerts the e-billing platform fires at the 80%-of-budget threshold.
5. **Invoice-review discipline.** The named practices to flag — block billing, first-year-associate research on partner-level questions, duplicative work across attorneys, vague time entries, rounding, internal-firm-training and new-associate-ramp-up time, overhead pass-through, travel-time billing at full rate. The write-off / dispute / approve decision mechanic.
6. **After-the-fact review cadence.** The quarterly / annual review of each matter and each firm against the engagement-letter terms; the named feedback loop into the Part D panel-review cadence.

Reference [chapter 07](../07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md) for the CLM / matter-management / e-billing tool stack that operationalises the discipline. Do not re-author the CLM-selection decision; name the hand-off.

### Part F — Fractional-GC bridge posture

Chapter 08 names the fractional-GC model as the intermediate arrangement at roughly $5–25M ARR. Corvellis Health sits inside that band today. Author the fractional-GC bridge decision:

1. **Decision.** Does Corvellis Health engage a fractional GC between now and the first full-time in-house hire — yes or no? Defend the choice on the chapter-08 signals (commercial-contract volume, board-level legal cadence, employment-matter volume, monthly outside-counsel bill) and on the three forcing functions in the problem statement.
2. **If yes — scope.** The named slice of day-to-day general-counsel work the fractional GC covers (commercial redlines within the playbook from [chapter 02](../02-contract-playbook-and-fallback-position-matrix.md), employment questions, vendor-contract management, outside-counsel-management-on-behalf-of-the-corporation).
3. **If yes — cadence.** Hours per month, response-time commitment, named day-of-week availability. Chapter 08 names 15–40 hours per month as the standard range.
4. **If yes — retainer envelope.** The monthly-retainer figure, deferred to `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — chapter 08 names the market range but Corvellis Health's negotiated retainer is not knowable in advance.
5. **If yes — conflicts and confidentiality.** The explicit conflict-check obligation, the vertical-exclusivity ask (no concurrent healthtech clients), and the confidentiality framework.
6. **If yes — sunset date.** The specific trigger that ends the fractional engagement — the Head of Legal hire's start date, or a named revenue / matter-volume threshold.
7. **If no — the specific gap the Head of Legal hire closes directly** and the operational cost Corvellis Health accepts for not bridging the gap with a fractional engagement.

### Part G — CEO-and-board memo

Author the one-page memo the Head of Legal (or the person recommending the Head of Legal hire — the CFO plus the current outsourced-GC lead partner plus the Head of People, acting as the hiring-decision triad) submits to the CEO, with the specific recommendation copying the board. The memo must:

1. **State the recommendation.** The named posture across Parts A–F — the first in-house hire's title and target start date, the fractional-GC bridge decision, the panel-firm composition at a one-line summary, the budget delta over 12 and 24 months.
2. **Carry the trade-off paragraph.** What the recommendation accepts, what it displaces, what it does not solve. Chapter 08's failure-modes list is the source of the risks the memo names explicitly.
3. **Name the budget delta over 12 and 24 months.** The two-scenario comparison from Part A, summarised to two or three lines — the dollar swing between the keep-outside and hire-in-house scenarios, and the point at which the hire-in-house scenario crosses over the keep-outside scenario on cumulative spend. Mark unknown figures with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.
4. **Name the risk the recommended path accepts.** The explicit risk the recommendation does not mitigate — the vertical-fit miss, the capture-by-the-outsourced-GC-firm risk, the panel-management-maturity gap, the fractional-GC availability variance.
5. **State the decision rule.** The named ask of the CEO — adopt the recommendation, counter with a specific alternative, or defer by a defined number of weeks pending named data. The board ask if any (chapter 08 reserves board approval for the GC title and the officer-level compensation band; defer the officer-and-compensation mechanics to [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) and [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) respectively).

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 08 is the primary reference. The seven parts map directly to its sections: Part A to the stage-progression discussion (outsourced-GC → fractional-GC → first in-house hire → full function); Part B to the buy-vs-build-by-specialty section; Part C to the first-in-house-hire profile and day-one scope; Parts D and E to the outside-counsel-management-discipline section; Part F to the fractional-GC section; Part G is the memo deliverable the chapter's framework drives.
- The CEO's question — "does the hire reduce total legal cost or just add to it?" — is the forcing function on Part A. Chapter 08's crossover-arithmetic discussion is the direct answer frame: run-rate steady-state outside spend vs. fully-loaded cost of the hire, with the qualitative overlay on how much of the outside spend is displace-able vs. structurally-outside.
- The healthtech vertical is the forcing function on Part B's healthcare-regulatory row and on Part C's vertical-fit requirement. Chapter 08 is explicit that the "generalist GC who will learn HIPAA on the job" is a failure mode; do not author Part C in a way that reproduces the pattern.
- The three forcing functions in the problem statement are the forcing function on Part F's bridge decision. The hospital-system renewal, the acquihire, and the DPA refresh are each discrete loads that the current firm will carry at Big Law rates unless an alternative capacity exists.
- The outsourced-GC-firm-captures-the-in-house-function failure mode (chapter 08) is the forcing function on Part C's sourcing posture. If Corvellis Health hires its Head of Legal out of the current firm, the memo in Part G names the conflict-of-interest disclosure discipline and the first-six-months rate-card re-negotiation requirement explicitly.
- Any dollar figure not reproduced in chapter 08 is marked with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — specific hourly rates by title (partner, senior associate, mid-level associate, junior associate, paralegal), specific fractional-GC monthly retainers, specific Head of Legal fully-loaded compensation at Series-B, specific CLM platform pricing, specific e-billing platform pricing, specific negotiated panel-firm rate discounts. The chapter cites market ranges; Corvellis Health's specific numbers are not knowable without current benchmark data.
- Do not invent real firm names in the panel composition. Reference specialties — "patent-prosecution boutique," "labour-and-employment firm," "health-system-regulatory firm," "specialist privacy boutique" — rather than brand names. Chapter 08's named-firm examples are illustrative of market patterns, not a prescriptive panel.
- Reference [chapter 07](../07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md) for the CLM / matter-management / e-billing tool stack and [chapter 09](../09-ownership-boundary-map.md) for the mod-109-vs-mod-111 ownership boundary (which legal-function work routes to mod-111's board / corporate-secretary scope rather than to the Head of Legal's day-one scope). Reference [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) wherever a compensation band is named and defer the band mechanic. Reference [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) wherever the privacy specialty is named and defer the DPA / HIPAA / state-consumer-privacy substantive framework. Reference [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) wherever the board / corporate-secretary / officer-duty mechanic is named.
- Nothing in this exercise is a legal determination. The ADA interactive-process standard, the HIPAA-business-associate-agreement substantive framework, the ITAR / EAR controls, the SEC-registration readiness mechanic, and the state-DOH licensure substantive standard are deferred to the sibling modules named above. Flag the deferrals; do not author the substantive mechanics.
