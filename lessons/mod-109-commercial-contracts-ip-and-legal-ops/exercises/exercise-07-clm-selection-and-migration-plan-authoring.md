# Exercise 07 — CLM selection and migration plan authoring

> Estimated time: **~8 hours** · Related chapter: [07 — The CLM stack and the legal-ops graduation](../07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md)

## Problem statement

Fernridge Devworks is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a developer-tooling product (CI/CD orchestration plus a hosted build-cache) into mid-market and enterprise software engineering organisations. The corporation is 155 FTE, closed a $55M Series B sixteen months ago, and books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions. Contract volume now runs at approximately **55 new contracts per month**, split roughly: 20 sell-side customer MSAs and order forms, 25 buy-side vendor subscriptions and SOWs, 5 contractor / consultant agreements, and 5 inbound and outbound NDAs. The current stack is the pre-CLM baseline that chapter 07 describes: executed contracts live across **two overlapping Google Drive folders** (`Legal/Contracts` and `Operations/Contracts` — nobody on the leadership team can authoritatively say which is the system of record); e-signature runs through a single DocuSign seat under the general counsel's login; the general counsel's paralegal maintains a **Smartsheet** labelled "Fernridge — critical renewals" that is **six weeks out of date**; and the Salesforce opportunity record carries a Drive hyperlink for sell-side deals only — buy-side vendor contracts have no system-of-record cross-reference at all. The legal function is one headcount — the general counsel — plus two outside firms (one commercial, one employment-and-IP) on retainer.

Three incidents in the last six months have forced the issue onto the board agenda. **(i)** A **$140k ARR mid-market customer auto-renewed on stale pricing** because the 60-day non-renewal notice window was missed; the Smartsheet entry had been marked `renewal: TBD` by a paralegal who left the corporation four months before the trigger date and nobody re-owned the row. **(ii)** An **MFN obligation on a Series-B-era strategic customer** surfaced only after the sales team priced a subsequent enterprise deal below the MFN floor; the MFN clause had never been logged as a post-signature obligation anywhere other than the body of the executed PDF. **(iii)** A **Series-C diligence team requested a complete contract manifest** three weeks ago as part of a preliminary diligence pass; the paralegal spent **17 calendar days** producing the manifest by hand, and flagged **23 counterparty-signed executed copies that the corporation has never recovered** — some of them customer MSAs where only the Fernridge-countersigned draft is on file. The lead diligence partner has already noted the operational finding in writing.

The board has sent a note to the general counsel asking her to recommend, within **twelve weeks**, whether Fernridge should "**buy a CLM or make its first legal-ops hire first**" — framed in exactly those terms. If a CLM is recommended, the target go-live window is **six months** from contract signature. The general counsel has to walk into the board's governance subcommittee with (a) the CLM-vs-first-legal-ops-hire decision memo, (b) if CLM, the vendor-selection write-up and a specific pick, (c) the migration plan including the 23-missing-counterparty-signature recovery programme, (d) the obligations-tracker configuration, (e) the three monthly operating metrics the legal-ops function will publish, and (f) the twelve-month post-go-live roadmap. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — CLM-vs-first-legal-ops-hire decision memo

Draft `decision-memo.md` — a one-to-two-page memo the general counsel will circulate to the board governance subcommittee ahead of the twelve-week decision point. Apply the chapter-07 framework to Fernridge's specific profile. Cover:

1. **The two investments compared on what they buy.** What a mid-market CLM licence plus a six-month implementation programme buys at Fernridge's current stage versus what a first dedicated legal-ops hire (title, scope, reporting line) buys. Reference chapter 07's "when CLM-first dominates" and "when legal-hire-first dominates" named conditions explicitly against Fernridge's three forcing incidents.
2. **The hybrid pattern.** Chapter 07's common conclusion is "both, sequenced." Author the specific sequencing Fernridge would run if the budget supported both — which quarter the CLM lands, which quarter the hire lands, and the named interlock between them.
3. **A specific recommendation.** Do not duck the choice. The memo recommends one of the three paths — CLM-first, legal-ops-hire-first, or both-sequenced — for Fernridge specifically. Carry the chapter-07 trade-off paragraph: what the recommended path explicitly accepts as cost and what it buys the corporation that the alternatives do not.
4. **The decision rule.** The specific decision the memo asks the board subcommittee to adopt — accept the recommendation, counter with a named alternative, or defer by a defined number of weeks pending named additional data.

### Part B — CLM-vendor selection

Draft `clm-vendor-selection.md`. Run the chapter-07 sub-capability matrix (template management, clause library, redline tracking, sign workflow, central storage / repository, obligations tracker, reporting and analytics, integrations) against **at least four named vendors** selected from the chapter 07 and `resources.md` list — the eligible set is **Ironclad, LinkSquares, DocuSign CLM, Concord, Agiloft, Juro, Icertis, SpotDraft**. For each selected vendor cover:

1. **Per-sub-capability scoring.** A short qualitative judgement (strong / adequate / weak) for each of the eight sub-capabilities above, with a one-line rationale per sub-capability. Do not fabricate feature coverage you have not verified; propagate `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` where current-release capability is uncertain.
2. **E-signature coupling.** Native e-signature vs. integration with DocuSign / Adobe Sign / Dropbox Sign, and the operational implication for Fernridge's existing DocuSign seat.
3. **Integration posture.** Salesforce (sell-side anchor), HRIS / IdP (user provisioning — Fernridge's HRIS deferral is owned by [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/)), Slack, Google Workspace, and data-warehouse export. Native connector vs. API-plus-glue; which integration posture the vendor's architecture favours.
4. **AI-feature coverage.** Metadata-extraction, AI-assisted redline review, clause-anomaly detection — treat vendor-marketing claims as marketing and name the POC evidence the corporation would require to validate each.
5. **Pricing posture.** Order-of-magnitude band (mid-market vs. enterprise-tier) and the implication for Fernridge's Series-B budget envelope. **Defer specific annual-licence dollar figures and specific per-seat / per-contract pricing tiers to `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`** — vendor pricing moves constantly and is heavily negotiated.
6. **A specific pick with trade-offs stated.** Name the vendor the memo recommends, the runner-up, and the chapter-07 trade-off paragraph: what the pick explicitly accepts as cost (feature gaps, integration gaps, licence tier) and what it buys that the runner-up does not. Reference the chapter-07 failure mode "buying an enterprise CLM at Series-A and never fully implementing it" and defend the pick against it.

### Part C — Migration plan

Draft `migration-plan.md`. The six-month implementation programme from signature to steady-state. Cover:

1. **Pre-migration audit.** The contract-inventory exercise against the two Google Drive folders and the Smartsheet — the authoritative-source reconciliation, the counterparty-dedup pass, the executed-vs-draft disambiguation. The **metadata-field schema design** for the new CLM — propose the schema explicitly, extending the chapter-07 minimum (counterparty legal name, contract type, effective date, initial term end, renewal mechanism and notice deadline, contract value, tier, primary business owner, security approver, key obligations). The **chain-of-custody discipline** for executed PDFs — hash-based integrity, originating-DocuSign-envelope-ID preservation, source-folder provenance.
2. **The migration cutover.** Parallel-run vs. big-bang; phased-by-document-type (sell-side first, then buy-side, then NDAs and contractors; or an alternative order the memo defends). The named cutover gates — when the Drive folder goes read-only, when the Smartsheet is deprecated, when the general counsel's personal DocuSign seat is retired in favour of the CLM-mediated signature workflow.
3. **The 23-missing-counterparty-signature recovery programme.** The named workflow for recovering the 23 missing counterparty-signed executed copies — counterparty-outreach cadence, who owns the outreach by contract type (account executive for customer, procurement for vendor, general counsel for strategic), the escalation path when a counterparty cannot or will not re-sign, the disposition for contracts where recovery fails (re-execution on current paper vs. documented best-efforts-recovery file memo). This recovery programme is a specific chapter-07 "diligence-manifest gap" symptom and the Series-C diligence team has already named it as an operational finding.
4. **Data-cleanup posture on incomplete metadata.** The discipline for contracts where effective date, counterparty legal name, or renewal notice window cannot be extracted cleanly from the historical PDF — the named human-review pass, the "best-known-value plus flagged-for-review" convention, the sunset date by which flagged-for-review records must be resolved. Reference chapter 07's warning that AI-assisted extraction hallucinates dates and notice windows in ways unsafe to rely on for obligation triggers.
5. **Deprecation of Google Drive and the Smartsheet.** The named date by which the two Drive folders become read-only, the archival disposition, the retention posture (legal-hold-aware), and the Smartsheet sunset. Defer the records-retention schedule to [mod-112](../../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/) where document-retention policy sits.
6. **Training programme.** Scoped curriculum for the three transacting personas — sales (how to initiate a contract and route for approval), procurement (how to request a vendor onboarding and route for approval; see [chapter 03](../03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md) for the vendor-onboarding workflow this plugs into), and legal (how to redline inside the CLM and record negotiation history). Stage-gated activation — no persona transacts in the CLM until their training module is complete.
7. **Six-month timeline with phase gates.** A month-by-month plan with the chapter-07 30 / 60 / 90-day success-criteria discipline extended to Day 180. Each phase names a published gate the general counsel will defend to the board subcommittee.

### Part D — Obligations-tracker configuration

Draft `obligations-tracker-config.md`. The post-signature obligations the CLM will track end-to-end. Cover:

1. **The obligation catalogue.** The named obligation types the tracker will carry: **auto-renewal non-renewal notice windows** (the direct corrective for incident (i)), **MFN monitors** (the direct corrective for incident (ii)), **SLA-credit obligations**, **insurance-certificate refreshes** (counterparty-held and Fernridge-held), **SOC 2 Type II refresh deliveries**, **DPA sub-processor-change notifications**, **co-marketing permissions and logo-use restrictions**, and any bespoke commitments extracted on a per-contract basis. For each named obligation type, author a short definition and the named trigger-point convention (date-based, event-based, lookback-window-based).
2. **The escalation workflow.** The named cadence of reminders ahead of the obligation window — the T-minus-N-days convention (e.g., T-120 / T-90 / T-60 / T-30 for a 60-day non-renewal notice; adopt or depart from the chapter-07 pattern explicitly). The escalation path when a reminder is not actioned — route to named owner, then to named owner's manager, then to the general counsel. The published posture that an un-actioned reminder past the final escalation window is a performance matter, not a tooling matter — chapter 07's "tracker populated but no operator responsible" failure mode is the thing to design against.
3. **Owner-assignment convention.** The named discipline that every obligation record carries a human owner at commit, not a distribution list and not a departed-employee's address. The reassignment mechanic when an owner departs (route to the owner's manager by HRIS lookup; see [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) for the HRIS-side offboarding workflow this reads from). The named audit cadence to catch orphaned obligations.

### Part E — Three operating metrics

Draft `operating-metrics.md`. Pick **three** operating metrics the CLM function will publish monthly from the chapter-07 candidate set (contract cycle time median and 90th percentile, redline count per deal, fallback-position hit rate per clause, escalation rate / standard-vs-non-standard-vs-bespoke mix, post-signature-obligations-met rate, cost per deal) or defensibly adjacent additions. For each selected metric, specify:

1. **Named numerator and denominator.** The precise definition — not "cycle time" but "median elapsed business days from Salesforce stage *contract-generation* to CLM state *fully-executed*, computed over the trailing-30-day cohort of sell-side deals." Carry the business-day convention and the sell-side vs. buy-side split.
2. **Publication cadence.** Monthly on a named day; the surface the metric lands on (the board governance subcommittee's monthly pack, the exec weekly dashboard, an internal legal-ops dashboard).
3. **Published target.** The specific target the general counsel will defend — not a range. For any benchmark number the general counsel wants to anchor against (CLOC community benchmarks, ACC Chief Legal Officer survey ranges, vendor-published medians), propagate `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — those benchmarks move annually and the memo must not cite stale figures.
4. **The chapter-07 defence of the choice.** One paragraph per metric naming why the metric is worth publishing for Fernridge specifically — against which of the three forcing incidents, against which chapter-07 failure mode, against which capability the twelve-month roadmap will build.

### Part F — Twelve-month post-go-live roadmap

Draft `twelve-month-roadmap.md`. The capabilities the legal-ops function will add in implementation cycles 2 through 4 (months 7–18 relative to signature; months 1–12 post-go-live). Cover:

1. **Cycle-2 capabilities (months 1–3 post-go-live).** Clause-library automation on the top-ten most-redlined clauses, the Salesforce-opportunity-to-CLM-record join fully live for buy-side as well as sell-side, retirement of the Fernridge legal shared inbox in favour of the CLM intake queue.
2. **Cycle-3 capabilities (months 4–6 post-go-live).** AI-assisted redline triage with a named human-in-the-loop discipline (chapter 07's warning on hallucinated extraction and the equivalent for AI-generated redline suggestions applies), counterparty-redline-pattern analytics (which clauses Fernridge concedes most often; feeds the chapter-02 playbook revision cycle — see [chapter 02](../02-contract-playbook-and-fallback-position-matrix.md)), deal-desk SLA dashboarding.
3. **Cycle-4 capabilities (months 7–12 post-go-live).** NDA / clickwrap automation, buy-side vendor-onboarding integration against the [chapter 03](../03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md) workflow, data-warehouse export live for cost-per-deal by segment and fallback-hit-rate by ARR band.
4. **The headcount pattern that unlocks them.** Each cycle names the staffing addition the capability depends on (a contract paralegal in cycle 2, a dedicated deal-desk analyst in cycle 3, a legal-tech engineer or shared RevOps resource in cycle 4). **Defer the substantive headcount-sequencing framework — the in-house-vs-outside-counsel decision, the GC-vs-deputy-GC profile, the outside-counsel-panel reshape — to [chapter 08](../08-in-house-vs-outside-counsel-decision-framework.md).** Reference the deferral explicitly; do not re-author chapter 08's framework.
5. **Review cadence.** The quarterly roadmap review (named stakeholders — general counsel, CFO delegate, CRO delegate, head of security), the trigger conditions that force an off-cycle review (a missed obligation above a defined ARR threshold, a vendor-licence renegotiation, a sibling chapter 02 playbook revision that invalidates clause-library content).
6. **Sunset conditions on legacy tooling.** The named milestones at which the two Google Drive folders are archived to cold storage, the Smartsheet is permanently retired, and the general counsel's personal DocuSign seat is deactivated. Each sunset carries a named date and a named owner.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 07 is the primary reference. Part A maps to the "CLM-vs-in-house-legal-hire decision at Series-B" section; Part B to the "CLM tool-selection matrix" plus the sub-capability anatomy; Part C to the "CLM-onboarding programme" (template migration, clause-library import, historical-contract import, integration configuration, user training, 30 / 60 / 90-day success criteria); Part D to the obligations-tracker sub-capability plus the "tracker populated but no operator responsible" failure mode; Part E to the reporting-and-analytics sub-capability plus the chapter-02 metrics interface; Part F to the "legal-ops function build-out over stages" progression with the chapter-08 handoff.
- Do not pre-decide the Part A recommendation. Chapter 07 explicitly presents three paths — CLM-first, legal-hire-first, both-sequenced — and the exercise wants the memo to defend one against Fernridge's specific profile, not to default to the common conclusion.
- Do not invent CLM-vendor pricing. The chapter-07 pricing-posture notes already carry `<!-- needs-research -->` on specific figures; propagate the discipline. The eligible vendor list for Part B is **Ironclad, LinkSquares, DocuSign CLM, Concord, Agiloft, Juro, Icertis, SpotDraft** (per chapter 07 and `resources.md`); do not add real peer vendors not on that list.
- Do not invent CLOC community benchmarks or ACC Chief Legal Officer survey figures for Part E targets. Any specific benchmark number the general counsel wants to anchor against gets `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — those benchmarks move annually and the general counsel will not want a stale citation in front of the board subcommittee.
- Chapter 07's named failure modes are the thing to design against — buying enterprise CLM at Series-A and under-implementing it; not migrating historical contracts; not integrating with Salesforce; not enforcing delegated-authority-matrix signature routing (see [chapter 03](../03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md) for the matrix the CLM must enforce); obligations tracker populated with no responsible operator; CLM-as-deal-desk-substitute. The migration plan and the obligations-tracker configuration should name the failure modes they defend against.
- The three forcing incidents (auto-renewal miss, MFN breach, missing counterparty signatures) each map to a specific sub-capability the CLM has to deliver. Part B's vendor selection should weight the sub-capabilities on the forcing incidents, not on vendor marketing.
- The 23-missing-counterparty-signature recovery programme in Part C is the specific operational finding the Series-C diligence team has flagged. The recovery programme is scope-of-work, not merely a note that counterparty signatures should be recovered.
- Chapter 08 owns the substantive headcount sequencing. Part F references it; it does not re-author it.
- No real peer-company names. Chapter 07's concrete example uses "a Series-B B2B-SaaS corporation" — follow the pattern.
- Nothing in this exercise is a legal determination. The records-retention schedule, the privacy / DPA sub-processor-notification mechanics, and the delegated-authority matrix all live in sibling chapters and sibling modules — reference them where the obligations tracker reads from or writes to those surfaces; do not re-author the mechanics.
