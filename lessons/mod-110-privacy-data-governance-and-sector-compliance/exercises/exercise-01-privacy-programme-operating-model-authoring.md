# Exercise 01 — Privacy-programme operating-model authoring

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — The GDPR compliance programme](../01-gdpr-compliance-programme.md) (with programme-operating-model substance drawn from chapters [02](../02-ccpa-cpra-compliance-programme.md), [08](../08-vendor-privacy-risk-assessment-programme.md), and the module [README](../README.md))

## Problem statement

Lindenvale Health Platforms Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a clinical-documentation assistant into mid-market US hospital systems and specialty-clinic networks. The product ingests structured EHR data over FHIR, ambient-room audio captured on a clinician's badge microphone, and dictation input; it produces draft encounter notes, draft billing codes, and a draft patient-visit summary that the clinician reviews before the record is signed into the customer's EHR. The corporation is 165 FTE, closed a $62M Series B seven months ago led by a healthcare-focused growth fund, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, is actively landing two UK NHS Trust pilots and one Dublin-headquartered private-hospital customer, and will touch Protected Health Information on behalf of US HIPAA Covered Entities as a Business Associate on every live US deployment.

The privacy footprint is already heavy. The corporation processes Article 9 special-category data in the clinical-documentation flow, Article 6(1)(b) contract-basis data for customer-organisation administrators, Article 6(1)(f) legitimate-interests data for product-telemetry and abuse-detection, and employee personal data for 165 FTE across a US-EU footprint (Dublin engineering office opened five months ago, 14 FTE). The data-residency posture is split: the US tenant processes in AWS us-east-1; the EU tenant is being stood up in AWS eu-west-1 to meet the Dublin and UK customers' data-residency asks. The corporation uses two foundation-model providers for the summarisation and coding layers, consumed API-only, both configured zero-retention per the vendor's enterprise posture (currently unverified in writing by the general counsel). The compliance-automation tooling budget is approved but the vendor has not been selected.

Three operating realities have forced the privacy-programme-operating-model question onto the general counsel's agenda. **(i)** The Dublin NHS Trust pilot contract routes a 41-item privacy addendum through procurement that asks, on page 3, "please identify your Data Protection Officer under GDPR Article 37 and provide their contact details" — Lindenvale has no DPO and no formal determination on whether Article 37(1)(b) or (c) is triggered by its processing. **(ii)** The largest US customer, a 14-hospital system, routed a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement two weeks ago and attached its own vendor-privacy questionnaire asking for the Record of Processing Activities, the data-flow map, and the data-classification matrix — none of which currently exist as living artefacts. **(iii)** The product team shipped an "ambient-mode" audio-capture feature to the Series-B cohort of design-partner customers six weeks ago without a DPIA; legal was not consulted; the EU-hosted design partner (a Munich-headquartered private clinic) has now asked for the Article 35 DPIA and the Article 36 prior-consultation disposition.

The general counsel has eight weeks to walk into the board governance subcommittee with a privacy-programme operating model the corporation will actually run. She owns the end-to-end package; the CTO owns the data-flow map and the production-systems inventory; the head of product owns the DPIA trigger list wired into the product-development lifecycle; the head of people owns the HRIS-side employee-data inventory feed; the head of security owns the TOMs and the control-framework mapping handed to [chapter 05](../05-soc-2-audit-readiness-programme.md). Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Role architecture and DPO determination memo

Author `role-architecture-and-dpo-determination.md` — the memo the general counsel will circulate to the board governance subcommittee. Cover:

1. **The Article 37 DPO determination.** Run the Article 37(1)(a) / (b) / (c) analysis against Lindenvale's processing. The relevant triggers for a US-headquartered healthtech with EU operations are Article 37(1)(b) (core activities require regular and systematic monitoring of data subjects on a large scale) and Article 37(1)(c) (core activities consist of large-scale processing of special categories of data under Article 9). Name the determination explicitly; do not duck. Reference the Article 29 Working Party Guidelines on DPOs (WP243 rev.01, endorsed by the EDPB) and the specific tests those guidelines set out. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current endorsement status of WP243 rev.01 and any EDPB superseding guidance before citing it as the controlling interpretive instrument. -->
2. **Role architecture.** Author the named roles at Lindenvale's current stage and headcount: **Data Protection Officer** (internal full-time or outsourced external DPO-as-a-service — defend the pick against the Article 38(3) independence requirement and the Article 38(6) no-conflicting-tasks requirement); **privacy counsel** (reporting line to the general counsel; scope boundary vs. the DPO); **privacy engineering** (initial headcount, reporting line to the CTO or to the head of security, scope boundary vs. the security-engineering function owned by `security-learning`); **privacy programme office** (deferred to Series-C / growth per chapter 01, but name the trigger).
3. **UK ICO representative under Article 27 and the EU Article 27 representative.** The analysis for a US-headquartered controller with EU data subjects — whether the Dublin establishment triggers the Article 3(1) establishment basis (removing the Article 27 obligation for EU data subjects) and whether the UK GDPR Article 27 representative is still required. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current ICO guidance on when a UK establishment removes the Article 27 representative requirement and the operational implications for a dual-jurisdiction footprint. -->
4. **Reporting lines.** The DPO's independent-reporting line to the highest management level under Article 38(3); the privacy-metrics reporting cadence to the board governance subcommittee or the AI-risk sub-committee stood up in [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md); the privilege posture when the DPO's Article 38(1) advice intersects with general-counsel legal advice (the DPO's advice is not privileged on its face and the practitioner discipline is to route privileged legal advice through the general counsel on a parallel track).

### Part B — Data-classification matrix

Author `data-classification-matrix.md`. The classification matrix is the single reference every downstream control reads from (encryption posture, access-control posture, retention-schedule tier, DSAR fulfilment routing, data-residency assignment). Cover:

1. **The classification tiers.** The named tiers (public / internal / confidential / restricted / regulated) and the named operational treatment per tier — encryption-at-rest and in-transit requirement, key-management posture, access-control pattern (role-based, attribute-based, break-glass), logging and monitoring posture, retention default. Reference [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md) for the retention-schedule mapping and defer the retention-period determination there rather than re-authoring it.
2. **The personal-data category mapping.** The GDPR Article 4(1) personal-data categories and the Article 9(1) special-category data, mapped to the classification tiers. Lindenvale's clinical documentation data, ambient-audio data, and billing-code output all land in Article 9 health data; name the control-posture delta between Article 9 data and other personal data.
3. **The HIPAA PHI mapping.** The 18 HIPAA identifiers at 45 C.F.R. § 164.514(b)(2) and the Covered-Entity vs. Business-Associate boundary; name which Lindenvale processing activities implicate PHI and the resulting Safeguard-Rule obligations that run through [chapter 04](../04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md).
4. **The matrix in operational form.** A table with one row per data category, columns for (classification tier, GDPR characterisation, HIPAA characterisation, lawful basis, retention tier, data-residency assignment, approved processors, approved sub-processors, approved regions). The matrix is the single reference the data-flow map reads from in Part D.
5. **Change-control discipline.** The named gate for reclassification — who proposes, who approves (the DPO, the general counsel, the head of security), the audit trail, the downstream-system re-propagation path when a category moves tiers.

### Part C — Record of Processing Activities

Author `record-of-processing-activities.md`. The RoPA is the Article 30(1) / (2) record the supervisory authority reads first. Lindenvale operates as **processor** on customer-organisation PHI / personal data under the Article 28 processor-obligation and as **controller** on employee data, prospect / marketing data, product-telemetry data, abuse-detection data, and the corporation's own business-administration data. Cover:

1. **The Article 30(1) controller RoPA.** Cover every material processing activity under the controller role at Lindenvale's current stage. Required content per Article 30(1): name and contact of the controller, DPO, and Article 27 representative; processing purpose; categories of data subjects and personal data; categories of recipients including third countries; transfers to third countries with the identified transfer mechanism; envisaged erasure time limits (tied to [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md)); a general description of the technical and organisational security measures.
2. **The Article 30(2) processor RoPA.** The shorter processor-side record for every customer tenant: name and contact of each controller for whom Lindenvale processes, the DPO contact, categories of processing per controller, transfers of personal data to a third country with the identified mechanism, a general description of TOMs.
3. **The minimum ten processing activities the controller RoPA must cover at Lindenvale's stage.** At least: (i) customer-organisation administrator data for account provisioning and billing; (ii) prospect / marketing data; (iii) product-telemetry data (first-party analytics); (iv) abuse-detection and fraud-prevention data; (v) support-ticket data; (vi) employee data (HRIS feed from [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/)); (vii) candidate data (ATS feed); (viii) contractor data; (ix) referrer and partner data; (x) corporate-record data (board minutes, equity cap table from [mod-101](../../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) and [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)).
4. **The data-inventory discipline that keeps the RoPA current.** The named operating cadence — the quarterly RoPA review, the product-launch gate that blocks on a RoPA entry before a new processing activity goes live, the DPO sign-off on material-change entries, the tooling (OneTrust / Transcend / Osano / a documented home-grown approach) with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current vendor feature parity and pricing.

### Part D — Data-flow map and sub-processor chain

Author `data-flow-map.md`. The data-flow map is the operational artefact that keeps the RoPA honest — the RoPA tells the regulator what the controller believes; the data-flow map tells the DPO what is actually happening. Cover:

1. **The system-level data-flow map.** For each processing activity in Part C, name the producing system (the source), the consuming systems (destinations), the data-category transiting the link, the lawful basis for the transit, the encryption posture, the retention disposition at the destination, and the region. The clinical-documentation flow specifically names: badge-microphone capture → voice-API ingress → transcription-model inference (foundation-model provider A) → summarisation-model inference (foundation-model provider B) → draft-note storage → customer-EHR egress over FHIR. Name every system in that chain and the region for each hop.
2. **The sub-processor chain.** For each third party processing personal data on Lindenvale's behalf, record the processor name, the processing purpose, the data category, the primary processing region, the Annex-II TOMs posture, and the DPA signature status. Reference the [chapter 08](../08-vendor-privacy-risk-assessment-programme.md) vendor-privacy-risk-assessment programme as the operating discipline the sub-processor list reads from; do not re-author the vendor-tiering mechanic.
3. **The cross-border transfer map.** For each cross-border flow, record the exporter, the importer, the transit mechanism (Article 45 adequacy, Article 46 SCC module, UK IDTA or Addendum, Swiss FDPIC addendum, DPF for US importer), and the Transfer Impact Assessment status. Flag the Frankfurt-to-US-East-1 and Dublin-to-US-East-1 flows explicitly; the TIA authoring itself is the subject of Exercise 02 and is cross-referenced here, not re-executed.
4. **The sub-processor change-notification mechanic.** The customer-facing public sub-processor list that reads from the data-flow map, the 30-day advance-notice window Lindenvale commits to customers, the inbound-outbound notice-ceiling discipline from [mod-109 chapter 06](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/06-ai-vendor-contracts-and-ai-dpa-pattern.md). The CLM-side mechanic (versioning, acknowledgement tracking, distribution) is deferred to [mod-109 chapter 07](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md).

### Part E — Privacy-by-design gate and DPIA trigger list

Author `pbd-gate-and-dpia-trigger-list.md`. Article 25 privacy-by-design and privacy-by-default is wired into the product-development lifecycle at design, not bolted on at launch. Article 35 DPIAs run alongside the design work, not after it. Cover:

1. **The product-lifecycle PbD gate.** Name the stages (discovery → design → implementation → launch → operation) and the privacy-engineering review each stage carries. The design-stage gate produces a data-flow diagram and a lawful-basis declaration before engineering commits; the implementation-stage gate verifies the Annex-II TOMs are configured; the launch-stage gate closes on the RoPA entry and, where triggered, the DPIA. A feature that cannot name its lawful basis and its data-category set at design cannot clear the design gate.
2. **The Article 35 DPIA trigger list.** The named triggers Lindenvale will apply, derived from Article 35(3) and from the ICO / CNIL / BfDI Article 35(4) lists. At minimum: any new AI-in-the-loop feature producing decisions or recommendations affecting data subjects, any new large-scale collection or combination of personal data, any new processing of Article 9 special-category data, any new systematic-monitoring feature (employee productivity, customer end-user behavioural tracking, in-product audio or video capture), any new transfer to a country without an Article 45 adequacy decision. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current ICO, CNIL, and BfDI Article 35(4) lists and incorporate any triggers specific to healthcare processing before treating this list as closed. -->
3. **The ambient-mode remediation DPIA.** The ambient-audio feature shipped six weeks ago without a DPIA is the forcing-function case. Author the Article 35 DPIA for that feature as a worked example — processing description, necessity and proportionality, risk assessment, Annex-II TOMs posture, residual risk, Article 36 prior-consultation disposition. If residual risk is still "high" after safeguards, name the Article 36 prior-consultation posture explicitly (consult the supervisory authority before continued processing or document why the residual risk is not "high" after safeguards).
4. **The DPIA register.** The living inventory of DPIAs, their status, their next-review date, and the owner. The DPIA register reports into the board governance subcommittee on a cadence named in Part F.
5. **The Article 22 automated-decision-making analysis.** For any processing that produces "a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects … or similarly significantly affects" the data subject, the Article 22(2) exceptions and the Article 22(3) safeguards obligation. The clinical-documentation draft note that the clinician reviews before signing is not solely-automated; the ambient-audio abuse-detection feature that auto-suspends an end-user account would be. Defer the full Article 22 analysis to Exercise 08.

### Part F — Privacy-metrics reporting cadence

Author `privacy-metrics-reporting-cadence.md`. The board governance subcommittee and the AI-risk sub-committee stood up in [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md) need a quarterly pack that is defensible against a Caremark compliance-system claim. Cover:

1. **The quarterly privacy-pack.** The named metrics the DPO will publish per quarter: the number of material processing activities on the RoPA and the change log; the number of DPIAs completed, in progress, and the pipeline; the DSAR volume and the on-time-closure rate against the Article 12(3) one-month clock; the breach-and-near-miss volume and the on-time-notification rate against the Article 33 72-hour clock, the HIPAA 60-day clock, and the SEC Item 1.05 four-business-day clock (the breach playbook itself is Exercise 05); the open regulator-inquiry log; the open data-protection-authority correspondence log; the TIA inventory and the next-review schedule.
2. **The metric definitions.** Each metric specifies the named numerator and denominator (e.g., "DSAR on-time-closure rate" is "DSARs closed within the Article 12(3) one-month clock as a share of DSARs received in the trailing quarter, computed on calendar-day elapsed from Article 12(3) 'receipt' as the DPO defines receipt in the intake SOP"); the publication cadence (quarterly on a named date); the surface (the governance subcommittee's quarterly pack, the exec weekly scorecard, the privacy office's internal dashboard).
3. **The published targets.** For every metric the DPO will anchor against, name the target the DPO will defend — not a range. Any benchmark figure the DPO wants to anchor against (IAPP survey ranges, DPO-circle benchmarks, published supervisory-authority enforcement patterns) carries a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker because the benchmark landscape moves and the pack must not cite stale figures.
4. **The escalation discipline.** The named path when a metric breaches its target — the DPO briefs the general counsel, the general counsel briefs the governance subcommittee chair, the governance subcommittee convenes an off-cycle review, and the CEO is briefed where the metric breach has board-reporting implications ([mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-fiduciary-duties-and-officer-liability.md) names the Caremark duty the subcommittee is designed against).
