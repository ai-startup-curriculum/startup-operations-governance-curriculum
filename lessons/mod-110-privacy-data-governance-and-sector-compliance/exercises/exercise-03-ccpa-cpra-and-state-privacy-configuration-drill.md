# Exercise 03 — CCPA / CPRA and state-privacy configuration drill

> Estimated time: **~9 hours** · Related chapters: [02 — The CCPA / CPRA compliance programme](../02-ccpa-cpra-compliance-programme.md) and [03 — The US state privacy landscape](../03-us-state-privacy-landscape-and-rolling-registration.md)

## Problem statement

Harborpoint Commerce Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a direct-to-consumer subscription-box product (curated home-goods, $29 / month, with a free-shipping-and-returns posture) across the 50 US states. Harborpoint is 210 FTE, closed a $48M Series B eighteen months ago, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, runs ~680,000 active monthly subscribers, and collects personal information well above every known state-privacy threshold. The corporation does not have an EU footprint and the GDPR analysis is out of scope for this exercise; the focus is the US state-privacy patchwork.

The current privacy posture is the pre-programme baseline the module [README](../README.md) describes. The privacy policy is a single long-form page authored two years ago by outside counsel and never materially refreshed; there is no "Notice at Collection" at the data-collection surface; the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link exists but routes to a form that only captures email-based opt-out and does not honour the Global Privacy Control signal in-browser; the ad-platform integrations (Meta Pixel, Google Ads, TikTok Pixel, Reddit Pixel, LinkedIn Insight Tag) are configured on every page without a consent gate; the right-to-delete and right-to-know requests are fielded ad-hoc by the customer-support team with no SLA tracking; the sensitive-personal-information "right to limit" at Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.121 is not implemented; and the California CPPA has indicated in its recent enforcement advisories that uniform-opt-out-signal non-honour and missing Notice-at-Collection are current enforcement priorities. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CPPA enforcement-priority list and any 2025–2026 advisory or settlement that bears on the configuration before treating a given priority as controlling. -->

Three forcing functions have landed. **(i)** The California Attorney General's office sent Harborpoint an informal inquiry letter three weeks ago asking Harborpoint to describe its Global Privacy Control response, its Notice-at-Collection posture, and its service-provider / contractor characterisation of its ad-platform integrations. The inquiry is informal and does not carry a right-to-cure sunset, but the general counsel treats it as a pre-enforcement signal. **(ii)** The chief marketing officer's team wants to launch a new "personalised offer" feature that combines the subscriber's purchase history with inferred-demographic signals from a data-broker partner (Partner Y, US-only) to produce a customised offer per subscriber. Legal was consulted after the engineering work started; the "sale and share" analysis under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ad) / (ah) has not been run; the "sensitive personal information" analysis has not been run against the inferred-demographic inputs. **(iii)** The corporation's board governance subcommittee has asked the general counsel for a single configuration that covers every enacted comprehensive state privacy statute, not a per-state engineering backlog — the subcommittee's framing is "one controller-side switch, not 20 variants."

The general counsel has seven weeks to walk the subcommittee through the full configuration, defend the configuration against the California AG inquiry, and sign off the personalised-offer feature with a defensible lawful-basis posture for the state regime it ships into. The general counsel owns the end-to-end package; the CTO owns the Global Privacy Control signal handler and the universal-opt-out mechanics; the head of marketing owns the Notice-at-Collection design and the cookie / tracker configuration; the head of engineering owns the sensitive-PI limit-use flow; outside privacy counsel will review before the AG response goes out. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — State-by-state enacted-statute map and threshold-applicability analysis

Author `enacted-statute-map.md`. The analysis is the starting point every downstream configuration reads from. Cover:

1. **The enacted comprehensive-state-privacy-statute list.** List every state that has enacted a comprehensive consumer privacy statute, with the statutory citation and the effective date. The eligible set at time of writing is: California (CCPA / CPRA, Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq.), Colorado (CPA), Virginia (VCDPA), Connecticut (CTDPA), Utah (UCPA), Oregon (OCPA), Texas (TDPSA), Montana (MCDPA), Tennessee (TIPA), Delaware (DPDPA), Iowa (ICDPA), New Hampshire (NHDPA), New Jersey (NJDPA), Minnesota (MCDPA), Maryland (MODPA), Indiana (INCDPA), Kentucky (KCDPA), Nebraska (NDPA), and Rhode Island. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current list of enacted comprehensive-state-privacy statutes and effective dates before quoting as canonical; the list is in motion and new statutes enact approximately quarterly. --> For each statute, name the citation, the effective date, the enforcement authority, and whether the statute carries a private right of action or is exclusively regulator-enforced.
2. **The threshold-applicability analysis.** For each statute, run the applicability threshold against Harborpoint's profile — the resident-count threshold, the revenue threshold where applicable, the sale-or-share threshold, and any sensitive-data threshold. California's threshold under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(d) is applicability at $25M annual revenue or 100,000 consumers or 50% of revenue from sale / share; Virginia's VCDPA threshold is 100,000 consumers or 25,000 consumers plus 50% of revenue from sale. The output is a per-state table with columns (statute, threshold type, Harborpoint value, applicable yes / no).
3. **The two dividing lines.** The sensitive-data-opt-in vs. sensitive-data-opt-out dividing line (California is opt-out-plus-right-to-limit; Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, and most 2024–2025-enacted statutes are opt-in); the universal-opt-out-mechanism (UOOM) obligation dividing line (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Oregon, Texas, and others require UOOM honour; Virginia does not). For each statute, record the posture and the operational implication.
4. **The out-of-scope flagging.** Any processing Harborpoint runs that is governed by a sector-specific statute (HIPAA for healthtech, GLBA for financial information, FERPA for education) is out-of-scope for the comprehensive-privacy-statute analysis and runs through [chapter 04](../04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md) and the sector-overlay framework (Exercise 04). Harborpoint's subscription-commerce profile does not implicate the sector overlays by default; name the carve-out explicitly.

### Part B — Single controller-side configuration

Author `controller-side-configuration.md`. The board governance subcommittee's framing is "one controller-side switch, not 20 variants." The engineering-implementable configuration is the common-baseline-plus-California-delta pattern [chapter 03](../03-us-state-privacy-landscape-and-rolling-registration.md) names. Cover:

1. **The common-baseline posture.** The set of controls Harborpoint runs uniformly across the applicable states regardless of per-state variance — the right-to-know fulfilment workflow, the right-to-correct workflow, the right-to-delete workflow, the right-to-portability workflow, the right-to-opt-out-of-sale-and-share, the right-to-opt-out-of-targeted-advertising, the sensitive-data-processing-opt-in or opt-out posture, the Notice-at-Collection content, the privacy-policy content, the UOOM handler. Document the baseline as a configuration — not a policy-document — with named fields, default values, and the engineering-implementable mechanic.
2. **The California delta.** The California-specific obligations the baseline does not cover: the "right to limit use of sensitive personal information" at Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.121; the specific "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" and "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" link text at Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.135(a)(1) and (2); the Notice-at-Collection format requirements the CPPA final regulations prescribe; the forty-five-day response window at Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.130(a)(2) and the forty-five-day extension right; the CPRA dark-patterns prohibition at § 1798.140(l); and the service-provider / contractor / third-party distinction at § 1798.140(ag) / (ah) / (ai) applied to the ad-platform integrations. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CPPA final-regulation text and any 2025–2026 amendments to the dark-patterns prohibition and the service-provider definition before treating specific regulatory text as controlling. -->
3. **The Colorado / Connecticut delta.** The sub-set of obligations the California delta does not cover — in particular the Colorado Rules' UOOM technical specification requirements and the Connecticut requirement that a UOOM be honoured for both sale / share and targeted advertising. Document the operational variance Harborpoint accepts in the common baseline (honour the most-protective state's posture by default).
4. **The deliberate rejection.** The subcommittee's framing specifically asks Harborpoint to reject the 20-variant per-state engineering backlog. Name the trade-off Harborpoint accepts in the common-baseline posture — specifically, that Harborpoint honours obligations across the applicable-state set that only a minority of states require (e.g., universally honouring the UOOM signal even in states where it is not legally mandated), and defend the trade-off against the engineering-cost argument the CTO will raise.

### Part C — Universal-opt-out-signal handler and Global Privacy Control response

Author `uoom-and-gpc-handler.md`. The California AG inquiry specifically asks about Harborpoint's Global Privacy Control response. The current configuration (GPC signal not honoured in-browser; opt-out routed to a form that only captures email) is the thing to replace. Cover:

1. **The GPC signal-handling specification.** The named technical spec Harborpoint will implement — the GPC signal is sent on every HTTP request to Harborpoint's web and API surfaces; Harborpoint reads the `Sec-GPC: 1` header and treats the signal as an opt-out-of-sale-and-share request for California, an opt-out-of-targeted-advertising for Colorado / Connecticut / Oregon / Texas where applicable, and the state-specific analog for other UOOM-honouring statutes. The signal is honoured without additional consumer action; Harborpoint does not require the consumer to also click the "Do Not Sell or Share" link to effectuate the opt-out. Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.135(b) and the CPPA Final Regulations §§ 7025–7026 are the citations. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CPPA Final Regulations citations and the CPPA's current enforcement posture on the GPC signal before quoting specific regulation sections. -->
2. **The authenticated-vs-unauthenticated handling.** The distinction between a GPC signal from a logged-in subscriber (where Harborpoint propagates the opt-out across the subscriber's account and persists it) and a GPC signal from an unauthenticated browser (where Harborpoint propagates the opt-out to the current session plus cookie-based persistence with a named retention window). The chapter 02 operational pattern.
3. **The ad-platform-pixel implication.** The engineering mechanic for suppressing the Meta Pixel, Google Ads tag, TikTok Pixel, Reddit Pixel, and LinkedIn Insight Tag firing on sessions where the GPC signal is received or the consumer has otherwise opted out. The "service-provider configuration" vs. "disable-the-pixel" decision — whether Harborpoint configures the ad platforms as service-providers under § 1798.140(ag) with the contractual restrictions the CPPA has named as necessary to defeat the "sale / share" characterisation, or whether Harborpoint accepts the sale / share characterisation and respects the opt-out by disabling the pixel. Reference the CPPA's 2024–2025 enforcement advisories flagging the service-provider characterisation as a frequent non-compliance mode. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CPPA enforcement-advisory posture on ad-platform service-provider configuration before relying on a specific characterisation. -->
4. **The audit-trail and record-of-opt-out.** The engineering record Harborpoint keeps — the opt-out event, the timestamp, the signal source (GPC header vs. click-through), the authenticated subscriber or unauthenticated session identifier, the persistence disposition, and the propagation record (which downstream systems received the suppression signal). The record is retained per [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md) and surfaces into the privacy-metrics pack from Exercise 01 Part F.

### Part D — Notice-at-Collection and Do-Not-Sell-or-Share link

Author `notice-at-collection-and-dnss-link.md`. The California AG inquiry flagged the absence of a Notice-at-Collection. Cover:

1. **The Notice-at-Collection content and placement.** The content required by Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.100(a)(1) and the CPPA Final Regulations — the categories of personal information collected, the purpose for which each category is used, whether personal information is sold or shared, the length of time each category is retained (or the criteria used to determine retention), and a link to the full privacy policy. The placement — "at or before the point of collection" — on Harborpoint's sign-up form, the subscription-checkout surface, and every surface where personal information is collected. Author the notice content and the placement specification.
2. **The "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" and "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information" links.** The link text Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.135(a)(1) and (2) mandate; the "clear and conspicuous" placement requirement — the CPPA Final Regulations' specific guidance on font size, colour contrast, and footer-vs-header placement; the alternative single "Your Privacy Choices" link under § 1798.135(a)(3). Name the choice Harborpoint will make and author the link text and the destination page.
3. **The sensitive-personal-information categorisation.** The SPI categories under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ae) — government identifiers (SSN, DL, passport), financial account identifiers, geolocation, racial / ethnic origin, religious or philosophical beliefs, union membership, mail / email / text contents, genetic data, biometric identifiers, health, sex-life / sexual-orientation. For each SPI category Harborpoint actually processes, name whether the processing is "to infer characteristics" or "necessary to perform the service," and the resulting right-to-limit mechanic Harborpoint owes under § 1798.121.
4. **The CPRA dark-patterns review.** The § 1798.140(l) prohibition on dark patterns applied to the opt-out and limit-use flows. The common failure modes the EDPB's deceptive-design-patterns guidelines and the CPPA's dark-patterns advisories flag — opt-out buttons positioned less prominently than opt-in buttons, multi-step opt-out flows where opt-in is single-click, confusing double negatives, pre-ticked boxes. Document the self-audit checklist Harborpoint will apply before the configuration ships. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CPPA dark-patterns advisory and the current EDPB deceptive-design guidelines before treating specific sub-patterns as controlling. -->

### Part E — Personalised-offer feature sale-and-share analysis

Author `personalised-offer-sale-and-share-analysis.md`. The CMO's "personalised offer" feature — subscriber purchase history combined with Partner Y's inferred-demographic signals to produce a customised offer — is the forcing-function new-processing-activity. Cover:

1. **The sale analysis.** The Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ad) "sale" definition — selling, renting, releasing, disclosing, disseminating, making available, transferring, or otherwise communicating personal information "for monetary or other valuable consideration." Run the analysis on the data flow: does Harborpoint's disclosure of subscriber data to Partner Y constitute a "sale" under the statutory test, considering that Partner Y is providing demographic signals in return (the "other valuable consideration" factor)? Does Partner Y's disclosure of demographic data to Harborpoint constitute a Partner-Y-side "sale" with Harborpoint as recipient? Name the posture Harborpoint will assert.
2. **The share analysis.** The § 1798.140(ah) "share" definition — disclosure for cross-context behavioural advertising. Does the personalised-offer activation surface (if the offer is served in Harborpoint's own transactional email and in-product surface, not a third-party ad platform) constitute cross-context behavioural advertising? Name the posture.
3. **The service-provider / contractor characterisation of Partner Y.** The § 1798.140(ag) / (ah) analysis — can Partner Y be characterised as a service-provider or contractor under a written contract that includes the § 1798.140(ag) contractual elements (purpose limitation, no-combining prohibition, cooperation-with-consumer-rights-requests obligation)? If yes, the disclosure to Partner Y is not a "sale" and Harborpoint's obligations shift to the service-provider-flow-down. If no, the disclosure is a sale / share with the resulting notice and opt-out obligations. Reference [chapter 02](../02-ccpa-cpra-compliance-programme.md) for the service-provider flow-down pattern and [mod-109 chapter 01](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/01-customer-contract-suite-msa-sla-dpa-security-aup.md) for the DPA / service-provider addendum structural anatomy. <!-- needs-research: confirm Partner Y's current contractual willingness to accept service-provider terms; the characterisation depends on the executable contract and Harborpoint cannot unilaterally declare the characterisation. -->
4. **The sensitive-personal-information analysis on inferred demographics.** Whether any of Partner Y's inferred-demographic signals constitute SPI under § 1798.140(ae) (specifically the inferred-ethnicity, inferred-religion, or inferred-health inferences) and the resulting right-to-limit mechanic.
5. **The configuration disposition.** The specific configuration change the personalised-offer feature requires — the Notice-at-Collection update, the Do-Not-Sell-or-Share / Limit-Use link update, the UOOM-signal propagation to the personalised-offer pipeline, the Partner-Y contract execution-or-non-execution gate, and the engineering-launch sign-off Harborpoint requires before the feature ships.
