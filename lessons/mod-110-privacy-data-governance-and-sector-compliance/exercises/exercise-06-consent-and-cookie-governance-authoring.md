# Exercise 06 — Consent and cookie-governance authoring

> Estimated time: **~9 hours** · Related chapters: [02 — The CCPA / CPRA compliance programme](../02-ccpa-cpra-compliance-programme.md) and [03 — The US state privacy landscape](../03-us-state-privacy-landscape-and-rolling-registration.md) (with EU ePrivacy / cookie-governance substance drawn from the module [README](../README.md))

## Problem statement

Linden & Company Media Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp operating a direct-to-consumer media-publishing property (editorial content plus a related newsletter product with a tiered-paywall subscription at $11 / month and $99 / year) and a related children's-media sub-property for readers ages 8–12 ($4 / month with a parent-managed-account structure). The parent product is accessible globally and has material audiences in the US, UK, EU (significant readership in Ireland, Germany, France, and the Netherlands), Canada, and Australia; the children's sub-property is US-and-UK-only by design, with age-gating at onboarding and a parent-controlled-subscription flow.

Linden runs a heavy ad-supported revenue mix on the free tier — approximately 58% of total revenue is advertising, split across direct-sold (~22%) and programmatic-marketplace (~36%). The programmatic stack is typical: Google Ad Manager as the primary exchange, with header-bidding across five SSPs (names redacted for the exercise), ~110 marketing-and-measurement-tag vendors on the parent-property web surface (comScore, Google Analytics 4, a customer-data-platform integration, a session-replay vendor, and a long tail of partner-marketing pixels), and ~40 vendors on the children's sub-property. The current cookie posture on the parent property is a floor-banner that reads "We use cookies. By continuing to use the site, you accept our cookie policy. [Accept] [Cookie Policy]" — single-click accept, no reject option, no granular toggle — and is otherwise identical across US, EU, UK, and other jurisdictions. The children's sub-property has no cookie banner at all; the parental-consent flow is a one-time email verification sent to the parent account at subscription onboarding.

Three forcing functions have landed. **(i)** The Irish DPC and the French CNIL have each opened preliminary inquiries in the last twelve months into Linden's cookie banner — the DPC letter asks Linden to describe its ePrivacy Directive compliance posture and its reliance on consent vs. legitimate interests across its tracker set; the CNIL letter focuses on the "reject all" absent-button and the dark-patterns analysis under the CNIL's 2020 recommendation deliberation on cookie banners. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CNIL cookie-banner-enforcement posture, the current Irish DPC cookie-enforcement posture, and the EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 and subsequent EDPB guidelines on deceptive-design patterns before treating the inquiry topics as closed. --> **(ii)** The California AG's office sent Linden an informal letter asking about Linden's CPRA dark-patterns compliance under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(l) and Linden's response to the Global Privacy Control signal; the letter references the chapter-03 operating pattern Exercise 03 is drafted against. **(iii)** The children's-sub-property FTC exposure is increasing: the FTC's 2024 COPPA Rule amendments change the material requirements on direct-notice, verifiable-parental-consent, and data-minimisation. Linden's current one-time-email-verification parental-consent flow is defensible under the pre-amendment Rule but is possibly insufficient under the amended Rule. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current FTC COPPA Rule amendments, their effective date, and the specific operative requirements before treating the compliance gap as closed. -->

The general counsel has eight weeks to walk the board governance subcommittee through the full consent-and-cookie-governance package — the preference-management-platform (PMP) selection, the parent-property cookie-banner redesign, the children's-sub-property verifiable-parental-consent flow redesign, the record-of-consent retention policy, and the response strategy to the Irish DPC, CNIL, and California AG inquiries. The general counsel owns the end-to-end package; the CTO owns the PMP implementation and the pixel-suppression mechanic; the head of product owns the user-facing-flow design (banner UI, parental-consent flow); the head of ad operations owns the vendor-list reconciliation and the Google Ad Manager / SSP configuration; outside privacy counsel will review before the regulator-response letters go out. Author the full package.

### Part B — Parent-property cookie-banner redesign

Author `parent-property-banner-redesign.md`. The current single-click-accept banner is the failure mode the Irish DPC and CNIL letters are drafted against. Cover:

1. **The EDPB-aligned authorities' requirements.** The specific requirements the EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent (as updated) and the 2023 EDPB cookie-banner taskforce report, the CNIL's 2020 deliberation on cookies and trackers, the ICO's cookie-banner guidance, and the German BfDI / state-DPA guidance each impose. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current EDPB consent guidelines version, the 2023 EDPB taskforce report publication date and conclusions, and the current national-DPA cookie-banner guidance for FR, IE, DE, and NL before treating specific requirements as controlling. --> At minimum: a "reject all" button equally prominent to the "accept all" button; no pre-ticked boxes; granular purpose-level consent with a "refuse" option at the same click-depth as "accept"; no "continue scrolling implies consent" pattern; no cookie-wall-with-no-alternative pattern (the "accept-or-pay" wall is permissible under specific conditions chapter-02 names, deferred to that chapter for the US side and flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` for the EU side).
2. **The jurisdiction-specific variance.** The specific banner variants per jurisdiction — EU opt-in (consent default off; explicit opt-in required for non-strictly-necessary cookies); UK opt-in under PECR (parallel to EU with the ICO's own enforcement posture); US opt-out (default on; GPC signal honour; per-state variance deferred to Part D); Quebec opt-in post-Law 25 where applicable; Australian "notice and choice" posture. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current Quebec Law 25 cookie-consent posture and the current Australian OAIC guidance on online-tracking consent. --> Do not drift into a 20-variant engineering backlog; the Part-A PMP delivers the per-jurisdiction routing. Describe the banner variants as PMP-configurations, not engineering-backlog items.
3. **The strictly-necessary-vs-optional classification.** The vendor-list review — for each of the ~110 parent-property vendor-pixels, classify as strictly-necessary (user-initiated-session cookies, security cookies, authentication cookies, load-balancer cookies, CSRF protection cookies), functional (user-preference cookies for display settings, consent-record cookie, locale cookie), analytics (Google Analytics 4, comScore, session-replay), advertising / marketing (the programmatic-ad-stack SSPs, retargeting pixels), or social / third-party-content. The specific "strictly necessary" exemption from the ePrivacy consent requirement is narrow — Article 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive and the EDPB opinion on the exemption. Flag any vendor the current ad-operations team classifies as "strictly necessary" that would not qualify under the narrow exemption.
4. **The banner copy.** Author the specific operative copy Linden will deploy on the parent-property EU variant: the top-layer banner text (plain language, under ~50 words, naming "cookies and similar technologies," naming "consent," naming the three button labels), the middle-layer purpose-selection text (one line per purpose: "strictly necessary," "functional," "analytics," "advertising"), the bottom-layer vendor-list anchor text and the link destination. Author the parallel US-variant copy, which uses the opt-out framing rather than the opt-in framing and references the Global Privacy Control response.
5. **The dark-patterns self-audit.** The Part-D of Exercise 03 dark-patterns checklist applied to the redesigned banner — equal-prominence buttons, no multi-step opt-out where opt-in is single-click, no confusing double negatives, no pre-ticked boxes, consistent language across layers. Document the audit explicitly before the banner ships.

### Part C — Children's-sub-property VPC flow redesign

Author `children-vpc-flow-redesign.md`. The children's sub-property's one-time-email-verification parental-consent flow is the target of the FTC 2024 COPPA Rule amendments' increased direct-notice, VPC, and data-minimisation obligations. Cross-reference Exercise 04 Part D for the full COPPA VPC-flow discipline; this part focuses on the consent-and-notice specifics for the Linden children's sub-property. Cover:

1. **The current flow audit.** The step-by-step of the current parental-consent flow — subscription-onboarding form asks the parent's email, a one-time verification email is sent, a click-through confirms consent, the child's account is activated. Name the specific operative gaps against 16 C.F.R. § 312.5's "reasonable efforts" standard for VPC methods and the FTC's 2024-amendment-expected enhancements. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current 16 C.F.R. § 312.5 VPC-method list and the specific FTC 2024 amendments; the "+1-800-" method and the credit-card-transaction method are the long-standing default-approved methods, with the FTC having expanded the list over successive rulemakings. -->
2. **The direct-notice redesign.** The 16 C.F.R. § 312.4 direct-notice content — the operator's name and contact, the data categories collected, the uses of the data, whether the operator discloses the data to third parties and the identity or category of those third parties, the parental-opt-out-pathway, the parent-review-right and parent-deletion-right at § 312.6. The specific redesign Linden will make — the direct-notice becomes a layered document (top-layer summary for the parent at onboarding, middle-layer detailed-notice at the consent moment, bottom-layer full policy at a named URL). Author the top-layer summary text.
3. **The VPC-method selection.** The specific VPC method Linden will adopt. The one-time-email-verification method is only acceptable under the "email-plus" sub-method at § 312.5(b)(2) where the data is used solely for internal use and not disclosed to third parties; the Linden children's sub-property's $4 / month subscription flow involves a payment transaction (chapter-04 pattern) which qualifies as a payment-card-verification VPC method under § 312.5(b)(2) and is substantively stronger than email-plus. Name the method Linden will adopt and the step-by-step operational flow.
4. **The data-minimisation posture.** The specific data-minimisation discipline Linden will apply — the fields Linden collects at onboarding (strictly what is necessary for the subscription-management function), the fields Linden *stops* collecting after the current pre-amendment-Rule practice (any inferred-demographic fields; any telemetry beyond what is necessary for the editorial-reading experience), the retention-period for the collected data under [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md), and the parent-visible deletion mechanic.
5. **The ongoing material-change-notification mechanic.** The 16 C.F.R. § 312.4(c) obligation to provide the parent with notice of a material change in practices for which prior parental consent was obtained; the operative workflow Linden will run — a material-change inventory, the parental-re-consent mechanic, the sunset-of-consent-and-data-deletion disposition if the parent does not re-consent. Reference [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md) for the deletion-workflow mechanic.

### Part D — Universal-opt-out-signal handler for the US state regimes

Author `uoom-signal-handler.md`. The US state-privacy regimes that recognise a universal-opt-out mechanism (California GPC, Colorado's Universal Opt-Out Mechanism under the CPA rules, Connecticut's UOOM obligation, Oregon, Texas, and others per the module [README](../README.md) list) require Linden's PMP to honour the signal across the free-tier browsing experience. Cross-reference Exercise 03 Part C for the full UOOM handler mechanic; this part covers the Linden-specific integration. Cover:

1. **The GPC signal-handling specification.** The specific technical spec Linden's PMP will implement — the `Sec-GPC: 1` header is read on every page load; the signal is treated as an opt-out-of-sale-and-share for California, an opt-out-of-targeted-advertising for Colorado / Connecticut / Oregon / Texas / others, and the state-specific analog for other UOOM-honouring statutes; the signal is honoured without additional consumer action; the signal is persisted per [chapter 03](../03-us-state-privacy-landscape-and-rolling-registration.md)'s common-baseline posture.
2. **The programmatic-ad-stack signal propagation.** The specific mechanic for suppressing the ~110 parent-property vendor-pixels on sessions where the GPC signal is received — the PMP's IAB TCF v2 integration must emit the "opt-out" TC-string across the vendor-list; the direct Google Ad Manager integration must honour the IAB US Privacy String (`uspapi`) analog and the newer IAB GPP (Global Privacy Platform) string for the state-by-state configuration. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current IAB GPP specification version and the current Google Ad Manager / major-SSP support for the GPP string vs. the US Privacy String. -->
3. **The authenticated-vs-unauthenticated handling.** The authenticated-subscriber flow (the opt-out persists across sessions and devices via the subscriber account), the unauthenticated-visitor flow (the opt-out persists across the current session via cookie-based persistence with a named retention window), and the login-event propagation (when an authenticated subscriber with an existing opt-out logs in from a session that previously did not carry the opt-out, the subscriber's account-level opt-out takes precedence).
4. **The audit-trail.** The engineering record Linden keeps — the opt-out event, the timestamp, the signal source (GPC header vs. click-through vs. subscriber-account preference), the authenticated subscriber or unauthenticated session identifier, the persistence disposition, the downstream-vendor-suppression propagation record. The record is retained per Part E and surfaces into the privacy-metrics pack from Exercise 01 Part F.

### Part E — Record-of-consent retention policy

Author `record-of-consent-retention-policy.md`. The record-of-consent retention is the Article 5(2) accountability obligation under GDPR, the "consent-record" discipline under PECR / the ePrivacy Directive national transpositions, the "proof of consent" obligation under the 2024 COPPA Rule, and the TCPA consent-record retention. Cross-reference Exercise 04 Part E for TCPA consent-record and [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md) for the retention-schedule authoring. Cover:

1. **The consent-record content.** The specific fields every consent record carries — the consent-giver identifier (authenticated subscriber or unauthenticated session), the consent moment (timestamp with timezone), the consent purpose (strictly necessary / functional / analytics / advertising, or COPPA-parental-consent, or TCPA-SMS-consent), the consent method (banner-accept / banner-reject / per-purpose-toggle / GPC-signal / email-plus-confirmation / payment-card-verification / etc.), the consent text version (versioned reference to the banner-copy-or-notice-text at the moment of consent), the consent IP / user-agent (where applicable and retained per data-minimisation), and the consent lifecycle (grant, re-grant after material change, withdrawal, expiration).
2. **The retention-period posture.** The specific retention-period per consent-record type — GDPR Art. 7(1) accountability requires retention for as long as the processing continues plus a reasonable period for proof against a data-subject-complaint claim; COPPA consent-records are typically retained for the duration of the child's account plus a reasonable period; TCPA consent-records are typically retained for at least four years to cover the statute-of-limitations window. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current TCPA statute-of-limitations period and the FCC's and state AGs' current record-retention posture before treating four years as canonical. --> Defer the specific retention-period-per-record-type table to [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md); this part names the retention-posture per consent-record type.
3. **The consent-withdrawal mechanic.** The operational flow when a subscriber withdraws consent — the withdrawal-event is recorded, the downstream-processing-cessation is propagated to the vendor-stack, the audit-trail records the withdrawal moment and the propagation record, the subscriber is informed that withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal (GDPR Art. 7(3)). Author the operative subscriber-communication text.
4. **The accountability-demonstration surface.** The surface the DPO or general counsel produces when a supervisory authority or AG asks for proof of consent — the audit-trail report, the consent-text-version history, the vendor-suppression-propagation record, the DSAR-style per-subscriber consent-history report. Reference Exercise 01 Part F for the privacy-metrics-cadence integration.
