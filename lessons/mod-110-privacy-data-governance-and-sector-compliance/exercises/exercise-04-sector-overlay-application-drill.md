# Exercise 04 — Sector-overlay application drill — HIPAA / GLBA / FERPA / COPPA / TCPA

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — HIPAA compliance for healthtech](../04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md) (with GLBA / FERPA / COPPA / TCPA / CAN-SPAM substance drawn from the primary statutes named in the module [README](../README.md))

## Problem statement

Kestrel Learning Systems Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp operating a portfolio of four distinct product lines under one corporate roof — the "one-corporation-many-overlays" case the module [README](../README.md) flags as common at Series-B / growth stage. Kestrel is 185 FTE, closed a $71M Series B nine months ago led by an education-focused growth fund, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, and runs the following product lines:

- **Product Line A — "Kestrel Kids"** — a mathematics-practice app targeted at US children ages 6–12, installed on a school-assigned tablet and used in the school classroom under the teacher's direction. The product is operated under a contract with the school district; the school district provides the student roster; the data categories are student name, teacher assignment, grade-level, practice-exercise performance, and per-session engagement telemetry. Approximately 420,000 US students across 1,100 school districts.
- **Product Line B — "Kestrel Teen"** — a career-exploration platform targeted at US high-school students ages 13–18, purchased by the student's school as a one-year licence, offering self-directed exploration, interest assessments, and college-and-career matching. The product collects student profile data (name, grade, interests, assessment results) and connects the student to external post-secondary institutions and employer partners. Approximately 160,000 US students across 480 high schools.
- **Product Line C — "Kestrel Wellness"** — a tele-therapy platform targeted at adults, operated as a direct-to-consumer subscription ($89 / month) and as an employer-benefit integration (sold to US employers who sponsor the benefit for their employees). The product connects the subscriber to licensed therapists, maintains session notes, and bills the subscriber's health insurance where applicable. Approximately 38,000 active subscribers across the DTC and employer-sponsored lines.
- **Product Line D — "Kestrel Capital"** — a personal-finance-coaching platform targeted at adults, operated as a direct-to-consumer subscription ($19 / month) and as a bank-partner-branded white-label (sold to regional US banks who offer the tool to their account holders). The product ingests bank-transaction data via Plaid-style open-banking connections, produces budget and savings recommendations, and markets partner financial products. Approximately 110,000 active subscribers across the DTC and bank-partner lines.

The corporation is also running an aggressive marketing operation across all four product lines — SMS-based re-engagement campaigns to lapsed subscribers, email marketing to prospects captured through top-of-funnel content, and a combination of in-product marketing to current users and partner-platform referrals. The chief marketing officer runs all four product lines' marketing from a single team; the general counsel (one headcount, no DPO yet) runs legal across all four.

Three forcing functions have landed. **(i)** The Federal Trade Commission updated the COPPA Rule with effect from a date that depends on the 2024–2026 rulemaking cadence <!-- needs-research: confirm the current effective date of the FTC's 2024 COPPA Rule amendments and any subsequent amendments before treating a specific date as controlling. -->, and the general counsel is uncertain whether Kestrel Kids' current verifiable-parental-consent flow (a one-time email-to-the-teacher check, with the school district acting as agent for parental consent under the "school consent" exception) remains defensible under the amended rule. **(ii)** Kestrel Wellness's largest employer-benefit partner has routed a HIPAA Business Associate Agreement two weeks ago — the general counsel's first BAA — and asked Kestrel to confirm its Covered Entity vs. Business Associate characterisation across its billing-insurance activity. **(iii)** The CMO's SMS re-engagement campaign is driving a ~0.4% revenue lift across all four product lines but the TCPA prior-express-written-consent capture has never been audited; a plaintiffs' firm has sent a pre-suit demand letter alleging a TCPA violation on three documented SMS messages to a lapsed Kestrel Capital subscriber.

The general counsel has eight weeks to walk into the board governance subcommittee with the full sector-overlay application plan. She owns the end-to-end package; the heads of the four product lines each own their line's overlay-applicability-and-compliance posture; the CMO owns the TCPA / CAN-SPAM marketing-consent-capture posture; outside privacy counsel and outside healthcare counsel will review before any customer-facing commitment is finalised. Author the full package.

### Part B — HIPAA Business Associate Agreement authoring

2. **The § 164.504(e) BAA content.** Author the operative clause text — not a summary — for each of the content elements § 164.504(e) requires: (i) the permitted and required uses and disclosures of PHI; (ii) the prohibition on use or further disclosure other than as permitted by the contract or as required by law; (iii) the appropriate safeguards; (iv) the obligation to report breaches, security incidents, and uses or disclosures not provided for in the contract; (v) the sub-contractor flow-down requirement; (vi) the obligation to make PHI available for § 164.524 individual access; (vii) the obligation to make PHI available for § 164.526 amendment; (viii) the obligation to make the accounting of disclosures under § 164.528 available; (ix) the obligation to make internal practices, books, and records available to HHS; (x) the obligation to return or destroy PHI at termination where feasible; (xi) the obligation to comply with Subpart C of Part 164 (the Security Rule) directly to the extent applicable; (xii) authorisation for termination of the contract by the Covered Entity for a material breach.

### Part E — TCPA prior-express-written-consent capture

Author `tcpa-consent-capture.md`. The CMO's SMS re-engagement campaigns and the plaintiffs' pre-suit demand letter are the forcing function. Cover:

1. **The current consent-capture audit.** The documented audit of Kestrel's current SMS consent-capture flow across all four product lines — the exact language in the subscriber's onboarding flow, the placement of the TCPA / SMS consent, the opt-in mechanism (single-click checkbox, double-opt-in, embedded-in-terms-of-service), the record-of-consent retention and the auditability of the record. Name the gaps against the 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9) "prior express written consent" definition — a signed writing that clearly authorises the sender to deliver advertisements or telemarketing messages using an automatic telephone dialing system or artificial / prerecorded voice, that identifies the party authorised to deliver messages and the telephone number to which messages may be delivered, and that includes a clear-and-conspicuous disclosure.
2. **The one-to-one consent rulemaking status and the posture Kestrel will adopt.** The FCC's December 2023 Order creating the "one-to-one consent" requirement at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(f)(9) and the Eleventh Circuit's January 2025 vacatur in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. 4. **The outbound consent-capture-flow redesign.** The new consent-capture flow Kestrel will deploy across all four product lines — the clear-and-conspicuous disclosure language, the distinctive-signing-mechanism (checkbox-with-signature-affidavit vs. double-opt-in-with-SMS-confirmation), the identification of the sending-party and the number from which the messages will arrive, the per-brand-consent discipline (the CMO cannot rely on a Kestrel Wellness subscriber's SMS consent to send Kestrel Capital messages), the retention of the consent record per [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md), and the "quiet-hours" and "frequency-cap" posture under 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200(a) and the state-level TCPA analogues (e.g., Florida Telephone Solicitation Act, Washington CEMA). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current list of state TCPA analogues and the specific operative obligations each imposes beyond the federal baseline. -->
5. **The CAN-SPAM posture for email marketing.** The parallel discipline for email — the 15 U.S.C. § 7701 et seq. and 16 C.F.R. Part 316 requirements (accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification of the message as an advertisement where applicable, a valid physical postal address, a clear-and-conspicuous opt-out mechanism, honour of opt-outs within 10 business days). The CAN-SPAM posture is less legally fraught than TCPA (no private right of action; FTC and state-AG enforcement only), but the practitioner discipline is to run the email programme to CAN-SPAM-plus-state-AG-expectations.
