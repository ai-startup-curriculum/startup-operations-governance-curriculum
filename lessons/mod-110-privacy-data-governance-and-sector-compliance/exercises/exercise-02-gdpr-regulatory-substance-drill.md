# Exercise 02 — GDPR regulatory-substance drill

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — The GDPR compliance programme](../01-gdpr-compliance-programme.md)

## Problem statement

Verdant Signal Analytics Inc. is a Series-A Delaware C-corp selling a B2B marketing-attribution and customer-data-platform product into mid-market retail and D2C commerce customers. The product ingests first-party customer-event data from customers' websites, apps, and point-of-sale systems; enriches the event stream with second-party partner data and third-party demographic signals purchased from a data-broker partner (Partner X, US-only); produces attribution models and audience segments the customer activates into its downstream marketing stack. Verdant is 78 FTE, closed a $19M Series A thirteen months ago led by a US venture firm, books ARR in the low-eight-figures, and has just landed three design-partner customers in the EU — a Paris-headquartered specialty-retail brand, a Berlin-headquartered D2C furniture brand, and a Milan-headquartered luxury-goods brand — across a combined ~2.4 million EU end-user records in the attribution pipeline.

Four processing activities are in flight on the EU design-partner data. **(i) Analytics and attribution-modelling on customer end-user event data**, where the customer's marketing team passes Verdant hashed email identifiers plus event data and Verdant returns an attribution score per identifier. **(ii) Audience-segment construction**, where Verdant combines the customer's first-party event data with purchased third-party demographic signals from Partner X (gender, estimated household income, estimated age band, estimated purchase propensity) and produces segment memberships that the customer activates into Facebook, Google, and TikTok ad platforms. **(iii) Fraud and abuse detection on the customer's end-user traffic**, where Verdant runs behavioural signals (device fingerprint, velocity signals, geo-IP) to flag probable bot traffic and probable account-takeover. **(iv) Verdant's own marketing to the customer's commercial contacts** (named buyers at the customer organisation), via Verdant's HubSpot-hosted email outreach and in-product nurture flows.

The regulatory footprint has moved fast. The Paris design partner's privacy counsel has asked for Verdant's **lawful-basis analysis per processing activity**; the Berlin design partner has asked for the **Article 13 / 14 layered-notice stack** Verdant publishes to the customer's end-users; the Milan design partner has asked for the **Data Protection Impact Assessment on the audience-segment construction activity**, flagging that the combination of first-party and third-party data to produce segments activated into Meta's / Google's / TikTok's ad platforms is likely-high-risk processing under Article 35(3)(a). Verdant's engineering team ships from Frankfurt to AWS us-east-1 (the EU design-partner data is currently stored in the US tenant); the design-partner contracts are blocking on the **Transfer Impact Assessment** that supports the Frankfurt-to-US-East-1 flow under the DPF + SCCs + UK Addendum + Swiss addendum transfer stack.

The general counsel (one headcount, no DPO yet — the Article 37 determination is the subject of Exercise 01 for a sibling corporation) has six weeks to produce the full regulatory-substance package before the three design-partner contracts sign. The product owns the processing-activity descriptions; the engineering head owns the data-flow diagram the TIA depends on; the general counsel owns the lawful-basis determinations, the notice stack, the DPIA, and the TIA. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Six-lawful-bases analysis

Author `lawful-basis-analysis.md`. For each of the four processing activities, run the Article 6(1)(a)–(f) analysis and name the lawful basis Verdant will declare. For activities that process Article 9 special-category data, run the Article 9(2) analysis in addition. Cover:

1. **Activity 1 — analytics and attribution modelling on customer end-user event data.** Analyse Verdant's role under Article 4 — controller, processor, or joint controller with the customer under Article 26? Name the lawful basis Verdant will declare in the RoPA for its role. Where Verdant is a processor acting on the customer's documented instruction under Article 28, the lawful basis flows from the customer's own analysis; name the posture Verdant will assert in its outbound DPA and flag the controller's responsibility to maintain its own lawful-basis record. Reference the EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 on controller / processor concepts.
2. **Activity 2 — audience-segment construction.** Analyse the combination of first-party customer event data with third-party demographic signals from Partner X. Name Verdant's controller role on the segment-construction output (the segment is a derived dataset Verdant creates; the customer is the controller on the first-party input; the three-way controller / processor / joint-controller characterisation is substantive here). Name the lawful basis per side of the analysis — the Article 6(1)(f) legitimate-interests balancing test with the Article 6(1)(f) three-part test (legitimate interest, necessity, balancing) documented explicitly. Address the Article 9 question — does any third-party demographic signal from Partner X constitute Article 9 special-category data (estimated purchase propensity typically does not; estimated-gender fields may constitute Article 9 data where they are derived from sensitive inference) and, if so, name the Article 9(2) condition. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current EDPB guidance on inferred special-category data and the French CNIL and Italian Garante enforcement posture on audience-segment building against the Partner-X signal profile before treating the Article 9 question as closed. -->
3. **Activity 3 — fraud and abuse detection.** Name the Article 6(1)(f) legitimate-interests basis under Recital 47 (fraud prevention) and author the three-part legitimate-interests balancing test the RoPA will carry. Address the Article 22 question — is the fraud-detection output "a decision based solely on automated processing … which produces legal effects … or similarly significantly affects" the data subject? Verdant's fraud score is consumed by the customer's abuse-detection system; the customer, not Verdant, makes the auto-suspend decision. Name the posture explicitly and reference the EDPB Guidelines on automated decision-making and profiling for the purposes of Regulation 2016/679 (WP251 rev.01, endorsed by the EDPB). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current endorsement status of WP251 rev.01 and any EDPB superseding guidance before treating it as the controlling interpretive instrument. -->
4. **Activity 4 — Verdant's own marketing to the customer's commercial contacts.** Name the Article 6(1)(f) legitimate-interests basis under Recital 47 (direct marketing) with the Article 21(2) absolute-right-to-object overlay. Address the ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC and the national transpositions — the UK PECR, the French Loi Informatique et Libertés, the German TDDDG — that layer prior-consent requirements on electronic marketing above Article 6. Name the operative pattern Verdant will run: soft-opt-in for existing-customer-relationship contacts, prior-opt-in for cold outreach into EU jurisdictions where the national transposition requires it. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current ePrivacy national-transposition status in France, Germany, Italy, the UK, and Ireland; the EU ePrivacy Regulation remains pending and the national-transposition landscape is uneven. -->

### Part B — Article 13 / 14 layered-notice stack

Author `layered-notice-stack.md`. Verdant publishes notice to multiple audiences — the customer's end-users (where Verdant is in the processor role under the customer's processing), the customer's commercial contacts (where Verdant is in the controller role), and Verdant's own website visitors. Cover:

1. **The Article 13 layered-notice pattern.** The top-layer banner / in-product notice (the "just in time" notice at the data-collection moment); the middle-layer summary (the "privacy highlights" or "privacy nutrition label" the EDPB guidelines on transparency recommend — [reference EDPB-endorsed WP260 rev.01 Transparency Guidelines](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/article29/items/622227)); the bottom-layer full privacy policy at Verdant's named canonical URL. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current endorsement status of WP260 rev.01 and any EDPB superseding transparency guidance. --> Each layer's content is bounded by what Article 13(1)–(2) requires for first-party collection; the full policy carries the complete Article 13 content.
2. **The Article 14 notice pattern — personal data not collected from the data subject.** The audience-segment construction activity in Part A ingests Partner-X third-party data that was not collected from the data subject by Verdant. Article 14 requires a notice to the data subject within one month of obtaining the data, or at the first communication to the data subject, or when disclosure to a third party is envisaged (whichever is earliest). Name the operative posture Verdant will run — the Article 14 notice Verdant will publish at its own canonical URL, the Article 14(5) exception analysis (whether disproportionate effort applies and whether Verdant can rely on it), the reliance on Partner X's own upstream notice as a factor in the Article 14(5) analysis. Author the Article 14 notice text.
3. **The notice content checklist.** The Article 13(1)–(2) content list that every notice layer must carry: controller identity and contact, DPO contact where applicable, processing purposes, lawful basis per purpose, legitimate-interests elaboration where Art. 6(1)(f) is invoked, recipient categories, third-country-transfer disclosures with the transfer mechanism, retention period or criteria, data-subject rights (Articles 15–22 plus the Article 77 right to lodge a supervisory-authority complaint), the Article 13(2)(e) statement on whether provision of data is a statutory / contractual requirement and the consequence of refusal, and the Article 13(2)(f) existence-of-automated-decision-making statement where applicable.
4. **The notice-change cadence.** The versioning discipline — monotonic version numbers on the full policy, a changelog surface, the notice-of-material-change mechanic for the layered summary, and the retention of superseded versions for the Article 5(2) accountability record. Reference [chapter 06](../06-records-retention-schedule.md) for the retention of superseded policy versions.

### Part C — Data Protection Impact Assessment on audience-segment construction

Author `dpia-audience-segment-construction.md`. The Milan design partner has asked for the DPIA on Activity 2. The processing — first-party customer event data combined with third-party Partner-X demographic signals producing segments activated into Meta's / Google's / TikTok's ad platforms — is likely-high-risk processing under Article 35(3)(a) (systematic and extensive evaluation including profiling with legal or similarly significant effects) and Article 35(3)(b) (large-scale processing of sensitive data where inferred-sensitive-attribute analysis applies). Cover:

1. **The processing description.** The Article 35(7)(a) systematic description of processing operations — the input data categories, the processing steps (ingest, cleanse, join, feature-engineer, segment, activate), the output data categories, the recipient categories, the retention disposition, the regions.
2. **The necessity and proportionality assessment.** The Article 35(7)(b) assessment — is the processing necessary for the stated purpose, is the data minimised under Article 5(1)(c), is the retention consistent with Article 5(1)(e), is the lawful basis for each processing step the one Verdant declared in Part A?
3. **The risk assessment.** The Article 35(7)(c) assessment of risk to data-subject rights and freedoms — the specific harms from the audience-segment construction pattern (unexpected inference about the data subject, discriminatory segment construction, loss of control once the segment is activated into a third-party ad platform, cross-site linkage of behavioural data, inferred-special-category risk). Rank the risks against the EDPB Article 29 WP248 criteria and reference the CNIL's Guide d'évaluation d'impact relative à la protection des données (PIA) methodology as a reference the DPIA can use. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current CNIL PIA methodology version and any 2025–2026 updates before citing it as the controlling methodology. -->
4. **The safeguards.** The Article 35(7)(d) measures envisaged to address the risks — the data-minimisation measures, the lawful-basis-defensibility measures, the Article 21(2) opt-out mechanic the end-user can exercise, the Article 22 analysis and the human-in-the-loop posture where applicable, the Annex-II TOMs posture. Reference the Partner-X vendor-privacy-risk assessment cross-reference in [chapter 08](../08-vendor-privacy-risk-assessment-programme.md) and the sub-processor characterisation of Meta, Google, and TikTok as the activation targets (named by role in the DPIA, not re-characterised as sub-processors of Verdant where the customer's own direct-connection to the ad platform is the actual path).
5. **The residual-risk disposition.** Whether residual risk is "high" after safeguards; if yes, the Article 36 prior-consultation disposition — consult the lead supervisory authority or document why the residual-risk-is-not-high conclusion holds.
6. **The DPIA register entry and the review cadence.** The DPIA register entry Verdant will carry (reference Exercise 01 Part E for the register format); the trigger for DPIA re-review (a material processing change, a change in the Partner-X signal mix, a change in the Meta / Google / TikTok activation pattern, a change in the customer's own role posture).

### Part D — Transfer Impact Assessment on Frankfurt-to-US-East-1

Author `transfer-impact-assessment-frankfurt-to-us-east-1.md`. The three design-partner contracts are blocking on the TIA that supports Verdant's cross-border transfer stack. Cover the Chapter V transfer analysis end-to-end:

1. **The transfer map.** The named exporter (the EU design partner acting as controller; Verdant acting as processor), the named importer (Verdant's US entity; Verdant's named US-based sub-processors), the data categories transiting the link, the processing purposes, the volume, the retention disposition at the importer, the region (AWS us-east-1).
2. **The transfer mechanism stack.** The layered mechanism Verdant will declare: the EU–US Data Privacy Framework under [Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dec_impl/2023/1795/oj) for Verdant's self-certification; the 2021/914 Standard Contractual Clauses as a fall-back layer; the UK IDTA or the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs for the UK design-partner flow where applicable; the Swiss FDPIC addendum for Swiss flows. Name whether Verdant is currently DPF-self-certified or whether self-certification is a prerequisite step; flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on the current DPF list status if Verdant is not already on the list.
3. **The Schrems II TIA analysis.** The six-step EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 on measures that supplement transfer tools analysis — (i) know the transfer, (ii) identify the transfer mechanism, (iii) assess the law and practice of the third country that may impinge on the effectiveness of the transfer mechanism, (iv) adopt supplementary measures where necessary, (v) take any formal procedural steps, (vi) re-evaluate at appropriate intervals. For step (iii), reference the specific US surveillance-law landscape — FISA Section 702 and Executive Order 12333 — and the 2022 Executive Order 14086 safeguards that underpin the DPF adequacy decision. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current status of EO 14086 implementation, the Data Protection Review Court operation, and any DPF-challenge litigation before treating the DPF-plus-SCC stack as a settled baseline. -->
4. **The supplementary measures.** The Annex-II TOMs posture and the specific supplementary measures Verdant commits to — encryption-in-transit with named protocols, encryption-at-rest with customer-held-or-vendor-held keys, pseudonymisation of direct identifiers at ingest, access-control posture, logging-and-monitoring posture, the Article 28 processor-chain commitments, the government-access-request transparency commitment (the "warrant canary" posture), and the Article 48 posture on foreign-government data requests.
5. **The re-evaluation cadence.** The named trigger for TIA re-review — a material change in the surveillance-law landscape, a Schrems-III or equivalent CJEU decision, a material change in Verdant's processing, a change in the US-side sub-processor stack. The TIA register entry Verdant will carry on the privacy-office dashboard (reference Exercise 01 Part F for the privacy-metrics surface).
