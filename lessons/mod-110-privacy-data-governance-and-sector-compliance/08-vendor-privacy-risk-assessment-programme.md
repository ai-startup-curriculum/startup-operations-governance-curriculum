# 8. The vendor-privacy-risk-assessment programme — inventory, tiering, DPA flow-down, evidence collection, and recertification

> A corporation's second-largest attack surface, after its own product, is the list of third parties its product depends on. The vendor-privacy-risk-assessment programme is the operating artefact that keeps that surface governed — a living inventory, a tier-based due-diligence depth, a DPA / BAA chain that imposes the controller's obligations downstream, an evidence catalogue the regulator and the enterprise buyer will both accept, and a recertification cadence that treats a stale SOC 2 report as a Sev-2 ticket on the privacy-ops queue.

## Motivation

The regulator does not care that the breach happened at the sub-processor. Under GDPR Article 28 the controller remains accountable for the processor's and the sub-processor's conduct; under the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule the covered entity owns the 60-day clock when its business associate is breached; under the California Privacy Rights Act the business remains responsible for the service provider's handling of the personal information it shared. The vendor-privacy programme is the operating control that makes this inherited liability governable — it is the difference between "we were breached through Vendor X, here is the DPA, the latest SOC 2, the sub-processor approval record, and the deletion-certification runbook" and "we were breached through a vendor our procurement team added last quarter and we cannot locate the contract."

The second reason the programme has to exist in a Series-B-and-later corporation is that enterprise procurement will demand to see it. A Fortune-500 infosec reviewer's vendor questionnaire asks, in some form, "how do you manage *your* vendors?" — and expects to see a vendor inventory, a tiering policy, evidence-collection standards, and a recertification cadence. A startup that cannot produce these artefacts fails the questionnaire on a procedural ground before the substantive security review begins. This chapter is the authoring and operating specification for that programme. It owns the privacy-and-data-protection posture of the vendor relationship. It does **not** own the structural contract language (deferred to [mod-109 chapter 03](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md)), the AI-specific overlay (deferred to [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md)), the broader procurement-function design (deferred to [mod-114](../mod-114-operations-function-design/)), or the security-engineering substance of the SOC 2, ISO, or HITRUST evidence (deferred to the `security-learning` and `ai-infra-security-solutions` curricula).

## Core concepts

### Vendor-inventory maintenance — the single source of truth

The vendor inventory is the register against which every other control in this chapter operates. If the inventory is incomplete the tiering is incomplete; if the tiering is incomplete the due-diligence depth is wrong; if the due-diligence depth is wrong the SOC 2 and DPA evidence is missing from the file.

**System of record.** The practitioner default is to anchor the inventory in the procurement system of record (Ironclad, Zip, Coupa, or an equivalent CLM — see [mod-109 chapter 07](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md)) and to mirror it in a Vendor-Risk-Management module that carries the risk metadata, questionnaire responses, and evidence artefacts. Two systems, one authoritative link — the procurement system owns the contract and the economic relationship; the VRM module owns the risk posture. A single-system model is possible at seed scale (a shared sheet linked to the Drive folder of DPAs) but fails at Series-B contract volumes; at that stage the two-system pattern is the default.

**Named owner.** The inventory has one named owner, typically in legal-ops or privacy-ops, who is accountable for its completeness and freshness. The owner is paged when a procurement request is routed around the intake form and when a vendor's evidence expires. A programme without a named owner is a programme that degrades to a stale spreadsheet inside two quarters. In a Series-B corporation the owner is typically a Legal-Operations Manager with a dotted line to the Data Protection Officer where one is appointed under Article 37 GDPR; in the absence of a DPO the owner reports to privacy counsel or, where no privacy counsel has yet been hired, to the GC directly.

**Discovery against ground truth.** The inventory build is never complete at the first pass. The quarterly reconciliation cross-references the procurement system of record against four independent data sources — the SSO application catalogue (every provisioned application is a candidate vendor record), the finance-team expense-card ledger (SaaS subscriptions routed around procurement surface here), the AWS or GCP account-level billing records (infrastructure sub-services routed under the primary cloud agreement surface here), and the engineering team's package-manifest dependency scan (open-source and infrastructure-tooling vendors with signed licence agreements surface here). Every record found in a secondary source but absent from the primary inventory is a gap; the inventory owner closes the gap by either onboarding the vendor properly or decommissioning the surfaced shadow-IT instance.

**Onboarding gate at contract signature.** No contract is countersigned until the new-vendor-intake form is complete. The intake form drives the tiering (data categories to be shared, volume, data-subject jurisdictions, sub-processor chain, AI-training posture) and triggers the correct DPA, BAA, or standard-contractual-clauses attachment. The gate is enforced in the CLM's approval workflow — the counter-signature button is literally not available until privacy-ops has signed off on the intake form. A procurement request that bypasses the gate is a programme defect; the quarterly audit surfaces bypasses by reconciling CLM-signed contracts against VRM-approved records.

**Offboarding gate at contract termination.** Termination is not complete at the "do not renew" click. The programme requires a deletion-certification artefact from the vendor (or a documented retention basis if the vendor is legally obliged to retain), a confirmation that sub-processors downstream have been instructed to delete, and a removal of the vendor from any customer-facing sub-processor list the corporation maintains under its own DPAs. The offboarding runbook is as prescribed as the onboarding runbook — and is the step most frequently skipped, which is why the quarterly inventory audit cross-checks terminated contracts against open evidence records. The deletion-certification deadline is specified in the base DPA (the practitioner default is 30 to 90 days after contract end, aligned to the controller's own records-retention schedule in [chapter 06](./06-records-retention-schedule.md)) and is enforced as a VRM ticket that remains open until the artefact is received.

### Vendor-tier classification by data-sensitivity

Tiering is the policy decision that converts an undifferentiated vendor list into a diligence budget. Three tiers are the practitioner default; more than three is noise, and fewer collapses the diligence depth into a single setting that is either too heavy for Tier 3 vendors or too light for Tier 1.

**Tier 1 — regulated or critical-availability vendors.** Any one of the following criteria places a vendor in Tier 1:

- Processes regulated-category data — protected health information under HIPAA, cardholder data under PCI-DSS, biometric identifiers under BIPA or equivalent, children's data under COPPA or UK Age-Appropriate Design, or GDPR Article 9 special-category data at scale.
- Processes highly-sensitive personal information at material volume — government identifiers, financial account numbers, precise geolocation.
- Has critical-availability exposure — an outage of the vendor causes a customer-facing outage of the corporation's product (identity provider, primary cloud, payments processor, core database-as-a-service).

Tier 1 due-diligence depth: deep review including SOC 2 Type II with current observation window; ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate plus Statement of Applicability where applicable; annual penetration-test summary letter; a DPIA or transfer-impact assessment for the specific data flow; sub-processor list review with named sub-processors mapped to the data flow; business-continuity and disaster-recovery exercise evidence. Recertification is annual with the option of an on-site or video audit.

**Tier 2 — personal data at scale, non-regulated category.** Vendors that process personal data of identifiable data subjects at material volume but outside the Tier 1 regulated-category list — the analytics platform, the customer-support tool, the marketing-automation suite, the HR-information system below the Workday scale. Tier 2 due-diligence depth: SOC 2 Type II (Security TSC minimum); sub-processor review at onboarding and at each sub-processor change; DPA with standard-contractual-clauses where relevant; evidence of a vendor-side privacy programme (DPO or equivalent, Article 30 processing records at the vendor). Recertification is annual on the SOC 2 refresh plus sub-processor-change review as triggered.

**Tier 3 — aggregate, de-identified, or no personal data.** Vendors that handle no personal data, or only aggregate or truly de-identified data (subject to the re-identification tests in [chapter 06](./06-records-retention-schedule.md)) — infrastructure-metrics vendors in read-only mode, certain developer-tooling vendors, open-source project memberships. Tier 3 due-diligence depth: self-attestation refresh using a short questionnaire; DPA where personal data could ever be incidentally collected (support ticket metadata, employee contact details). Recertification is annual self-attestation with a spot-check sample drawn at random from the Tier 3 cohort each quarter.

The tier is assigned at onboarding and reviewed on data-flow change. A Tier 2 vendor that acquires access to PHI under a feature expansion is a Tier 1 vendor from that point forward, and the programme catches this at the quarterly data-flow mapping review — not when a breach reveals the misclassification. The tiering policy is documented, signed off by the GC and the CISO, and reviewed annually against the data-flow map the privacy-ops team maintains as the Article 30 processing-records substrate.

**Criticality and data-sensitivity are separate axes.** A vendor can be Tier 1 on criticality alone even where its data-sensitivity profile would otherwise classify it as Tier 3 — an identity provider that sees only session metadata is Tier 1 because its outage is the corporation's outage. The programme recognises this and carries two tiering dimensions in the VRM module (data-sensitivity tier and criticality tier) with the effective due-diligence depth driven by the higher of the two. The practitioner default collapses the two dimensions into one Tier 1 bucket for policy simplicity, but the underlying distinction matters for the DR-exercise evidence and the BC-plan review within the Tier 1 dossier.

### DPA / sub-processor-agreement flow-down

The flow-down requirement is statutory, not optional, and the content of the flow-down is prescribed. Three regimes converge on the vendor file; the AI overlay is a fourth lens on top of them.

**GDPR Article 28 — the "same data-protection obligations" rule.** Where a processor engages a sub-processor to carry out processing on behalf of the controller, Article 28(4) requires the processor to impose on the sub-processor "the same data protection obligations as set out in the contract or other legal act between the controller and the processor." The content of the controller-processor DPA is prescribed in Article 28(3)(a)-(h): processing only on documented instructions; confidentiality of authorised personnel; security measures under Article 32; sub-processor controls under Article 28(2) and (4); data-subject-rights assistance; Article 32-36 assistance; deletion or return at end of service; and audit rights. Every one of these obligations must appear in the processor-to-sub-processor contract. The practical artefact is a sub-processor-DPA template that reproduces the controller-DPA substance paragraph-for-paragraph, with the processor positioned as the "controller" for flow-down purposes under Article 28(4). The customer-facing sub-processor list the corporation maintains under its own customer DPAs is the public face of this chain.

**CCPA / CPRA — the service-provider flow-down.** California Civil Code § 1798.140(ag) defines "service provider" and requires that a business, when disclosing personal information to a service provider for a business purpose, enter into a contract that (i) prohibits the service provider from selling or sharing the personal information, (ii) prohibits retention, use, or disclosure for any purpose other than the specified business purpose, (iii) prohibits retention outside the direct business relationship, (iv) prohibits combining the personal information with personal information received from other sources except as expressly permitted, and (v) requires the service provider to comply with applicable CCPA/CPRA obligations and provide the same level of privacy protection. The service-provider-to-sub-service-provider chain inherits the same restrictions. The CPRA Regulations further specify the mandatory contract terms — the vendor-DPA template has a CCPA/CPRA addendum, not a standalone contract, and the addendum reproduces the statutory list verbatim.

**HIPAA — the BAA and sub-BAA chain.** A covered entity that discloses PHI to a business associate must enter into a business-associate agreement under 45 C.F.R. § 164.504(e)(1)(ii). The business associate, under § 164.308(b) of the HIPAA Security Rule, must obtain satisfactory assurances — in the form of a written contract — that each sub-business-associate will safeguard the PHI it receives. The sub-BAA mirrors the BAA; the chain can nest as many levels deep as the data flow requires. See [chapter 04](./04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md) for the BAA content and the covered-entity-vs-business-associate role assignment that drives which template is the parent.

**AI-vendor overlay.** For vendors whose processing involves machine-learning model training or inference, the DPA inherits an AI-specific overlay: a training-on-inputs prohibition (the vendor may not use the controller's data to train its generally-available models absent explicit opt-in), a model-provenance disclosure (the vendor discloses the base models in use and material changes thereto), and an EU AI Act posture where the vendor is a general-purpose AI model provider. The AI overlay lives in its own addendum because its content evolves faster than the base DPA — see [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md) for the overlay template and the GPAI-provider obligations.

**International data-transfer mechanics.** For vendors processing EU, UK, or Swiss personal data outside the EEA / UK / Switzerland, the DPA incorporates the appropriate transfer mechanism — the European Commission Standard Contractual Clauses (the June 2021 modules, selecting the controller-to-processor or processor-to-processor module as the data flow requires); the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the SCCs; the Swiss revised SCCs posture. Post-*Schrems II*, the transfer is accompanied by a Transfer Impact Assessment that evaluates the destination-jurisdiction law and any supplementary technical, contractual, and organisational measures required. The programme owns the TIA artefact as an evidence item; the controller's data-flow documentation under Article 30 references the TIA by vendor and by data-flow line item.

### Evidence collection — the standard dossier

The evidence catalogue is the second half of the vendor file. The DPA governs the contractual obligations; the evidence pack governs the demonstrable implementation of those obligations. The standard Tier 1 dossier:

- **SOC 2 Type II report.** Current-dated and covering the most recent 12-month observation window; delivered under non-disclosure agreement; reviewed for the control-exception section and any qualified opinion. If the observation window has lapsed, a bridge letter (management assertion of no material change) covers the gap for a limited grace period — the common industry-practice ceiling is around 90 days of bridge-letter coverage before buyers expect the new report, extendable with supplementary evidence, and buyer policies vary so the reader should confirm against its own customer-contract commitments before treating a specific number as a hard threshold.
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate and Statement of Applicability.** The certificate alone is insufficient — the SoA identifies which Annex A controls are in scope and which are excluded with justification, and is the artefact the privacy reviewer reads to assess the certification's substance.
- **HIPAA attestation and HITRUST CSF certification.** For vendors processing PHI, a HIPAA compliance attestation plus, at Tier 1 scale, a HITRUST CSF certification (r2 or equivalent current level) demonstrating the control environment maps to the HIPAA Security Rule.
- **PCI-DSS Attestation of Compliance.** For payment-card vendors, the current AOC (self-assessment or ROC-backed depending on merchant level / service-provider level) with the covered services clearly identified.
- **Penetration-test summary letter.** Annual third-party penetration test with a summary letter describing scope, methodology, and remediation of material findings. The full report is rarely shared; the summary letter is the industry-standard disclosure artefact.
- **DPIA / TIA for the specific data flow.** Under GDPR Article 35 the controller runs the DPIA; the vendor's contribution is the assessment of its own technical and organisational measures and, where the transfer is to a third country post-*Schrems II*, a transfer-impact assessment under the Standard Contractual Clauses.
- **Sub-processor list.** Published URL or a signed document listing current sub-processors, their locations, and their functions. Change-notification mechanism (email list, RSS, or portal) is captured so the inventory owner is paged on changes.
- **Incident-response runbook summary.** Enough of the vendor's incident-response posture to confirm the vendor can meet the controller-DPA notification clocks — notification SLAs of 24 to 72 hours are the practitioner default, keyed to GDPR Article 33 awareness.
- **Business-continuity and disaster-recovery evidence.** Evidence of a DR exercise in the prior 12 months, including recovery-time-objective and recovery-point-objective statements aligned to the vendor's service commitments.

**Questionnaire standardisation.** The programme standardises on the Shared Assessments **Standardized Information Gathering (SIG)** questionnaire family — SIG-Lite for Tier 3 and lower-sensitivity Tier 2, SIG-Core or full SIG for higher-sensitivity Tier 2 and Tier 1 — or on the Cloud Security Alliance **Consensus Assessments Initiative Questionnaire (CAIQ)** for cloud-service vendors. Standardising removes the per-vendor bespoke-questionnaire drag and lets the VRM module score responses against a known rubric. A bespoke questionnaire is authored only where the vendor's processing is sufficiently unusual that the standardised questionnaire fails to surface the risk-material items — generally only for highly-tailored data-flow configurations within Tier 1.

**NDA posture on evidence under restriction.** The SOC 2 Type II report, the ISO Statement of Applicability, and the penetration-test summary letter are typically delivered under non-disclosure agreement. The programme maintains a standing NDA template that is executed once per vendor rather than per-artefact, with a durable confidentiality obligation that survives contract termination. Evidence stored in the VRM module inherits the NDA posture — access is restricted to named privacy-ops, security-ops, and legal reviewers, and the audit log in the VRM module demonstrates the NDA perimeter is maintained.

**Exception register.** The programme maintains an exception register for vendors whose evidence falls short of the tier-defined standard but whose use is approved by the Privacy lead and the CISO on a compensating-control basis. Each exception has a stated compensating control (for example, a data-minimisation configuration that reduces the volume of personal data flowing to the vendor below the Tier 1 threshold), a stated expiry date, and an owner responsible for closing the exception by the expiry. The exception register is reviewed at the quarterly audit-committee read-out; exceptions that recur year-on-year are a programme defect that the CISO is accountable for closing or escalating.

### Annual-recertification cadence

Evidence expires. A SOC 2 observation window closes and the clock to the next report starts; an ISO 27001 certificate has a three-year cycle with annual surveillance audits; a penetration-test letter is current for a year and stale thereafter. The programme's recertification cadence is the mechanism that keeps the file from drifting into "we had this evidence in 2024" territory.

**Tier 1 — annual deep review.** The vendor is re-questionnaired on the full SIG or CAIQ; the new SOC 2 Type II is collected; the ISO 27001 surveillance-audit letter is collected; the DPIA or TIA is refreshed where the data flow has changed; the sub-processor list is reconciled against prior-year baseline. For the highest-criticality Tier 1 vendors the review includes an on-site or video audit option — the right is reserved in the DPA under GDPR Article 28(3)(h) and is exercised sparingly (the audit is expensive on both sides) but credibly.

**Tier 2 — annual SOC 2 refresh.** The SOC 2 Type II is collected and reviewed; the sub-processor change-log for the prior year is reviewed; the DPA is checked against the current template for amendment candidates. A fresh questionnaire is not required annually if the prior response is unchanged and the vendor so attests in a short refresh form.

**Tier 3 — annual self-attestation refresh.** The vendor signs a short attestation that the processing scope, data categories, and sub-processors are unchanged from the prior year. The inventory owner draws a random spot-check sample — a modest double-digit percentage of Tier 3 is the common operating default, with the specific rate set by the programme owner against buyer-contract commitments and risk appetite — and runs a Tier 2 depth review on those vendors as a sampling control.

**Automatic flagging.** The VRM module raises a privacy-ops ticket when any of the following fires: a vendor's SOC 2 observation window closes without a bridge letter received; a bridge letter ages past the grace period without a new report; an ISO 27001 surveillance-audit date passes without a new letter; a penetration-test letter ages past twelve months; a sub-processor list changes without a corresponding review entry. The ticket is routed to the named inventory owner and escalates to the Privacy lead on a defined SLA. The alert surface is the single most valuable automation feature of the tooling stack.

**Material-change triggers between recertifications.** The annual cadence is the floor; material changes between recertifications trigger an off-cycle review. Triggers include: the vendor announces a merger, acquisition, or change of control (ownership change may alter the sub-processor chain and the data-residency posture); the vendor adds or removes a sub-processor on the notified list; the vendor announces a material security incident (disclosed publicly or privately notified under the DPA breach-notification clause); the vendor publishes a material change to its privacy policy or DPA template; the data-flow between the controller and the vendor changes (new data categories, new volume tier, new jurisdiction). The material-change trigger shortens the review cycle from "annual" to "immediate" and the VRM module routes the ticket on the trigger-fire event rather than the calendar date.

**Board and executive reporting.** The programme produces a quarterly dashboard for the audit committee covering: total vendor count by tier, new vendors added and offboarded in the quarter, recertifications completed on time and overdue, material-change triggers fired, and any Tier 1 vendors with lapsed evidence. The dashboard is a required input to the ERM register ([mod-112 chapter 01](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md)) and to the board's audit-committee read-out ([mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/)).

### Tooling stack

Two tool categories matter. The first is the dedicated privacy-and-consent platform with a VRM module — **OneTrust** (the broadest footprint; Vendor Risk Management is a module within the OneTrust Trust Intelligence platform), **Osano**, **Ketch**, **Transcend**. The second is the compliance-automation platform with a VRM module — **Vanta** Vendor Risk and **Drata** Vendor Risk — both of which couple the vendor-risk workflow to the same evidence-collection pipeline that runs the SOC 2 and ISO 27001 readiness programmes (see [chapter 05](./05-soc-2-audit-readiness-programme.md)).

Dimensions to evaluate across the stack:

- **Continuous-monitoring integrations.** Can the platform pull the vendor's SOC 2 and ISO certificate directly from the vendor's trust portal or auditor, or does it require the vendor to upload the documents manually? Direct-pull integrations materially reduce the operational drag at recertification time. Feature-parity between the platforms on this dimension changes release-to-release; integration coverage should be confirmed against each platform's current integration catalogue before relying on a specific claim.
- **Questionnaire automation.** Can the platform send a SIG, SIG-Lite, or CAIQ questionnaire to a new vendor, receive the response, and score it against a rubric? Can it pre-fill the questionnaire from prior responses on file? Can it maintain a corporation-side response library for the reciprocal case where *this* corporation is answering an enterprise buyer's questionnaire?
- **Evidence-expiry alerting.** Does the platform raise the recertification ticket described in the prior subsection, and does it route the ticket to a named human on an SLA?
- **Integration with the procurement system of record.** Is there a bidirectional sync with Ironclad, Zip, Coupa, or the CLM in use? New-vendor records in the procurement system should create VRM records automatically, and VRM-approval states should block or unblock the CLM counter-signature step.
- **AI-vendor overlay support.** Does the platform surface the AI-specific questionnaire items (training-on-inputs, model-provenance, GPAI posture) as a first-class overlay, or does it require the questionnaire owner to append a bespoke section? See [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md).

The governance test for tool selection is the integration density with the corporation's existing CLM and compliance-automation estate. A VRM module that duplicates the evidence feed already running inside Vanta or Drata adds overhead without new capability; a VRM module that unifies the privacy-and-security evidence flow across SOC 2 readiness and vendor-risk management is a reuse of infrastructure that already exists.

**The build-versus-buy question at Series B.** A Series-A corporation with fewer than approximately 30 vendors can run the programme out of the CLM alone, with the evidence artefacts stored in a Drive folder structure and the recertification cadence driven by calendar reminders. Above roughly 50 vendors the manual cadence fails — bridge-letter lapses are missed, sub-processor changes go unlogged, and the quarterly audit reveals gaps the privacy-ops team cannot close inside the audit cycle. The Series-B transition is from manual-with-CLM to automation-with-VRM; the governance decision is which VRM platform rather than whether to adopt one. The corporation that delays the decision past the vendor-count threshold accumulates an evidence-backlog that is expensive to clear retroactively.

**Trust-portal reciprocity.** The compliance-automation platforms also host the *reciprocal* case — the corporation's own trust portal, through which enterprise buyers pull the corporation's SOC 2, ISO, and DPA artefacts. Running the same platform on both sides of the relationship (Vanta or Drata for the reader's own compliance posture; Vanta or Drata for the vendor-risk programme) is the standard integration pattern. The trust-portal substrate is the counter-party to this chapter's programme: the reader's own evidence feed to the reader's enterprise buyers mirrors the evidence feed this chapter demands from the reader's vendors.

## Concrete example

**Northwind Analytics, Inc.** is a Delaware C-corp Series-B SaaS corporation selling a product-analytics platform to mid-market and enterprise customers. ARR is in the mid-eight-figures, headcount is approximately 180, and the customer base spans US, UK, EU, and Canadian jurisdictions with a nascent APAC presence. The corporation has a SOC 2 Type II and is tracking to ISO 27001 within the next audit cycle (see [chapter 05](./05-soc-2-audit-readiness-programme.md)). The GC has asked privacy-ops to stand up a formal vendor-privacy programme ahead of the next enterprise-buyer-driven security review season.

The privacy-ops lead begins with the inventory build. The procurement system of record is Ironclad; the VRM module is Vanta Vendor Risk, selected because the existing Vanta deployment is already running the SOC 2 evidence feed. The Ironclad-Vanta bidirectional sync is configured such that any new contract-request record in Ironclad creates a draft VRM record in Vanta, pre-populated with the counterparty name and the intake-form responses. Over a three-week discovery exercise the team reconciles the procurement spend-ledger, the SSO application catalogue, the AWS account billing records, and the finance-team card-expense report against the draft inventory, and lands on a baseline of 112 vendors with any form of data-processing relationship.

Tiering the top 12 — the vendors to which the enterprise buyer's infosec review will inevitably ask — produces the following picture:

- **AWS** — Tier 1. Primary infrastructure provider; processes essentially all customer personal data in transit and at rest; critical-availability exposure.
- **Snowflake** — Tier 1. Primary customer-data warehouse; processes identifiable personal data at the full corpus scale.
- **Datadog** — Tier 1. Observability platform; logs may incidentally contain personal data; critical-availability exposure for operational visibility.
- **Okta** — Tier 1. Workforce identity provider; critical-availability exposure, authentication substrate for the corporation itself.
- **Stripe** — Tier 1. Payments processor; cardholder data handled through Stripe's PCI scope.
- **Workday** — Tier 1. HR information system; processes employee personal data including sensitive categories.
- **Segment** — Tier 2. Customer-data pipeline; processes identifiable personal data at scale but not in a regulated category.
- **Zendesk** — Tier 2. Customer-support platform; processes support-ticket content including personal data.
- **HubSpot** — Tier 2. Marketing automation; processes prospect and customer personal data.
- **Ironclad** — Tier 2. CLM; processes contract content which may include personal data (counterparty signatories).
- **Mixpanel** — Tier 3. Product-analytics vendor used by Northwind itself for internal product telemetry on de-identified event data.
- **Anthropic** — Tier 1 with AI overlay. AI-model vendor; processes customer-support-draft and content-generation prompts; the AI overlay governs training-on-inputs posture and model-provenance disclosure. See [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md).

The Tier 1 due-diligence dossier for Snowflake, as the data-warehouse exemplar, is the first pack the team authors. The file contains: the signed Snowflake DPA with Standard Contractual Clauses for the EU and UK data flows; the signed BAA because a subset of customers process PHI in Snowflake and Northwind has elected to offer a HIPAA-eligible configuration (see [chapter 04](./04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md)); the current SOC 2 Type II report, SOC 1 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate with SoA, ISO 27017 and ISO 27018 certificates, PCI-DSS AOC, and HITRUST CSF certification as published on Snowflake's trust portal; the current penetration-test summary letter; the sub-processor list with change-notification subscription; and the DPIA for the specific Northwind data-flow pattern (customer event ingestion, derivation of aggregate analytics, retention and deletion mechanics mapped to the records-retention schedule from [chapter 06](./06-records-retention-schedule.md)). The TIA covers the EU-to-US transfer under the current SCC module and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework posture where relevant. The dossier is reviewed and signed off by the privacy-ops lead; the file is recertified on the anniversary date each year. Framing throughout the file is general — the Snowflake posture is treated as typical of major Tier 1 data-warehouse vendors in the category, not as a specific posture claim about the vendor; any specific vendor-posture claim should be verified against the vendor's own current trust-portal disclosures before publication in any procurement-facing artefact.

The sub-processor-list reconciliation is where the programme pays for itself three months later. Snowflake notifies of a new sub-processor on the sub-processor-change mailing list; Vanta Vendor Risk raises a ticket; privacy-ops reviews the new sub-processor's SOC 2 and determines it processes personal data in a region Northwind's DPA requires to be covered by SCCs; the DPA addendum is amended within the 30-day sub-processor-objection window specified in the Snowflake DPA; no customer-facing incident results. Absent the programme the sub-processor change would have been noticed, if at all, during the next annual review — nine months downstream of the change.

The Anthropic record carries the AI overlay in addition to the base DPA. The overlay confirms training-on-inputs is disabled for the API traffic Northwind sends under its enterprise agreement; records the base model family in use for each production workflow and the data-retention window the vendor applies to API inputs; and confirms the vendor's posture under the EU AI Act general-purpose-AI-model-provider obligations where Northwind's European customers are in scope. The AI-overlay evidence is reviewed on the AI vendor's model-release cadence rather than the annual calendar — a new base model release is a material-change trigger. See [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md) for the overlay substance and [mod-109 chapter 06](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/06-ai-vendor-contracts-and-ai-dpa-pattern.md) for the contractual template.

Six months into the programme, the enterprise-buyer security review arrives. The Fortune-500 buyer's infosec team sends a SIG-Core questionnaire; Vanta pre-fills approximately 70 per cent of the response from the standing library and routes the remaining items to the Security lead and the Privacy lead for completion. The buyer asks, as expected, "how do you manage *your* vendors?" and Northwind produces the tiering policy, the Tier 1 vendor inventory, the sub-processor-change-notification sample log for the quarter, and the Vanta screenshot showing the recertification cadence. The question closes in two business days. Absent the programme the same question would have taken four to six weeks to answer and would have exposed a Tier 1 vendor with lapsed SOC 2 evidence that the buyer's infosec team would have raised as a blocking finding.

## Summary

- The vendor-privacy-risk-assessment programme is a privacy-ops-owned operating control; its purpose is to govern the regulator-inherited liability for processor and sub-processor conduct under GDPR Article 28, HIPAA § 164.504(e), and CCPA/CPRA service-provider obligations.
- The inventory is anchored in the procurement system of record and mirrored in a Vendor-Risk-Management module; one named owner (legal-ops or privacy-ops) is accountable for its completeness and freshness.
- Onboarding and offboarding are gated — contract counter-signature requires a completed new-vendor intake, contract termination requires a deletion-certification and sub-processor-removal artefact.
- Tiering is three tiers by data-sensitivity: Tier 1 (regulated or critical-availability), Tier 2 (personal data at scale, non-regulated), Tier 3 (aggregate, de-identified, or no personal data). Due-diligence depth and recertification cadence are set per tier.
- Flow-down is statutory, not optional — the sub-processor DPA reproduces Article 28(3)(a)-(h); the sub-service-provider contract reproduces the Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.140(ag) restrictions; the sub-BAA mirrors the BAA under 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(b).
- Evidence standardises on the SIG family or the CSA CAIQ for questionnaires, and on a defined dossier (SOC 2 Type II under NDA, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certificate + SoA, HIPAA/HITRUST attestations, PCI-DSS AOC, penetration-test summary, DPIA/TIA, sub-processor list, IR runbook summary, BC/DR evidence).
- Recertification is annual, cadenced by tier, with automatic flagging when SOC 2 observation windows lapse, ISO surveillance dates pass, penetration-test letters age out, or sub-processor lists change outside the review cycle.
- Tooling is OneTrust / Osano / Ketch / Transcend on the privacy-platform side and Vanta / Drata on the compliance-automation side; the selection criterion is integration density with the corporation's existing CLM and SOC 2 evidence estate rather than isolated feature claims.
- The programme hands off to [mod-109 chapter 03](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md) for structural DPA language, to [chapter 09](./09-ai-vendor-privacy-overlay.md) for the AI-vendor overlay, to [mod-114](../mod-114-operations-function-design/) for procurement-function design, and to the security-learning curricula for the control substance behind the evidence.
