# mod-110 — Privacy, Data Governance & Sector Compliance

> The privacy-programme operating model (DPO / privacy counsel / privacy engineering, Record of Processing Activities, data-classification matrix, privacy-by-design gate), the GDPR / UK GDPR regulatory substance under Articles 5 / 6 / 13–14 / 15–22 / 28 / 30 / 32 / 33–34 / 35 / 44–49, the CCPA / CPRA stack and the US state-privacy patchwork, the sector overlays (HIPAA / HITECH, GLBA, FERPA, COPPA, TCPA / CAN-SPAM), the breach-response playbook across GDPR Article 33, the 50-state breach-notification matrix, and SEC Item 1.05 of Form 8-K, the consent / preference-management / cookie-governance programme, the SOC 2 / ISO 27001 audit-readiness programme and the Vanta / Drata / Secureframe compliance-automation choice, and the AI-system privacy governance layer (training-opt-out verification, Article 22 automated-decision-making analysis, EU AI Act downstream-provider obligations) — the privacy and data-governance infrastructure the corporation needs so every enterprise DPA, every regulator inquiry, every Fortune-500 vendor-security review, and every M&A buyer's privacy counsel finds a programme that is actually run rather than a binder of policies no-one has read.

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) stood up the Delaware C-corp, the officer-authority framework, and the board-resolution mechanics that give data-processing commitments their force. [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) authored the employment-contract substrate — PIIA, confidentiality, DTSA whistleblower notice — against which the employee-data processing posture is drafted. [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) wired the HRIS / PEO stack that is itself a privacy processor of the largest employee-data store in the corporation. [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/) wrote the internal AI acceptable-use policy and the data-class matrix that governs what employees may put into what tool. [mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/) authored the **structural** Data Processing Addendum and the AI-DPA pattern — the controller / processor paragraph, the Annex-I / Annex-II shape, the Model-SCC schedule, the breach-notification clause, and the audit-rights section.

What none of those modules does is answer the substantive questions every Fortune-500 procurement team, every data-protection authority, every California Attorney General inquiry, every HIPAA Office for Civil Rights letter, every HHS breach-reporting workflow, every Series-C technical-diligence reviewer, and every SOC 2 Type II auditor eventually asks: *what is the lawful basis for each category of processing, where does the personal data physically sit, how do you respond to a Chapter III data-subject right within one month, how did you decide that your transfers from Frankfurt to US-East-1 are adequate, and can the DPO show the Record of Processing Activities on the day the ICO or the CNIL walks in?*

This module owns that regulatory-substance layer.

Privacy, data governance, and sector compliance decompose into eight operating decisions the corporation has to make on purpose, not by drift:

1. **The privacy-programme operating model.** The role architecture (Data Protection Officer where required under GDPR Article 37; privacy counsel; privacy engineering; a privacy programme office at Series-B / growth); the data-classification matrix (public / internal / confidential / restricted / regulated, mapped to personal-data categories and special-category data under GDPR Article 9); the Record of Processing Activities (RoPA) under GDPR Article 30 and the data-inventory discipline that keeps it current; data-flow mapping with producing / consuming systems and the sub-processor chain; the privacy-by-design and privacy-by-default gate under Article 25 wired into the product-development lifecycle; the Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) under Article 35 and the trigger list; and the privacy-metrics reporting cadence to the board or the AI-risk sub-committee set up in [mod-111 chapter 04](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md).
2. **GDPR / UK GDPR regulatory depth.** The six lawful bases under Article 6 and the special-category conditions under Article 9; the transparency obligations under Articles 13–14 and the layered-notice pattern; the data-subject-rights machinery under Articles 15–22 (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, automated decision-making); the processor obligations under Article 28 and the content-list the DPA tracks; the Article 30 RoPA; the Article 32 security of processing and the Technical and Organisational Measures (TOMs); the Article 33 breach-notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours and the Article 34 communication to data subjects without undue delay; the Article 35 DPIA; and the Chapter V international-transfer stack — Commission adequacy decisions, the 2021/914 Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK IDTA and UK Addendum, the Swiss FDPIC addendum, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework under Decision (EU) 2023/1795, and the Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) under the *Schrems II* analytical framework.
3. **CCPA / CPRA and the US state-privacy patchwork.** California's Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq. with the service-provider / contractor / third-party distinctions at § 1798.140(ag) / (ah) / (ai); the sensitive-personal-information carve-outs and the right-to-limit-use at § 1798.121; the opt-out-of-sale-and-share machinery and Global Privacy Control; the California Privacy Protection Agency final regulations and risk-assessment / cybersecurity-audit / ADMT draft regulations; the state-by-state map covering Colorado (CPA), Virginia (VCDPA), Connecticut (CTDPA), Utah (UCPA), Oregon (OCPA), Texas (TDPSA), Montana (MCDPA), Tennessee (TIPA), Delaware (DPDPA), Iowa (ICDPA), New Hampshire (NHDPA), New Jersey (NJDPA), Minnesota (MCDPA), Maryland (MODPA), Indiana (INCDPA), Kentucky (KCDPA), Nebraska (NDPA), and Rhode Island, with the sensitive-data-opt-in vs. opt-out dividing line and the universal-opt-out-mechanism obligation; and the operating pattern that collapses the patchwork into one controller-side configuration so the engineering team does not maintain 20 variants. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current list of enacted comprehensive state privacy statutes and effective dates before quoting as canonical; the list is in motion and new statutes enact approximately quarterly. -->
4. **Sector-specific privacy overlays.** HIPAA / HITECH for Protected Health Information — the Privacy Rule at 45 C.F.R. Part 164 Subpart E, the Security Rule at 45 C.F.R. Part 314 for financial information and the 2023-amendment incident-notification requirement; FERPA at 20 U.S.C. § 1232g for education records; COPPA at 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506 and 16 C.F.R. Part 312 for under-13 online services and the FTC's 2024 amendments; TCPA at 47 U.S.C. § 227 and the FCC's 2024 one-to-one consent rule (currently stayed) <!-- needs-research: confirm the current status of the FCC's "one-to-one consent" rulemaking after the Eleventh Circuit's January 2025 decision in *Insurance Marketing Coalition v. The job here is to identify which overlays apply to which product line and to fold them into the single privacy programme rather than operating five parallel programmes.

6. **Consent, preference-management, and cookie governance.** The ePrivacy Directive and the national transpositions, the EDPB guidelines on consent and cookie banners (2022 cookie-banner guidelines, 2023 deceptive-design-patterns guidelines), the CPRA prohibition on dark patterns at § 1798.140(l), the Global Privacy Control signal at § 1798.135(b) and the state statutes that recognise universal opt-out mechanisms, the preference-management-platform choice (OneTrust / TrustArc / Didomi / Usercentrics / Transcend) <!-- needs-research: confirm current vendor feature parity and pricing before treating a specific selection as canonical. --> , the COPPA verifiable-parental-consent flow for under-13 users, the TCPA prior-express-written-consent capture for SMS marketing, and the record-of-consent retention discipline.

8. **AI-system privacy governance.** The AI-system DPIA pattern under GDPR Article 35 and the EDPB guidance on AI; the Article 22 automated-decision-making analysis and the human-in-the-loop design that falls out of it; the training-data lawful-basis determination and the EDPB December 2024 Opinion 28/2024 on legitimate interests for AI training; the downstream-controller DPA with the foundation-model provider, including the training-opt-out verification against the vendor's published posture, zero-data-retention election where available, and the logging / retention configuration; the EU AI Act downstream-provider / deployer obligations — Article 26 deployer obligations for high-risk systems, Article 50 transparency obligations for certain AI systems, Chapter V general-purpose-AI-model obligations for upstream providers, and the Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment where applicable — as the regulatory depth underneath the AI-DPA pattern authored in [mod-109 chapter 06](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/06-ai-vendor-contracts-and-ai-dpa-pattern.md); the inference-time logging / retention / review posture; and the interface with the AI-risk sub-committee.

A ninth problem-space closes the loop:

9. **The ownership-boundary map.** The reference that decides — for any privacy / data-governance / compliance question — whether it belongs to mod-110, to a sibling module (mod-101 for entity-level data-processing authorisations; mod-103 / mod-104 for employee-data processing and the HRIS-side data posture; mod-108 for the internal AI acceptable-use policy and the data-class matrix; mod-109 for the structural DPA / AI-DPA clauses and the commercial-contract surface area; mod-111 for board-level privacy reporting, the AI-risk sub-committee, and the compliance-system Caremark posture; mod-112 for the cyber-insurance tower and the post-incident indemnity recovery; mod-113 for the international-privacy overlay and country-specific regimes beyond GDPR; mod-114 for the privacy-adjacent systems selection and procurement), or to a non-role track (`security-learning` for security-engineering substance underneath the audit-readiness programme, `head-of-ai-governance-learning` / `chief-ai-officer-learning` / `ai-risk-engineer-learning` for AI model-risk methodology and evaluation-suite construction that sits underneath the AI-system privacy governance layer, `startup-exit-curriculum` for the privacy / compliance diligence workstreams at M&A or IPO).

Get this right and the corporation compounds — the DPO (or the privacy-programme owner where Article 37 does not require a formal DPO) can produce the RoPA, the DPIA register, the TIA library, and the consent-management records on the day the ICO or the CNIL asks; the state-by-state sensitive-data configuration collapses into one switch rather than 20 engineering backlogs; a HIPAA BAA is executed with every covered-entity customer before the first PHI record is written; a breach-response playbook that runs within the 72-hour GDPR clock and the four-business-day SEC Item 1.05 clock without improvisation; a SOC 2 Type II report that covers a 12-month observation window and arrives in the Series-C data room without a bridge letter; a cookie-and-consent posture that survives a California AG inquiry and a Berlin BfDI audit on the same banner; an AI-system DPIA for every material model-in-the-loop product line; and a board-level privacy report that the audit committee or AI-risk sub-committee can review on a quarterly cadence without the GC rewriting it from scratch each time. Get it wrong and the corporation absorbs the predictable failure set — a GDPR Article 83 fine at 4% of worldwide turnover for a transfer-impact assessment that was never run, a California CPPA enforcement action for a universal-opt-out signal that was never honoured, a HIPAA settlement with OCR for a BAA that was never countersigned, a 50-state breach-notification exercise that misses the three states with regulator-notification clocks shorter than the general consumer-notification clock, a cookie banner that the EDPB-aligned authorities reject for dark patterns on day one of a privacy sweep, a SOC 2 Type II that lapses between observation windows and the sales team quietly ships bridge letters that fail the Fortune-500 buyer's own vendor-security policy, and an AI-system that is quietly making Article 22 automated decisions about creditworthiness or housing that the DPIA never considered.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical multi-stage corporation, produce and defend:

1. **A privacy-programme operating model** — the role architecture with DPO / privacy counsel / privacy engineering, the data-classification matrix mapped to Article 9 special-category data, a Record of Processing Activities for every material processing activity, a DPIA trigger list and register, and a quarterly privacy-metrics report to the audit / AI-risk sub-committee.
2. **A GDPR / UK GDPR compliance substrate** — the six-lawful-bases determination for every processing activity, the Article 13 / 14 layered-notice stack, a data-subject-rights fulfilment workflow that meets the one-month clock, an Article 28 processor configuration consistent with the mod-109 DPA, a Chapter V transfer-mechanism stack (DPF + SCCs + UK Addendum + Swiss addendum) with completed Transfer Impact Assessments, and a Technical and Organisational Measures schedule.
