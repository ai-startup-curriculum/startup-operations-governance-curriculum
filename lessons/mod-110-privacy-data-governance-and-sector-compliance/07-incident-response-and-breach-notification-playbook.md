# 7. The incident-response and breach-notification playbook — severity taxonomy, team composition, notification decision-tree, and the cyber-carrier interface

> An incident-response runbook is a document you write when you are calm, so that you can execute when you are not. The severity taxonomy, the team roster, the notification decision-tree, the four template families, the post-incident-review cadence, and the carrier-notification first-hour discipline are the artefacts that convert a 0300 page into a coordinated 72-hour response rather than a panicked three-day improvisation.

## Motivation

The GDPR Article 33 awareness clock runs in hours, not days. The HIPAA Breach Notification Rule 60-day individual-and-HHS clock runs in days, not months. The SEC Item 1.05 four-business-day materiality clock runs in business days from the registrant's materiality determination. The cyber-carrier notification clock is a policy condition and, if missed, is a coverage-defence pathway the carrier will not waive. A Sev-0 incident puts all four clocks in motion simultaneously, and every clock has a different definition of start, a different notice content, and a different audience.

A startup that writes the runbook *during* its first real incident will miss at least one of those clocks. The runbook has to exist on the shelf, be rehearsed through at least one tabletop per year, be socialised with every person whose name appears in it, and be revision-controlled against the current state of the statutes it cites. This chapter is the authoring and operating specification for that runbook. It owns the *sequencing* of the response and the regulatory-notification substance. It does **not** own the carrier interface (deferred to [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)) nor the forensic-engineering substance (deferred to the `security-learning` and `ai-infra-security-solutions` curricula).

A second reason the runbook has to exist before the incident is the privilege posture. Every substantive decision about scope, root cause, and remediation is made in channels covered by attorney-client privilege and work-product doctrine, and the privilege is only reliable if the breach-counsel engagement is in place *before* the forensic firm starts generating work product. A Series-B corporation that engages its usual outside counsel on Tuesday morning to "help us figure out what happened" — without first pre-signing a breach-counsel engagement — produces forensic reports that are discoverable in the inevitable follow-on class-action litigation. The runbook pre-wires the privilege so the privilege attaches at T + 01:00, not at T + 24:00 when outside counsel is finally contacted.

## Core concepts

### The incident-severity taxonomy

A severity classification is the first page of the runbook because every downstream decision — who gets paged, which bridge gets opened, whether the CEO is in the room — keys off it. Three classes are the practitioner default; more than three is noise and fewer is undifferentiated.

**Sev-0 — customer-impacting outage or confirmed data exfiltration.** Threshold criteria, any one of which triggers Sev-0:

- Data loss confirmed (not suspected) — personal data of data subjects has left the trust boundary.
- Customer-facing impact — production outage or service degradation visible to paying customers.
- Special-category data involved (Art. 9 GDPR — health, biometric, children, etc.) regardless of volume.
- Regulated-sector data involved — PHI under HIPAA, cardholder data under PCI-DSS, NPI under GLBA, or regulated-financial data under NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500.

Sev-0 paging and cadence:

- Executive paging tree fires immediately — CEO, COO, CISO, GC, Head of Communications.
- 24-hour war-room cadence with a named incident commander on rotation.
- Mandatory CEO or COO attendance at scheduled briefings.
- Board audit-committee notification within 24 hours of classification (see [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/)).
- Pre-retained breach counsel engaged within the first hour; cyber-carrier notified through counsel within the first two hours.

The Sev-0 cadence has a shape:

- Hourly briefing at T + 0–12 — raw status, no polish.
- Every two hours at T + 12–24 — scope firming up, first notification drafts in review.
- Every four hours at T + 24 onwards until facts stabilise and the incident moves into supplemental-notification cadence.
- Daily executive update to the CEO and board liaison.
- A running internal timeline file, authored under privilege, that is the single source of truth for every subsequent regulator or customer narrative.

The war-room has one incident commander on at a time; hand-offs between commanders are logged with an explicit "I have the conn" moment so no two commanders are directing containment simultaneously.

**Sev-1 — contained breach or exposure.** Threshold criteria: data loss suspected but not confirmed, or confirmed but without exfiltration to an external party; limited customer-facing impact; no special-category or regulated-sector data; blast radius contained by the technical team. Paging: incident-commander page; VP-level named sponsor (VP Engineering or VP Security as the operational owner, with the CISO as the executive sponsor). Business-hours cadence after the first 24 hours; 24-hour cadence during the initial containment window. The Privacy lead is engaged at Sev-1 regardless of whether notification is ultimately required — the Article 33(5) internal register entry is opened, and the decision not to notify externally is itself a documented position that the supervisory authority may later test.

**Sev-2 — anomaly or near-miss.** Threshold criteria: anomalous signal under investigation; near-miss (an incident that would have been Sev-1 or Sev-0 had a control not caught it); no confirmed data impact; no customer-facing impact. Paging: routine ticket triage. A blameless post-mortem is optional at Sev-2 but recommended for near-misses — the signal-to-noise discipline depends on learning from them. Sev-2 incidents aggregate into the quarterly metric pack that feeds the ERM register ([mod-112 chapter 01](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md)) and the board audit-committee read-out ([mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/)).

The classifier is the on-call incident commander, promoted (never demoted) during the incident. A Sev-1 that reveals exfiltration becomes a Sev-0 and the executive paging tree activates immediately; a Sev-0 does not de-escalate to Sev-1 mid-incident, even if the early scope assessment proves conservative. The asymmetric-promotion rule is deliberate — a prematurely downgraded incident is the single most common failure mode in post-incident reviews, because downgrading closes the war-room before the full scope is understood.

### The incident-response-team composition

The runbook names humans, not functions. The five seats on the incident-response team:

- **The Security lead — incident commander.** Operational owner of detection, containment, eradication, and recovery. Runs the war-room, owns the technical timeline, is the single point of coordination between the forensic workstream and the regulatory/legal workstream. At Sev-0 the Security lead is a senior security engineer or the CISO; the title matters less than the authority to direct both employees and outside forensic vendors.
- **The Privacy lead — regulator-notification clock owner.** Runs the Article 33 72-hour clock, the HIPAA 60-day clock, the state breach-notification matrix, and the DSAR-fallout queue (incidents produce a surge of Article 15 access requests from affected data subjects). The Privacy lead is the Data Protection Officer where one is appointed under Art. 37 GDPR, otherwise the privacy counsel or privacy-ops lead.
- **The Legal lead — privilege, carrier liaison, outside counsel.** Owns the privilege posture (see [chapter 06 on breach counsel engagement under privilege](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)), owns the cyber-carrier liaison (first-hour notice, panel-counsel election, panel-forensic selection), and owns the outside-counsel engagement. The Legal lead is the General Counsel or an in-house privacy/security counsel at scale; at seed/Series-A scale it is the fractional GC and the pre-retained breach-counsel firm.
- **The Communications lead — customer and press messaging.** Owns the customer-notification draft, the press release or media statement, the social-media and status-page cadence, and the employee all-hands messaging. Reports to the Legal lead on substance (nothing substantive goes out without privileged review) but owns the channels.
- **The Executive sponsor — CEO or COO for Sev-0.** Decision authority on customer-facing posture, on public disclosure timing, on ransom-payment questions (jointly with Legal and the carrier), and on commercial remediation (credit monitoring, service credits, contract-level cure). The sponsor is accountable upward to the board; the incident commander is accountable sideways to the sponsor.

**The outside-forensic-firm pre-retainer.** Every Sev-0-capable corporation has a pre-signed outside-counsel engagement letter with a breach-counsel firm (named on the carrier's panel — see [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)) and, under that counsel, a pre-arranged forensic-firm engagement structure. The pattern:

- The breach counsel (a firm of the Kirkland / Latham / Covington / Mullen Coughlin / Baker Hostetler class — the firm-names vary and the carrier's panel will constrain the choice; panel rosters shift per carrier and per renewal, so the operative list is whatever the corporation's current cyber-policy declarations page names) is engaged first.
- Breach counsel then engages the forensic firm (Mandiant / CrowdStrike / Stroz Friedberg / Kroll / Arete / Charles River Associates — names vary by panel and preference; daily-rate ranges likewise vary by firm, incident type, and year, and are set in the engagement letter at the time of retention rather than quotable from this chapter) *on behalf of* the insured.
- The forensic engagement letter names breach counsel as the engaging party and states the purpose is to provide legal advice to the insured — the standard privilege-preservation structure.
- Forensic work product is directed by counsel for the purpose of legal advice and is covered by attorney-client privilege and work-product doctrine.
- Engaging forensics directly — without counsel in the signature block — leaves the forensic report discoverable in subsequent litigation.

**Named rotations, not functional placeholders.** The runbook names specific humans — "Alex Chen, on-call incident commander; backup Priya Shah" — not generic function titles. The paging tree is validated against the on-call rota every quarter; names of departed employees are a common runbook defect that surfaces only during the first real 0300 page. The out-of-band contact list (personal mobile numbers, Signal handles, home addresses for courier delivery of forensic imaging drives if the corporate network is compromised) is maintained under the same cadence and stored in a location *not* dependent on the primary identity provider — because a Sev-0 incident may include compromise of the identity-provider itself, and a runbook accessible only via the compromised SSO is not accessible at all.

### The notification decision-tree by jurisdiction and record-type

The notification matrix is the Privacy lead's working artefact. It has three axes: jurisdiction of the affected data subjects, record-type (personal data vs. special-category vs. PHI vs. financial NPI vs. cardholder data), and legal entity acting as controller vs. processor.

**GDPR — Article 33 and Article 34.**

- **Article 33 — supervisory authority within 72 hours of awareness.** The clock starts when "the controller becomes aware" of the breach — awareness at any employee authorised to triage security incidents will usually suffice, so the runbook's internal escalation-to-privacy-office step is the clock-start event. The notice content follows Art. 33(3): nature of the breach, categories and approximate numbers of data subjects and records, the DPO's contact point, likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed to address the breach. Article 33(4) expressly permits **phased notification** — a partial notice at the 72-hour deadline followed by updates as the facts develop — which is how most Sev-0 notices are actually filed, because forensic scope is rarely complete inside 72 hours.
- **Article 33(5) — the internal breach register.** Regardless of whether the breach is externally notifiable, the controller must document it in an internal register sufficient to allow the supervisory authority to verify compliance with Article 33. The register is the first document the DPA asks for in a post-incident investigation.
- **Article 34 — data-subject notification.** Where the breach is likely to result in a **high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons**, the controller must notify affected data subjects "without undue delay." Article 34(3) carve-outs: encryption rendering the data unintelligible, subsequent measures ensuring the high risk is no longer likely to materialise, or disproportionate effort (in which case a public communication substitutes). The high-risk determination is the controller's call in the first instance but is reviewable by the supervisory authority under Art. 34(4), which may *compel* notification where the controller has elected not to notify. The practitioner default is to document the high-risk analysis in the incident file whether or not notification is made, because the supervisory authority will ask for it in the Art. 33 follow-up.

**HIPAA — Breach Notification Rule (45 C.F.R. §§ 164.400–414).** For covered entities and business associates handling PHI:

- **Individual notification within 60 days** of discovery of the breach (45 C.F.R. § 164.404). First-class mail unless the individual has agreed to electronic notice.
- **HHS Office for Civil Rights notification.** Breaches affecting fewer than 500 individuals are reported annually in a log; breaches affecting 500 or more are reported to HHS OCR **contemporaneously with the individual notice** (within 60 days) (45 C.F.R. § 164.408).
- **Prominent media notice** for breaches affecting 500 or more residents of a state or jurisdiction (45 C.F.R. § 164.406).
- **Business-associate-to-covered-entity** notification without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days (45 C.F.R. § 164.410); the BAA customarily tightens this to a shorter contractual clock (see [mod-109 customer-contract DPA breach-notification clause](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/) — usually a 24–72-hour processor-to-controller window).

**CCPA / state breach-notification matrix.** Every US state plus DC, Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, and Guam has a breach-notification statute. The matrix is a working spreadsheet, not a paragraph in a runbook; relevant anchor points:

- **New York — NYDFS 23 NYCRR Part 500 § 500.17(a).** Covered financial-services entities must notify the Superintendent as promptly as possible but no later than **72 hours** after determining that a "cybersecurity incident" has occurred at the covered entity, its affiliates, or a third-party service provider. The [Second Amendment to Part 500](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/12/rf23_nycrr_part_500_amend02_20231101.pdf), finalised on 1 November 2023, introduced the distinct "cybersecurity incident" definition that scopes the § 500.17(a) trigger and added a parallel 24-hour clock for ransom-payment notice with a 30-day written-explanation follow-up. A parallel notification obligation under the NY SHIELD Act (General Business Law § 899-aa) applies to non-NYDFS-regulated entities holding private information of New York residents.
- **Massachusetts — 201 CMR 17.** Comprehensive data-security regulation requiring every business that owns or licenses personal information about a Massachusetts resident to maintain a written information security programme (WISP), with associated breach-notification duties under M.G.L. c. 93H. Notification runs to the Massachusetts Attorney General, the Office of Consumer Affairs and Business Regulation (OCABR), and each affected resident "as soon as practicable and without unreasonable delay"; the 93H notice form must state whether the business maintains a WISP and describe the steps taken or planned to address the breach, and must not include the compromised personal information itself.
- **Washington — RCW 19.255.** Notification to affected residents and to the Washington Attorney General for breaches affecting 500 or more Washington residents. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current RCW 19.255 notification clock (currently 30 days from discovery, verify). -->
- **Texas — Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053.** Notification to affected residents without unreasonable delay and in any event within **60 days** of discovery; notification to the Texas AG required for breaches affecting 250 or more residents. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current § 521.053 individual and AG thresholds. -->
- **California — Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.29 and 1798.82.** Notification in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay; AG notification for breaches affecting 500 or more California residents. The CCPA/CPRA private right of action under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.150 is a separate litigation exposure, available where specified categories of personal information are exfiltrated as a result of a failure to implement reasonable security — statutory damages between $100 and $750 per consumer per incident.
- **Illinois — 815 ILCS 530 (Personal Information Protection Act).** Notification to residents and, for breaches affecting 500 or more Illinois residents, to the Illinois Attorney General. BIPA (740 ILCS 14) carries a separate private right of action for biometric-data incidents with statutory damages per violation. <!-- needs-research: confirm current PIPA/BIPA trigger thresholds and interactions. -->

The matrix is maintained by privacy counsel and refreshed at least annually. It is *not* authored inside the runbook — the runbook points to the matrix. The practitioner reality is that a Sev-0 incident with a nationally distributed US customer base typically produces notification obligations in 30–40 states simultaneously, and the per-state submission is a mechanical task once the master facts and affected-resident counts are frozen. The bottleneck is not the per-state filing; it is agreeing the master facts under privilege.

**SEC — Item 1.05 of Form 8-K (17 C.F.R. § 229.106).** For SEC-registrant issuers, a determination that a cybersecurity incident is **material** triggers an obligation to file an Item 1.05 Form 8-K within **four business days** of the materiality determination. The materiality analysis is the standard Basic v. Levinson / TSC Industries analysis — would a reasonable investor consider the incident significant in the mix of information. The pre-public-filing stage still requires the materiality analysis to be run and documented; for a private pre-IPO corporation the analysis is run on a going-concern basis because the item will trigger as soon as registration takes effect. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current 17 C.F.R. § 229.106 and Item 1.05 scope, including any national-security-based deferral under the Attorney General / Director of National Intelligence consultation provision. -->

**Sector overlays.** Depending on the regulated-sector profile, additional notification duties stack on top of the four clocks above:

- **GLBA Safeguards Rule.** For financial-institution covered entities under the FTC's jurisdiction, notice to the FTC within 30 days of a notification event affecting 500 or more consumers. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current GLBA Safeguards Rule amendment and 30-day FTC-notification threshold -->
- **CIRCIA reporting.** Covered-critical-infrastructure incidents under CISA rules — 72-hour notification of covered cyber incidents and 24-hour notification of ransom payments. <!-- needs-research: confirm current CIRCIA final-rule scope and reporting windows -->
- **NIS2.** Essential-and-important entities subject to member-state transpositions of the NIS2 Directive face parallel national-CSIRT notification obligations on their own clocks.
- **PCI-DSS.** Card-brand incident-response protocols trigger on suspected cardholder-data compromise, including card-brand-directed forensic investigation (PFI) engagement.

**DPA breach-notification clause interaction.** Where the corporation acts as processor to a B2B customer, the DPA breach-notification clause ([mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/)) will typically require processor-to-controller notice within 24, 48, or 72 hours of awareness — often tighter than the GDPR Art. 33 clock the controller itself must then meet. The runbook treats the contractual clock as the operational deadline; the regulatory clock is the fallback. A processor that misses the contractual clock has given the customer a DPA-breach claim even if the controller then files its own Art. 33 notice on time.

### The four notification-template families

Every template is held *pre-drafted and pre-reviewed* in the runbook with named variable-substitution slots. The template discipline is that **substance is not drafted during the incident** — only the variables are filled in. The four families:

- **Customer notice.** Two sub-templates: the **processor-to-controller notice** for B2B SaaS where the customer is the data controller and the corporation is the processor (triggering the customer's own Article 33 clock); and the **customer-direct notice** for B2C or for prosumer tiers where the corporation is the controller to the end user. The processor-to-controller template honours the DPA breach-notification clause ([mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/) — typically 24–72 hours from awareness, with a phased-update regime).
- **Vendor-impact notice.** Used when a sub-processor is the source of the breach and the corporation needs to pass through notification to its own customers; and separately when a vendor used by the corporation is affected and the corporation is a victim-recipient of the vendor's own notification. Includes the sub-processor-change notification posture under the DPA.
- **Regulator notice.** Three sub-templates aligned to the three regulators most commonly engaged: the GDPR supervisory authority filing (filed via the lead authority's online portal under the Article 56 one-stop-shop where applicable); the HHS OCR filing (via the OCR breach-report portal); and the state attorney-general filing (format varies by state — the matrix carries the per-state submission URL and content requirement).
- **Press release / media notice.** Public statement for Sev-0 incidents that have crossed the public-disclosure threshold (SEC Item 1.05 for registrants, HIPAA prominent-media notice for 500-plus PHI, or a voluntary disclosure where the executive sponsor determines the posture requires it). Co-drafted by the Communications lead and the Legal lead under privilege until the moment of release.

**Template discipline — hold substance until forensics complete.** Every template has three states:

- The **initial notification** — factual only. What is known, what is not known, what is being done, who to contact. No root-cause assertions.
- The **supplemental update** — filed as the forensic scope firms up. Revises the earlier scope statement; adds affected-record counts and category confirmations.
- The **final notification** — filed when the facts are stable. Root cause stated with forensic support; remediation described; contact point for ongoing questions.

Article 33(4) GDPR expressly contemplates this phased pattern. The template discipline *prohibits* speculation on root cause in any non-privileged channel — a root-cause statement in a customer notice that is later contradicted by forensics is the raw material for subsequent litigation. All template variables flagged <!-- needs-research --> where jurisdiction-specific content (e.g. the mandatory statutory disclosures under state breach-notification laws) requires current confirmation.

**Preserve the privilege posture.** The non-privileged channels — customer-facing notices, press statements, status-page updates, employee all-hands — carry only facts the corporation is prepared to defend in litigation. The privileged channels — the breach-counsel engagement, the forensic report, the war-room timeline annotated with counsel questions — carry the working analysis. The seam between the two is actively managed by the Legal lead; employees are briefed at the start of the incident that substantive speculation stays inside the privileged channel and that external statements require Legal sign-off. Breach of the seam (an engineer forwarding the forensic preliminary read to a customer contact, for example) is a privilege-waiver event with consequences well beyond the incident itself.

**Regulator-notice content requirements by regime.** The GDPR Art. 33(3) content list is set out above; the HHS OCR breach-report web form drives a structured submission (number of individuals affected, type of PHI, safeguards in place, actions taken); the state AG submissions vary — some states require a sample individual-notice letter; some require a cover letter addressed to the AG and a count of state residents affected; some require submission through a specific electronic portal. The runbook's regulator-notice sub-templates are the output of a one-time authoring exercise against each regime, maintained by privacy counsel and refreshed whenever a supervisory authority revises its submission format.

### The post-incident-review discipline

Within **30 days** of containment, the Security lead and the Legal lead jointly produce a written after-action report — the blameless post-mortem — delivered to the executive team and, for Sev-0 incidents, to the board audit committee. Required sections: timeline of the incident; root-cause determination; controls that failed and controls that worked; notification actions taken; regulatory posture; and the remediation backlog with named owners and dates. The post-mortem itself is prepared under privilege (breach-counsel direction) because a litigant in subsequent class-action or regulator-enforcement proceedings will seek it in discovery; a non-privileged post-mortem written candidly can become the plaintiff's best exhibit.

**Blameless discipline.** The post-mortem names *systems and decisions*, not people. The purpose is to identify the control gap, not to assign fault — fault-assignment destroys the signal quality of future incident reports because engineers who fear blame withhold facts. The CEO's cultural sponsorship of blamelessness is the single highest-leverage factor in the long-term quality of the incident-response programme.

The remediation backlog does not live in a separate file. It ties into the SOC 2 corrective-action tracker from [chapter 05](./05-soc-2-operating-manual.md) so that the next Type II observation window covers the remediation; and it feeds the board-reporting entry via the audit-committee template in [mod-111 chapter 04](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/). A finding that is logged, remediated, and tracked becomes a Caremark-posture asset; a finding that is logged, promised, and then forgotten becomes a Caremark-posture liability.

**Tabletop cadence.** The runbook is rehearsed at least annually through a facilitated tabletop exercise — ideally with the pre-retained breach-counsel firm in the room and with a ransomware or exfiltration scenario tuned to the corporation's actual threat model. The tabletop output is a runbook-revision list and a controls-gap list; both feed the same remediation backlog as a real incident would.

**Runbook maintenance cadence.** Beyond the annual tabletop, the runbook is version-controlled in the same repository as the security policies and reviewed on a quarterly cadence by the Security, Privacy, and Legal leads. Statutory references are checked at each review (statutes drift — NYDFS Part 500 amended materially in 2023, state breach-notification clocks update frequently, new state comprehensive privacy laws enter force on staggered timetables). Personnel references are checked at the same cadence; a laminated runbook card listing the departed-six-months-ago CISO's mobile number is a defect the first real incident will surface in the worst possible way.

### The cyber-carrier interface

The carrier interface is **governed by [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)**. The incident-response playbook refers out for substance. The hand-off points the privacy and security teams need to retain:

- **First-hour notice to the broker and carrier.** Notification within the policy's "as soon as practicable" window — tested in practice against the carrier's outside-deadline (often 30 days but *do not test it*). First-hour notice is the operational standard because late notice is a coverage-defence pathway.
- **Panel-counsel election.** The breach-counsel firm engaged during the incident must be on the carrier's approved panel, or carrier consent must be obtained for off-panel counsel — otherwise defence costs may be reimbursed at reduced rate or denied entirely.
- **Panel-forensic-firm selection.** Same discipline for the forensic firm; the engagement runs under breach-counsel direction (for privilege) *and* through the panel (for coverage).
- **Coverage-under-reservation acknowledgement.** Expect a coverage-under-reservation letter from the carrier within 24–72 hours. The letter preserves carrier defences while permitting the panel engagement to proceed; its terms are reviewed by breach counsel and are not themselves a coverage decision.
- **Insured-vs-uninsured scope.** Not every incident cost is covered. Business-interruption waiting periods, funds-transfer-fraud sub-limits, ransomware sub-limits, regulatory-fine insurability by jurisdiction — the policy conditions define the reimbursable envelope. Expense authorisation above the uninsured threshold is an executive-sponsor decision.
- **No direct carrier communications by company personnel.** Every substantive communication to the carrier runs through breach counsel; unrepresented statements by company personnel to the carrier can become coverage-defence evidence.

The policy-condition compliance is a **hard blocker** on the incident-response sequence. Failing to notify timely, engaging off-panel without consent, paying a ransom without OFAC screening (see [mod-112 chapter 04](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)), or admitting liability in a customer or press communication without carrier consent can each void coverage or furnish a coverage-defence argument the carrier can later invoke.

### Interactions and hand-offs

The playbook sits inside a web of sibling chapters and parallel curricula; the hand-off map is the following.

- **[mod-109 — customer-contract DPA breach-notification clause](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/).** The structural anatomy of the processor-to-controller notification clause — the clock (typically 24–72 hours), the content requirement (nature of incident, categories of data, affected data subjects, mitigation steps, contact point), and the pass-through to the controller's own Article 33 filing. This chapter runs the clock; mod-109 drafts the clause.
- **[mod-111 — board reporting of material incidents](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/).** The audit-committee notification cadence for Sev-0, the board-level materiality discussion feeding the SEC Item 1.05 analysis, and the directors' Caremark oversight record. This chapter produces the facts; mod-111 governs their delivery to the board.
- **[mod-112 chapter 03 — cyber-insurance carrier interface](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md).** First-hour notice, panel-counsel and panel-forensic selection, coverage-under-reservation posture, ransom-payment OFAC gate, claims workflow. This chapter triggers the notice; mod-112 chapter 03 governs what happens once the carrier is on the call.
- **[mod-112 chapter 04 — sanctions and export-controls compliance](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md).** The OFAC screening gate on ransomware-payment decisions and the self-reporting obligation to OFAC and CISA. This chapter identifies the trigger; mod-112 chapter 04 owns the programme.
- **[Sibling chapter 05 — SOC 2 operating manual](./05-soc-2-operating-manual.md).** The corrective-action tracker that absorbs the remediation backlog from the post-incident review; the control-gap findings from tabletop exercises; and the auditor's post-incident interest in the IR plan's actual operation.
- **`security-learning` / `ai-infra-security-solutions` — forensic-engineering substance.** Detection engineering, containment playbooks per threat-model (ransomware, business-email compromise, supply-chain compromise, AI-model data poisoning), eradication technique, recovery runbooks, and the forensic imaging and preservation discipline. This chapter does not reproduce that content; the runbook points to the engineering playbooks.

## Concrete example

Treble Labs, Inc. is a Series-B B2B SaaS corporation headquartered in Delaware, with EU customers served from a Frankfurt region and US customers served from Northern Virginia. The CISO has pre-retained breach counsel through the cyber broker; the breach-counsel firm has in turn pre-briefed a forensic partner; the cyber policy has a 72-hour outside notification condition and names both firms on the carrier's panel. The runbook is laminated in the SOC; the executive-paging tree is current.

The tabletop scenario: at 0247 on a Tuesday, EDR flags suspicious encryption activity on a customer-data processing node. By 0315 the on-call security engineer has confirmed ransomware behaviour on three nodes; the production trust-boundary isolation runbook has been executed; and the incident commander has paged the CISO. The CISO classifies Sev-0 at 0335 — customer-facing impact confirmed, data-exfiltration suspected pending forensic confirmation.

**The first 72 hours, overlaid on the four clocks:**

- **T + 00:00 (incident commander classifies Sev-0).** Executive paging tree fires. CEO, COO, CISO, GC, Head of Communications join the bridge within 30 minutes. War-room opens in both the Dublin and San Francisco offices; the follow-the-sun rota is set.
- **T + 01:00.** The GC telephones the pre-retained breach-counsel firm. Verbal engagement confirmed; written engagement letter transmitted within the hour naming breach counsel as the engaging party for forensic work. The GC and the Head of Communications place a hold on all customer and public-channel outbound communication.
- **T + 02:00 (carrier first-hour notice).** Breach counsel notifies the cyber broker and the carrier via the 24×7 hotline. Case number issued; coverage-under-reservation acknowledgement expected within 48–72 hours.
- **T + 03:00.** Breach counsel engages the panel forensic firm on behalf of the insured. Kickoff call scheduled for T + 06:00. Containment continues in parallel under the CISO's direction.
- **T + 06:00.** Forensics kickoff. Scope framed as: root cause, blast radius, exfiltration determination, and persistence assessment. Containment preliminary: affected nodes isolated; service-account credentials rotated; customer workloads failed over to the clean region.
- **T + 12:00.** The Privacy lead begins the Article 33 draft. Internal awareness has been established at the DPO level; the 72-hour Art. 33 clock is running from T + 00:00 (or, conservatively, from the T − 00:48 EDR alert — the DPO's working position is the earlier of the two). The internal breach register entry is opened under Art. 33(5).
- **T + 24:00.** First carrier update filed through breach counsel. First executive-team briefing. SEC Item 1.05 materiality analysis initiated by the GC with outside securities counsel (Treble Labs is not public but has filed an S-1 draft; the analysis is run on the forward-looking registration basis). Preliminary materiality position: pending forensic exfiltration determination — too early to conclude.
- **T + 48:00.** Forensics preliminary read: ingress via a compromised sub-processor credential; lateral movement via a legacy service account without MFA; exfiltration of a dataset containing ~40,000 EU-resident records including basic contact data (not Article 9 special-category). Privacy lead begins the state-by-state and EU-DPA notification analysis against the notification matrix.
- **T + 60:00.** Draft Article 33 notice to the lead supervisory authority (one-stop-shop lead identified as the Irish DPC given the Dublin establishment) finalised in privileged review. Draft customer processor-to-controller notice finalised. The SEC Item 1.05 materiality position firms up: given the volume and nature of the data, and given the operational impact to customers, the GC and outside counsel conclude materiality — the Item 1.05 four-business-day clock begins from this determination.
- **T + 72:00.** Article 33 notice filed with the Irish DPC (phased — Art. 33(4) reservation explicit on the face of the notice). Processor-to-controller customer notices transmitted. Press holding statement published on the status page. Board audit-committee notification completed; the Item 1.05 draft is in Communications-plus-Legal co-review for filing inside four business days. The post-incident-review calendar entry is set for T + 30 days.
- **T + 96:00.** Supplemental forensic read: exfiltration confirmed for a subset of the earlier scope — approximately 12,000 records with name, email, and hashed password. The Privacy lead files an Art. 33 supplemental update under Art. 33(4). The Communications lead and Legal lead draft the Article 34 data-subject notification; the high-risk analysis (Art. 34(1)) concludes that credential-exposure risk crosses the high-risk threshold and individual notice is required. State-matrix analysis identifies obligations in New York (Shield Act), California (Civ. Code §§ 1798.29 / 1798.82), Texas (§ 521.053), Washington (RCW 19.255), and others; the per-state notification letters are generated from the regulator-notice template.
- **T + Day 5.** SEC Item 1.05 Form 8-K filed inside the four-business-day window from the materiality determination at T + 60. The filing itself is reviewed by outside securities counsel and signed by the GC; the content tracks the Art. 33 supplemental in substance but is drafted to the Item 1.05 content requirement.
- **T + Day 15.** Individual Article 34 notifications transmitted by email; a status-page notice carries the public communication under Art. 34(3)(c) for the subset of records with stale email addresses.
- **T + Day 30.** Post-incident review complete. Root cause: an undocumented service account retained after a 2024 integration project, authenticating without MFA because the integration predated the universal-MFA rollout. Remediation backlog: service-account inventory cleanup (owned by Platform Engineering, due T + 60), universal-MFA posture audit (owned by IAM team, due T + 45), and a sub-processor-credential-rotation policy (owned by Vendor Risk, due T + 90). Backlog items tied into the SOC 2 corrective-action tracker. Board audit-committee read-out scheduled at the next regular meeting.

Every step of that timeline cross-references a runbook section, a template, a statute, and a policy provision. The runbook is the artefact that allowed the sequence to execute on time. The pre-work — the pre-retained counsel, the pre-briefed forensic firm, the pre-drafted templates, the pre-mapped state matrix, the rehearsed tabletop — is what made the runbook executable. Treble Labs did not write any of that on Tuesday at 0247.

## Summary

- **The severity taxonomy is the first page of the runbook.** Sev-0 (confirmed exfiltration or customer-facing outage; special-category or regulated-sector data), Sev-1 (contained breach), Sev-2 (anomaly or near-miss). Named threshold criteria, named paging trees, named cadences.
- **Severity classifications promote but do not demote.** A Sev-1 that reveals exfiltration becomes a Sev-0; a Sev-0 does not de-escalate mid-incident. The asymmetric-promotion rule prevents the most common failure mode — closing the war-room before the full scope is understood.
- **The team has five seats — Security, Privacy, Legal, Communications, and Executive sponsor.** Outside breach counsel pre-retained; forensic firm engaged *through* counsel for privilege; both named on the carrier's panel for coverage. The runbook names specific humans, not functions, and is refreshed quarterly against the on-call rota.
- **The notification decision-tree runs four clocks in parallel.** GDPR Art. 33 (72 hours to supervisory authority) and Art. 34 (without undue delay to data subjects at high risk, with Art. 34(3) carve-outs); HIPAA (45 C.F.R. §§ 164.400–414, 60 days plus 500+ media notice); the state matrix (NYDFS Part 500 § 500.17(a), 201 CMR 17, RCW 19.255, Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 521.053, Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.29 / 1798.82, and the rest of the 50-state-plus-territories map); SEC Item 1.05 of Form 8-K (17 C.F.R. § 229.106 — four business days from materiality determination).
- **Article 33(4) phased notification is the practitioner default** — a factual initial notice at the 72-hour deadline, supplemental updates as forensics firm up, a final notice when the facts are stable. The internal Art. 33(5) breach register is maintained regardless of external notifiability.
- **Templates are pre-drafted and pre-reviewed.** Four families: customer notice (processor-to-controller and customer-direct variants), vendor-impact notice, regulator notice (DPA, HHS OCR, state AG), and press / media notice. Substance held until forensics complete; no root-cause speculation in non-privileged channels.
- **The privilege posture is actively managed.** Breach counsel is engaged first; forensics are engaged under counsel; the war-room timeline is authored under privilege; substantive customer or press statements run through counsel review. A privilege-waiver event — the engineer who forwards the forensic preliminary read to a customer contact — has consequences well beyond the incident itself.
- **The post-incident review runs on a 30-day clock.** Blameless post-mortem to the executive team and, for Sev-0, to the board audit committee ([mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/)). Remediation backlog ties into the SOC 2 corrective-action tracker ([chapter 05](./05-soc-2-operating-manual.md)). Tabletop rehearsal at least annually against a threat-model-tuned scenario.
- **The cyber-carrier interface is a hard-blocking policy condition.** First-hour notice to broker and carrier; panel-counsel election; panel-forensic selection; insured-vs-uninsured expense authorisation. Substance owned in [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md).
- **Ownership boundaries.** DPA breach-notification clause structure sits in [mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/); board reporting of material incidents sits in [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/); the carrier interface sits in [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md); forensic-engineering substance (detection, containment, eradication, recovery) is deferred to the `security-learning` and `ai-infra-security-solutions` curricula.
- **The runbook must exist before the incident.** Written when calm, rehearsed through at least one tabletop per year, revision-controlled against the current statutes, socialised with every person named in it. A runbook authored during the incident will miss at least one of the four clocks.
