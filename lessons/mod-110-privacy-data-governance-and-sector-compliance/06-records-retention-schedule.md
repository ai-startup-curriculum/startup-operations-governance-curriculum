# 6. The records-retention schedule — by-record-type matrix, legal hold, deletion workflow, and the international-variance overlay

> A retention schedule is the quietest document in the privacy programme. Nobody reads it until a regulator asks for it, a plaintiff's counsel issues a preservation letter, or a GDPR Article 17 request lands against a record that is simultaneously under litigation hold — and then it is the only document that matters.

## Motivation

A records-retention schedule is the matrix that says, for every category of record the corporation holds, how long it is kept, who owns it, where it lives, how it is deleted, and under what circumstances deletion is suspended. It is simultaneously a privacy artefact (GDPR Article 5(1)(e) storage-limitation; CCPA minimum-necessary posture), a tax artefact (IRS Publication 583 record-keeping), a securities artefact (Sarbanes-Oxley § 802; 18 U.S.C. §§ 1519–1520), an employment artefact (EEOC record-keeping under 29 C.F.R. § 1627.3; I-9 retention under 8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(2)(i)), a litigation artefact (FRCP Rule 37(e) and state-law analogues on spoliation), and — once the corporation transacts internationally — a conflict-of-laws artefact. The schedule is the one document that reconciles those regimes into a single operational instruction that the retention engine, the eDiscovery platform, the HRIS, and the CLM can all execute.

The reason it is the quietest-but-most-load-bearing document in the privacy programme is that every other privacy artefact — the RoPA, the DSAR workflow, the DPIA register, the Transfer Impact Assessments — assumes the schedule works. The specific dependencies:

- **The RoPA's Article 30(1)(f) "envisaged time limits for erasure" line is the retention schedule.** A RoPA that cites a schedule the engine does not enforce is a RoPA that asserts a posture the corporation does not actually hold, and the supervisory authority reads the asserted retention in the first-pass audit.
- **The DSAR workflow's Article 17 erasure branch depends on knowing, per system of record, how to delete and when deletion is lawful.** An Article 17 request that cannot be executed because the deletion mechanism is not specified is a one-month SLA miss and an Article 12(3) violation.
- **The breach-notification workflow depends on knowing which data was in-scope and whether its retention was already past expiry.** A breach that exposes records the corporation had an independent obligation to have deleted is a compounding regulatory posture — the breach itself plus the storage-limitation violation plus the under-retention-expiry notification question.
- **The litigation-hold machinery and the preservation-notice obligations under FRCP Rule 37(e) attach *through* the retention schedule.** The hold is the exception, and the exception only makes sense if the default is operating.

A schedule that is not wired into tooling is a compliance-theatre document; a schedule that is wired is the single point from which the entire programme becomes auditable, and the single artefact that holds up under a supervisory-authority investigation, an eDiscovery dispute, or a DSAR escalation.

## Core concepts

### The retention-by-record-type matrix

The matrix is a table whose rows are record classes and whose columns are, at minimum:

- **Retention trigger** — the event that starts the retention clock (contract execution, employment separation, fiscal-year close, record creation, DSAR-request receipt).
- **Retention period** — the duration from trigger to default deletion, stated in days or years rather than relative phrases.
- **Legal basis for the period** — the specific statute, regulation, contractual commitment, or documented business-necessity rationale that supports the period.
- **System of record** — the single source-of-truth system where the record authoritatively lives.
- **Owner** — the named functional owner of the row (GC for contracts, VP People for HR files, CFO for financial records, DPO for privacy records, corporate secretary for board records).
- **Deletion mechanism** — the specific technical operation that effects deletion (soft-delete-then-hard-delete, object-expiration, retention-label purge, HRIS archival, media-sanitisation).
- **Jurisdictional overlay** — any non-US regime that extends or shortens the row for specific data-subject populations.

The practitioner row-set for a B2B-SaaS corporation runs through the classes below. The bullets state the floor and the default; the actual period must be set by counsel against the corporation's specific state footprint, international footprint, and regulated-industry overlay.

**Customer PII.** Retained only as long as a lawful basis under GDPR Article 6 persists and the Article 5(1)(e) storage-limitation principle is respected, with the CCPA § 1798.100(c) minimum-necessary posture overlayed for California residents. The default pattern is contract-term plus a bounded post-termination tail — typically a short window (30 to 90 days) for export and transition, and a longer window (commonly aligned with the governing-law contract statute of limitations) for records needed for billing dispute, SOC 2 audit evidence, or statute-of-limitations defence on commercial claims. The exact tail is stated in the Data Processing Addendum and in the retention schedule rather than negotiated per deal; a tail in the DPA that the retention engine does not enforce is a breach of contract once the first customer notices. Derived or aggregated data under Recital 26 that is no longer personal data falls out of the schedule entirely but should be labelled as such in the data inventory so the aggregation decision is auditable. Backups-in-scope for Article 17 erasure are a separate line and must be addressed explicitly (see the deletion-workflow section below).

**Employee PII.** Retained through the employment relationship and for a post-separation tail tied to the longest-applicable statute-of-limitations window for employment claims. US federal floors include EEOC record-keeping under 29 C.F.R. § 1627.3 (ADEA — one year from date of making the record or personnel action, longer on charge-filing), 29 C.F.R. § 1602 (Title VII — one year from date of making the record or personnel action, with longer periods on charge-filing), and FLSA payroll records under 29 C.F.R. § 516.5 (three years for payroll records; two years for records used to compute wages). State-law tails are typically longer — California, New York, and Massachusetts each run longer windows on specific record categories and should be checked against the corporation's state footprint. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current state-by-state employment-records-retention floors for the corporation's actual footprint, which drift. --> The practitioner posture is to set the schedule to the longest applicable floor plus a defensive margin and to tie deletion to the separation date, not to the hire date. Separation-triggered deletion automation lives in the HRIS (see [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-hr-ops-tooling-stack-hris-ats-pto-engagement-performance.md)) and is the specific place the retention schedule ties into the people-ops offboarding checklist.

**Financial records.** The IRS Publication 583 guidance is the practitioner reference — generally three years from the return filing date for records supporting income, deductions, and credits; six years where income is substantially understated; seven years for bad-debt deduction or worthless-security losses; and indefinite for records relating to returns not filed or fraudulent. SOX § 802 and its codification at 18 U.S.C. § 1520 require issuers and their auditors to retain audit workpapers and records supporting audit conclusions for seven years; 18 U.S.C. § 1519 criminalises document destruction in anticipation of a federal investigation, matter in bankruptcy, or matter within the jurisdiction of any federal department or agency, and has no retention period attached — it is a prohibition on destruction, not a retention line. The practitioner default for a pre-IPO corporation is seven years on financial records, moving to the SOX workpaper retention regime on the first 10-K filing. The schedule should explicitly flag the § 1519 prohibition — unlike the other lines, § 1519 applies regardless of the schedule and is the statute that turns retention into a criminal-law question at the point litigation is anticipated.

**Contracts.** Retained through the governing-law statute of limitations on the contract. US state statutes of limitation on written contracts commonly run four or six years — California at four years under Code of Civil Procedure § 337; New York at six years under CPLR § 213(2); Delaware at three years under 10 Del. C. § 8106 for most contracts, with longer windows for sealed instruments and for specific commercial-code claims. The practitioner default is to retain executed contracts through the longest applicable limitations window from the contract-executed date plus a defensive tail, and to retain them indefinitely where the contract contains perpetual IP assignments, perpetual confidentiality obligations, or perpetual indemnification obligations. See [mod-109 chapter 07](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md) for the CLM substrate on which this line runs — the CLM's obligations tracker is where the retention clock lives, keyed off the contract-executed date rather than the import date, so a historical-contract import exercise must correctly transcribe the executed date or the retention line is wrong by the age of the contract at import.

**HR files.** Employment-application records retained per EEOC 29 C.F.R. § 1627.3 and § 1602 floors. I-9 forms retained under 8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(2)(i) for three years after the date of hire or one year after the date of termination, whichever is later — the "whichever is later" construction means a short-tenure hire has a longer retention line than a long-tenure hire, which is counter-intuitive and routinely implemented wrong without HRIS-level automation. Benefits records, ERISA plan documents, and payroll records follow their own longer floors (ERISA § 107 requires six years on records supporting the Form 5500 series). Personnel files — including performance reviews, disciplinary records, and separation documentation — retained through the statute of limitations on the longest-running discrimination claim window applicable to the employment relationship, with state overlays where state statutes extend federal floors. See [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-hr-ops-tooling-stack-hris-ats-pto-engagement-performance.md) for the HRIS as the system of record.

**Board minutes and corporate records.** Retained permanently. Board minutes, shareholder consents, articles of incorporation, bylaws, charter amendments, stock-issuance records, 83(b) filings, and cap-table history are the corporate-existence substrate and do not come off retention. The permanence is not a policy preference — it is a duty-of-care posture under Caremark and its progeny, where the absence of contemporaneous board records of compliance deliberation is itself evidence of oversight failure. See [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) for the minute-book and board-portal substrate and for the Caremark compliance-system overlay that makes permanent retention of board-level deliberation a duty-of-care question.

**Communications.** Slack, email, and equivalent messaging retained per GDPR Article 5(1)(e) and per the corporation's litigation-preservation policy. The practitioner default is a short-to-medium retention window on general messaging — commonly 30 to 180 days, with the specific figure set by the corporation's own risk appetite and reviewed with counsel — with longer or permanent retention on specific channels or distribution lists that carry governance content (board-deliberation channels, incident-response channels, compliance channels, privacy-office channels). The short default is a privacy posture (less data retained, less data in a breach, less data in a DSAR) and a litigation-cost posture (less data to review in discovery); it is not a litigation-avoidance posture — the retention policy must be reasonable and consistently applied, and ad-hoc deletion to avoid discovery is sanctionable under FRCP Rule 37(e). The specific channel-override list should be reviewed annually and whenever a new governance process creates a channel or distribution list that carries decision records. <!-- needs-research: communications-retention defaults drift with product-feature changes in Slack and Microsoft Purview; verify current platform capabilities before setting the figure. -->

**Source code.** Retained through the version-control history of the corporation for the life of the corporation. Release-tagged versions retained indefinitely to support security-advisory response, customer-indemnity response on IP claims, and SOX/audit evidence for in-scope controls. Build artefacts and ephemeral CI logs follow a shorter retention line governed by [security-learning](../../../security-learning/) immutable-log engineering practice. The schedule distinguishes the repository line (indefinite) from the release-tag line (indefinite, with a separate archival mechanism) from the CI-artefact line (short, typically 30 to 90 days) so that a retention expiry on CI artefacts does not accidentally clear the release-tag history.

The matrix is maintained in the same tooling as the RoPA — commonly OneTrust, Transcend, or a home-grown spreadsheet with structured ownership — with named owners per row and a cadence (typically annual, with event-driven reviews on any material regulatory change) for the review discipline. The review cadence is a governance artefact in its own right; a schedule that has not been reviewed in the current year is one that regulators and plaintiffs' counsel will treat as stale.

### The legal-hold override

FRCP Rule 37(e) governs failure to preserve electronically stored information once litigation is reasonably anticipated. The duty-to-preserve attaches at the point the corporation knows or reasonably should know that litigation is imminent, which commonly predates service of process — a credible demand letter, a regulator's investigation notice, a formal customer dispute escalation, an internal whistleblower complaint, or a serious internal incident with external-exposure potential can each trigger the obligation. State-law analogues (common-law spoliation doctrines and, in some states, codified preservation duties) apply in parallel and sometimes impose a lower threshold. The 2015 amendments to Rule 37(e) narrowed the sanctions regime to require intent-to-deprive for the most severe sanctions (adverse-inference instructions, dismissal, default judgement) but preserved the ability to order remedial measures for negligent loss, and the practitioner posture is to design the preservation programme so the intent-to-deprive question never arises.

The practitioner machinery runs through four disciplines:

**The preservation-notice obligation.** Once the duty attaches, counsel issues a written preservation notice — commonly called a "litigation hold letter" — to every identified custodian whose records are reasonably likely to contain responsive ESI. The notice states the subject matter at a level of specificity sufficient to identify relevant records without over-disclosing, instructs the custodian to preserve relevant records, suspends routine deletion of the custodian's records, and establishes an acknowledgement mechanism. Counsel keeps a hold register — hold identifier, matter, custodians, in-scope data sources, effective date, hold-release date, acknowledgements, modifications — as the auditable record of the preservation programme. The register is itself under a long retention line because it is the evidence the preservation obligation was discharged.

**Separation of the legal-hold data store from the retention-policy deletion engine.** This is the single most important architectural point in the chapter. The retention schedule drives a deletion engine (OneTrust's retention module, Microsoft Purview retention labels, Google Vault retention rules) that deletes records on schedule. The legal hold must operate *on top of* that engine in a way that pauses deletion for in-scope records without disabling the engine globally and without depending on the engine's deletion logic to "notice" the hold. The pattern is to route in-scope custodians' records to an eDiscovery platform (Microsoft Purview eDiscovery, Google Vault, Logikcull, Everlaw, Relativity) that holds the records independently of the retention engine, so that a configuration mistake in one system does not silently defeat the hold in the other. A hold that "fails silently" — the engine deleted records the hold was supposed to preserve, nobody noticed until discovery — is the archetypal FRCP Rule 37(e) sanctions fact pattern and is the one failure mode the architectural separation is designed to make impossible.

**The hold-release discipline.** Holds end. When the matter closes (settlement, dismissal, judgement, closure of the regulatory inquiry), counsel issues a written hold-release notice and the records revert to the default retention schedule. Records that were preserved solely because of the hold and that are now past their default retention are deleted on the next retention cycle. The hold-release is logged in the hold register alongside the hold-issuance, and the custodians are notified so their individual preservation obligations cease. Hold release is a routine source of over-retention when neglected — records sit indefinitely in the eDiscovery platform because no one issued the release and the matter is forgotten — and the hold register should be reviewed annually to identify holds that have outlived their underlying matter.

**The custodian-interview discipline.** On hold issuance, counsel interviews each custodian (or runs a structured questionnaire for larger custodian sets) to identify non-obvious data sources — personal devices used for work, departed-colleague archives, SaaS tools outside the corporate IdP, printed records, physical notebooks, voice-recording apps, personal email used for work. The custodian interview is the mechanism by which the preservation obligation extends beyond the corporate systems the IT team already knows about. The interview is also the mechanism by which the corporation discovers shadow-IT — a SaaS tool a product team adopted without procurement involvement, a personal Slack workspace used alongside the corporate one — that would otherwise be invisible to the retention engine and to the eDiscovery platform.

The legal-hold machinery is the overlay on the retention schedule, not a replacement for it. The schedule remains authoritative for the default; the hold suspends the default for identified records; and the release restores the default. The audit record — hold register plus custodian acknowledgements plus eDiscovery platform's hold-preservation record plus engine-level deletion-suspension logs — is the evidence that the duty was discharged, and is the specific artefact that opposing counsel will subpoena in the spoliation motion the hold was designed to prevent.

### The deletion workflow

Deletion is the active face of the retention schedule, and the discipline is the one that most commonly fails in practice. A schedule that specifies retention periods without specifying how deletion is performed is a schedule that will not be enforced; retention that never deletes is accretion, and accretion is the posture that GDPR Article 5(1)(e) is written to prevent. The workflow runs through five disciplines:

**Automatic deletion by retention clock.** The retention engine queries the record stores on a schedule, identifies records whose retention clock has expired, and either deletes them or flags them for owner-approved deletion. The deletion mechanism depends on the record class and the system — database-row soft-delete-then-hard-delete, object-store object expiration via lifecycle policy, CLM metadata-driven archive-then-delete, HRIS personnel-file archival, Slack/email retention-label-driven purge, backup-tape rotation, immutable-log store expiration. Each system has its own deletion semantics and its own quirks (soft-delete windows, legal-hold-aware lifecycle policies, cross-region replication lag) and the schedule must state, per row, which mechanism applies and what verification is performed. A retention line that has no corresponding engine configuration is a retention line that does not execute.

**The legal-hold check.** Before any record is deleted, the engine checks whether the record is subject to an active hold. The check runs against the hold register and against the eDiscovery platform's independent hold-preservation state — the two sources must agree, and a disagreement blocks deletion pending reconciliation. A record under hold is suspended from deletion and remains in the hold store; deletion resumes on hold release. The check is mandatory and non-bypassable — the engine does not have a "force delete" mode exposed to anyone short of counsel plus a documented exception, and the exception itself is logged.

**The erasure-request integration.** GDPR Article 17 (right to erasure) and CCPA § 1798.105 (right to delete) create a third deletion trigger that runs on the data-subject's clock rather than on the retention clock. The DSAR workflow (see [chapter 01](./01-gdpr-compliance-programme.md) on GDPR Articles 12–23 and the CCPA chapter on the right-to-delete workflow) terminates in a deletion operation against the same record stores that the retention engine touches, and must go through the same legal-hold check. An Article 17 request against a record under litigation hold is not honoured in the deletion sense — the response to the data subject states the hold as the basis for retention under Article 17(3)(e) (establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims), without disclosing the specifics of the hold to the data subject, and the record is preserved under the hold until the hold releases. CCPA § 1798.105(d) lists parallel exceptions (legal-obligation retention, fraud-detection, internal uses reasonably aligned with consumer expectations, legal claim). The audit trail notes the Article 17 or § 1798.105 request, the hold intersection, the specific exception relied on, and the basis for continued retention.

**The audit-trail requirement.** Every deletion is logged: what was deleted (record identifier, record class), when (timestamp with timezone), by what mechanism (engine, manual approval, DSAR response), under what retention rule (or what erasure-request identifier), who approved if human approval was required, and what verification was performed (hash of pre-deletion state, confirmation of downstream propagation, media-sanitisation certificate). The log is immutable (see [security-learning](../../../security-learning/) on the immutable-log substrate) and retained per its own retention line — typically long, because the audit trail is the evidence that the retention programme operated, and the log of a deletion is the only post-hoc artefact that the deletion was performed correctly.

**Secure-deletion standards.** For records in media-sanitisation scope — hard drives, SSDs, backup tapes, decommissioned servers, employee-returned laptops — the practitioner reference is NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 1 "Guidelines for Media Sanitization." The standard distinguishes Clear (overwrite), Purge (cryptographic erase or degauss), and Destroy (physical destruction) sanitisation categories by media type and data sensitivity, and the schedule should state, per record class, which sanitisation category applies on media decommission. Cloud-stored records inherit the cloud provider's media-sanitisation attestations (AWS, Azure, GCP each publish their alignment to SP 800-88) and the corporation does not independently sanitise cloud media; on-premise media and employee-device media sanitisation is the corporation's own responsibility, and the vendor the corporation engages to perform media destruction should provide SP 800-88-conformant certificates of destruction per device.

### International variance

Non-US jurisdictions impose retention floors and ceilings that differ from the US defaults and that frequently *extend* the required retention period for specific record classes. The practitioner-relevant overlays:

- **France.** Code du travail L.3243-4 requires employers to retain payslips for five years. Other employment records carry their own code-of-labour floors — the general obligation to retain employment records runs five years for most employment-related documents under code-of-labour provisions, with longer windows for specific categories.
- **Germany.** HGB § 257 requires retention of commercial books, inventories, opening balance sheets, annual financial statements, management reports, and the working papers necessary to understand them for ten years; business letters, incoming and outgoing commercial correspondence, and accounting vouchers for six years. The Abgabenordnung (AO) § 147 imposes parallel tax-record retention floors in the same ten-year/six-year pattern. The German floors are among the longest in the EU and routinely drive the ceiling of the matrix for financial and commercial-correspondence record classes once the corporation has any German operation.
- **Spain.** Article 30 of the Commercial Code imposes a six-year retention floor on accounting records. Separate statutes of limitation apply to specific record classes — commercial-law claims commonly run under the general limitations provisions, with the five-year default under the modernised Civil Code § 1964 for personal-obligation claims — and interact with the national data-protection regime.
- **Canada (PIPEDA).** The PIPEDA openness principle (Principle 8 — Openness) and retention principle (Principle 5 — Limiting Use, Disclosure, and Retention) impose a "retain only as long as necessary for the purpose" posture in the same family as GDPR Article 5(1)(e), with sector-specific overlays and provincial-privacy-act interactions (Quebec Law 25, BC PIPA, Alberta PIPA). Provincial employment-standards legislation imposes its own retention floors on payroll and employment records.
- **China (PIPL).** The Personal Information Protection Law imposes storage-limitation obligations and cross-border transfer controls that interact with the retention schedule at the point where data about Chinese data subjects is held outside China. The practitioner overlay is to treat PIPL data as a separate jurisdictional row in the matrix with its own retention and deletion logic, and to anchor the cross-border transfer dimension to the CAC security-assessment or standard-contract path that supported the original transfer.
- **Japan (APPI).** The Act on the Protection of Personal Information imposes a retention-necessity principle, breach-notification obligations, and cross-border transfer controls. APPI's recent amendments have tightened the regime around pseudonymised personal information and around cross-border transfers, and the practitioner posture is to overlay APPI as a separate jurisdictional row for data about Japanese data subjects.

The pattern across the overlay is to state, in the matrix, a **global floor** (the shortest retention that satisfies every regulator with jurisdiction) and a **global ceiling** (the longest retention that any regulator with jurisdiction requires), and then to let the overlay ride on the ceiling rather than building per-country schedules that fork the matrix into unmaintainable variants.

The operational rule, stated once and applied everywhere:

- **The longest applicable retention wins.** A record that French law requires to be kept five years, German law requires to be kept ten, and the default schedule would delete in seven is retained ten.
- **The shortest applicable deletion-right triggers.** A record that is subject to a GDPR Article 17 erasure right, a CCPA § 1798.105 right-to-delete, and a Chinese PIPL deletion right is deleted on the earliest-triggered of those rights, subject to the legal-hold check and the Article 17(3)(e) exceptions.
- **Jurisdictional scope is per data-subject, not per corporation.** The French overlay applies to data about French data subjects regardless of where the data is stored; the German overlay applies to records of German commercial operations regardless of where the holding entity is incorporated. The matrix encodes the scope of each overlay rather than treating "international" as a single column.

The overlay is a conflict-of-laws exercise, and the discipline is to encode the conflict rule once rather than per record class. The operational cost of the "longest-wins / shortest-triggers" rule is that it tends to lengthen US retention lines beyond their domestic floor; the operational benefit is that the engine runs a single rule rather than a fork-per-jurisdiction and the audit trail reads coherently across the corporation.

### Tooling stack

The tooling that runs the schedule in production falls into six groups. The point of listing them is not to recommend a specific vendor — selection is stage-dependent and the practitioner evaluation runs through the usual SaaS-vs-self-hosted, integration-surface, and workflow-customisation dimensions — but to name the categories so the schedule can be wired to actual systems rather than left as a Word document.

**Contracts.** DocuSign CLM, Ironclad, Agiloft, LinkSquares, Concord. Retention keyed off the contract-executed date and the governing-law statute of limitations, with the CLM obligations tracker surfacing retention expiry alongside auto-renewal and post-signature-obligation events. The CLM is the authoritative system of record for executed contracts and for contract metadata (counterparty, effective date, term, governing law, perpetual-obligation flags) that drives the retention clock. See [mod-109 chapter 07](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md).

**Documents.** Microsoft SharePoint, Google Drive, Microsoft Purview, Google Vault. Purview retention labels and Vault retention rules apply retention at the item level and integrate with the eDiscovery platforms of the same ecosystem (Purview eDiscovery; Vault). The practitioner posture is to configure retention labels per record class at the tenant level and to tag documents at creation (via template inheritance or workflow-driven labelling) rather than relying on after-the-fact classification; classification-at-creation is the only approach that scales past a few thousand documents.

**Communications eDiscovery and legal hold.** Google Vault, Microsoft Purview eDiscovery. Both provide hold mechanics that are independent of the underlying mailbox/channel retention, and both are the practitioner default for the eDiscovery leg of the hold-vs-retention separation described above. Larger holds and holds spanning data sources outside the Google or Microsoft ecosystem commonly route into a dedicated eDiscovery platform (Logikcull, Everlaw, Relativity) that ingests from the native tools and holds independently.

**DSAR, consent, and inventory.** OneTrust, Osano, Transcend. These platforms integrate the data-subject-rights workflow (intake, verification, routing, response) with the retention engine so that an Article 17 erasure request executes as a deletion operation against the identified systems of record, subject to the legal-hold check. The practitioner posture is to maintain the data inventory in the same platform that runs the DSAR workflow, so that the RoPA and the DSAR system of record are the same tool and the inventory does not drift from the operational-deletion routing table.

**HR retention on separation.** Workday, BambooHR, Rippling. The HRIS is the system of record for employee files and runs the separation-driven retention clock — hire date, separation date, I-9 retention expiry (automated on the three-years-after-hire-or-one-year-after-termination-whichever-is-later rule), benefits-record retention expiry — with the retention schedule's employee-PII row implemented as HRIS lifecycle automation. Where a PEO sits in front of the HRIS (Justworks, TriNet, Insperity) the retention responsibility is split between the corporation and the PEO per the PEO agreement; the schedule should state who owns which record class to prevent a double-delete or a no-delete gap. See [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-hr-ops-tooling-stack-hris-ats-pto-engagement-performance.md).

**Source control and immutable logs.** GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket for version control. Splunk, Datadog, AWS CloudWatch, GCP Cloud Logging for operational logs. The source-control retention line runs on the repository and release-tag lifecycle; the immutable-log line runs on the log-store retention configuration and is governed by the engineering substrate documented in [security-learning](../../../security-learning/). The immutable-log retention is a separate and often longer line than the operational-log retention — security-relevant events, audit events, and compliance-control evidence log entries are commonly retained seven years or longer, while operational-debugging logs are retained 30 to 90 days.

Feature parity across these vendors drifts — retention-label semantics, eDiscovery hold mechanics, DSAR integration depth, cross-tenant hold-preservation, and the audit-trail surface each evolve on vendor release cycles — and the selection matrix should be refreshed on every vendor renewal rather than assumed to be stable. <!-- needs-research: specific feature-parity claims among OneTrust, Transcend, Osano, Purview, and Vault drift; verify against current vendor documentation before relying on any one claim in selection. -->

### Hand-offs

The retention schedule is a central document and connects outward to several sibling modules. Each hand-off is a point at which the schedule is authored by the privacy counsel but owned operationally by a different function; the boundary must be drawn explicitly or the record class falls through the gap.

- **Board-minute retention and D&O indemnity preservation** — [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/). Permanent retention of the minute book and of director-indemnification evidence sits in the corporate-records substrate and is governed by the corporate-governance programme rather than by the privacy programme. The corporate secretary owns the row; privacy counsel ensures the row is on the matrix and is not accidentally scoped by a general-communications retention rule.
- **Contract retention** — [mod-109 chapter 07](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md). The CLM is the system of record and the retention line runs in CLM metadata, keyed off the contract-executed date and the governing-law statute of limitations. The GC or head of legal ops owns the row; privacy counsel ensures the DPA's data-return-and-deletion terms are enforced by the same retention engine that governs customer PII.
- **Employee-file retention through the HRIS/PEO stack** — [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-hr-ops-tooling-stack-hris-ats-pto-engagement-performance.md). HRIS lifecycle automation runs the employee-PII retention clock, with PEO-split records handled per the PEO agreement. The VP People or head of HR operations owns the row; privacy counsel ensures the HRIS retention configuration matches the schedule and that the I-9 three-years-after-hire-or-one-year-after-termination-whichever-is-later rule is correctly automated.
- **Cyber-insurance claims-file retention** — [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md). Claims-file retention is governed by the insurance programme and is a separate line in the schedule tied to the policy-claim lifecycle, with the broker and insurer both having their own retention of claim-related materials. The head of risk or the CFO's risk-and-insurance delegate owns the row; privacy counsel ensures incident-response records that are also claim-file records are retained under the longer of the two retention lines.

## Concrete example

A Series-B B2B-SaaS corporation, roughly 200 employees, US-headquartered (Delaware) with a UK subsidiary acquired in a tuck-in, and a growing EU customer base served from an AWS eu-west-1 deployment. The corporation has a GC, a privacy counsel reporting to the GC, a DPO (external, UK-based, covering the UK and EU footprint), a head of legal ops, and a security team with an incident-response lead. It runs OneTrust for the RoPA, DSAR workflow, and consent; Ironclad as the CLM; Workday as the HRIS; Microsoft 365 with Purview retention labels and Purview eDiscovery as the documents and communications substrate; Splunk for immutable logs; and AWS-native tooling for infrastructure data.

**The matrix build.** Privacy counsel drafts the retention schedule over a six-week cycle, with one record-class row per week and a cross-functional review on each. The build proceeds class by class:

- **Customer PII.** Anchored to the DPA language ([mod-109 chapter 01](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/01-customer-contract-suite-msa-sla-dpa-security-aup.md)) — contract term plus a 30-day export window plus a 90-day billing/dispute tail, with backups-in-scope purged on a 180-day rotation. The DPA's Article 28 return-and-deletion terms are mapped line-by-line to the OneTrust retention-engine configuration so the DPA commitment is enforced automatically on contract termination.
- **Employee PII.** Anchored to the longest applicable US-federal floor plus a state-overlay check for California and New York employees, with the HRIS-driven separation-date retention clock running through Workday. I-9 retention automated on the 8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(2)(i) rule.
- **Financial records.** Seven-year line in NetSuite, mapped to IRS Publication 583 and to the forthcoming SOX § 802 posture that will apply on first 10-K filing. The 18 U.S.C. § 1519 prohibition flagged separately as an always-on constraint.
- **Contracts.** Six-year line from contract-executed date (anchored to New York CPLR § 213(2) as the corporation's governing-law default) with permanent retention on contracts containing perpetual IP assignments, perpetual confidentiality obligations, or perpetual indemnification obligations. The Ironclad metadata schema tags each contract with its retention category at execution.
- **HR files.** Workday-managed with lifecycle automation on hire date, separation date, and I-9 expiry.
- **Board minutes.** Diligent board portal with permanent retention and corporate-secretary ownership.
- **Communications.** 180-day Purview retention label on Slack and email, with permanent overrides on the board-channel, the incident-response channel, the privacy-office channel, and the compliance-committee channel. The override list is maintained in a OneTrust-tracked governance-channel register with an annual review.
- **Source code.** GitHub with indefinite repository retention and permanent release-tag retention; CI artefacts on a 60-day lifecycle.
- **International overlays.** The UK subsidiary's employment-records retention sits on the longer of the US federal floor and the UK-statutory floor; the EU customer data sits on the DPA contract-term-plus-tail line with the backups-in-scope purge; no German operation yet, so HGB § 257 is not triggered but is flagged as a dependency if a German operation is acquired.

**The legal-hold wiring.** The hold register sits in Ironclad's matter-management module (shared with the outside-counsel-matter tracker); the preservation operates through Purview eDiscovery for documents and communications, and through a database-level hold flag in the product database and the data-warehouse. Workday records are placed on hold by a HRIS-level retention-override flag that suspends separation-driven retention for in-scope departed employees. The engine (OneTrust retention module plus Purview retention labels plus Workday lifecycle plus a custom Splunk-logged deletion-approval workflow) is wired to check the hold register before any deletion runs; the check is logged, auditable, and non-bypassable. A quarterly reconciliation runs across the hold register, the Purview eDiscovery hold-preservation state, and the engine's deletion-suspension log to catch drift before it becomes a spoliation question.

**The scenario — Article 17 meets a live hold.** A former EU customer's end-user — a German resident — files a GDPR Article 17 erasure request against the corporation in week 14 of the matrix build, routed through the customer (as controller) under the DPA. The customer forwards the request. In parallel, in week 11, counsel issued a litigation hold on all records relating to a different customer, and the hold's custodian list includes three product-engineering employees whose Slack messages and email mention the German end-user's account by ID (because the account surfaced in a performance-regression discussion the engineering team had around the same time as the unrelated customer dispute).

The Article 17 request would, under the default schedule, trigger a deletion operation against the product database (user record), the data-warehouse (usage events), the customer-support ticket store (tickets filed by the end-user), and the communications stores (Slack channels and email mentioning the user ID). The legal-hold check catches the collision: the communications stores for the three custodians are under hold on the unrelated matter and the hold is live.

Counsel's call, worked through the Article 17(3) exception framework:

- The deletion operations against the product database, the data-warehouse, and the support tickets proceed on the Article 17 timeline, because those records are not within the scope of the hold and the Article 17 right applies.
- The deletion against the communications stores is suspended for the three custodians under Article 17(3)(e) — processing necessary for the establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims.
- The response to the end-user (via the customer as controller) states that a portion of the records is being retained under Article 17(3)(e) with a hold identifier, without disclosing the details of the hold or its subject matter.
- The hold register, the eDiscovery platform's hold-preservation record, and the OneTrust DSAR audit trail collectively document the decision, the basis, and the records affected.
- On hold release (three months later, after the unrelated matter settles), the suspended records are deleted on the next retention cycle, the end-user is not re-notified (the original response having stated the Article 17(3)(e) basis), and the audit trail records the release-triggered deletion.

The scenario is the one the chapter exists to prepare for. It is quiet, procedural, boring, and — because the matrix, the hold machinery, and the deletion engine were wired together before the collision happened — resolved in the course of a normal operating week rather than as a crisis.

## Summary

- The records-retention schedule is the matrix of record class, retention trigger, retention period, legal basis, system of record, owner, deletion mechanism, and jurisdictional overlay; it is the single document that reconciles privacy, tax, securities, employment, and litigation obligations into a single operational instruction.
- The by-record-type rows cover customer PII (GDPR Article 5(1)(e) and CCPA minimum-necessary), employee PII (EEOC and FLSA floors plus state overlays), financial records (IRS Publication 583 and SOX § 802 / 18 U.S.C. § 1520), contracts (governing-law statute of limitations, with perpetual carve-outs), HR files (8 C.F.R. § 274a.2(b)(2)(i) I-9 and the EEOC and benefits floors), board minutes (permanent), communications (short default with governance-channel overrides), and source code (version-control life with release-tag permanence).
- The legal-hold override operates through a preservation-notice obligation under FRCP Rule 37(e) and state-law analogues, a hold register, a hold-release discipline, and a custodian-interview discipline, with the legal-hold data store (eDiscovery platform) separated from the retention-policy deletion engine so the hold cannot silently fail.
- The deletion workflow combines automatic retention-clock deletion, a mandatory legal-hold check, erasure-request integration for GDPR Article 17 and CCPA § 1798.105, an immutable audit trail, and NIST SP 800-88 media sanitisation for on-premise and employee-device media.
- International variance is handled by an overlay — French Code du travail L.3243-4 payslips at five years, German HGB § 257 commercial books at ten years, Spanish, PIPEDA, PIPL, and APPI overlays — with the rule that the longest applicable retention wins and the shortest applicable deletion-right triggers, encoded once rather than per-country.
- The tooling stack runs the schedule in production — DocuSign CLM / Ironclad / Agiloft for contracts, SharePoint / Google Drive / Microsoft Purview for documents, Google Vault / Microsoft Purview eDiscovery for communications and legal hold, OneTrust / Osano / Transcend for DSAR and inventory, Workday / BambooHR / Rippling for HR, GitHub / GitLab for source control, Splunk / Datadog / CloudWatch for immutable logs.
- The hand-offs run to [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) for board-minute and D&O indemnity preservation, [mod-109 chapter 07](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md) for contract retention, [mod-104 chapter 06](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/06-hr-ops-tooling-stack-hris-ats-pto-engagement-performance.md) for employee-file retention, and [mod-112 chapter 03](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) for cyber-insurance claims-file retention.
- A schedule that is not wired into tooling is compliance theatre; a schedule that is wired is the quiet document that makes the rest of the privacy programme — RoPA, DSAR, DPIA, breach, transfer — auditable, and the one document that holds up the day a GDPR Article 17 request intersects a live litigation hold mid-drafting.
