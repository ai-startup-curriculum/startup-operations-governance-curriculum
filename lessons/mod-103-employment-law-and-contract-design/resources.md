# Resources — mod-103 Employment Law, Worker Classification & Contract Design

Primary statutes, regulations, agency publications, case law, and standard-form references cited across the chapters. Prefer these to secondary summaries.

## Federal statutes — classification, wage-and-hour, and benefits

### Internal Revenue Code and IRS

- **IRC § 3121** — Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA) wages. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/3121
- **IRC § 3301 et seq.** — Federal Unemployment Tax Act (FUTA).
- **IRC § 3402** — federal income-tax withholding; **§ 3402(d)** partial-mitigation pathway where the worker paid the underlying income tax.
- **IRC § 6672** — trust-fund recovery penalty; personal, non-dischargeable liability of a "responsible person" who willfully failed to collect or pay over withheld taxes.
- **Rev. Rul. 87-41** — IRS 20-factor common-law test for worker classification.
- **IRS Publication 15-A** — Employer's Supplemental Tax Guide (independent-contractor-vs-employee common-law test, three-category framework). https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p15a.pdf
- **IRS Form SS-8** — Determination of Worker Status for Purposes of Federal Employment Taxes and Income Tax Withholding. https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-ss-8
- **Section 530 of the Revenue Act of 1978** (uncodified) — safe harbour for consistent historical treatment of workers as non-employees. Narrow eligibility.
- **IRS Announcement 2011-64 and subsequent guidance** — Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (VCSP). <!-- needs-research: verify current VCSP eligibility and payment-computation terms before recommending to a client. -->
- **Rev. Proc. 2004-56** — partial-mitigation procedure for employer reclassification tax relief.

### Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) and Department of Labor

- **FLSA — 29 U.S.C. §§ 201–219.** Minimum wage, overtime, child labor, record-keeping. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/chapter-8
  - **§ 207** — overtime.
  - **§ 213** — exemptions (executive, administrative, professional, outside sales, computer-employee).
  - **§ 216(b)** — private right of action, liquidated damages, collective-action mechanism, attorneys' fees.
  - **§ 216(e)** — civil money penalties for repeated or willful violations.
  - **§ 255(a)** — 2-year statute of limitations; 3-year for willful violations.
- **29 C.F.R. Part 541** — DOL regulations on white-collar exemptions (executive, administrative, professional, computer, outside sales); salary-basis test at § 541.602. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-A/part-541
- **29 C.F.R. Part 795** — DOL regulations on independent-contractor classification under the FLSA. <!-- needs-research: Part 795 was rewritten by the DOL's 2024 final rule restoring a totality-of-circumstances economic-realities test; verify operative-rule text and litigation status. -->
- **DOL Wage and Hour Division — Fact Sheet #13** on employment relationship under the FLSA. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/13-flsa-employment-relationship
- **DOL opinion letters** — advisory guidance on specific FLSA questions. https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/opinion-letters

### Trade secrets and IP

- **Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA)** — 18 U.S.C. §§ 1836–1839.
  - **§ 1833(b)(1)–(2)** — whistleblower immunities.
  - **§ 1833(b)(3)** — notice requirement to employees and contractors.
  - **§ 1836(b)(3)(C)–(D)** — exemplary damages up to 2× compensatory and attorneys' fees.
  - **§ 1839(3)** — trade-secret definition, including "reasonable measures."
- **Copyright Act — 17 U.S.C. § 101** — work-made-for-hire definition.
- **17 U.S.C. § 201(b)** — ownership of works made for hire.
- **Patent Act — 35 U.S.C. § 261** — assignments of patents (context for *Stanford v. Roche*).

### Arbitration

- **Federal Arbitration Act (FAA)** — 9 U.S.C. §§ 1–16. https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/9/chapter-1
- **Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021 (EFAA)** — Pub. L. No. 117-90, codified at 9 U.S.C. §§ 401–402, effective March 3, 2022. https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/house-bill/4445

## Federal case law

### Classification and employment

- **Nationwide Mutual Insurance Co. v. Darden**, 503 U.S. 318 (1992) — common-law test for "employee" under ERISA.
- **Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid**, 490 U.S. 730 (1989) — common-law test for "employee" under the Copyright Act.
- **Dynamex Operations West, Inc. v. Superior Court**, 4 Cal. 5th 903 (2018) — ABC test under California wage orders.
- **S.G. Borello & Sons v. Department of Industrial Relations**, 48 Cal. 3d 341 (1989) — multi-factor test applicable to AB 5 statutory exceptions.
- **Cochran v. Schwan's Home Service, Inc.**, 228 Cal. App. 4th 1137 (2014) — Cal. Lab. Code § 2802 reimbursement of personal-cell-phone bill used for work.
- **Vega v. CM & Associates Construction Management, LLC**, 175 A.D.3d 1144 (1st Dep't 2019) — private right of action and liquidated damages for NY Lab. Law § 191 pay-frequency violations.
- **AMN Healthcare, Inc. v. Aya Healthcare Services, Inc.**, 28 Cal. App. 5th 923 (2018) — employee non-solicits as restraints under Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600.

### Discrimination and accommodation

- **McDonnell Douglas Corp. v. Green**, 411 U.S. 792 (1973) — burden-shifting framework for disparate treatment.
- **Griggs v. Duke Power Co.**, 401 U.S. 424 (1971) — disparate-impact theory.
- **Bostock v. Clayton County**, 590 U.S. 644 (2020) — "sex" under Title VII includes sexual orientation and gender identity.
- **Groff v. DeJoy**, 600 U.S. 447 (2023) — undue-hardship standard for Title VII religious accommodation.
- **Christiansburg Garment Co. v. EEOC**, 434 U.S. 412 (1978) — asymmetric fee-shifting under Title VII.
- **Faragher v. City of Boca Raton**, 524 U.S. 775 (1998), and **Burlington Industries, Inc. v. Ellerth**, 524 U.S. 742 (1998) — affirmative-defense framework for supervisor-harassment claims.

### IP, trade secrets, PIIA

- **Stanford v. Roche Molecular Systems, Inc.**, 583 F.3d 832 (Fed. Cir. 2009), aff'd on other grounds, 563 U.S. 776 (2011) — "hereby assigns" vs. "agrees to assign" present-assignment holding. https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/10pdf/09-1159.pdf
- **PepsiCo, Inc. v. Redmond**, 54 F.3d 1262 (7th Cir. 1995) — inevitable-disclosure doctrine (recognised in some states).
- **Whyte v. Schlage Lock Co.**, 101 Cal. App. 4th 1443 (2002) — California rejection of inevitable disclosure.
- **Cothron v. White Castle System, Inc.**, 2023 IL 128004 (Ill. Feb. 17, 2023) — per-collection accrual under BIPA.

### Arbitration

- **Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis**, 584 U.S. 497 (2018) — enforceability of class-action waivers in employment arbitration under the FAA.
- **Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams**, 532 U.S. 105 (2001) — § 1 transportation-worker exemption narrowly construed.
- **Southwest Airlines Co. v. Saxon**, 596 U.S. 450 (2022) — § 1 exemption for cargo-loading airline employees.
- **Bissonnette v. LePage Bakeries Park St., LLC**, 601 U.S. 246 (2024) — § 1 exemption does not require the employer to be in the transportation industry.
- **American Express Co. v. Italian Colors Restaurant**, 570 U.S. 228 (2013) — class-waiver enforceability outside the FAA context.
- **Viking River Cruises, Inc. v. Moriana**, 596 U.S. 639 (2022) — PAGA individual-claim arbitrability.
- **Adolph v. Uber Technologies, Inc.**, 14 Cal. 5th 1104 (2023) — standing to pursue non-individual PAGA claims after individual arbitration.
- **Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services, Inc.**, 24 Cal. 4th 83 (2000) — substantive-unconscionability requirements for employment arbitration in California.
- **Chamber of Commerce v. Bonta**, 62 F.4th 473 (9th Cir. 2023) — FAA preemption of Cal. Lab. Code § 432.6 (AB 51).
- **Olivieri v. Stifel, Nicolaus & Co., Inc.**, No. 23-2168, 2024 WL 3870447 (2d Cir. Aug. 20, 2024) — EFAA case-level treatment of bundled claims. <!-- needs-research: verify current circuit-split status on EFAA case-level vs. claim-level treatment. -->
- **Lynn's Food Stores, Inc. v. United States**, 679 F.2d 1350 (11th Cir. 1982) — judicial / DOL supervision of FLSA settlements.
