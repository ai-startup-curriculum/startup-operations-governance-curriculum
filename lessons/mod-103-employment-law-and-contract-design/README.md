# mod-103 — Employment Law, Worker Classification & Contract Design

> W-2 vs. 1099, FLSA exempt / non-exempt, offer letters, PIIAs and NDAs, non-competes, EEOC-protected categories, state-law variance, and arbitration clauses — the employee-facing legal architecture of a US startup.

**Track:** Startup Operations & Governance (level 50) · **Stage:** SEED / SERIES-A · **Pillar:** legal · **Hours:** 28

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) stood up the entity. [mod-102](../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/) authored the founder-side legal architecture — SPAs, 83(b)s, PIIAs, DTSA notices, the founder-employment relationship, the founder-departure playbook. This module owns everything about the *rest* of the workforce: the second, tenth, and hundredth hire; the contractors the corporation retains; the vendors and candidates and investors who see confidential information.

The employee-facing legal architecture is what the corporation runs into every single time it hires, promotes, changes compensation, moves someone across state lines, adds an inside-sales rep, engages a designer as a 1099, opens an office in a new state, or terminates a role. Get it wrong once and the corporation buys a Department of Labor audit, an EEOC charge, a Private Attorneys General Act (PAGA) representative action in California, a Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) collective action for unpaid overtime, or a worker-misclassification back-tax assessment. Get it right and the corporation has a clean paper trail that survives Series-A diligence, an audit, and a departure lawsuit.

This is also the module where the legal ground moves fastest. The Federal Trade Commission proposed and then had blocked a national non-compete ban (2024). The US Department of Labor's independent-contractor rule has been rewritten twice in the last four years and remains actively litigated. California's AB 5, the Ending Forced Arbitration Act of 2022, the state-by-state salary-transparency wave (Colorado 2021, New York 2023, Washington 2023, California 2023 with more coming), and the state-and-city expansion of protected categories are all live. A COO or GC hired in 2026 who is running a 2022 employment-law playbook is running a legally-broken playbook. This module teaches the framework so the incoming operator can update the specifics from the primary sources.

## Chapters

1. [W-2 employee vs. 1099 independent contractor](./01-w2-vs-1099-worker-classification.md) — IRS common-law test (Rev. Rul. 87-41), FLSA economic-realities test, the California ABC test (*Dynamex* / AB 5), the EDD / SS-8 / DOL audit surface, and the payroll-tax / withholding / benefits / workers-comp consequences of misclassification.
2. [FLSA exempt vs. non-exempt](./02-flsa-exempt-vs-non-exempt.md) — the salary basis test, the five duties tests (executive, administrative, professional, computer employee, outside sales), the misclassification exposure (unpaid overtime, liquidated damages, attorneys' fees), and the practical implication for startup engineers, PMs, designers, and inside-sales reps.
3. [Offer letters and pay transparency](./03-offer-letter-architecture-and-pay-transparency.md) — at-will framing (with the Montana carve-out), start date, position and reporting line, compensation (cash + equity + benefits), reference architecture to the standalone PIIA / arbitration / relocation agreements, employee-handbook reference, contingent conditions (I-9, background check), and the state-of-the-art pay-transparency-compliant salary-range disclosure.
4. [The employee PIIA](./04-employee-piia.md) — the employee-side extension of the mod-102 founder PIIA: pre-employment invention carveouts, at-employment assignment, post-employment confidentiality, DTSA whistleblower-immunity notice, and the drafting pattern that keeps a side project the employee's own without dragging it into the corporation's ownership.
5. [NDAs and MNDAs](./05-nda-and-mnda-layer.md) — mutual vs. one-way NDAs for customer, vendor, candidate, recruit, and investor conversations; the standard exceptions (already-known / independently-developed / publicly-available / compelled disclosure); the practical dispute-resolution clauses (governing law, venue, injunctive relief, attorneys' fees).
6. [The non-compete landscape and its alternatives](./06-non-compete-landscape-and-alternatives.md) — the FTC non-compete rule (currently blocked in federal court, persistent on the enforcement agenda); state-level bans (California, Minnesota, Oklahoma, North Dakota); state-level restrictions (New York, Massachusetts, Washington, and others); the surviving practitioner defaults (non-solicits of employees / customers, garden-leave clauses, targeted trade-secret protection carveouts).
7. [The EEOC-protected-category framework across the employment lifecycle](./07-eeoc-protected-category-framework.md) — Title VII (race, color, religion, sex, national origin), the ADA, the ADEA, the PDA, the EPA, GINA; plus the fast-moving state-and-city protected-category layer (gender identity, sexual orientation, source of income, criminal history "ban the box"); applied across recruiting, interviewing, hiring, comp-setting, performance, promotion, and offboarding.
8. [State-law variance across California, New York, Illinois, Washington, Colorado](./08-state-law-variance.md) — CA (AB 5, CFRA, PDL, Fair Chance, PAGA), NY (paid family leave, salary transparency), IL (BIPA for HR-adjacent biometric use), WA (paid family leave, salary transparency), CO (POWR, salary transparency); the highest-common-denominator vs. per-state operating-policy decision.
9. [Arbitration and class-action waivers](./09-arbitration-and-class-action-waivers.md) — the post-*Epic Systems* (2018) enforceability landscape, the state-level carveouts (California PAGA, the sexual-harassment / assault carve-out under the Ending Forced Arbitration Act of 2022), and the practical trade-off between per-claim arbitration cost and class-action exposure.
10. [Ownership boundary map](./10-ownership-boundary-map.md) — what this module owns vs. what it hands off to mod-102, mod-104, mod-105, mod-113, and a possible future labour-organising module.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical hiring plan across multiple states, produce and defend:

1. A worker-classification decision matrix, one row per role, applying the IRS common-law test, the FLSA economic-realities test, and the state ABC test where relevant, arriving at a defensible W-2 vs. 1099 answer.
2. An FLSA exempt vs. non-exempt classification for every W-2 role, with the salary-basis and duties-test analysis on the record.
3. A state-of-the-art offer letter, cross-referencing the PIIA and NDA suite, with pay-transparency-compliant range disclosure and the contingent-condition checklist (I-9, background check, reference).
4. An employee PIIA (adapted from the founder PIIA in mod-102) with pre-employment carveouts, present-assignment language, DTSA notice, and applicable state-law invention-assignment carveouts.
5. An NDA suite — mutual and one-way variants — with the correct exception language and dispute-resolution clauses.
6. A non-compete-and-alternatives clause pack that survives the current state-law and FTC-rule landscape.
7. An EEOC-protected-category checklist and an interview-question review that surfaces disparate-impact and disparate-treatment risk before an offer goes out.
8. A state-law variance map for the corporation's actual hiring footprint, with a documented highest-common-denominator vs. per-state operating-policy decision.
9. An arbitration and class-action-waiver clause with the PAGA and sexual-harassment / assault carveouts drafted correctly.
