# 2. Sequencing the first ten operations hires — triggers, comp benchmarks, and hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource

> The operations function's first ten hires cost the company several million dollars a year in fully-loaded comp by the time the tenth seat lands. Sequencing them against real triggers — not "everyone at Series B has one" — is the difference between an operations org that earns its cost and an ops payroll the CFO is quietly resenting.

## Motivation

Every ops seat can be justified in the abstract. Every ops seat can also be deferred, outsourced, or fused with an adjacent seat for another twelve months. The right question is never "should we have a Head of Business Systems?" — every scale-up eventually should. The right question is "**what has to be true today, at this headcount and this operating tempo, for this seat to pay for itself?**"

This chapter names the ten seats, gives each a trigger, sets an expected-comp band grounded in the market benchmarks the compensation architecture chapter ([mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/)) uses, and calls the hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource pattern for each. It is a sequencing map, not a mandate — the specific order changes with the CEO's variables from [chapter 01](./01-coo-vs-cos-and-bizops-org-design.md).

The comp bands in this chapter are stated as bands, not point estimates, and every band is marked with a research flag because Carta, Pave, and Radford data moves each year, differs by geography, and differs by company stage and industry. Treat the bands as **directionally correct as of the last time you re-benchmarked**; do not treat any single number as a market truth.

## The comp-benchmark sources

Three commercial benchmarks dominate the venture-scale-up market:

- **Carta Compensation.** Free tier for Carta cap-table customers, plus a paid tier for deeper data. Coverage skews toward VC-backed startups on Carta's cap-table product. Strong data for tech / SaaS / early-stage. Publishes periodic *State of Startup Compensation* reports.
- **Pave.** Peer-benchmarking product with real-time survey data from participating companies. Coverage skews toward mid-to-late-stage tech. Strong data for role-and-level-specific bands across the executive-through-IC ladder.
- **Radford (Aon).** The enterprise-standard survey, expensive, extensive coverage across public and private technology companies. Standard for compensation-committee benchmarking at growth-stage and pre-IPO companies (see [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)).

Supplementary sources: **Option Impact (Advanced-HR)** for equity-heavy analysis, **Payscale / Salary.com** for lower-quality but free ranges, **Levels.fyi** for public-company IC compensation data.

## The ten seats, sequenced

The default sequence below assumes a US-headquartered venture-backed SaaS company, CEO who has decided to hire a CoS first and a COO later, and a Series-A → Series-C trajectory over 24–36 months. Reorder as the specific company's variables demand.

### Seat 1 — Chief of Staff

**Trigger.** CEO spending >50% of the week on operating machinery; executive team crossing 5–7 direct reports; Series A closed or closing; the CEO's next 6-month plan includes at least one cross-functional programme that no one else on the team can run.

**Timing.** Late Series A to early Series B. Company headcount typically 40–100.

**Comp band.** Highly variable — the CoS market splits into "operator CoS" (former founder, former operator, senior background) at one end and "consultant CoS" (former McKinsey / Bain / BCG consultant, 3–5 years post-MBA) at the other. Directional bands for a US venture-backed SaaS company: base US$180–300k, target bonus 10–20%, equity typically 0.15–0.50% at Series B (varies enormously by tenure, stage, and the CoS's expected trajectory to COO / VP). <!-- needs-research: verify Chief of Staff comp bands in Carta / Pave / Radford data for Series A/B/C companies — the role is under-benchmarked historically and the bands widen fast at senior levels. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire.** There is no meaningful outsourced version of this seat — the CoS's value is proximity to the CEO, which cannot be contracted out. Defer only if the CEO is unwilling to actually delegate anything, in which case any CoS will fail.

### Seat 2 — First BizOps analyst

**Trigger.** CoS is spending more than half their time on recurring analytical work (KPI packs, board-metric reconciliation, ad-hoc financial modelling) that could be done by a strong analyst; executive team is asking questions the FP&A team is not sized to answer.

**Timing.** ~3–6 months after CoS lands, sometimes concurrent.

**Comp band.** US$110–160k base, 5–10% bonus, 0.02–0.08% equity at Series B for a 2–4-years-experienced analyst; higher at Series C for a senior analyst. <!-- needs-research: verify BizOps analyst / associate comp bands in Carta / Pave for Series B–C venture-backed tech. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire.** Some early-stage companies rent BizOps capacity from a fractional-BizOps firm (SVB / Silicon Valley Bank Alliance-partner firms and independents like BBG Ventures' operator network and Chief of Staff Network); these can bridge for 6 months but do not scale as the analytical remit grows. Hire once the volume justifies a dedicated seat.

### Seat 3 — Head of Workplace / Facilities

**Trigger.** First direct lease signed (typically a Series-A / late-Series-A 3-year short-term lease); or headcount past ~75 in a single coworking space where the coworking arrangement is degrading; or second office coming online; or an active RTO / hybrid policy with attendance targets.

**Timing.** Late Series A to Series B.

**Comp band.** Wide range depending on scope — a Head of Workplace running a single office is a very different seat from one running a hub-and-spoke footprint across three cities. Directional bands: US$140–230k base, 10–15% bonus, 0.03–0.15% equity at Series B. <!-- needs-research: verify Head of Workplace / Head of Facilities / Head of Real Estate comp bands in Radford / Pave data — the role often benchmarks against Facilities Manager (lower) or VP Real Estate (higher) depending on the seat's construction. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Fractional first.** Many companies use a fractional Workplace lead (10–20 hours/week for 6–12 months) via a specialised firm (e.g., Envoy's advisory, EverWest, or independent workplace consultants) to run the first lease negotiation and TI build-out. Convert to a full-time hire once the operational footprint (multi-office, on-site services, workplace-experience programme) needs day-to-day management.

### Seat 4 — Ops Program Manager (Cross-Functional PMO)

**Trigger.** Two or more concurrent cross-functional programmes (systems migration, pricing rollout, workplace expansion, org restructure) — each needing a dedicated project owner who is not the CoS, not a line VP, and not a consultant.

**Timing.** Series B.

**Comp band.** US$130–190k base, 10–15% bonus, 0.03–0.10% equity at Series B for a 5–8-years-experienced program manager. <!-- needs-research: verify Senior Program Manager / Ops PM comp bands. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Contract-to-hire is common.** Program-management work often comes into the company via a contract program manager on a specific initiative (a Workday implementation, a Salesforce migration). Convert to a full-time hire when the pipeline of programmes is a steady state rather than one-off surges.

### Seat 5 — Head of Procurement

**Trigger.** Annual third-party spend crossing ~US$5–10M; SaaS-vendor count crossing ~50–75; the CFO reporting that vendor-management overhead is a real drag on Finance; the first evidence of vendor-consolidation opportunity (multiple overlapping tools).

**Timing.** Series B to Series C.

**Comp band.** US$150–220k base, 10–15% bonus, 0.03–0.10% equity at Series B/C. <!-- needs-research: verify Head of Procurement / Procurement Manager comp bands for scale-up-stage venture-backed tech; the role is under-benchmarked in Carta / Pave until later stages and often benchmarks against a controller-adjacent finance seat. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Defer or outsource until the trigger fires.** Below the spend threshold, procurement is a distributed responsibility — each department head owns their vendors, with Finance approving above a threshold. Above the threshold, hire a dedicated seat; the leverage on vendor consolidation and rate negotiation pays for the role quickly. See [chapter 05](./05-procurement-operating-model.md).

### Seat 6 — Head of Business Systems (IT / IT-Ops)

**Trigger.** SaaS-vendor count past ~75; multiple integrations between core systems (HRIS ↔ payroll ↔ finance ↔ IAM) that need a dedicated owner; the first evidence that "the CRM is talking to the ERP wrong" is costing multiple people multiple hours per week; the security posture requires a real IT function (SSO / SCIM enforcement, endpoint management, IAM governance).

**Timing.** Series B to Series C.

**Comp band.** US$180–260k base, 10–15% bonus, 0.05–0.15% equity at Series B/C. <!-- needs-research: verify Head of Business Systems / Head of IT comp bands in Pave / Radford data. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hybrid.** Endpoint management (laptop provisioning, MDM, help desk) can be outsourced to an MSP (Managed Service Provider) — Electric, ExpressIT, or a regional MSP — from day one and often should be. Business-systems architecture (systems selection, integration design, SaaS governance) needs a dedicated owner once the trigger fires; outsourcing it via a consultancy tends to produce a plan the internal team cannot execute. Hire the seat, keep the endpoint / help-desk work outsourced.

### Seat 7 — First BizOps team lead

**Trigger.** BizOps team growing past 3–4 analysts; the head-of-BizOps / CoS spending more than half their time on people-management of the analyst team; a distinct sub-function (Analytics, Strategic Finance, PMO) big enough to warrant its own lead.

**Timing.** Series C.

**Comp band.** US$180–260k base, 10–15% bonus, 0.08–0.20% equity at Series C. <!-- needs-research: verify BizOps team-lead comp bands. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire.** Sub-function leadership is not outsourceable. The one-year-earlier alternative is to promote from within — one of the existing analysts to team lead — which works well when the bench is strong and less well when it is not.

### Seat 8 — Analyst backfills and specialist ICs

**Trigger.** BizOps team growing to fill the sub-functions — Strategic Finance analyst, Business Analyst, Program Manager, Special Projects associate, Operations analyst.

**Timing.** Series C onward.

**Comp band.** IC-level analyst / associate bands from Seat 2 apply; senior IC bands (5–8 years) rise to US$150–200k base, 10% bonus, 0.03–0.10% equity at Series C. <!-- needs-research: verify senior BizOps IC bands in Pave / Radford. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire.** Sub-function IC hiring is the incremental growth of the BizOps team as the org scales; the leverage is durable and the cost is well within the operating-org budget.

### Seat 9 — International Ops Lead

**Trigger.** First international office opening (see [mod-113](../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/)); international headcount crossing 15–25; the workflow of "who owns Workplace / IT / Procurement / Business Systems in the London office" is being answered by the US team asynchronously and badly.

**Timing.** Series C onward, or earlier if the international footprint moves faster.

**Comp band.** US$180–260k base (in-country, adjusted for local market), 10–15% bonus, 0.05–0.15% equity at Series C. Local-market comp benchmarks (Carta International, Pave, or country-specific sources like Options Group in the UK) apply on top of the US comp architecture. <!-- needs-research: verify international ops-lead comp benchmarks by country — the market is highly local and Carta / Pave coverage varies. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire once the trigger fires.** Before the trigger, the US ops team plus a local admin (via EOR or via the first local direct-entity hire) handles the workflow.

### Seat 10 — Chief Operating Officer

**Trigger.** Company past 200–300 employees; CEO ready to hand over line authority for GTM, internal operations, or both; executive-team density such that a COO layer is warranted; board narrative asking for the seat.

**Timing.** Late Series B to Series C, sometimes deferred to Series D depending on CEO variables.

**Comp band.** US$350–500k base, 25–50% bonus, 1.0–3.0% equity at Series C for a first COO hire; higher at growth stage. The COO is a compensation-committee-level seat (see [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)) and the specific offer runs through the comp committee. <!-- needs-research: verify COO comp bands in Radford executive-comp data and Carta State-of-Comp reports for Series C+ US venture-backed tech. -->

**Hire vs. defer vs. outsource.** **Hire — but only when the CEO trigger is real.** A premature COO is the single most expensive ops-org mistake ([chapter 01](./01-coo-vs-cos-and-bizops-org-design.md)) — hundreds of thousands of dollars of base plus multi-point equity, absorbed for 12–24 months, then unwound with a costly executive-departure conversation. Defer until the CEO can honestly answer "I am ready to let this person overturn my line calls without a re-litigation every time."

## The hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource matrix

The pattern-recognition across the ten seats:

| Seat | Bridge option before hiring | Latest defensible defer point |
|---|---|---|
| CoS | None (proximity to CEO is the value) | Only defer if CEO cannot actually delegate |
| BizOps analyst | Fractional BizOps firm | ~50–75 employees |
| Workplace | Fractional Workplace lead; tenant-rep firm | First direct lease |
| Ops PM | Contract PM per programme | Two concurrent cross-functional programmes |
| Procurement | Distributed by department + Finance approval | ~US$5–10M annual third-party spend |
| Business Systems | Outsourced MSP for endpoint / help desk | ~75+ SaaS vendors, first integration owner needed |
| BizOps team lead | Promote from within | 3–4 analyst team size |
| Analyst backfills | Contract analysts on specific projects | As sub-function volume grows |
| International Ops Lead | Local admin + US ops async | 15–25 international headcount |
| COO | Strong CoS acting up | Only hire when CEO is ready to hand over line authority |

## Fully-loaded cost by stage

The cumulative fully-loaded cost of building out the ten seats is meaningful:

- **Series A (Seats 1 and part of 3):** ~US$0.5–1.0M / year fully loaded (CoS plus fractional Workplace).
- **Series B (Seats 1–4, part of 5–6):** ~US$1.5–3.0M / year fully loaded (CoS + BizOps analyst + Head of Workplace + Ops PM + emerging Procurement / Business Systems).
- **Series C (Seats 1–8, opening 9–10):** ~US$4.0–8.0M / year fully loaded (full BizOps team, dedicated Procurement + Business Systems, international ops opening, COO landing).
- **Growth stage (all ten seats + team growth):** ~US$10–20M+ / year fully loaded.

Fully-loaded means base + bonus + equity (at burn rate) + employer taxes + benefits + workspace + tools. The rule of thumb multiplier on base salary is **1.4–1.6×** for the fully-loaded number. <!-- needs-research: verify the 1.4–1.6× multiplier for fully-loaded cost in the current US benefits / employer-tax / equity-burn environment. -->

The board test on any ops-org headcount plan is: **does the incremental leverage of the new seat exceed its fully-loaded cost inside 12 months?** For each of the ten seats above, the leverage case has to be made explicitly at the time of hire — not on general principles.

## Coordination with the comp architecture chapter

The comp bands in this chapter are inputs to the compensation architecture the compensation function owns ([mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/)) and the equity-policy architecture the compensation committee owns ([mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/)). Specifically:

- **Band placement** — where each seat sits in the company's job architecture (level, band, geo-differential) is set by the People / Comp function against the benchmark sources named above.
- **Equity guidelines** — the equity grant for each seat conforms to the company's equity-grant guidelines set by the comp committee.
- **Executive-officer treatment** — any seat that reports to the CEO and is a Section-16-officer-equivalent (COO in particular; CoS occasionally) runs through the comp committee for offer approval.

This chapter names *what the market pays for the seat*. [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) authors *how the company translates that market into its own comp architecture*. Do not build the ops-org plan against comp bands that do not agree with the People / Finance function's architecture — the offers will land as outliers and the resulting inconsistency will surface at the first comp-review cycle.

## Common failure modes

- **Hiring the tenth seat before the third.** A CEO hires a COO at Series A without any of the underlying ops infrastructure (BizOps analyst, systems, cadence). The COO spends 12 months building the org they were supposed to inherit, and the CEO concludes the hire was premature. Fix: sequence bottom-up. The COO sits on top of a functioning ops org, not a blank slate.
- **Comp bands set from a single source.** The company uses Carta only, or Pave only, or asks its board members' comp advisors — and lands offers that are outliers to at least one relevant peer set. Fix: triangulate across at least two sources and reconcile to the company's comp architecture.
- **Outsourced ops leadership.** A fractional COO or fractional Chief of Staff is retained for 12+ months in what should be a full-time seat. The fractional model works for specific project surges (workplace lease, systems migration, first-close M&A diligence) and fails as steady-state ops leadership.
- **Deferring Business Systems until it is a crisis.** SaaS vendor count crosses 100 with no owner; integrations break silently; SSO / SCIM enforcement lapses; the first security-audit finding calls out identity governance. Fix: hire Business Systems at the trigger, not after the incident.
- **Skipping the trigger check on every hire.** Every ops seat gets hired because "companies our stage have one," without a specific written trigger. Result: an ops payroll several million dollars a year without a defensible leverage case. Fix: write the trigger down at the time of hire and revisit at every promotion / backfill.

## Summary

- The ops function's first ten hires are: **CoS → BizOps analyst → Head of Workplace → Ops PM → Head of Procurement → Head of Business Systems → BizOps team lead → analyst backfills → international ops lead → COO.** Order is a default, not a mandate.
- Each seat has a specific **trigger** — headcount, spend, systems count, programme count, footprint — that must be true for the seat to earn its cost. Hire against the trigger, not against a stage label.
- **Comp bands** come from Carta, Pave, and Radford (plus supplementary sources). Triangulate across at least two, reconcile to the company's comp architecture ([mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/)), and treat all bands as directional and periodically re-benchmarked.
- The **hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource** call for each seat has a defensible bridge option (fractional lead, contract PM, outsourced MSP, promoted-from-within) that lets the company defer full-time hires until the trigger is real.
- Cumulative fully-loaded cost grows from **~US$0.5–1M / year at Series A to ~US$10–20M+ / year at growth stage.** Every seat's leverage case must exceed its fully-loaded cost inside 12 months.
- The most expensive ops-org mistake is a **premature COO** ([chapter 01](./01-coo-vs-cos-and-bizops-org-design.md)). The second most expensive is **hiring the tenth seat before the third.**
