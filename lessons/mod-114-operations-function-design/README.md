# mod-114 — Operations Function Design

> The COO vs. Chief-of-Staff decision at Series A/B, the first-ten-ops-hires sequencing against stage triggers and Carta / Pave / Radford comp benchmarks, the systems-selection meta-framework (buying-criteria matrix, graduation triggers by category, migration change-management pattern), the workplace progression from remote-first to coworking to first direct lease to long-term hub to hub-and-spoke (with tenant-representation firm engagement and sublease exit design), the procurement operating model (spend taxonomy, approval-authority matrix, annual vendor review, consolidation playbook, tooling stack), the four-layer operating cadence (weekly / monthly / quarterly / annual), the organisational-evolution canon (Greiner, Adizes, Scaling Up / Rockefeller Habits, OKRs), the CEO–CoS or CEO–COO operating relationship, and the executive-team operating rhythm (E-team weekly, 1:1s, quarterly offsite, annual 360, 100-day onboarding, Lencioni / Horowitz-canon conflict management) — the operating infrastructure that converts an executive team of talented individuals into an executive team that actually runs the company.

**Track:** Startup Operations & Governance (level 50) · **Stage:** SERIES-A / SERIES-B / GROWTH · **Pillar:** operations · people-ops · **Hours:** 36

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) through [mod-113](../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/) author the *substantive* functions of a scale-up — legal entities, employment-contract substrate, HR operations, equity-comp architecture, performance and offboarding, culture, commercial contracts, privacy, governance, risk, international. Each is a vertical, function-level body of practice owned by a specific executive seat (GC, CPO, CFO, Head of People, Head of Risk).

But every scale-up past about 50 employees discovers that having each vertical function in place is not the same thing as having an organisation that runs. The weekly rhythm, the cross-functional programme management, the systems architecture, the workplace infrastructure, the vendor and procurement discipline, the executive-team mechanics — these are the *horizontal* infrastructure that makes the vertical functions interoperable. Without it, each function optimises locally, the executive-team meetings degrade into update theatre, systems multiply without consolidation, workplace decisions are made in panic, procurement sprawls, and the CEO burns down trying to personally hold the whole machine together.

This module owns the horizontal infrastructure.

The operations function decomposes into ten operating decisions the corporation has to make on purpose, not by drift:

1. **The COO vs. Chief-of-Staff decision at Series A/B.** The four-seat architecture of operations (CoS, COO, Head of BizOps, Head of Workplace), the CEO's variables that drive the decision (comparative advantage, willingness to delegate, executive-team shape, fundraising-narrative pressure), the Series-A / B / growth default org designs, and the CoS-to-COO graduation path.
2. **The first ten operations hires sequenced against stage triggers and comp benchmarks.** CoS → BizOps analyst → Head of Workplace → Ops PM → Head of Procurement → Head of Business Systems → BizOps team lead → analyst backfills → international ops lead → COO. Carta / Pave / Radford comp bands and the hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource call for each.
3. **The systems-selection meta-framework.** The six-criterion buying matrix (build-or-buy, integration, security, scale, TCO, exit), the graduation triggers by system category (HRIS, ATS, comp tool, CLM, privacy, GRC, board portal, spend management, procurement, IT and endpoint), and the standard migration pattern (scope → vendor selection → implementation → data migration → UAT → cutover → decommission).
4. **The workplace progression from remote-first to hub-and-spoke.** Coworking at Series A, first direct short-term lease at late Series A / Series B, long-term hub lease at Series B, hub-and-spoke at growth. Tenant-representation firms (CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Newmark, Colliers, Savills, Cresa), TI negotiation, sublease exit option, and the RTO / hybrid-policy interaction.
5. **The procurement operating model.** Spend taxonomy, board-approved approval-authority matrix, annual vendor review, vendor-consolidation playbook, corporate-card / invoice / PO split, tooling stack (Ramp, Brex, Rho, Bill, Airbase, Spendesk, Zip, Vendr, Sastrify, Coupa, Ariba), vendor-diligence checklist.
6. **The four-layer operating cadence.** Weekly ops review, monthly business review gated on the CFO's monthly close, quarterly OKRs cycle (setting / mid-quarter check-in / retrospective / recalibration), annual planning and E-team offsite 6–8 weeks in advance of fiscal-year start.
7. **The organisational-evolution canon.** Greiner's phases (creativity → direction → delegation → coordination → collaboration) and crises (leadership → autonomy → control → red-tape); Adizes's lifecycle (Infancy → Go-Go → Adolescence → Prime → Aristocracy → Bureaucracy); Verne Harnish's Scaling Up / Rockefeller Habits (People / Strategy / Execution / Cash); the OKRs canon (Grove / Doerr / Wodtke).
8. **The CEO–CoS or CEO–COO operating relationship.** The decision-rights division (CEO exclusive / CoS or COO unilateral / joint), the calendar-and-prioritisation partnership, the difficult-conversation partnership, the E-team meeting choreography, the board-preparation partnership, and the CoS-to-COO graduation.
9. **The executive-team operating rhythm.** E-team weekly meeting, CEO-executive 1:1 cadence, quarterly executive offsite, annual 360, 100-day executive-onboarding programme, Lencioni / Horowitz-canon conflict management, interaction with the compensation-committee executive-performance review.
10. **The ownership-boundary map.** What mod-114 owns end-to-end; what it hands off to sibling modules (mod-101 through 113, the finance-fundraising curriculum, the product-gtm curriculum); what it defers to specialist advisors (executive-coaching firms, tenant-rep firms, real-estate counsel, procurement specialist firms, business-systems implementation partners, executive-search firms, fractional operators).

Get this right and the corporation compounds — the CoS lands with a written operating relationship with the CEO; the ten ops hires sequence against defensible triggers; the systems architecture migrates deliberately rather than by crisis; the workplace footprint sizes to the actual peak-day attendance; procurement discipline saves 8–15% of controllable spend; the four-layer operating cadence produces the raw material for the board pack; the executive team meets weekly in a working session rather than an update session; and new executives land through a 100-day plan rather than figuring it out alone. Get it wrong and the corporation absorbs the predictable failure set — a premature COO that unwinds expensively at month 18; an ops payroll of several million dollars a year with no leverage case per seat; a 10-year lease signed to today's headcount that becomes a stranded asset at the next pivot; a vendor list that nobody consolidates and that silently auto-renews; an executive team that meets but does not decide; a 360 that scores but does not develop.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical scale-up at Series A, B, and growth stage, produce and defend:

1. **An operations-function org design** — the four-seat architecture (CoS, COO, Head of BizOps, Head of Workplace) at the current stage, reporting lines, decision rights, and the hire triggers for each seat; the Series-A / B / growth default designs calibrated to the specific CEO's variables.
2. **A first-ten-ops-hires sequencing plan** — the ten seats sequenced by stage trigger, comp bands triangulated across Carta / Pave / Radford, the hire-vs-defer-vs-outsource call per seat, the cumulative fully-loaded cost by stage, and a defensible leverage case per hire.
3. **A systems-selection meta-framework and migration plan** — the six-criterion buying matrix applied to a specific system category, the graduation triggers by category, a complete migration plan (scope → vendor selection → implementation → data migration → UAT → cutover → decommission) with change-management artefacts.
4. **A workplace progression plan** — the current-stage workplace footprint, the next-stage transition plan (coworking → direct lease; direct lease → long-term hub; single hub → hub-and-spoke), the tenant-rep firm engagement, the lease-term negotiation points, the sublease exit design, and the RTO / hybrid-policy coordination.
5. **A procurement operating model** — the spend taxonomy, the board-approved approval-authority matrix, the renewal calendar, the vendor-consolidation playbook, the vendor-diligence checklist, and the tooling-stack selection (corporate card / bill pay / PO split).
6. **A four-layer operating cadence** — the weekly ops review, the monthly business review gated on the CFO's monthly close, the quarterly OKRs cycle, the annual planning cycle, with meeting agendas, pre-reads, action-item tracking, and executive-team artefacts.
7. **A stage-model framing** — the current Greiner / Adizes stage, the next predictable crisis, the operating-rhythm adjustment to the stage, and the ongoing Prime-maintenance discipline against the natural drift toward either chaos or bureaucracy.
8. **A CEO–CoS or CEO–COO operating relationship document** — the one-page decision-rights division, the calendar-and-prioritisation partnership, the difficult-conversation partnership, the E-team meeting choreography, the board-preparation partnership with the CFO and GC, and the graduation path.
9. **An executive-team operating rhythm** — the E-team weekly meeting agenda and norms, the CEO-executive 1:1 cadence, the quarterly offsite programme with strategy / OKRs / team-health content, the annual 360 design, the 100-day executive-onboarding programme, the Lencioni-canon team-health programme, and the interaction with the compensation-committee annual review.
10. **A defensible ownership-boundary map** — "does this operations question belong here, in a sibling module ([mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) through [mod-113](../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/), the finance-fundraising curriculum, the product-gtm curriculum), or with a specialist advisor (executive-coaching firm, tenant-rep firm, real-estate counsel, procurement specialist firm, business-systems implementation partner, executive-search firm, fractional operator)?"
