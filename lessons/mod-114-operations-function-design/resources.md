# Resources — mod-114 Operations Function Design

The chapters of this module reference a specific body of operator and management-canon literature. The references below are the primary sources that an operator building the operations function should read or at least know. Where a reference is cited in a specific chapter, the chapter number is noted.

## Organisational-growth canon (chapter 07)

- **Larry E. Greiner, *Evolution and Revolution as Organizations Grow*.** First published in *Harvard Business Review* (1972); revised in *HBR* May–June 1998 with an added sixth phase. The canonical model of organisational growth through creativity / direction / delegation / coordination / collaboration, each followed by its predictable crisis. <!-- needs-research: confirm the specific HBR issue references for the 1972 original and the 1998 revision, and verify Greiner's own naming of the added sixth phase. -->
- **Ichak Adizes, *Managing Corporate Lifecycles*** (first published 1988; subsequent editions through the Adizes Institute, including *Corporate Lifecycles* and *Managing Corporate Lifecycles — Volume 1 and 2*). The lifecycle model (Infancy → Go-Go → Adolescence → Prime → Aristocracy → Bureaucracy → Death) and the Adizes Institute's practitioner publications.
- **Verne Harnish, *Scaling Up: How a Few Companies Make It... and Why the Rest Don't*** (Gazelles Inc., 2014). The "Four Decisions" (People, Strategy, Execution, Cash) framework; the daily huddle / weekly meeting / monthly meeting / quarterly meeting / annual meeting rhythm; the One-Page Strategic Plan.
- **Verne Harnish, *Mastering the Rockefeller Habits*** (2002, revised 2012). The predecessor to *Scaling Up*; the ten Rockefeller Habits.

## Operating cadence and OKRs canon (chapters 06, 07)

- **Andy Grove, *High Output Management*** (Random House, 1983; reissued 1995). The foundational text on management output as leverage; the weekly 1:1; the operations review. The source material for the OKRs (then "iMBOs") practice at Intel.
- **John Doerr, *Measure What Matters: How Google, Bono, and the Gates Foundation Rock the World with OKRs*** (Portfolio, 2017). The canonical modern OKRs text; case studies from Google, Intuit, and others; the 0.7 "stretch goal" convention.
- **Christina Wodtke, *Radical Focus: Achieving Your Most Important Goals with Objectives and Key Results*** (Boxes & Arrows, 2016; revised 2021). The practitioner reference for scale-up OKRs; the "connected" (vs. "cascaded") model; the cadence-of-commitment framing.
- **Christina Wodtke, *The Team That Managed Itself: A Story of Leadership*** (Cucina Media, 2019). A practitioner narrative on building a self-managing team around OKRs.

## Executive-team canon (chapters 08, 09)

- **Patrick Lencioni, *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team: A Leadership Fable*** (Jossey-Bass, 2002). The pyramid of absence-of-trust → fear-of-conflict → lack-of-commitment → avoidance-of-accountability → inattention-to-results.
- **Patrick Lencioni, *The Advantage: Why Organizational Health Trumps Everything Else in Business*** (Jossey-Bass, 2012). The broader framework around organisational health, extending the five-dysfunctions model to the whole executive team.
- **Patrick Lencioni, *The Four Obsessions of an Extraordinary Executive*** (Jossey-Bass, 2000). An earlier Lencioni text on the executive's job inside the team.
- **Ben Horowitz, *The Hard Thing About Hard Things: Building a Business When There Are No Easy Answers*** (HarperBusiness, 2014). The canonical operator text on hard conversations, difficult decisions, and the CEO's responsibility for the direct conversations; the COO-decision framing.
- **Frank Slootman, *Amp It Up: Leading for Hypergrowth by Raising Expectations, Increasing Urgency, and Elevating Intensity*** (Wiley, 2022). The operating-discipline framing from the Data Domain / ServiceNow / Snowflake CEO; the COO-as-enforcer-of-operating-discipline pattern.
- **Horst Schulze, *Excellence Wins*** (Zondervan, 2019). The operator-executive canon from the former Ritz-Carlton COO; service-excellence operations and executive-team formation.
- **The Table Group (Lencioni's firm)** at [tablegroup.com](https://www.tablegroup.com) — the organisational-health consulting practice derived from Lencioni's writing.
- **ghSMART** at [ghsmart.com](https://www.ghsmart.com) — executive assessment, executive onboarding, and executive-team coaching practice.
- **Reboot.io** at [reboot.io](https://www.reboot.io) — executive and CEO coaching practice founded by Jerry Colonna.
