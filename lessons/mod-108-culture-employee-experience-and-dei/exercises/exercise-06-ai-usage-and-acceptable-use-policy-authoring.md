# Exercise 06 — AI-usage and acceptable-use policy authoring

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [06 — AI-usage and acceptable-use policy](../06-ai-usage-and-acceptable-use-policy.md)

## Problem statement

Marrowfield Health Technologies is a Delaware C-corp at Series-B, headquartered in Boston with engineering concentrated in Seattle and a go-to-market team in New York, London (eight employees — out-of-scope for this exercise but on the roadmap), Austin, and remote-distributed across CA, CO, IL, MA, NY, TX, and WA. Headcount is 198 and tracking to cross 220 before the fiscal year closes. The product is a workflow-and-analytics platform sold into mid-market provider groups and digital-health vendors; the corporation is not itself a HIPAA covered entity but operates as a business associate for most of its customer base, and signed BAAs with 46 of its 61 enterprise customers cover identified protected health information inside the production tenant. The corporation files EEO-1 Component 1 under the 100+ private-employer threshold and has a non-federal-contractor posture (no OFCCP jurisdiction).

A board advisor's "AI governance" note landed in the CEO's inbox three weeks ago. Over the subsequent fortnight, four shadow-use incidents surfaced inside the exec staff meeting: (1) two senior engineers acknowledged they have been using personal-tier ChatGPT and personal-tier GitHub Copilot subscriptions "for productivity" on customer-code repositories, including a repo that reads from the BAA-covered production tenant; (2) a senior AE pasted a 30-record prospect-contact export — including names, email addresses, employer, and inferred buying-intent notes — into a free-tier Claude account to generate an outbound-sequencing recommendation; (3) the GC's deputy summarised a term sheet for a strategic partnership through a personal ChatGPT Plus account over the weekend because "the enterprise tool was slow"; (4) the CEO drafted the last two all-hands messages, including a paragraph previewing an upcoming comp-band revision, using a free-tier Gemini account at home. The head of people also learned, in a Slack DM from an NYC-based employee, that a recruiter in the TA function has been using an AI resume-screening tool licensed on a personal credit card to rank inbound applications for engineering roles — a configuration that likely sits inside NYC Local Law 144's AEDT scope and that the corporation has made no bias-audit, candidate-notice, or vendor-security review preparation for.

The CEO has given the head of people and the CTO six weeks to deliver a written AUP — tier framework, data-class red-lines, human-in-the-loop norms, enterprise-licence vs personal-licence posture, regulator plan, training and discipline — that the GC and the head of engineering will co-sign and that will land in the handbook via the chapter-02 acknowledgement mechanic. Author the full package Marrowfield will publish and operate against.

## Requirements

Make an initial tier assignment per tool and name the DPA / SSO / audit-logging / retention-posture requirements the tool must meet to retain its Tier 1 or Tier 2 slot.
3. Treat any enterprise-licence pricing, current enterprise-tier contractual data-use posture, zero-data-retention availability, or IP-indemnity scope that is not explicitly stated in chapter 06 or in resources.md as `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`. Do not invent pricing or terms.
4. State the per-tool registry record Marrowfield will maintain: current tier, assignment date, approving reviewers (head of security, GC, head of AI-governance or interim-owner), permitted data classes, permitted use cases, retention posture, sub-processor list at the vendor, review-due date. 5. Name the five tier-movement criteria from chapter 06 (enterprise-licence execution, ToS change, regulator-jurisdiction change, security incident, vendor viability) and state the Marrowfield-specific review cadence (quarterly minimum, ad-hoc on trigger).
6. State the "not on the registry equals Tier 4" default and the amnesty-and-reporting posture that applies during the initial six-week rollout.

### Part B — Data-class red-line table

Author the data-class red-line table that governs what may be pasted into which tier for Marrowfield. Rows (one row per data class): customer PII (end-user PII flowing through the production tenant and prospect-contact PII in the CRM), PHI (identified protected health information under the 46 BAAs covering identified PHI, and the broader "likely-PHI-adjacent" class in customer datasets Marrowfield has not fully inventoried), source code (first-party proprietary source, open-source-derivative work-product, and customer-shared integration code), non-public financials (pre-release ARR and revenue data, in-flight comp-band revisions, cash-position data, forecast models), personnel data (candidate PII and resumes, employee performance data, compensation data, EEO-1 Component 1 data). Columns (one column per tier): Tier 1 (permitted), Tier 2 (restricted), Tier 3 (sanctioned use case only), Tier 4 (forbidden). For each cell, state the operating posture in one line — permitted / permitted-with-constraint / not-permitted — and the specific constraint if the cell is permitted-with-constraint (for example: PHI permitted in Tier 1 only inside a tool covered by an executed BAA with Marrowfield as business associate).

Below the table, state the two operating notes from chapter 06 in Marrowfield's language: (a) **inference is data** — a prompt that lets a reasonable reader infer a customer or a patient is still customer or PHI data; (b) **aggregation raises the class** — bulk paste is treated at the highest class present.

### Part C — Human-in-the-loop and disclosure norms per use case

Per use case, author the review requirement, the disclosure requirement, and the named accountable role for the output. Minimum use cases to cover:

1. **AI-generated code.** Covering the engineer-shadow-use case in the problem statement. State the pull-request-description requirement, the code-review posture, the second-reviewer rule on security-sensitive surfaces (auth, crypto, PHI-touching paths, third-party API-key handling), and the accountable role (the author engineer, backed by the reviewing engineer and the head of engineering as escalation). Explicitly defer the SDLC-governance substance (secure-coding evaluation methodology, model-risk framing on code assistants) to `chief-ai-officer-learning` and `ai-risk-engineer-learning`.
2. **AI-generated content (customer-facing and internal-facing).** Covering the sales-shadow-use case and the CEO all-hands case. State the editorial-review posture, the fact-check-against-source rule for product claims and numbers, the "material AI use" disclosure norm per chapter 06 Section 4a, and the accountable role (the author, backed by the marketing / content reviewer and ultimately the exec whose name is on the artefact). Address the CEO-authored all-hands specifically: internal-comms with MNPI-adjacent content (an unannounced comp-band revision is MNPI-adjacent inside a company in pre-IPO cadence or with equity-plan reporting obligations) is not an acceptable use of a free-tier tool under any Marrowfield posture, and the review requirement for exec internal comms needs to be stated explicitly.
3. **AI-generated decisions (hiring, employment, regulated).** Covering the recruiter-AEDT case. State the "no AI tool scores, ranks, or rejects a candidate or employee absent explicit sign-off from the head of people and the algorithmic-governance owner" rule from chapter 06. Describe the human-review requirement for any AI-assisted screening output that does make it into a decision chain (reviewer identity, documentation requirement, candidate-notice requirement under NYC LL 144 for the NYC-exposed role). Name the accountable role (the hiring manager, backed by the recruiter and the head of people). Defer the bias-audit methodology, the AEDT-vendor evaluation, and the broader algorithmic-governance stack to `chief-ai-officer-learning` and `head-of-ai-governance-learning`.
4. **AI-generated legal and financial work-product.** Covering the GC-deputy term-sheet case. State the "accountable licensed professional reviews before use / filing / reliance" rule from chapter 06 Section 3. Name the specific MNPI-handling rule — the term sheet is a non-public strategic-partnership document and is categorically off-limits for a personal-tier tool regardless of personal-ChatGPT-Plus posture — and point at the GC as accountable, with the CFO as the parallel accountable role for financial work-product.

For each use case, also state the Marrowfield-specific disclosure norm: internal (code-review / doc-review / decision-documentation note), external (customer disclosure under the "material AI use" posture and under customer-contract-specific AI restrictions tracked in the CRM per [mod-109](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/) — treat the mod-109 cross-reference as the handoff for the actual contract-term tracking).

### Part D — Enterprise-licence vs personal-licence posture

2. **BAA requirement for the PHI-touching tools.** Which Tier 1 tools Marrowfield will require a BAA for before any PHI touches the tool, and which tools Marrowfield's security team has preliminarily indicated will or will not sign a BAA (treat specific vendor BAA availability as `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` where not stated in chapter 06 or resources.md).
3. **Enterprise-licence tenant configuration standards.** The minimum configuration required before a tool clears for Tier 1 — SSO via the corporation's IdP, admin-controlled retention set to the minimum the business function requires, audit-logging enabled, tenant-isolation attestation on file, zero-training-on-inputs posture confirmed in writing (treat vendor-specific posture as `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`).
4. **Personal-licence posture.** The default rule: personal-tier accounts of any tool for which an enterprise tier exists are Tier 4 at Marrowfield. The narrow Tier-3 exception process from chapter 06 Section 5 (public-data-only sanctioned use, named-approver sign-off, time-boxed, registry entry).
5. **Shadow-use enforcement posture during the six-week rollout.** The amnesty-and-reporting mechanic from chapter 06 Section 5 applied to the four shadow-use cases in the problem statement — which of the four will be routed through amnesty (no formal discipline, migration to Tier 1), which will be routed through documented-warning (because of the data class involved), and which will be routed through the material-breach investigation path.
