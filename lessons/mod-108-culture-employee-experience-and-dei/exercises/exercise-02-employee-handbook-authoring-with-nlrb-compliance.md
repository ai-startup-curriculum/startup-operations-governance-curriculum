# Exercise 02 — Employee handbook authoring with NLRB compliance

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [02 — Employee handbook — NLRB-compliant structure post-Stericycle (2023)](../02-employee-handbook-nlrb-compliant-post-stericycle.md)

## Problem statement

Marrow Harbor is a Series-B B2B infrastructure-security SaaS company at 180 employees, Delaware-incorporated, headquartered in San Francisco with an engineering centre of mass in San Francisco and Oakland, a growing go-to-market team seeded out of Chicago and Denver, a two-person security-research pod in Seattle, and a recently-expanded enterprise-sales effort anchored by a senior AE who relocated to Brooklyn seven months ago without the people team updating the state footprint. The exec roster is the founder-CEO, founder-CTO, VP Engineering (hired fourteen months ago from a public-company security team), VP Sales (hired eight months ago, running the New York, Chicago, and Denver sellers), VP Marketing, CFO (hired four months ago out of a late-stage fintech), a general counsel who joined ninety days ago as the first in-house legal hire, and a CHRO who starts on Monday after four months of CEO-plus-CFO coverage of people operations. The handbook today is a thirty-eight-page PDF the founder-CTO assembled in 2018 off a since-deprecated PEO template, lightly edited twice since by the previous head of people, never run past employment counsel in the three states added after 2021, and never re-acknowledged by any of the forty-two employees in the 2021 hire wave or by anyone hired through the PEO migration in 2023.

Three live tensions are driving the drill. First, the 2018 confidentiality chapter still reads, "Employees are prohibited from discussing the terms and conditions of their employment, including compensation, benefits, and performance evaluations, with any third party, including other employees," and the general counsel flagged on day eleven that the clause is facially invalid under **NLRB Stericycle, Inc., 372 NLRB No. 113 (2023)** and has been exposed as such for the full window since the Board overruled *Boeing*. Second, the severance-and-release template the CFO and the departing head of people used for two separations in Q2 still contains a broad non-disparagement clause covering "any statement, oral or written, about the Company, its officers, directors, employees, products, or services," and the general counsel has flagged it under **NLRB McLaren Macomb, 372 NLRB No. 58 (2023)** — the two separated employees were both individual contributors, both received the clause verbatim, and neither release has been tested but both are substantially unenforceable on the parts that mattered. Third, the Brooklyn-based senior AE's relocation surfaced in a passing comment at a VP Sales staff meeting; the general counsel traced it back and discovered the corporation has been running New York City-based job postings for sales roles for six months without a good-faith salary range (NYC Local Law 32 of 2022) and has deployed an automated resume-screening tool across the SDR funnel for ninety days without a NYC Local Law 144 of 2023 bias audit or candidate notice. The CFO's eyebrows climbed somewhere between the second and third of those facts.

The incoming CHRO has asked you (as her principal advisor and the operator-in-residence through her first ninety days) to walk into her first month with a full handbook re-authoring package — Stericycle-posture audit, chapter list and ownership map, state-and-local supplement matrix for the actual footprint (California, New York / New York City, Illinois, Colorado, Washington), rewrites of the two facially-invalid legacy clauses, acknowledgement-and-signature workflow, and update cadence — scoped to publish v4.0 before the end-of-quarter all-hands. The general counsel will co-sign the clause-by-clause audit but has told the CHRO explicitly that she is not re-authoring the handbook; the people team owns the structure, the clause rewrites, and the signature workflow, and employment counsel reviews. Author the package Marrow Harbor will publish and operate against.

## Requirements

### Part A — Stericycle-posture audit checklist

A one-page auditable checklist the CHRO and the general counsel will walk every workplace rule in the handbook through before v4.0 publishes. It must:

1. Restate the Stericycle test from chapter 02 in Marrow Harbor's language — the General Counsel's burden on "reasonable tendency to chill Section 7 activity," the economically-dependent-employee reading, the presumptive-unlawfulness consequence, and the employer's narrow-tailoring rebuttal.
2. Walk the six clause categories chapter 02 names — **confidentiality, social media, non-disparagement, recording, civility and professional conduct, off-duty conduct** — and for each give (a) the specific question the auditor is asking the clause, (b) the narrow-tailoring pattern per chapter 02, (c) a pass / rewrite / escalate-to-counsel decision rule. Confidentiality carries the additional requirement of a drafted Section 7 savings clause at the end of the section.
3. Specify the Section 7 savings clause posture — one in the handbook introduction and one at the end of each of the six policy categories above — and the rule that a general savings clause does not cure a facially-invalid rule.
4. Name the escalation path when the auditor cannot decide pass vs. rewrite from the chapter 02 narrowing pattern alone — specifically to the general counsel first, outside employment counsel second, with a documented note in the audit log either way.

### Part B — Core handbook chapter list and ownership map

Author the chapter-by-chapter structure for Marrow Harbor's v4.0 handbook, following the reference table-of-contents in chapter 02 but adapted to the actual footprint. For each chapter the deliverable captures:

1. Chapter number and title.
2. Owner-of-substance — the function that drafts the chapter (people team, general counsel, information security, finance, benefits-broker-of-record).
3. Draft channel — in-house by the people team, drafted by the general counsel, drafted by outside employment counsel, pulled from a PEO or HRIS vendor state-supplement template and reviewed in-house, or pulled from a sibling-module reference policy (see cross-references below).
4. Cross-reference target — where the substantive policy lives if it lives outside the handbook. At minimum the chapters covering at-will drafting, PIIA / IP, non-compete posture, and arbitration-clause posture defer to [mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/); the HRIS / PEO-side mechanics defer to [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/); compensation and benefits substance defers to [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/); the termination and severance mechanics defer to [mod-107 chapter 05](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/05-termination-playbook.md); the equity-grant mechanics defer to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).
5. Appendix flag — which chapters trigger a state-supplement appendix entry (Part C) and which do not.

The ownership map is the artifact the CHRO will walk the exec team through at a thirty-minute operating review before Draft 1 goes out for counsel review. Keep it legible from a single page.

### Part C — State-and-local supplement matrix

Author the state-and-local supplement matrix for Marrow Harbor's actual footprint — **California, New York (state and NYC overlay), Illinois, Colorado, Washington** — plus a one-row placeholder-and-escalation entry for any additional state that surfaces during the audit (an acquired team, a founder's home state, a distributed hire the exec team did not know about). For each jurisdiction the matrix captures:

1. The specific statutes and ordinances the supplement responds to — pay transparency, paid sick leave, paid family and medical leave, non-compete restrictions, biometric consent (where applicable), AEDT and AI-in-hiring rules (where applicable), lactation accommodation, meal-and-rest-break rules, state-specific sexual-harassment-training mandates, and any state analogue to the Silenced-No-More non-disclosure restrictions. Cite statutes by name and section number using [resources.md](../resources.md) as the primary-source index.
2. The handbook obligation — what the supplement says the employer must do, in one or two lines per statute.
3. The published location in the handbook — supplement-appendix section, main-chapter cross-reference, or standalone standalone-notice document (e.g., BIPA written consent, CCPA employee-data notice).
4. The specific New-York footprint sub-matrix must call out the NYC overlay — ESSTA, Local Law 32 (pay transparency in job ads), **Local Law 144 of 2023 (AEDT bias audit and candidate notice)** — and name the AEDT exposure the senior AE's relocation created on the SDR resume-screening tool.

Where a cite in the matrix depends on current-year rulemaking that chapter 02 or [resources.md](../resources.md) flagged with a `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` marker (current NY PFL contribution rate, current Colorado FAMLI contribution rate, current Washington RCW 49.62 non-compete earnings threshold, current NYC DCWP AEDT enforcement guidance, current Illinois EPRC filing cadence, current-year DEI executive-order posture), carry the marker through into the supplement rather than invent a number.

### Part D — Clause rewrites (before / after)

Rewrite the two facially-invalid legacy clauses from the problem statement, plus one third clause of your choice from the chapter 02 six-category list that the audit in Part A surfaces as plausibly broken in a 2018 PEO template. For each rewrite deliver:

1. **Before.** The clause as it exists in the current handbook or severance template (confidentiality clause for the handbook; non-disparagement clause for the severance template; your third choice for the third rewrite — e.g., a recording ban, a civility rule, a social-media pre-approval requirement, an off-duty-conduct catch-all).
2. **Diagnosis.** The specific sentence or phrase that fails the Stericycle reasonable-economically-dependent-employee reading, and the specific NLRB authority (Stericycle for the handbook clauses; McLaren Macomb for the severance clause).
3. **After.** The rewritten clause, drafted to the narrow-tailoring pattern chapter 02 specifies for that clause category, with the Section 7 savings clause appended where the chapter requires one.
4. **Where it lives.** Whether the clause sits in the handbook, the severance template, both, or an at-hire PIIA (cross-reference [mod-102](../../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/) and [mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/)). The severance-template rewrite must name the McLaren Macomb reset explicitly and the carve-out language for the non-supervisory pool.

Do not file-serve a generic template. The rewrites are drafted against Marrow Harbor's actual footprint — a California-majority company with New York, Illinois, Colorado, and Washington employees — and should carry the state-specific carve-outs the matrix in Part C surfaces (e.g., the Silenced-No-More overlay in California and Washington on the severance non-disparagement clause).

### Part E — Acknowledgement and signature workflow

Author the end-to-end acknowledgement-and-signature workflow Marrow Harbor will operate on top of its HRIS. Specify:

1. **At-hire signature.** When the handbook is delivered relative to day 1; the HRIS task that captures the acknowledgement; the specific fields the acknowledgement records (version-and-revision date, at-will reaffirmation, not-a-contract disclaimer, agreement-to-abide); the audit metadata captured (timestamp, IP address, document hash). Cross-reference [mod-104 chapter 07](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/07-hris-peo-payroll-stack.md) for the HRIS-side mechanics but do not re-author them.
2. **Material-update re-acknowledgement.** The specific definition of "material update" per chapter 02 (modifies rights or obligations, responds to a legal-compliance requirement, reflects a policy-level decision); the time-to-task-push after effective date (chapter 02 says within 5 business days); the time-to-complete (10 business days); the delta-only presentation pattern that prevents the "click-through-without-reading" failure mode.
3. **Failure-to-sign escalation.** The specific weekly report the HRBP runs; the manager-notification pattern at day 7; the people-head notification at day 11; the explicit guidance that failure-to-sign is not disciplinary on its own but renders the handbook unenforceable on the delta against the non-signing employee until signed, which is the actual operating consequence the manager should communicate.
4. **Retention.** The retention configuration per chapter 02 — at least four years post-termination for federal claims, longer where state statute requires. Version history of the handbook itself retained indefinitely. Prior-version production obligation when a dispute surfaces a policy from a specific point in time.
5. **The hire-wave backlog.** The specific mechanic that resolves the 2021 hire wave and the 2023 PEO-migration cohort who have never re-acknowledged — a one-time company-wide re-acknowledgement push at v4.0 publish, bounded by the 10-business-day completion window, with the explicit manager-script line for employees who ask why.

### Part F — Update cadence and change-log policy

Author Marrow Harbor's handbook-update cadence and version-log policy. Specify:

1. **Annual full review.** The month the review runs (chapter 02 suggests Q4 ahead of the new plan year); the attendees (head of people, general counsel, HRBP, outside employment counsel for state-supplement review); the agenda; the default closure of either a batch update effective January 1 or a documented no-changes note in the version log.
2. **Out-of-cycle triggers.** The explicit trigger inventory per chapter 02 — new state or local statute in a jurisdiction of operation, new NLRB decision or General Counsel memorandum, new EEOC guidance, new state AI-in-hiring statute, IRS or DOL rulemaking, corporate policy decision, litigation or agency-action feedback. Call out specifically the NYC Local Law 144 enforcement-guidance pathway and the open Silenced-No-More and non-compete pathways in California and Washington.
3. **Counsel-review gate.** The rule that any material update that touches a workplace rule (confidentiality, social media, non-disparagement, recording, civility, off-duty) or a state-supplement obligation routes through outside employment counsel before publish, independent of the general counsel's in-house review.
4. **Change-log format.** The version-log entry per update — date, version number, trigger event, affected clauses, counsel sign-off initials, re-acknowledgement scope (jurisdiction-limited vs. company-wide), re-acknowledgement completion percentage, and the explicit flag for any clause that was escalated to and defended by outside counsel.
5. **Re-publish-and-re-acknowledge.** The rule that a material update does not take effect against an employee until that employee has re-acknowledged. Name the specific scenario the chapter 02 motivation section opens with — a dispute arising over a 2024 policy an employee legitimately never saw — and the operating consequence that re-acknowledge-rate is a defensive-posture metric the people team reports to the CHRO weekly during a push and monthly otherwise.
