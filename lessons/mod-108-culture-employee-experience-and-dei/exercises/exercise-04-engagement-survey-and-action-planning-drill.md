# Exercise 04 — Engagement survey and action-planning drill

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — Engagement measurement and action planning](../04-engagement-measurement-and-action-planning.md)

## Problem statement

Marlowe Grid is a Series-C, developer-platform company at 340 employees. It sells a build-and-release-automation product into mid-market and lower-enterprise engineering organisations. Headquarters is in Austin; engineering has a 90-person pod in Berlin; a 25-person customer-engineering and support team sits in Singapore; sales, success, and marketing (~120 people combined) are split between Austin and a smaller New York hub. The exec team is nine-strong: founder-CEO, founder-CTO, President (external hire, 11 months in, running GTM), VP Engineering, VP Product, VP Design, CFO, General Counsel, and the Chief People Officer (CPO) who is 18 months into the seat and owns the engagement programme. There are 42 people-managers across the company, including seven VPs / directors on the leadership team.

Marlowe Grid has just closed its first annual deep-dive engagement survey (62 items plus open-text, Culture Amp as the incumbent vendor, 71% response rate on the deep-dive — above the 70% working target in chapter 04). Quarterly pulses have run on and off for two years but were never calendared against the operating rhythm. The headline numbers from this cycle, before executive-team review:

- **Overall engagement composite** is a plausible-looking internal mid-range score, but **manager effectiveness** is materially below the rest of the dimension set and below last year's score on the two items that persisted. The gap is concentrated on "my manager gives me actionable feedback on a regular basis" and "my manager sets clear expectations about my work." Compare to industry benchmark `<!-- needs-research: Culture Amp 2026 developer-platform / B2B SaaS Series-C manager-effectiveness benchmark panel -->`.
- **eNPS is flat year-over-year, with a slight downward drift** inside the margin where chapter 04 says eNPS should not be the only metric the executive team looks at. The qualitative open-text themes point to career-growth stall and perceived slow decision-making at the VP layer.
- **DEI sub-cut:** the inclusion-dimension composite (belonging, fairness, voice) in the Berlin engineering pod runs ~12 internal-score points below the company mean on "I feel that I can be my authentic self at work" and "my perspective is heard and valued by my team"; a separate cut on women in individual-contributor engineering roles across the whole company runs below the company mean on the same two items. Both cuts clear the n-threshold the chapter requires. External comparison is `<!-- needs-research: Culture Amp inclusion-dimension benchmarks for engineering-heavy B2B SaaS companies, Series-C stage, and the women-in-engineering-IC industry comparator -->`.
- **Manager-cohort heterogeneity.** Two managers in GTM (an SMB sales-manager cohort of ~8 people each) score materially above the company manager-effectiveness distribution and are being asked by their own skip-levels to teach what they are doing. One engineering director in Berlin and one customer-engineering manager in Singapore score materially below the distribution across both this cycle and the two pulses that preceded it.
- **Psychological-safety signal (per Edmondson 1999):** the two items Marlowe Grid runs that most closely map to the Edmondson construct ("I feel comfortable raising concerns with my manager" from the manager-effectiveness block and "it is safe to take a considered risk on this team" from the engagement block) show a bimodal distribution — some teams at or near ceiling, others materially below the mean. The CPO wants to add explicit psychological-safety items in the next cycle but has not yet decided whether to break the anchor-set discipline the chapter warns against.

Two forcing functions compress the next four weeks. First, the CEO has told the exec team she wants an all-hands response within the chapter's four-week norm from survey close; today is week 2 post-close, which means the exec briefing lands this week and the all-hands lands in two weeks. Second, the exec team is divided on publication. The President (GTM) and CFO argue for publishing dimension-level composite scores but holding sub-dimension and demographic-cut scores inside the exec and HRBP layer, on the grounds that "half-told numbers are worse than no numbers" and the Berlin cut will land badly without careful framing. The VP Engineering, the General Counsel, and the CPO argue for publishing the full dimension picture (including the manager-effectiveness and inclusion gaps) because publication is the forcing function on executive follow-through and because, per the chapter, "executive silence read as disinterest" is the single most-reliable way to kill the next cycle's response rate. The CEO has not yet called the publication question and has asked the CPO for a written recommendation.

The CPO has asked you (as her principal advisor / operator-in-residence) to author the full engagement-response package before the all-hands lands: the twelve-month cadence calendar; the vendor-selection memo (stay-with-Culture-Amp or switch, defended on the chapter's selection criteria); the dimension and item-design revisions; the executive-response plan including the all-hands script; the manager-cohort action-planning loop; and the publication-posture decision memo. The CPO wants the package executed against the four-week norm, the anchor-set discipline, and the anonymity thresholds in chapter 04 — not against a generic engagement-vendor playbook.

Author the engagement-response package Marlowe Grid will operate against for the remainder of the cycle and into the next.

## Requirements

### Part A — Twelve-month cadence calendar

A written specification for the Marlowe Grid engagement cadence, tied to the operating rhythm:

1. **Quarterly pulse schedule.** Author a twelve-month calendar in the format of the chapter 04 example — week number, event, owner, output — covering four quarterly pulses (10–15 items, 5–10 minutes each; 4–6 anchor items constant; 4–8 rotating items per quarter) and one annual deep-dive. Specify which quarters carry which rotating-item themes so that the pulse acts as a measurement loop on specific executive-team investments, not a vague temperature check.
2. **Tie to planning rhythms.** The calendar must explicitly align pulses and the deep-dive against:
   - The OKR-planning cycle (which quarter the pulse closes and feeds into OKR-setting; which pulse closes before the mid-cycle review).
   - The [mod-106 chapter 04](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/04-annual-comp-cycle.md) comp cycle — the pulse that must close *before* comp decisions are communicated and the pulse that must *not* close in the window when comp conversations are happening (signal contamination).
   - The [mod-107 chapter 01](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/01-performance-management-operating-system.md) performance-review cycle — the pulse that reads post-review and the pulse that reads pre-cycle.
3. **Onboarding and exit instruments.** Day-30 and day-90 onboarding pulses and the exit / alumni instrument are run on the hire or departure, not on the cadence calendar; name the owner and the data-handoff into the main engagement report without lumping them into the quarterly cohort (per the chapter's "do not dilute the signal" rule).
4. **Action-tracker publish moments.** Mark the week numbers at which the company-level and team-level action trackers must publish (within 9 weeks of each cycle close), and the subsequent pulse that explicitly re-checks whether committed actions shipped.
5. **Executive-briefing and all-hands windows.** Mark the two-week results-briefing deadline and the four-week all-hands deadline against every pulse and the deep-dive, not just the annual — pulse theatre and deep-dive theatre fail for the same reason.

### Part B — Instrument and vendor selection memo

A one-to-two-page memo to the CEO and exec team recommending the vendor posture for the next 12 months. The memo must:

1. **Make a recommendation** — stay with Culture Amp, switch to a named alternative, or run a parallel cycle (per the chapter's vendor-switch discipline). "Keep both and decide later" is not a recommendation.
2. **Defend on the chapter's six selection criteria** — question-bank quality, benchmarking data availability, manager-level reporting UX, HRIS integration, action-planning workflow, data-export capability. Reference, by name, the vendors in [resources.md](../resources.md) that were considered (Culture Amp, Lattice Engagement, 15Five Engage, Officevibe / Workleap, Peakon / Workday Peakon Employee Voice, Microsoft Viva Glint, Qualtrics EmployeeXM). For each considered vendor, give a one-paragraph read against the Marlowe Grid stage (Series-C, 340 headcount, three geographies, Rippling HRIS, Lattice on performance) and the specific dimensions above.
3. **Flag the needs-research surfaces.** Pricing tiers, HRIS-integration availability, benchmark-panel coverage, item-library specifics are `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` per the resources index — do not invent them. The memo should name what the CPO will verify before signing a renewal or a new MSA, not fabricate a comparison table.
4. **Anchor-set preservation.** If the memo recommends a switch, specify the parallel-run plan (one cycle, same population, both instruments) and the specific anchor items that must survive the switch so year-over-year comparison is preserved. If the memo recommends staying, say explicitly which items in the current instrument are the anchor set and what the policy is for touching them.
5. **Benchmark-panel discipline.** Address the chapter's warning that external benchmarks are useful for context but dangerous as a target. The memo should state how Marlowe Grid will report external benchmarks internally — context framing, not the primary metric — and should not promise an executive team that the next cycle will "close the gap to benchmark."

### Part C — Dimension and item design revisions

A written revision of the Marlowe Grid annual deep-dive instrument for the next cycle. The revision must:

1. **Preserve the anchor set.** List the specific anchor items (across eNPS, engagement, manager effectiveness, inclusion, wellbeing, leadership confidence, strategy clarity) that will not change between this cycle and next. Document explicitly that these items are frozen.
2. **Add rather than replace.** For each dimension, name the items being added for next cycle and the rationale. The additions must include at least one new psychological-safety item that reads against **Edmondson (1999)** — reference the resources entry for the Edmondson construct — and the four Edmondson-style domains (speak-up safety, learning safety, contribution safety, challenger safety as framed in the 2018 book) should be visible in the item set, even if not every domain gets its own item.
3. **Gallup Q12 anchoring.** The CPO wants the deep-dive to be legible to a reader familiar with the Gallup Q12. Map which Marlowe Grid items are the Q12 analogues (resources entry: Gallup Q12 and Harter et al. 2002) and which Marlowe Grid-specific items extend beyond the Q12. Do not simply adopt Q12 — the point is to show the mapping, not to replace the instrument.
4. **DEI / inclusion-dimension revisions.** Per the chapter and the [chapter 03](../03-dei-programme-design-post-sffa.md) legal frame, the inclusion items measure **inclusion outcomes** — belonging, fairness, voice, authentic-self — not demographic preferences or programmatic inferences from race / gender. Specify the items that will remain, the items being added to sharpen the Berlin and women-in-engineering-IC cuts, and the items being cut (if any) for being double-measurements of the same construct.
5. **Items to cut.** List any items the CPO is removing from the current instrument on the grounds that they load weakly onto their dimension, are duplicative, or are survey-fatigue bloat. Justify each cut against the year-over-year cost (chapter 04 is explicit that cuts are costly).
6. **Open-text question design.** Specify the two or three open-text prompts the instrument carries, how they are coded, and the chapter's rule on open-text (used for theme detection, not reproduced verbatim where it could identify the respondent).

### Part D — Executive-response plan and all-hands script

The written plan for the CEO's all-hands response within the four-week norm. The plan must:

1. **CEO all-hands script.** A written script (not slide bullets) for the opening section of the all-hands that lands two weeks from today. The script must open on what the executive team heard, including the uncomfortable parts (manager-effectiveness gap, Berlin inclusion cut, women-in-engineering-IC inclusion cut, flat eNPS); name the committed-vs-deliberately-held decisions; name the specific exec who owns the response for each committed decision; and state the follow-up timing. The script must satisfy the chapter's test that "a difficult response delivered on time is orders of magnitude better than a polished response delivered late."
2. **Committed-vs-deliberately-held decisions.** A written list, with each decision classified as:
   - **Committed and sized** — a specific action with a named exec owner, a deadline, and a visible-in-the-tracker status field.
   - **Committed and investigating** — a specific area of action with a named exec owner and a date by which the deliberately-ambiguous owner will convert this into either a committed-and-sized item or a decision-not-to-act (also publishable).
   - **Deliberately held** — an area the exec team is choosing not to act on this cycle, with the honest reason stated in the script (resource constraint, prior-commitment prioritisation, insufficient signal). The chapter is explicit that silence reads as disinterest; a stated "we are not acting on this because X" is not silence.
3. **Exec-ownership map.** A written table mapping each sub-dimension action to the single exec who owns it. Multiple exec co-owners is not a map; it is a diffusion of accountability. Address at minimum: manager-effectiveness composite, Berlin inclusion cut, women-in-engineering-IC inclusion cut, leadership-confidence / strategy-clarity composite, wellbeing composite, and the manager-cohort coaching workstream.
4. **Follow-up cadence.** How the executive team re-checks progress against committed actions — frequency, forum, who publishes the status change in the action tracker, and the specific pulse (per the Part A calendar) that reads progress on each commitment.

### Part E — Manager-cohort action-planning loop

The written mechanic for the manager-level cascade (per the chapter's week-5 cascade and week-8 team-action-conversation deadlines). The mechanic must:

1. **Low-performing manager cohort delivery.** A written plan for how the two managers whose scores are materially below the distribution (the Berlin engineering director and the Singapore customer-engineering manager) receive their results — one-on-one with HRBP present or not, timing relative to the broader cascade, what the manager hears vs. what the manager's manager hears, and the chapter's rule that manager-effectiveness scores are not published publicly.
2. **Action-planning template.** The scripted 45-minute team action-planning conversation agenda from chapter 04, adapted to Marlowe Grid — share the results, identify strengths, identify areas to improve, commit to 1–3 team-level actions, log in the tracker. Specify the Culture Amp (or alternative, per Part B) workflow surface the manager runs this through, and the tracker surface the commitments land on.
3. **Follow-up pulse timing.** Specify which pulse in the Part A calendar re-checks the manager-cohort actions, and how the re-check is sliced (team-level or cohort-aggregated).
4. **Escalation path to HRBP.** For managers whose scores remain materially low across *multiple* cycles despite coaching, the chapter is explicit that the pattern feeds into [mod-107 chapter 01](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/01-performance-management-operating-system.md). Specify the trigger — how many cycles, how large a gap, how the HRBP escalates, who signs off on moving a sustained-low-score manager into the performance-management loop and under what evidence standard. Reference, but do not re-author, the mod-107 mechanic.
5. **High-performing manager extraction.** The GTM managers whose scores sit materially above the company distribution are a direct learning opportunity. Specify the mechanic for extracting what they do differently (structured interview, observation, written artefact), the audience (the broader manager cohort), and the forum. Do not promote them into "manager coaches" without a defined scope — the chapter is implicit that under-defined coaching roles corrode the manager-development programme.
6. **HRBP coverage.** Marlowe Grid's HRBP coverage ratio and geographic coverage are inputs to this mechanic. Flag any coverage gap as `<!-- needs-research: HRBP coverage ratio norm for Series-C B2B SaaS at 340 headcount across three geographies -->` rather than inventing a number.

### Part F — Publication-posture memo

A written one-page memo to the CEO resolving the publish-vs-hold argument on the exec team. The memo must:

1. **Make a specific recommendation per cut.** For each of the following cuts, state whether it publishes org-wide, publishes manager-only, publishes HRBP-only, or does not publish:
   - Dimension-level composite scores (engagement, manager effectiveness, inclusion, wellbeing, leadership confidence, strategy clarity, eNPS).
   - Sub-dimension item-level scores inside each dimension.
   - Function-level cuts (Engineering, Product, Design, GTM, G&A).
   - Geography-level cuts (Austin, Berlin, Singapore, New York).
   - Demographic cuts (women-in-engineering-IC; the Berlin inclusion cut specifically).
   - Manager-effectiveness scores per manager.
2. **Demographic-slice confidentiality threshold.** State the minimum team-size or slice-size (n) at which a sub-cut is publishable at all. The chapter's rule is >=5 as a hard floor and >=8 as a safer rule for team-level anonymity; the demographic-slice floor is often set higher. State Marlowe Grid's threshold and the vendor-platform enforcement surface that enforces it (not the head of people's memory).
3. **Argue the exec-team disagreement explicitly.** The President and CFO's "half-told numbers are worse than no numbers" position vs. the VP Engineering, General Counsel, and CPO's "publication is the forcing function on executive follow-through" position. The memo should name both positions, say which Marlowe Grid is choosing, and give the operating reason. Papering over the disagreement with ambiguous language fails the test.
4. **Cross-reference HR-data privacy.** The engagement-survey dataset is HR data under [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) and, for Berlin respondents, under [mod-113](../../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/) (GDPR interaction on multi-country survey data). Name the specific handoff — the memo does not re-author the privacy mechanic but states which decisions in the publication posture are constrained by it (identifiability of a Berlin sub-cut given the pod size; works-council consultation if applicable; cross-border transfer of raw response data; retention of individually identifiable survey records).

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 04 is the primary reference. The four-week executive-response norm, the >=5 / >=8 anonymity threshold, the action-tracker publish discipline, and the "add questions, do not replace anchors" year-over-year rule are the substantive spine.
- The four surveys (quarterly pulse, annual deep-dive, onboarding pulse, exit / alumni) have different purposes and should not be collapsed in Part A's calendar. The chapter is explicit about not lumping onboarding into the quarterly cohort.
- Vendor selection in Part B is a *written recommendation*, not an RFP. Pricing, benchmark panels, and current enterprise terms are `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` per the resources index — the exercise is scored on how the memo reasons against the six selection criteria, not on invented market data.
- The anchor-set discipline in Part C is the single most-common place engagement programmes self-destruct. Resist the temptation to replace items; add. If the Edmondson psychological-safety construct is going in, it extends the instrument.
- Part D's all-hands script is the deliverable the exercise is really testing. A polished script that avoids the uncomfortable sub-dimensions fails. A difficult script delivered honestly on the four-week deadline passes.
- Marlowe Grid's two low-scoring managers and two high-scoring managers in Part E are concrete cases — do not sidestep them with generic "we will develop managers" language. Chapter 04 is explicit that sustained low scores feed into the mod-107 performance system; the exercise wants the specific escalation trigger.
- The publication-posture argument in Part F is where the real executive disagreement lives. The exercise expects a specific recommendation per cut, not a general "we will publish selectively" statement.
- Do not invent industry benchmark numbers, Culture Amp pricing, specific comparator-company engagement scores, or GDPR-specific thresholds. Where a market number appears and it is not in chapter 04 or [resources.md](../resources.md), flag it with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.
- No real peer-company names as comparators. Where the chapter uses "a peer company," so do you.
- The cross-references to [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/), [mod-107](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/), [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/), and [mod-113](../../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/) are hand-offs, not re-authors. State what is being handed off, not how the sibling module does its work.
