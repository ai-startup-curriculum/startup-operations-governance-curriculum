# Exercise 03 — DEI programme design post-SFFA

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — DEI programme design (post-SFFA)](../03-dei-programme-design-post-sffa.md)

## Problem statement

Rivermark Diagnostics is a Series-C precision-diagnostics company at 240 employees headquartered in Oakland, California, with a wet-lab and clinical-affairs team in Boston, Massachusetts, a data-and-engineering pod in Austin, Texas, and a small regulatory-affairs team in Chicago, Illinois. The company crossed the EEO-1 Component 1 100-employee threshold eleven months ago when the Boston acquisition closed and now files its first federal EEO-1 this cycle; it also crossed the California CRD 100-California-employee threshold, the Illinois EPRC 100-Illinois-employee threshold is not yet triggered (Chicago headcount is 24), and the Massachusetts wage-data reporting threshold under Chapter 141 of the Acts of 2024 is being tracked as the Boston team scales. The exec team is nine: founder-CEO (clinical background, PhD), founder-CSO, COO, CFO (external hire, Series-B), VP Engineering, VP Clinical Affairs, VP Commercial, newly-promoted **CHRO** (internal promotion from head of people, 10 weeks in role), and **general counsel** (external hire, 5 months in role, prior in-house experience at a healthcare company).

The legacy DEI portfolio includes a programme the CHRO's predecessor launched in 2022: the **Rivermark Access Fellowship** — a 9-month paid externship for underrepresented-minority (Black, Latine, and Indigenous) early-career candidates considering a transition into diagnostics / life-sciences data roles, funded out of the people-team budget at ~$180k/year, with eligibility explicitly limited to candidates self-identifying in those race categories. The programme was launched before SFFA was decided. It has produced three full-time hires and significant positive press. It has also, in the last six weeks, drawn two external pressures: (1) **a state-attorney-general inquiry letter** from a non-operating-state AG (part of a multi-state coordinated letter campaign to Series-B/C life-sciences employers) requesting documentation of the fellowship's eligibility criteria, selection methodology, and legal review, with a 45-day response window; and (2) an **investor DEI diligence request** from a prospective Series-C lead who has flagged post-SFFA DEI exposure as a diligence item and asked for the fellowship's current legal-review memo before term sheet. Internally, a Slack thread in the `#rivermark-everyone` channel surfaced three weeks ago when a mid-career engineer posted a Substack piece arguing the fellowship was legally exposed; the thread drew 180+ reactions and a mix of supportive, hostile, and anxious comments before the CHRO closed replies.

The CEO has asked the CHRO and general counsel to walk the board through a redesign at the next board meeting (6 weeks out), with the AG response and the investor-diligence response both resolved before then. The CHRO has asked you (as operator-in-residence to the people function) to author the full redesign package — the legal landscape, the programme rewrite, the EEO-1 and state-pay-data operating plan, the pay-equity audit cadence, the ERG framework, and the anti-DEI-backlash operating norms the CHRO will use in writing and in the room. The redesign has to survive section-1981 and Title VII scrutiny, keep moving the representation outcomes Rivermark cares about, satisfy the AG letter without conceding more than the law requires, and read defensibly to the incoming investor's diligence counsel.

## Requirements

### Part A — Legal landscape memo

A 2–3 page memo the general counsel will co-sign, written for the CHRO and the exec team (non-lawyer audience). It must translate the post-SFFA authorities into operating constraints for Rivermark specifically, not restate the cases in the abstract. Cover, at minimum:

1. **SFFA v. Harvard / UNC**, 600 U.S. 181 (2023) — direct holding (admissions, 14th Amendment / Title VI), the reasoning the plaintiff bar has carried into private-employer programmes, and the operating implication for Rivermark's race-restricted fellowship.
2. **42 U.S.C. § 1981** — the section-1981 exposure of a stipend-carrying externship with race-based eligibility; the longer statute of limitations and absence of a small-employer floor; the specific contracting-adjacency theory that reaches a fellowship stipend.
3. **Title VII**, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. — the Rivermark-is-newly-covered question (now that headcount is above 100 and the federal 15-employee Title VII floor was crossed years ago), the race-and-sex-and-national-origin surface Title VII covers, and the overlap with § 1981.
4. **Muldrow v. City of St. Louis**, 601 U.S. 346 (2024) — the lowered "some harm" bar for Title VII adverse-action claims and the implication for ERG-participation, sponsorship-cohort, and rotational-assignment design at Rivermark.
5. **American Alliance for Equal Rights v. Fearless Fund**, 103 F.4th 765 (11th Cir. 2024) — the directly on-point authority for a race-restricted stipend / grant programme and the preliminary-injunction posture Rivermark's fellowship would sit in if challenged today.
6. **Ames v. Ohio Department of Youth Services** — treat as live-uncertainty; flag the majority-plaintiff prima-facie question with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` for current citation, holding scope, and circuit-split resolution.
7. **2025 federal executive-order landscape on DEI and OFCCP** — Rivermark has no federal contracts today but is bidding on an NIH-adjacent federal grant that could change federal-contractor status within 12 months. Flag the EO-11246 status, the OFCCP AAP question, the federal-contractor-DEI-certification question, and the DOJ Civil Rights Division interpretive-guidance question with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` markers tied to the specific operating decision each unknown feeds.
8. **State-AG letter posture** — frame the AG inquiry as a litigation-adjacent communication; name the response discipline (counsel-led, factual, non-admitting, documented); flag any state-anti-DEI-statute reach with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` where private-employer application is unsettled.

The memo should close with a one-page operating-constraints table — four columns: *Programme element*, *Legal exposure*, *Redesign direction*, *Counsel sign-off required (Y/N)*.

### Part B — Programme-design rewrite

Author the rewritten **Rivermark Access Fellowship** programme design — a programme-spec document the CHRO and GC will approve and the recruiting-ops team will operate.

1. **Name the redesign principle** explicitly — pick one and justify it against the chapter: *behaviour-anchored eligibility* (e.g., "candidates without a four-year degree from a Carnegie R1 institution," "candidates who have not previously held a role at a VC-backed life-sciences company"), *universal access with high-opportunity targeting* (open to all, with outreach channels that reach the historically underrepresented pools), or a hybrid.
2. **Rewrite the eligibility criteria** so no criterion references race, ethnicity, or national origin. Each criterion must be: (a) demographic-blind on its face, (b) tied to a documented programme purpose (early-career transition into diagnostics / life-sciences data roles), and (c) auditable by a selection committee reading an application in isolation.
3. **Rewrite the recruiting / outreach channels** — the chapter is clear that outreach to historically-underrepresented pools (HBCUs, HSIs, women-in-STEM organisations, community-serving institutions, named named-type partner orgs) remains legally distinct from restricted eligibility. Specify the channels Rivermark will source through and the operating cadence.
4. **Rewrite the selection mechanic** — application rubric, scoring dimensions, selection committee composition, documentation discipline, demographic-blind-review posture. Reference the structured-interview principle from the chapter.
5. **Specify the stipend and benefits** — amount, tax treatment, benefits eligibility, conversion-to-FTE criteria, the on-ramp to a Rivermark offer. The fellowship cannot be structured as a race-gated contract; the stipend specification is where that risk concentrates.
6. **Specify the transition plan** — what Rivermark does with the three fellows currently in programme under the old eligibility rules; whether the current cohort completes under the legacy criteria or is transitioned; the honest external-communications line to the one offer-letter-signed-but-not-yet-started fellow.
7. **Specify the metric-and-reporting framework** — what representation outcome Rivermark will measure (pool composition, cohort composition, conversion rate, retention of fellowship-pathway hires), on what cadence, through what documentation, and under what privilege posture.

### Part D — Pay-equity audit cadence design

The CHRO is standing up Rivermark's first formal pay-equity audit this fiscal year. Author the audit-cadence design, cross-referencing [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) for the regression methodology and comp-cycle integration (do not re-author those mechanics):

1. **Frequency** — annual; timing anchored to feed Rivermark's compensation cycle (name the specific lag before the merit cycle; the chapter suggests 60–90 days).
2. **Methodology posture** — regression-adjusted on total cash (base + target bonus) and separately on equity; controls (level, function, sub-function, tenure, geographic cost-of-labour zone across Oakland, Boston, Austin, Chicago, and any remote-first-category employee); population slices (gender, race / ethnicity, age 40+ / under-40, veteran status, disability status where self-identified); defer the regression specification to [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) and name what is being deferred.
3. **Attorney-client-privilege posture** — engagement letter structure (counsel engages the compensation consultant, not people-team direct-engaging the consultant); work-product marking; access control on the audit output; separation between the privileged diagnostic artefact and the operational merit-cycle record. Reference the fact that the audit *as privileged diagnostic* is on solid legal footing per the chapter — the privilege architecture is load-bearing to that defensibility.
4. **Remediation authority** — unexplained-variance threshold (chapter suggests 3–5% adjusted gap); who authorises remediation (CHRO + general counsel + CFO sign-off); how remediation flows (standard merit-cycle adjustment per mod-106; not labelled as a demographic-based adjustment in the individual comp letter); governance brief to the compensation committee per [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).
5. **External disclosure posture** — what Rivermark will say publicly about the audit (that it conducts one annually and remediates; results stay privileged); what it will say to the Series-C lead investor's diligence team (same frame; counsel in the room if the diligence team asks for the underlying regression output); the specific line the CHRO will use at the board meeting.

### Part E — ERG framework

Rivermark has three informal affinity groups today (Women@Rivermark, Black@Rivermark, Pride@Rivermark), none chartered, each operating on an ad-hoc basis with no budget and no exec sponsor. Author the ERG-framework spec the CHRO will roll out:

1. **Charter template** — the full content of a Rivermark ERG charter: mission, membership criteria (**open-membership norm** called out explicitly — see point 2), activities, budget, executive sponsor, chair / vice-chair roles, chair term, chair recognition / compensation posture, reporting cadence, corporate-activity boundary (no external lobbying / political activity).
2. **Open-membership norm** — the one load-bearing legal-defensibility decision. Draft the exact charter language that opens membership to any employee aligned with the ERG's mission, without race, gender, sexual-orientation, or other protected-category gating. Reference the Title VII and § 1981 posture explicitly. Address the predictable concern — "but we need a safe space for Black employees to talk about being Black at Rivermark" — honestly, naming the operating mechanic (open membership plus optional sub-group conversations convened within the ERG by the chair; the chapter's framing is that allyship is welcome).
3. **Funding mechanic** — the budget-allocation model (per-ERG baseline, activity-based top-up, aggregate cap in the people-team budget), the budget-cycle integration with [mod-109](../../mod-109-finance-budgeting-and-financial-controls/), the receipt-and-expense discipline, the funding-equity principle across ERGs (how Rivermark avoids a "well-connected ERGs get more" failure mode).
4. **Executive-sponsor model** — the pairing mechanic (which VP sponsors which ERG), the sponsor's role (advocate, resource-securer, signal-sender; *not* the ERG's chair-in-waiting), the sponsor's time commitment, the chair / sponsor cadence.
5. **Legal posture summary** — a one-paragraph memo appended to each ERG charter that the general counsel signs, confirming the ERG's design is compliant with Title VII and § 1981 as applied in the post-SFFA landscape, naming the open-membership norm as the load-bearing element.
6. **Transition plan** — how Rivermark migrates the three informal groups to chartered ERGs over 60–90 days, how the open-membership norm is communicated to current members (who may have joined under an implicit affinity-only expectation), the CHRO's talking points for the first chartered-ERG town hall.

### Part F — Anti-DEI-backlash operating norms

Author the operating norms and three specific response scripts the CHRO will carry (and share with the CEO, GC, and head of comms) for the three live-pressure scenarios Rivermark is already in or likely to face. For each scenario, draft:

- The **first-24-hours operating sequence** — who is told, in what order, who takes point, what counsel is engaged, what external-comms posture is held pending the full response.
- The **CHRO's response script** — the actual words the CHRO uses in the room / on the written response, drafted at full specificity (not a template).
- The **documentation discipline** — what gets written down, where it lives, who has access.

The three scenarios:

1. **A hostile shareholder letter** — Rivermark receives a letter from a minority preferred shareholder (holding 2% on an as-converted basis) demanding the fellowship be retired immediately as a condition of their continued support in the Series-C and threatening to copy the letter to the Series-C lead and to the WSJ. The CHRO's response script must hold the company's position (programme redesigned on counsel review; not retired under pressure; outcome-focused commitment preserved) without conceding the legal argument the shareholder is making.
2. **A state-AG inquiry** — the AG inquiry Rivermark has already received. The response script is the written response to the AG, co-signed by the GC, delivered within the 45-day window. Draft the response's substantive position (fellowship has been redesigned on counsel review; demographic-blind eligibility now in place; underlying programme-purpose documentation available on reasonable request; Rivermark reserves applicable privileges; cooperates in good faith). Non-admitting; factual; narrow in scope of what is produced.
3. **A Slack-channel visibility complaint** — a mid-career engineer DMs the CHRO after the Substack thread, writing: "I'm Black. The fellowship is why I joined. If you're killing it, I'm leaving, and I'm not going to be quiet about why." The CHRO's response script is the 1:1 conversation (not an email — the chapter is explicit that honest in-person / live conversation is the right posture here). Draft the actual words. Preserve the employee's agency and voice; be honest about what changed and why; name the retention-and-sponsorship investments that are *not* being retired; do not promise what the company cannot deliver.

Close Part F with a short memo (half a page) on the retention-over-performance principle from the chapter — why Rivermark will *not* swing between over-commitment and abandonment, and the CHRO's answer at the all-hands when a loud question is asked "so is Rivermark giving up on DEI?"
