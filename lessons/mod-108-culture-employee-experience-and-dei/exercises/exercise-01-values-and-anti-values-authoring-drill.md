# Exercise 01 — Values and anti-values authoring drill

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — Values, behaviours & anti-values](../01-values-behaviours-and-anti-values.md)

## Problem statement

Halden Signal is a Series-B B2B SaaS company at 95 employees, headquartered in Toronto with a distributed engineering pod in Lisbon and a small go-to-market team in New York. The company sells an observability-and-alerting product into mid-market platform-engineering teams. The exec team is seven-strong: founder-CEO, founder-CTO, VP Engineering (external hire, 14 months in), VP Product (promoted from senior PM eight months ago), VP Sales (external hire, six months in), CFO (external hire, three months in), and a newly-hired CHRO who starts in three weeks. There is no head of people today — the outgoing head of people left after a difficult decision six months ago when the founders overrode her recommendation on a senior-leader values case (an SVP who was a repeat "silent-disagreement, back-channel Slack" offender was retained rather than exited; three ICs on that leader's team resigned within eight weeks and one filed a wrongful-constructive-dismissal complaint that is still open with counsel).

Two more facts matter:

- The company has an aspirational values list published on the careers page — "Curiosity, Integrity, Craft, One Team, Customer Love" — authored by the founders at 20 employees and never revisited. It is invoked in zero operating forums today. The last engagement survey scored "I understand what our company values mean in practice" at 2.4 / 5.
- The founder-CTO and the VP Engineering are in an unresolved disagreement about how directness should show up on engineering teams. The CTO believes "the room should feel safe first, and disagreement will follow"; the VP Engineering believes "the room should surface disagreement immediately, and safety follows from that being normal." Both have said, in exec off-sites, that they would leave over the wrong answer. The incoming CHRO has been briefed that this fault-line is the reason the values-authoring workshop is now on the calendar.

The incoming CHRO has asked you (as their principal advisor / operator-in-residence) to walk into her first month with the two-session authoring workshop, the cross-functional read group, and the final publish package fully designed. She wants the drill executed against the four tests in chapter 01, not against a generic off-site template, and she wants the anti-values framing sharp enough to survive the exec-team argument that will almost certainly happen in Session 2.

Author the values and anti-values package Halden Signal will publish and operate against.

## Requirements

### Part A — Value-selection framework memo

A one-page memo the CHRO will circulate to the exec drafting team before Session 1. It must:

1. Restate the four tests from chapter 01 — specific-and-non-generic, behaviourally anchored, trade-off explicit, disagreement-tolerant — in Halden Signal's language.
2. For each test, describe the *specific mechanic* the drafting team will run to apply the test in the room (e.g., the peer-plausibility inversion for Test 1; who writes the trade-off paragraph and when for Test 3).
3. Name the failure modes the memo is trying to head off — exec-off-site echo chamber, aspirational drift, softening under legal / PR review, consensus theatre — and the specific facilitator move for each.
4. State the decision rule the drafting team will use if a candidate value splits the room: pass, defer, or kill.

### Part B — Long-list with specificity check

The Session 1 output: a long-list of **12–18 candidate values**. For each candidate:

- The candidate value in one line.
- A one-line specificity check against Test 1 — name the plausible-opposite value a peer company could claim and still function. If you cannot name a plausible opposite, the candidate is a platitude and should be flagged for removal before Session 2.
- A one-line note on whether the candidate came from (a) how Halden Signal actually operates today, (b) how the exec team wishes it operated, or (c) direct inversion of a known offboarding pattern.

Do not prune to the final list in this part. The point of Part B is to expose the long-list to the specificity test *before* the emotional attachment to individual candidates sets in.

### Part C — Pruned final list of 5–7 values

The Session 2 output: the final **5–7 values**, each with the full package the chapter specifies:

1. The one-sentence value statement.
2. Two or three behavioural anchors — observable, scoreable, specific enough that an interviewer or a peer-recognition author could point to them.
3. The trade-off paragraph — what this value costs Halden Signal (hires it loses, decisions it slows, incidents it accepts, senior-leader friction it generates).
4. Optional but recommended: one or two "this is not" clarifications heading off near-neighbour misreadings.

At least one of the final values must address the CTO / VP-Engineering directness fault-line in a form both leaders can operate against (or in a form that names, honestly, which of the two positions the company is choosing).

### Part D — Final anti-values list

**3–5 anti-values** in the "we do NOT ..." framing. Each carries:

- The one-sentence anti-value statement, drafted at full sharpness (no softening).
- One behavioural example — the specific behaviour the anti-value is refusing, described concretely enough to be recognisable in a Tuesday meeting.
- One enforcement expectation — a description of the enforcement action the org should expect the *first* time the anti-value is violated by a senior leader, so that the anti-value is not decorative from the day it publishes.

At least one anti-value should be the direct inversion of a positive value from Part C. At least one should be sourced from the offboarding-retrospective technique in chapter 01 — including, honestly, the retained-SVP case from the problem statement.

### Part E — Integration memo

A one-page memo covering how each of the 5–7 values plugs into the operating stack. For each value, specify:

- **Hiring (mod-104).** Which values-fit-interview behavioural prompt the value produces; which function-specific interview (engineering, product, sales, G&A) carries an interleaved values-anchored question against this value.
- **Performance and promotion (mod-107).** How the value appears in the review-template values section; the specific behavioural anchor a calibrator would invoke; whether the value shows up on the promotion bar for manager promotions.
- **Recognition.** The peer-recognition tag; whether a spot-bonus citation could reasonably reference the value; whether the annual formal award is warranted for this value.
- **Offboarding.** Whether a values-violation termination path is realistic against this value, and — if so — the specific behavioural anchor documentation would rest on. Note where the mechanics defer to mod-103 (legal architecture) and mod-107 chapter 05 (termination playbook) rather than owning the mechanic yourself.

### Part F — Retrospective-cadence memo

A short memo (half a page) covering:

1. The **annual light-touch review** — when it runs (tied to which planning cycle), who attends, the three questions from chapter 01, and the default outcome.
2. The **on-major-inflection full review** trigger conditions Halden Signal will operate against — name them specifically (e.g., new CEO, transformational acquisition, IPO, product pivot; be explicit whether the incoming CHRO herself counts).
3. A short paragraph on why Halden Signal will *not* commit to rewriting values every two-to-three years, and what the CHRO will say at the publish all-hands if someone in the room asks "so are we going to redo these in two years?"

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 01 is the primary reference. The four tests (Decision 1), the two-layer authoring workshop, the anti-values framing (Decision 2), and the integration surfaces (Decision 3) map directly to Parts A through E.
- The two-session drafting-team structure and the cross-functional read group are non-negotiable — do not collapse them into a single off-site or a single working session. Part A's decision rule matters most in Session 2, when the pruning argument happens.
- For Part E, cross-reference the sibling modules the chapter defers to: [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) for hiring loop architecture, [mod-107 chapter 01](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/01-performance-management-operating-system.md) for the review-template mechanics, [mod-107 chapter 02](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/02-promotion-architecture.md) for promotion mechanics, and [mod-107 chapter 05](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/05-termination-playbook.md) plus [mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) for the values-violation termination path. Your job is to plug the values *into* these systems, not re-author the systems themselves.
- The CTO / VP-Engineering directness fault-line is a deliberate forcing function on Part C. Ducking it — by picking values so generic that both leaders can read what they want into them — is a fail. Chapter 01 is explicit that a value nobody argued with is a value nobody will operate against.
- The retained-SVP case in the problem statement is the exact scenario the offboarding-retrospective technique in Decision 2 is designed to produce anti-values from. Use it. Do not, however, name the SVP in the anti-value text — the anti-value describes behaviours, not individuals.
- Do not invent Halden Signal financials, engagement-survey benchmarks outside the one number given, industry churn rates, or ARR figures. If a market number appears in your memos and it did not come from chapter 01, flag it with `<!-- needs-research -->`.
- The "peer-plausibility" test in Part B is the sharpest anti-generic instrument in the chapter. Apply it ruthlessly — "we value curiosity" is the kind of candidate the founder-CEO will fight to keep and the exercise wants killed.
- Anti-values under legal / PR review will drift toward pablum. Part D asks you to hold the line pre-emptively; the enforcement-expectation column is the mechanism that keeps the sharpness auditable.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. Part A's memo restates the four tests, gives a specific facilitator mechanic per test, and states the pass / defer / kill decision rule the drafting team will use in Session 2.
2. Part B produces a long-list of 12–18 candidates, each with a named plausible-opposite value from a peer company and a labelled source (operates-today / aspirational / offboarding-inversion). Candidates that fail the peer-plausibility check are flagged, not silently retained.
3. Part C's final list contains 5–7 values, each with a one-sentence statement, 2–3 behavioural anchors, an honest trade-off paragraph, and (where drafted) "this is not" clarifications. Each of the 5–7 final values passes the peer-plausibility test — a peer company could plausibly claim the opposite value and still function.
4. At least one value in Part C engages the CTO / VP-Engineering directness fault-line explicitly, rather than papering over it with a generic "we value candour" formulation.
5. Part D lists 3–5 anti-values in "we do NOT ..." framing, each with a behavioural example and a first-violation enforcement expectation. At least one is a direct inversion of a Part C value; at least one is sourced from the offboarding-retrospective technique.
6. Part E maps every value from Part C onto all four operating surfaces (hiring, performance/promotion, recognition, offboarding) with a specific mechanic per surface. Where the mechanic is owned by a sibling module (mod-103, mod-104, mod-107), the memo names *what* is being handed off, not merely that a handoff exists.
7. Part F specifies the annual light-touch review cadence, names the inflection triggers Halden Signal will operate against, and includes the CHRO's publish-all-hands answer to "are we going to redo these in two years?"
8. Any market number cited (engagement benchmarks, recognition-programme cost points, ARR, headcount ratios beyond those given) is either taken from chapter 01 or flagged with `<!-- needs-research -->`.
9. Nothing is left as `[TBD]` or `[FILL IN]`. Where the drill requires a defensible choice, make the choice.
10. No real company names are invented as peer examples. Where the chapter uses "a peer company," so do you — the exercise is not a competitive-intelligence drill.
