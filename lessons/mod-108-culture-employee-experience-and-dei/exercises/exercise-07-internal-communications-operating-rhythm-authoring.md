# Exercise 07 — Internal-communications operating-rhythm authoring

> Estimated time: **~5 hours** · Related chapter: [07 — Internal-communications operating rhythm](../07-internal-communications-operating-rhythm.md)

## Problem statement

Cordage Robotics is a Delaware C-corp, a growth-stage warehouse-automation and robotics-middleware company at 540 employees, pre-IPO on a formally approved dual-track process (bank-led M&A exploration in parallel with an S-1 drafting kickoff targeted for the back half of next year). Headcount is split across three geographic poles: a US-east hub in Boston (sales, G&A, part of product), a US-west hub in the Bay Area (CEO, exec office, half of engineering, design), and a Lisbon engineering pod (platform, robotics-firmware, and a growing SRE / on-call rotation). Engineering is 55% of total headcount (roughly 300 of 540) and Lisbon alone is 95 engineers. The exec team is eight-strong today — founder-CEO, COO, CTO, VP Engineering, CRO, Chief Product Officer, General Counsel, and a CFO who the board has quietly agreed to transition out of the seat on a mutually-agreed timeline.

Three live internal-comms problems are stacked on top of each other and have forced the comms rhythm onto the exec-team agenda this cycle.

- **(a) The monthly all-hands has drifted into a 90-minute slide dump.** Twelve months ago it was a 55-minute meeting with 15 minutes of live Q&A; today it is seven exec functional updates back-to-back, averaging 92 minutes, with Q&A either cut for time or reduced to two softball questions the chief of staff has pre-selected. Measured attendance (live + 7-day recording views) has dropped from 88% of headcount to 54% in six months. The Lisbon pod — which joins at 18:00 local for a 10:00 Pacific start — has effectively stopped attending; last month seven Lisbon engineers watched the recording, three joined live, and the rest did neither.
- **(b) A C-suite departure is partly leaked already.** The CFO's transition out of the seat has not been formally announced. The exec team agreed on the plan 11 days ago. Within the last 72 hours a Blind thread has appeared ("Cordage CFO exit — anyone else hearing this?") with 40+ comments and two LinkedIn posts from executive-search recruiters have surfaced hinting at a Cordage CFO search. The comms team is now in a reactive posture — the "announcement" is going to land into a workforce that already half-knows.
- **(c) The company is in dual-track IPO readiness and the replacement must be announced under Reg-FD-adjacent discipline.** Cordage is private today, so Regulation FD (17 C.F.R. §§ 243.100–243.103) does not formally bind the company. The audit committee and outside counsel have nevertheless imposed Reg-FD-adjacent discipline on all exec-change comms as part of IPO readiness — selective disclosure to any external party (bank, auditors, prospective investors, the dual-track M&A counterparty, named enterprise customers whose contracts require executive-change notice) must be sequenced carefully, and the eventual public comms must assume the audience includes anyone who could later credibly claim to have received material information ahead of the broader market.

The chief of staff has asked you (as their principal advisor / operator-in-residence) to author the operating rhythm Cordage will run from this cycle forward, the specific CFO-departure comms playbook that lands this quarter, and the leak-management posture the comms team will adopt when the news has already partially moved. The deliverables must be sharp enough that the general counsel can sign off on the Reg-FD-adjacent sequencing, honest enough that the Lisbon pod reads the new cadence and concludes they have been designed into the rhythm rather than tolerated by it, and durable enough that the next exec-change (there will be one) runs through the playbook rather than through improvisation.

Author the internal-comms operating rhythm Cordage will publish and operate against.

## Requirements

### Part A — All-hands redesign

Author the redesigned all-hands format Cordage will operate from this cycle forward. The deliverable must specify:

1. **New cadence** — frequency (monthly, bi-weekly, or a hybrid of monthly corporate all-hands plus function-level all-hands on off-weeks per chapter 07's growth-stage pattern); the rationale for the chosen cadence against Cordage's 540-headcount / three-geo / pre-IPO stage.
2. **New length** — the target meeting length, with an explicit cut-list from the current 92-minute agenda. Which exec updates get cut, which get compressed, which get moved to the written channel.
3. **Standing agenda** — a shaped agenda in minutes-per-segment, covering at minimum: numbers (metrics against the plan), OKR / strategic-priority progress, people-change (joiners, promotions, departures), and live CEO Q&A. The agenda must leave at least 15 minutes for live Q&A.
4. **Explicit cuts** — a short memo on what the current 90-minute format is doing wrong (seven exec updates back-to-back, no deep-dive rotation, Q&A as the sacrificial segment) and the specific editorial discipline the chief of staff will enforce against each failure mode.
5. **Written-cascade pairing** — the written-summary artefact that pairs with every all-hands (length, publish deadline, owner, channel), with explicit treatment of what the summary carries that the live meeting does not.

### Part B — Announcement taxonomy applied

Below is a specific list of **seven upcoming announcements** Cordage will land across the next six weeks. Classify each as **informational / decision-required / feedback-invited**, route each to the correct channel (all-hands / email / Slack `#announcements` / Loom / 1:1 cascade / dedicated channel for comment), and specify the response expectation and the named author / owner for follow-up. Each announcement must also carry an explicit note on whether the Reg-FD-adjacent discipline applies and, if so, which parts of the sequencing are gated by that discipline.

1. **Customer win** — a seven-figure expansion with a named Fortune 500 logistics customer that the CRO wants to broadcast company-wide this Friday.
2. **Pricing change** — a 12% price-list increase on the mid-market product tier effective in 45 days, which GTM reps need to understand before customer conversations in the next two weeks.
3. **Policy change** — a new company-wide expense-reimbursement policy (tightening on travel class and per-diem) effective at the next pay-period boundary.
4. **Exec hire** — the new VP Product Marketing, external hire, start date in 30 days, replacing an open seat the company has carried for 11 months.
5. **Exec departure** — the CFO transition in Part C.
6. **Product launch** — a new SKU targeting mid-market distribution-centre customers, launching to GA in six weeks, with a controlled beta already live with four customers under NDA.
7. **Security incident** — a limited-scope credential-exposure incident in a non-production environment, contained within three hours, no customer data affected, but a Slack thread in `#engineering` has already surfaced the incident and the SRE on-call wants a formal workforce update.

Format the deliverable as a table (or a per-announcement memo block) with the following columns per announcement: **Class** (Info / Decision / Feedback), **Primary channel**, **Secondary channel(s)**, **Response expectation**, **Named author**, **Named follow-up owner**, **Reg-FD-adjacent?** (Y/N and the sequencing note), **Timing**.

### Part C — CFO-departure comms playbook

Author the full comms playbook for the CFO transition, sequenced in four lanes. The playbook must be concrete enough that the chief of staff can execute from it in the week it lands.

1. **Board sequencing.** Which board subset is already informed (audit committee, compensation committee); the full-board communication (written vs. live meeting); the timing relative to the external announcement. Cross-reference [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) for the board-communication substance.
2. **Exec sequencing.** Which members of the exec team are told, in what order, by whom, within what window. The specific inclusion of the General Counsel and the audit-committee chair as gate-keepers on the external-stakeholder lane.
3. **Employee sequencing.** The CFO's direct reports first (in person or synchronous video, named roster), the finance organisation second (same day), the broader workforce third (same-day written note from the CEO if the exec-team announcement is in-flight, or at the next all-hands if the window permits). Explicit treatment of the Lisbon pod given the time-zone offset — Lisbon finance staff do not learn from a US-morning Slack post twelve hours after their US peers.
4. **External-stakeholder sequencing.** The four external audiences and the sequencing norm: (a) IPO-track bank and auditors (contractually or practically entitled to advance notice of a CFO change); (b) dual-track M&A counterparty under existing NDA; (c) key enterprise customers whose master services agreements include executive-change notice provisions (name the contractual trigger even if hypothetical); (d) current and prospective institutional investors on the IPO-track distribution list. For each, specify the author, the medium, the timing relative to the internal announcement, and the explicit Reg-FD-adjacent constraint — that no external party in categories (a)–(d) receives material information meaningfully in advance of the broader workforce or in a form that would be improper if the company were already public.
5. **Confidential-communications norm.** The explicit norm the comms team will operate against — who is on the "informed list" for the next 72 hours before announcement; what each person on that list may and may not say to peers / direct reports / spouses; the "need to know" discipline that keeps the list to the smallest viable circle. Reference the handbook's confidential-comms section (per [chapter 02](../02-employee-handbook-nlrb-compliant-post-stericycle.md)) and the executive-offboarding substance in [mod-107 chapter 07](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/07-executive-team-offboarding.md).
6. **Reg-FD-adjacent discipline, in writing.** A one-paragraph note the General Counsel will sign off on, citing **17 C.F.R. §§ 243.100–243.103** by name, acknowledging that Cordage is not yet subject to Reg FD, and stating that the company has nonetheless chosen to sequence the CFO announcement under Reg-FD-adjacent discipline for IPO-readiness reasons. The paragraph must specify what "Reg-FD-adjacent" operationally means — no selective disclosure of material information to external parties ahead of the workforce-wide communication, broad-dissemination mechanics for the eventual external announcement, and a documented record of who was informed, when, and why.
7. **Equity and separation mechanics (deferred).** Note explicitly that the CFO's separation-equity treatment (acceleration, buyback, extended-exercise window) is owned by [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) and is handled through the compensation committee, not through the comms playbook. Hand off the substance; do not re-author it.

### Part D — Leak-management playbook

The Blind thread and the LinkedIn executive-search posts have already surfaced the CFO transition in partial form. Author the comms-team response playbook, including:

1. **Verification of the leak.** How the comms team confirms the signal is real — reading the thread for specific facts (not inferences), cross-checking against the exec-team informed-list, and the chief-of-staff / General-Counsel decision on whether the leak materially changes the sequencing timeline.
2. **The pull-forward-timeline decision mechanic.** The specific decision tree the exec team will run: (i) is the leak accurate on the material facts; (ii) is the leak likely to spread further within the next 24–48 hours; (iii) is the current sequencing (board → exec → employee → external) still executable within the pulled-forward window; (iv) if not, which sequencing steps are compressed and which are preserved. Include the explicit decision rule: the choreography is compressed in time but the *order* is preserved — the workforce still hears from the CEO before Blind becomes the authoritative source.
3. **The holding statement.** The 2–3 sentence holding statement any Cordage employee can give if asked about the leak before the formal announcement — honest, bounded, and not denying what is true. Draft the specific text. The statement must honour the "we do not comment on personnel matters until an announcement is made" discipline without lying.
4. **The post-announcement framing.** How the eventual CEO announcement handles the fact that part of the workforce has already seen the Blind thread. The announcement should not pretend the leak did not happen; it should acknowledge the sequencing, name honestly that the company intended to communicate in a specific order, and signal that the comms-team learning from this cycle will be documented in the after-action review.
5. **After-action review trigger.** The named owner (chief of staff), the window (within two weeks of the announcement), and the three questions the review will answer: how the leak originated (to the extent determinable without a witch-hunt), what the sequencing decision was and whether it held under pressure, what the comms team will do differently next cycle. The output feeds the crisis-comms playbook per chapter 07.

### Part E — Cross-time-zone fix for the Lisbon pod

The current 10:00 Pacific all-hands lands at 18:00 Lisbon and the Lisbon pod has effectively opted out. Author the cross-time-zone fix as a decision memo, comparing at least **four options** against each other and recommending one. The four options to compare at minimum:

1. **Rotate the time** across cycles — some all-hands at a Lisbon-friendly hour (e.g., 09:00 Pacific / 17:00 Lisbon or an earlier Pacific start), some at the current time, so the burden is shared across geographies rather than concentrated on Lisbon.
2. **Record plus async Q&A** — keep the live meeting at 10:00 Pacific but add a structured asynchronous Q&A window (Slido or equivalent) that opens 24 hours after the meeting and closes 72 hours after, with CEO written responses within one week.
3. **Two live sessions** — run two sequential live all-hands on the same day: one at a Lisbon-and-US-east-friendly morning hour, one at the current US-west hour. Both are live, both have CEO Q&A, both are recorded.
4. **Async-first with live Q&A** — publish the full all-hands content (metrics, OKR update, people moments) as a written and recorded package 24 hours in advance of a short synchronous Q&A-only session, with the Q&A session scheduled at a globally-friendly time.

For each option, name the pros, the cons, the operating cost (CEO time, chief-of-staff production time, workforce time), and the honesty posture (who is still being under-served and how it is acknowledged). Make the recommendation and write the tradeoff paragraph in full — the paragraph that names, out loud, what the chosen option gives up and why it is still the right answer.

Then: specify how the chosen cadence will be announced to the Lisbon pod — not as a procedural tweak but as a visible acknowledgement that the previous cadence had excluded them. The language belongs in the next all-hands and in a written note from the CEO to the Lisbon pod specifically.

### Part F — CEO-Q&A pattern

Author the CEO-Q&A / AMA pattern Cordage will operate at every all-hands. The deliverable must cover:

1. **Written-questions-in-advance vs. live-mic.** The decision between the two patterns from chapter 07 — live-answer-with-upvoting vs. written-response-with-live-follow-up — with the rationale for Cordage's 540-headcount / three-geo stage. Pick one and commit; chapter 07 is explicit that switching between patterns reads as evasion.
2. **The upvote mechanism.** The tool (Slido, Pigeonhole, a Slack workflow, or an equivalent); the submission window (open 48 hours before, close two hours before, per chapter 07); the anonymity architecture (CEO sees the text, not the submitter); the moderator role and the moderator's power to combine duplicate questions.
3. **The "we will come back on this" discipline.** The specific CEO language for the three categories of question that cannot be answered in the moment — the "I don't know yet, I will publish by [date]" category, the "we are deciding on a timeline, I will tell you when the decision lands" category, and the confidential-terrain category ("there is a category of thing we do not discuss publicly until [event], and I cannot say more today" per chapter 07). Draft the language in each category.
4. **The follow-up written answer timing.** The chief-of-staff-owned tracker for every submitted-but-not-answered-live question; the one-week commitment on the written response; the ageing-escalation path when a question slips past one week.
5. **Hostile-question handling.** How the CEO will handle the hostile-question category — restate charitably, acknowledge what is true, defer with a specific commitment per chapter 07. Draft one worked example against a plausible Cordage hostile question (e.g., "why did the exec team sit on the CFO transition for 11 days while Blind was lighting up?").
6. **The non-skippable cadence.** The explicit discipline that the AMA runs at every all-hands and is never cut for time; the specific escalation when an exec functional-update segment runs long and threatens the AMA window.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 07 is the substantive anchor. The seven operating decisions (all-hands cadence, executive-comms portfolio, AMA pattern, announcement taxonomy, confidential-comms norm, cross-time-zone norm, crisis playbook) map directly onto Parts A–F; do not re-author the frame, apply it to Cordage.
- The stage-and-cadence table in Decision 1 places a 540-person pre-IPO company at the **growth / late stage** — monthly corporate all-hands plus function-level all-hands on off-weeks is the chapter's default. Part A should either adopt that pattern or justify the deviation on the specifics of Cordage's three-geo split.
- The CFO transition is an **executive change** under Decision 5. The chapter's choreography — direct reports first, affected function same day, broader company at the next all-hands or via same-day CEO note — is the backbone of Part C. The dual-track / Reg-FD-adjacent layer is an addition specific to this fact pattern.
- Reg FD (**17 C.F.R. §§ 243.100–243.103**) does not formally bind a private company. Cordage is choosing to operate under Reg-FD-adjacent discipline *as a matter of policy* because the IPO-track S-1 drafting is in motion and the audit committee has asked for it. Part C's one-paragraph discipline statement is the artefact that documents this choice; it is the General Counsel's sign-off instrument.
- The exec-offboarding substance — package negotiation, confidentiality carve-outs post-*McLaren Macomb*, retention-grant handling for direct reports, the transition-period scope, the departure-rationale message — is owned by [mod-107 chapter 07](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/07-executive-team-offboarding.md). Part C should cross-reference it; it should not re-author the offboarding mechanics. The CFO separation-equity treatment (acceleration, buyback, extended-exercise window) is owned by [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) and handed off through the compensation committee.
- The operating cadence this chapter wires into — WBR, QBR, planning cycle, board pack — is owned by [mod-114](../../mod-114-operations-function-design/) and the board-facing side is owned by [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/). The internal-comms rhythm authored here is choreographed with those cadences, not merged with them. Part A's written-cascade pairing and Part C's board sequencing should name the handoff explicitly.
- For the leak-management playbook (Part D): the chapter's "Slack-leak that gets ahead of the announcement" failure mode is the exact pattern Cordage is in. The remediation is pull-forward-with-order-preserved, not panic. The holding statement is the piece of operating infrastructure the comms team should have authored before the leak happened; Cordage is authoring it now under pressure, and that is a lesson for the after-action review.
- Reference **Horowitz, B. (2014).** *The Hard Thing About Hard Things.* HarperBusiness. The chapters on CEO communications, the "wartime CEO," and telling the truth to the company at scale are directly relevant to Part C (the CFO announcement) and Part F (the hostile-question handling). The Horowitz framing — tell people exactly what is going on, including the hard parts — is the posture the Cordage CEO should adopt in both.
- Reference **Grove, A. (1983 / rev. 1995).** *High Output Management.* Vintage. The staff-meeting cadence, one-on-one cadence, and "task-relevant maturity" framing from Grove sit under the executive-comms portfolio in Decision 2 and inform the discipline Part A applies to the all-hands redesign — the all-hands is one instrument in a portfolio, not the only one, and the portfolio has to be a portfolio.
- Reference **Lencioni, P. (2012).** *The Advantage.* Jossey-Bass. The four-meeting cadence (daily check-in, weekly tactical, monthly strategic, quarterly off-site) is the lineage the chapter's executive-comms portfolio is drawing on; Part A should not accidentally collapse the meeting layers into a single "all-hands" that is doing three jobs badly.
- Reference the **Amazon six-pager pattern** (Bezos annual shareholder letters, particularly 2004 and 2017, on the use of six-page narrative memos in lieu of PowerPoint). The six-pager is a useful discipline for the written-cascade artefact in Part A and for the board-communication artefact that Part C hands off to mod-111 — a dense, considered memo is a different instrument from a deck of exec slides and should be used where the content warrants it.
- Do not invent Cordage financial numbers, specific churn benchmarks, Blind-thread engagement benchmarks, or specific industry comp data. If a market number appears in a memo and it did not come from chapter 07 or resources.md, flag it with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.
- Do not name real company peers. Where the exercise or chapter uses a generic "peer company" or "named Fortune 500 logistics customer," so do you.
- The exercise is not asking for the CFO's separation terms, the dollar amount of the retention pool for the finance team, or the specific identity of the dual-track M&A counterparty. Those are deliberately bracketed as handoffs or as "named-but-not-detailed" artefacts.
