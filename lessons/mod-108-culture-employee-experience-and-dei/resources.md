## NLRB Section 7, *Stericycle*, and workplace-rule doctrine

- **National Labor Relations Act (NLRA)** — 29 U.S.C. §§ 151–169.
  - **§ 157 (Section 7)** — right of employees to self-organisation, to form, join, or assist labor organisations, and to engage in "other concerted activities for the purpose of collective bargaining or other mutual aid or protection." Protects non-union employees discussing wages, hours, and terms and conditions of employment on internal channels and externally.
  - **§ 158 (Section 8)** — unfair labor practices. Section 8(a)(1) makes it a ULP for an employer to interfere with, restrain, or coerce employees in the exercise of Section 7 rights, and is the vehicle by which overbroad handbook rules and severance-agreement clauses are challenged.
- ***Stericycle, Inc. and Teamsters Local 628***, 372 NLRB No. 113 (August 2, 2023). Adopted a modified *Lutheran Heritage* framework: a workplace rule is presumptively unlawful if a reasonable employee could interpret it to restrict Section 7 activity, and the employer must prove the rule advances a legitimate and substantial business interest that cannot be advanced with a more narrowly tailored rule. Overruled ***Boeing Co.***, 365 NLRB No. 154 (2017) and its category framework, and abrogated ***LA Specialty Produce Co.***, 368 NLRB No. 93 (2019).
- ***McLaren Macomb***, 372 NLRB No. 58 (February 21, 2023). Severance agreements offered to non-supervisory employees containing overly broad confidentiality and non-disparagement clauses violate Section 8(a)(1). Practical consequence for the handbook and separation-agreement template: narrowly tailor confidentiality and non-disparagement language and include an explicit Section 7 carve-out. Cross-referenced in [mod-107 chapter 04](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) and its resources index.
- **NLRB General Counsel Memorandum GC 23-05** (March 22, 2023). GC guidance on the scope and application of *McLaren Macomb*. https://www.nlrb.gov/news-outreach/news-story/nlrb-general-counsel-issues-memo-on-mclaren-macomb <!-- needs-research: track subsequent NLRB General Counsel memoranda superseding, amending, or rescinding GC 23-05 after the 2024–2025 change in Board and GC leadership. -->

## Federal EEO and anti-discrimination statutes across the handbook and DEI programme

- **Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964** — 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. Foundational statute prohibiting employment discrimination on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, or national origin; applied to sponsorship, mentorship, ERG, and DEI-programme design after *SFFA*.
- **Section 1981 of the Civil Rights Act of 1866** — 42 U.S.C. § 1981. Race-based discrimination in the making and enforcement of contracts, including employment contracts and (as litigated in *Fearless Fund*) grant and investment contracts. Longer statute of limitations than Title VII and no small-employer floor.
- **Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA)** — 29 U.S.C. §§ 621–634.
- **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)** — 42 U.S.C. § 12101 et seq., and implementing regulations at **29 C.F.R. Part 1630** (reasonable-accommodation obligations relevant to remote-work and RTO policy in chapter 05).
- **Pregnancy Discrimination Act (PDA), Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA, 2022), and PUMP Act (2022)**.
- **Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA)** — 42 U.S.C. § 2000ff et seq.
- Deeper coverage of the EEOC-protected-category framework across the employment lifecycle sits in [mod-103 chapter 07](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/07-eeoc-protected-category-framework.md).

## DEI legal landscape (post-*SFFA*)

- ***Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard* / *SFFA v. UNC***, 600 U.S. 181 (2023). Held that Harvard's and UNC's race-conscious admissions programmes violate the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment (UNC) and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act (Harvard). The employment-law bar has treated the decision as reshaping the risk landscape for employment DEI programmes even though the case arose in higher-education admissions; the direct employment-law standard remains Title VII, § 1981, and Executive Order 11246 (to the extent operative).
- ***Muldrow v. City of St. Louis***, 601 U.S. 346 (2024). Rejected the heightened "materially adverse" standard for Title VII discriminatory-transfer claims; an employee challenging a transfer under Title VII need only show "some harm" to a term or condition of employment, not significant harm. Directly relevant to ERG-participation, sponsorship-programme, and rotational-assignment design.
- ***American Alliance for Equal Rights v. Fearless Fund Management, LLC***, 103 F.4th 765 (11th Cir. 2024). Held that a grant contest limited to Black women-owned businesses is likely a § 1981 violation and enjoined the programme; case subsequently settled. Cited for the § 1981 exposure of race-restricted employment-adjacent programmes (fellowships, scholarships, supplier-diversity, and internally sponsored external programmes).
