# 9. Ownership boundary map

> International expansion sits across every operating function. The ownership map decides, for any international question that walks in, whether it belongs to mod-113, to a sibling module, or to a specialist advisor the group retains.

## Motivation

The incoming operator's inbox after a few quarters of international expansion looks nothing like the pre-expansion inbox. A Berlin researcher's § 19a EStG election question, a Toronto engineer's H-1B transfer, a Lisbon contractor's IR35-equivalent re-characterisation, a Singapore nominee-director replacement, a UK VAT-threshold crossing, a French CIR claim deadline, a Netherlands wage-tax audit, a Chinese cross-border-transfer filing, a Canadian Law 25 PIA request — all arrive on different cadences, through different channels, with different ultimate owners. Without a written boundary map, three predictable failure modes emerge:

1. **Over-reach.** mod-113 absorbs US-side comp architecture questions, US-side offboarding mechanics, FCPA training delivery, and detailed transfer-pricing structuring that belong to sibling modules or specialist advisors.
2. **Under-reach.** International work drifts to whichever US-side owner has visibility — a Berlin HRIS configuration ends up at the US HR-ops team without the mod-113 overlay; a UK sales commission plan skips the UK-employment-law review; an India vendor contract bypasses the local-counsel review.
3. **Advisor-dependency drift.** Specialist advisors (transfer pricing counsel, immigration counsel, global-mobility practice) are given decisions to make rather than inputs to a decision the operator leads. Advisor bills grow without a corresponding growth in operator competence.

This chapter is the written map. It states what mod-113 owns end-to-end, what mod-113 hands back to each sibling module (where the US-side substrate already lives), what mod-113 defers to specialist external advisors (because the depth exceeds what any operator should attempt internally), and the pattern that resolves disputes.

## What mod-113 owns

The eight substantive chapters of mod-113 own the following, end-to-end:

- **EOR / PEO / direct-entity decision framework** — [chapter 01](./01-eor-vs-peo-vs-direct-entity-decision-framework.md). The decision that governs every new-country hire: three doors, four framework questions, country-specific regulatory tilts (Germany AÜG, France portage, UK IR35, Canada provincial variance), and the exit-cost analysis.
- **First international entity setup** — [chapter 02](./02-first-international-entity-setup.md). Country-selection against the four drivers (talent, GTM, R&D credit, IP holding), the entity-form choice per country (UK Ltd, Ireland DAC, Canada CBCA + provincial, Australia Pty Ltd, Singapore Pte Ltd, Germany GmbH, Netherlands BV, France SAS, India Pvt Ltd, Japan KK / GK, Israel Ltd), the local-director requirements, local bank-account and payroll setup, and the 90–180 day formation timeline.
- **Intercompany and transfer-pricing structure** — [chapter 03](./03-intercompany-and-transfer-pricing-structure.md). The startup-default cost-plus services agreement (ICSA), the IP ownership / licensing chain, the arm's-length framing at the operator level, the BEPS 2.0 Pillar 2 awareness, and the specialist-advisor interface.
- **US immigration programme** — [chapter 04](./04-us-immigration-programme.md). The H-1B lottery cycle, O-1 extraordinary-ability track, L-1 intra-company transfer (L-1A / L-1B), TN under USMCA, E-3 for Australian nationals, F-1 OPT / STEM OPT bridges, and the EB green-card trajectory (EB-1 / EB-2 / EB-2 NIW / EB-3 / PERM).
- **International employment-law variance** — [chapter 05](./05-international-employment-law-variance.md). Notice periods, termination protection, works-council / employee-representative regimes, statutory paid-leave floors, and the per-country operator view across UK, Germany, France, Canada, Australia (and a lighter review of Ireland, Netherlands, Spain, Poland, Israel, India, Japan, Singapore).
- **International benefits and equity compensation** — [chapter 06](./06-international-benefits-and-equity-comp.md). Country-appropriate health / pension / retirement, country-specific equity regimes (UK EMI / CSOP, French BSPCE, German § 19a EStG, Israeli § 102, Australian ESS, Indian ESOP deferral), the global equity-plan administration overlay, and the FX / tax-residency / social-security-totalisation layer.
- **International privacy and compliance overlay** — [chapter 07](./07-international-privacy-and-compliance-overlay.md). GDPR Chapter V cross-border transfers, country-specific privacy regimes (UK GDPR, revFADP, PIPEDA / Law 25, LGPD, PIPL, PDPA, APPI, PIPA, DPDP), FCPA-equivalents (UK Bribery Act, CFPOA, Sapin II), non-US export-control regimes (EU 2021/821, UK ECJU, FEFTA, DEC), and the deemed-export analysis for foreign-national hires.
- **International workforce-reduction playbook** — [chapter 08](./08-international-workforce-reduction-playbook.md). Country-specific collective-consultation regimes (UK TULRCA s. 188, Germany KSchG § 17 + BetrVG § 102, France PSE + DREETS homologation, Netherlands WMCO, Canada provincial, Australia Fair Work Act s. 530), immigration-status implications per visa category, EOR-unwind mechanics, and cross-module coordination with the US-side layoff playbook.

If a question maps to one of the above, mod-113 owns it. If it maps to one of the below, mod-113 hands off or defers.

## What mod-113 hands back to sibling modules

The pattern: **mod-113 owns the international overlay; the sibling module owns the US-side substrate and the generic (non-country-specific) layer**. A question that is "how does X look in Germany" is mod-113; a question that is "what is the baseline X" is a sibling module.

### mod-101 — Legal entity formation and corporate structure

- The **Delaware C-corp** architecture, charter, bylaws, officer-authority framework, and corporate record for the US parent.
- The **subsidiary approval** mechanics — board resolutions authorising formation of international subsidiaries, officer authority to execute the inbound share-subscription and intercompany-services agreements, and the group-structure diagram maintained in the corporate record.
- The **wholly-owned subsidiary governance mechanics** at the US-parent level — annual sole-shareholder resolutions, group-management-committee minutes, intercompany-charter alignment.

mod-113 drafts the local entity's constitutional documents under local law; mod-101 authorises the formation from the US-parent side. See [mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/).

### mod-102 — Founding team legal architecture

- Founder-side pre-formation IP assignment, 83(b) elections, founder-stockholder agreements, and the pre-expansion founder-legal substrate.
- Founder-level international mobility — a founder who relocates abroad is a corporate-governance and personal-tax event that mod-102 and specialist tax counsel handle; mod-113 does not re-author the founder-side pack for international relocation.

See [mod-102](../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/).

### mod-103 — Employment law and contract design

- The **US employment-contract substrate** — at-will language, arbitration, PIIA, DTSA whistleblower notice, non-compete / non-solicit state variance, offer-letter mechanics.
- The **state-law variance** analysis within the US (California, New York, Illinois, Washington, Colorado, Texas).

mod-113 owns the international employment-contract overlay — the German indefinite-term contract with ArbnErfG acknowledgement, the UK statement-of-particulars + IR35 posture, the French CDI with CDD-exception analysis, the Canadian provincial employment-standards variance, the Australian modern-award interaction. The *US contract substrate* belongs in mod-103 and is not re-authored in mod-113. See [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/).

### mod-104 — Hiring, onboarding, and HR operations

- The **US-side HRIS / PEO stack** that administers US employment contracts.
- The **US onboarding workflow** — I-9 / E-Verify, equipment provisioning, benefits enrolment, orientation, 30/60/90-day check-ins.
- The **global HRIS architecture** at the operations level — Rippling, BambooHR, Workday, HiBob — insofar as the HRIS configuration is a cross-cutting HR operations concern.

mod-113 owns the international-overlay HR questions — the EOR integration into the HRIS, the country-specific onboarding addenda (local employment contracts in local language, country-specific benefits enrolment, country-specific immigration documentation), and the local-payroll-provider engagement. The US-side onboarding mechanics are in mod-104. See [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/).

### mod-105 — Equity compensation policy and comp committee

- The **US equity-incentive plan** itself, 409A valuations, ISO/NSO/RSU grant templates, the comp-committee cadence.
- **Repricing / exchange** programmes, Rule 701 compliance, and the US-side equity-administration platform (Carta / Pulley / Shareworks).

mod-113 owns the country-specific overlays on the US plan — UK CSOP sub-plans, French BSPCE where applicable, Israeli § 102 trustee arrangement, German § 19a EStG election mechanics, Australian ESS startup-concession architecture. The underlying US plan and the comp-committee governance belong in mod-105. See [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/).

### mod-106 — Compensation architecture and total rewards

- The **US compensation architecture** — job architecture / levelling, broad-based pay bands, geographic differentials across US metros, equity refresh cadence, performance-management-linked comp.
- The **US total-rewards** package — medical / dental / vision, 401(k), FSA / HSA, commuter, and other US-specific benefits.

mod-113 owns the international-overlay comp-architecture questions — how a UK Head of ML's package compares to the US Head of ML's, whether the Berlin researcher's offer sits above or below the UK researcher's cash plus equity, FX treatment on the equity grant, and country-specific statutory-benefit loadings. The US-side comp architecture belongs in mod-106. See [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/).

### mod-107 — Performance, promotion, and offboarding

- The **US-side layoff and offboarding** playbook — WARN Act, OWBPA release language, 21/45-day age-band disclosure for 40+ employees, severance-template structure, exit-interview mechanics, return-of-property workflow.
- The **US performance-management** cycle (PIP, ratings calibration, promotion packets).

mod-113 owns the international reductions playbook ([chapter 08](./08-international-workforce-reduction-playbook.md)) — the TULRCA s. 188 consultation, the Betriebsrat consultation and Sozialplan, the French PSE, the Canadian provincial collective-dismissal, the EOR unwind. The US-side mechanics belong in mod-107. The two playbooks are choreographed when a group-wide reduction crosses jurisdictions. See [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/).

### mod-108 — Culture, employee experience, and DEI

- The **US employee handbook**, internal AI-usage policy, acceptable-use policy, remote-work / RTO policy at the US-side baseline.
- **Engagement surveys**, internal-communications cadence, DEI programme design.

mod-113 owns the country-specific handbook addenda — the German handbook addendum reflecting Betriebsrat co-determination and BDSG § 26 employee-data processing rules, the French handbook addendum reflecting CSE consultation, the UK handbook addendum reflecting statement-of-particulars obligations and Equality Act 2010 overlays. The baseline US handbook and internal policies belong in mod-108. See [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/).

### mod-109 — Commercial contracts, IP, and legal ops

- The **customer and vendor contract suite** at the US-parent-sold / US-parent-bought baseline — MSA / Order Form / SLA / DPA / Security Addendum / AUP; vendor MSA / SOW / contractor / MNDA.
- The **IP protection strategy**, OSS hygiene / SBOM programme, AI-vendor / AI-DPA pattern, CLM stack.

The baseline commercial-contract architecture belongs in mod-109. See [mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/).

### mod-110 — Privacy, data governance, and sector compliance

- The **data-classification scheme**, data-inventory / RoPA, DPIA methodology at the group baseline.
- **DSAR / subject-access-request** operational programme.
- The **substantive GDPR analysis** — Article 28 processor obligations, Article 32 security of processing, Article 33 breach notification, Article 34–39 DPO obligations, Article 44–49 cross-border-transfer substance.

mod-113 owns the international overlay on the privacy programme — the operational configuration of the GDPR Chapter V transfer mechanism (DPF self-certification, SCCs executed, TIA documented), the country-specific regime amplification (UK GDPR, revFADP, PIPEDA / Law 25, LGPD, PIPL, PDPA, APPI, PIPA, DPDP), the data-residency overlay for localisation jurisdictions. The substantive privacy programme belongs in mod-110. mod-113's [chapter 07](./07-international-privacy-and-compliance-overlay.md) is explicit about this boundary. ### mod-111 — Corporate governance, board operations, and officer duties

mod-113 does not own operations-function design. ## What mod-113 defers to specialist advisors

Some international questions have depth that no operator should attempt without specialist external advice. mod-113 trains the operator to **direct the relationship** with these advisors — scope the engagement, read the output, challenge the recommendation — but does not substitute for them.

### Big-Four and specialist transfer-pricing firms

- **Deloitte, EY, KPMG, PwC** — Global transfer-pricing practices.
- **Baker McKenzie, Fenwick & West, DLA Piper, Latham & Watkins, Skadden** — Specialist international-tax and transfer-pricing legal practices.
- **Transfer-pricing boutiques** — Economic-analysis-driven transfer-pricing specialists (variable by region).

**What they own.** The detailed transfer-pricing documentation package — Local File, Master File, Country-by-Country Report (OECD BEPS Action 13); the comparable-company analysis supporting the chosen cost-plus markup; the Advance Pricing Agreement (APA) negotiation when the group reaches APA-appropriate scale; the IP migration restructuring (cost-sharing arrangements under Treas. Reg. § 1.482-7, platform-contribution-transaction analyses); the BEPS 2.0 Pillar 2 structural analysis as the group approaches the €750M threshold.

**What mod-113 teaches.** The operator's conversational competence to engage the advisor — know what cost-plus-services means, know what the OECD TPG arm's-length standard is, know what the ICSA should contain, know when the structure needs refresh. [Chapter 03](./03-intercompany-and-transfer-pricing-structure.md) is explicit about this.

### Country-specific employment and corporate counsel

Local counsel in each jurisdiction — not the Big-Four, but local employment-and-corporate specialists (e.g., UK: Taylor Wessing, Lewis Silkin, Osborne Clarke; Germany: Hengeler Mueller, Taylor Wessing DE, CMS Hasche Sigle; France: August Debouzy, Fidal; Netherlands: NautaDutilh, Van Doorne; Canada: Stikeman Elliott, Blakes, Osler; Australia: Clayton Utz, King & Wood Mallesons, Allens; Singapore: Allen & Gledhill, Rajah & Tann; India: Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas, Khaitan & Co, AZB & Partners; Israel: Herzog Fox & Neeman, Meitar).

**What they own.** The local employment-contract template, the local corporate-governance filings, the local statutory-audit interface, the local tax-authority correspondence, the local works-council / employee-representative-body consultation, the local individual-dismissal and collective-dismissal procedure, local notarial deeds, and local litigation defence.

**What mod-113 teaches.** Who to retain, how to brief them, what deliverables to expect, and how to integrate their output into the group programme.

### Immigration counsel

**Specialist immigration firms** — e.g., Fragomen, Berry Appleman & Leiden, Jackson Hertogs, Mintz Levin's immigration group, Reichert Immigration Law, boutique practices across the US and major receiving countries.

**What they own.** Every visa petition filing (H-1B registration and petition prep, O-1 package preparation and advisory opinion coordination, L-1 individual and blanket petition prep, PERM labour certification, I-140 and I-485 adjustment, EB-1 extraordinary petitions, EB-2 NIW petitions, international consular processing); audit response; litigation under RFEs and denial reviews.

**What mod-113 teaches.** The visa map, the operator-level decisions at hire time (which visa fits this candidate), the H-1B lottery calendar, the sponsorship-cost budgeting, and the post-termination immigration-status mechanics. [Chapter 04](./04-us-immigration-programme.md) is the operator's manual; immigration counsel runs the filings.

### Global-mobility advisors

**Specialist global-mobility firms and the Big-Four global-mobility services practices** — Deloitte GES, KPMG GMS, PwC GES, EY People Advisory; specialist firms (e.g., Mazars, Vialto Partners, BDO Expatriate Services).

**What they own.** Cross-border assignment structuring (short-term assignments, long-term assignments, tax-equalisation and tax-protection policies, assignment letters), host-country tax-return preparation for assignees, totalisation-certificate filings (SSA Form USA/DE etc.), tax-residency analysis for mobile employees, equity-grant allocation across jurisdictions when the employee has moved.

**What mod-113 teaches.** The frame — grant-currency / pay-currency conventions, tax-residency rules, totalisation agreements, host-vs-home-country analysis. [Chapter 06](./06-international-benefits-and-equity-comp.md) sets the frame; the global-mobility advisor executes the structuring.

### Country-specific equity-plan trustees and administrators

**Israeli § 102 trustees** (Altshuler Shaham, IBI Trust, ESOP Experts); **UK EMI / CSOP administrators**; global equity-administration platforms (Carta Global, Shareworks, Global Shares, Pulley international).

**What they own.** The trustee custody of options under § 102, the HMRC filings for EMI / CSOP grants, the country-addendum structuring under the US equity plan, the global withholding integration with local payrolls.

**What mod-113 teaches.** When the trustee or administrator is required, what the operator must do before the first grant (Israeli pre-grant plan filing, UK EMI notification), and the structural interaction with the US plan.

### Specialist privacy counsel and DPO services

**Specialist privacy firms** — Hunton Andrews Kurth, Wilson Sonsini Privacy & Data Security, Covington & Burling, DLA Piper privacy practice; EU-based firms (e.g., Fieldfisher, Pinsent Masons, Bird & Bird), fractional-DPO services (OneTrust Advisory, Fieldfisher's DPO services).

**What mod-113 teaches.** The international privacy overlay ([chapter 07](./07-international-privacy-and-compliance-overlay.md)) at the operator level — know the transfer mechanisms, know the country-specific overlays, know the regulators, know when to escalate. ## Boundary worked examples

Six vignettes. Each one arrives on the general counsel's / CFO's / head-of-people's desk in a normal quarter of a US Series-B startup operating in the US + four international footprints.

**1. A Berlin ML researcher asks the Head of People, "Can my new NSO grant be taxed as capital gain in Germany rather than as wage income?"**

Question: who answers and who executes? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 06](./06-international-benefits-and-equity-comp.md) owns the operator-level answer — Germany does not offer a UK-EMI-equivalent regime; the default treatment is wage-income + social contributions on exercise-spread; the Zukunftsfinanzierungsgesetz § 19a EStG permits deferral under qualifying-employer criteria. The *election* mechanics (§ 19a filing, employer co-signatures, trustee or escrow arrangements) are executed by a **German tax advisor** and a **global-mobility advisor** — not by mod-113's operator. mod-105 is checked for the underlying US-plan sub-plan architecture. The researcher receives a written explanation of the operator-level answer and a referral to the external advisor for the personal election.

**2. The US CTO wants to open a Toronto engineering office with 10 engineers by end of year.**

Question: EOR or direct entity? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 01](./01-eor-vs-peo-vs-direct-entity-decision-framework.md) runs the framework — 10 engineers crosses the EOR-to-direct-entity break-even for Canada, SR&ED creates R&D-credit-generation value, Canadian common-law reasonable notice makes direct employment contracts preferable to EOR for senior roles, Quebec Charter-of-the-French-Language overlays if Quebec residents are in the hire set. The direct-entity path is run through mod-113 [chapter 02](./02-first-international-entity-setup.md). Local Canadian counsel is retained for formation (CBCA federal + Ontario provincial registration, or Ontario-direct); a Canadian accountant is retained for SR&ED programme design; mod-113 [chapter 03](./03-intercompany-and-transfer-pricing-structure.md) runs the ICSA with the Canadian subsidiary; mod-111 produces the US-parent board resolution authorising the formation; mod-101 updates the corporate record.

**3. The sales team wants to engage a UK contractor through their personal-service-company Ltd.**

Question: contractor or employee? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 01](./01-eor-vs-peo-vs-direct-entity-decision-framework.md) runs the IR35 analysis — the HMRC off-payroll working rules (Chapter 10, Part 2, ITEPA 2003) require a status determination by the end-client. The practitioner-canon outcome for sales roles with employment-like control, substitution, and mutuality-of-obligation characteristics is "inside IR35" with full PAYE / NIC obligation. The cleaner path is to engage the worker via an EOR as a UK employee or (if scope justifies) hire them to a UK Ltd subsidiary. mod-109 [chapter 03](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/03-vendor-contract-suite-and-onboarding-workflow.md) owns the contractor-paper template; mod-113 owns the UK re-characterisation analysis. UK employment counsel confirms the IR35 status determination.

**4. The Series-B lead investor's diligence asks for "the group transfer-pricing documentation."**

Question: what artefact goes to diligence? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 03](./03-intercompany-and-transfer-pricing-structure.md) owns the operator-level answer — the group should have (a) executed ICSAs between the US parent and each operating subsidiary, (b) a transfer-pricing memo documenting the cost-plus characterisation and the markup, (c) the intercompany-cash-settlement cadence, (d) BEPS 2.0 Pillar 2 analysis showing the group is below the €750M threshold. Where any of these are missing, **Big-Four or Baker McKenzie / Fenwick / DLA Piper transfer-pricing counsel** is retained to produce the memo and the comparable-company analysis; mod-113 directs the retention and reviews the output. The diligence response is coordinated with mod-111 (board diligence governance) and the investor's requested data room.

**5. The CFO plans a 15% group-wide reduction crossing US, UK, Germany, Canada, France, and India.**

Question: how does the international side run? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 08](./08-international-workforce-reduction-playbook.md) owns the international reductions playbook — per-country collective-consultation thresholds, representative-body consultation sequencing, severance-cost estimation. The US-side runs under mod-107. The two playbooks are choreographed on timing (French PSE takes 2–4 months; US-side announcement cannot precede Betriebsrat consultation in Germany). Immigration-status analysis per US foreign-national under mod-113 [chapter 04](./04-us-immigration-programme.md). Local employment counsel is retained per jurisdiction for the formal process steps. EPLI endorsement coverage confirmed under [mod-112 chapter 02](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md).

**6. A customer in Shanghai asks to execute the MSA against the group's Hong Kong entity, but the group has no Hong Kong entity.**

Question: can the group accommodate? Answer: mod-113 [chapter 02](./02-first-international-entity-setup.md) runs the country-first-hire analysis — is this a single-deal pattern (deflect to US-parent or Singapore sub execution) or a market-entry trigger (justify Hong Kong Pte Ltd formation). mod-109 [chapter 01](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/01-customer-contract-suite-msa-sla-dpa-security-aup.md) owns the MSA governing-law and forum-selection modifications. mod-113 [chapter 07](./07-international-privacy-and-compliance-overlay.md) runs the China cross-border-transfer and PIPL analysis (data-flow mapping, Chinese SCC filing requirements, security-assessment thresholds). mod-112 [chapter 04](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md) runs the US export-control + sanctions-screening analysis against the China-touch customer. Specialist China privacy and export-control counsel retained for the structural work.

## Using this map

- When a question arrives, look first at **what mod-113 owns**. If it maps to one of the eight chapters, mod-113 owns the operator-level answer end-to-end.
- If it does not map cleanly, look at **what mod-113 hands back to sibling modules**. The pattern: mod-113 owns the international overlay; the sibling module owns the US-side substrate.
- If the question has specialist depth (transfer-pricing documentation, immigration petitions, country-specific equity-plan execution, Chinese cross-border transfer filing, FCPA investigation, non-US litigation), look at **what mod-113 defers to specialist advisors**. mod-113 trains the operator to direct the specialist relationship; it does not substitute for the specialist.
- When two modules could plausibly own the question, apply the pattern: **mod-113 owns the "how does X look internationally" layer; the sibling module owns the "what is X at the US baseline" layer; the specialist advisor owns the "produce the formal filing / certification / defence" layer**.

The point of the map is to keep mod-113 **competent within its scope**. International expansion is a horizontal function that touches HR, finance, legal, security, product, GTM — mod-113 is assertive about owning the international-overlay layer, but it does not become an alternative COO function. The sibling modules own the US-side baseline; the specialist advisors own the depth; mod-113 is the hub that integrates them so the group can operate internationally without rebuilding its operating model per country.

## Summary
