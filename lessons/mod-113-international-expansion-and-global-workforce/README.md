# mod-113 — International Expansion & Global Workforce

> The EOR / PEO / direct-entity decision at the point of each new-country hire, the country-selection and first-entity setup against engineering talent / GTM / R&D credit / IP holding, the intercompany cost-plus-services and transfer-pricing structure at the operator level, the US immigration programme (H-1B / O-1 / L-1 / TN / E-3 / F-1 OPT / EB green cards), the international employment-law variance across UK / Germany / France / Canada / Australia, the international benefits and country-specific equity-comp regimes (UK EMI / French BSPCE / Israeli § 102 / German § 19a), the international privacy and FCPA-equivalent overlay (GDPR Chapter V, UK Bribery Act, Sapin II, CFPOA, non-US export controls), and the international workforce-reduction playbook (TULRCA, Betriebsrat, PSE) — the operating infrastructure that lets a US startup hire the engineer in Berlin on Monday without accumulating a compliance debt it will discover at the next diligence round.

**Track:** Startup Operations & Governance (level 50) · **Stage:** SEED / SERIES-A / SERIES-B / GROWTH · **Pillar:** people-ops · legal-ops · **Hours:** 36

## Why this module exists

[mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) authored the US employment-contract substrate — at-will, arbitration, PIIA, DTSA whistleblower notice, non-compete / non-solicit variance across states. [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) wired the HRIS / PEO stack that administers those contracts. [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) stood up the US-plan equity architecture; [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) the US compensation architecture. [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) authored the US-side offboarding playbook. [mod-110](../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) built the US-side privacy programme. [mod-112](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/) built the insurance tower and the FCPA / sanctions / export-controls baseline.

Every one of those modules operates on US-side defaults. None of them answers the question the incoming COO / GC / Head of People inherits the day the CEO signs a candidate the recruiter met at a conference in London, or the day the CTO wants to open a six-person offshore engineering centre in India, or the day the Head of Sales needs a Toronto closer: *how do we legally hire this specific person on Monday, under which structure, in which jurisdiction, with what documentation, and with what obligations the US-side programme does not already carry?*

This module owns that layer.

International expansion decomposes into eight operating decisions the corporation has to make on purpose, not by drift:

1. **EOR vs. PEO vs. direct entity.** The three-door framework (Employer of Record in the worker's country; US-only Professional Employer Organisation; wholly-owned direct subsidiary) and the four questions (headcount trajectory, country regulatory regime, IP / tax / regulatory posture, exit cost) that choose between them per country.
2. **First international entity setup.** Country selection against engineering-talent supply, GTM presence, R&D-tax-credit optimisation, and IP-holding jurisdiction; the entity form per country (UK Ltd, Ireland DAC, Canada CBCA, Australia Pty Ltd, Singapore Pte Ltd, Germany GmbH, Netherlands BV, France SAS, India Pvt Ltd, Japan KK / GK, Israel Ltd); local-director requirements; local bank-account and payroll setup; the 90–180 day formation timeline.
3. **Intercompany and transfer-pricing structure.** The cost-plus-services agreement (ICSA) under IRC § 482 and the OECD TPG, the IP ownership and licensing chain, the typical markup ranges (R&D 8–12%, admin 5–10%, sales-support 5–8%), the BEPS 2.0 Pillar 2 awareness, and the interface with specialist tax advisors.
4. **US immigration programme.** The H-1B lottery cycle (March registration, April–June petition, October 1 start), the cap-exempt distinction, the O-1 extraordinary-ability alternative, the L-1A / L-1B intra-company transfer, the TN under USMCA, the E-3 for Australian nationals, the F-1 OPT / STEM OPT bridges, and the EB green-card trajectory (EB-1 / EB-2 / EB-2 NIW / EB-3 / PERM).
5. **International employment-law variance.** The at-will spectrum (US exception, Canada common-law reasonable notice, UK / Germany / France protected-employment regimes), per-country statutory notice and termination protection, works-council / employee-representative overlays, statutory paid-leave floors, and the implications for the exec severance package design.
6. **International benefits and equity compensation.** Country-appropriate health / pension / retirement, country-specific equity-comp regimes (UK EMI with US-parent limitations, UK CSOP, French BSPCE, Israeli § 102 trustee tracks, German § 19a EStG deferral, Australian ESS start-up concession, Indian ESOP deferral), the global equity-plan administration overlay, and the FX / tax-residency / social-security-totalisation layer.
7. **International privacy and compliance overlay.** GDPR Chapter V cross-border transfers (DPF + SCCs + TIA), country-specific privacy regimes (UK GDPR, revFADP, PIPEDA / Law 25, LGPD, PIPL, PDPA, APPI, PIPA, DPDP), FCPA-equivalents (UK Bribery Act 2010, Canadian CFPOA, French Sapin II), non-US export-control regimes (EU 2021/821, UK ECJU, FEFTA, DEC), and the deemed-export analysis for foreign-national hires.
8. **International workforce-reduction playbook.** Country-specific collective-consultation regimes (UK TULRCA s. 188, German KSchG § 17 + BetrVG § 102, French PSE + DREETS homologation, Netherlands WMCO, Canadian provincial, Australian Fair Work Act s. 530), immigration-status implications per visa category, EOR unwind mechanics, and cross-module coordination with the US-side layoff playbook.

A ninth problem-space closes the loop:

9. **The ownership-boundary map.** The reference that decides — for any international question — whether it belongs to mod-113, to a sibling module (mod-101 / 102 / 103 / 104 / 105 / 106 / 107 / 108 / 109 / 110 / 111 / 112 / 114 for the US-side substrate), or to a specialist advisor (Big-Four and specialist transfer-pricing firms, country-specific employment counsel, immigration counsel, global-mobility advisors, country-specific equity-plan trustees, specialist privacy / anti-bribery / sanctions / export-control counsel).

Get this right and the corporation compounds — the Berlin ML researcher is on-boarded cleanly through the German GmbH with § 19a EStG election and ArbnErfG acknowledgement; the Toronto engineering team runs through a Canadian subsidiary generating SR&ED credits; the UK sales lead is engaged on a UK Ltd employment contract with IR35-compliant status; the Indian offshore team sits on a direct Pvt Ltd with STPI structuring; the group diligence package includes executed ICSAs, documented TIAs, country-specific anti-bribery policy, and an immigration-status census for every US foreign-national hire. Get it wrong and the corporation absorbs the predictable failure set — a long-tenured German worker on an EOR platform statutorily converted to an EOR employee under AÜG; an uncapped unfair-dismissal exposure in the UK for a terminated 2-year-tenure employee; a French PSE non-compliance that voids 15 dismissals and triggers reinstatement orders; an Israeli § 102 forfeited because the 30-day pre-grant plan filing was missed; an H-1B employee terminated without the required return-transportation offer and 60-day grace-period communication; a GDPR Chapter V transfer running on legacy Privacy Shield assumptions after the DPF has survived its third CJEU scrutiny; a UK Bribery Act Section 7 corporate offence prosecuted on the strict-liability footing because the adequate-procedures programme was never written down; and a US-only reductions announcement that lands in Germany, France, and the Netherlands before the works councils have been consulted.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical multi-stage corporation expanding internationally, produce and defend:

1. **A per-country EOR / direct-entity decision** — the four-question framework applied to each candidate country, documented in a one-page decision memo, with the country-specific regulatory tilts (Germany AÜG, France portage, UK IR35, Canada provincial) addressed.
2. **A first-international-entity setup plan** — country selected against the four drivers (talent, GTM, R&D credit, IP holding), entity form and formation mechanics, local-director arrangement, bank-account and payroll setup, 180-day formation-and-onboarding timeline, and the first-direct-hire offer package.
3. **An intercompany and transfer-pricing structure** — the executed ICSA with cost-plus markup, the IP ownership and licensing chain, the transfer-pricing memo at the operator level, BEPS 2.0 Pillar 2 awareness, and the specialist-advisor engagement letter.
4. **A US immigration programme** — the H-1B lottery operating calendar, the visa-decision tree for new candidates, the sponsorship-cost budget, and the green-card trajectory for retained foreign-national employees.
5. **A country-specific employment-contract pack** — UK, Germany, France, Canada, Australia employment-contract templates with the country-specific notice / severance / works-council / IP-assignment / leave provisions, and the global exec-severance architecture that is compliant across all jurisdictions.
6. **A country-specific benefits-and-equity-comp pack** — country-appropriate benefits packages across UK / Germany / France / Netherlands / Ireland / Canada / Australia / Singapore / India / Israel; country sub-plans implementing UK CSOP, French BSPCE where applicable, Israeli § 102, German § 19a EStG; the global equity-administration operating procedure.
7. **An international privacy and compliance programme** — the GDPR Chapter V transfer-mechanism stack (DPF + SCCs + TIA + UK Addendum + Swiss Addendum), the per-country compliance register, the UK Bribery Act 2010 adequate-procedures programme, the deemed-export-analysis workflow for foreign-national hires, and the sanctions-screening amplification for international payments.
8. **An international workforce-reduction playbook** — the per-country collective-consultation playbook, the immigration-status-cascading plan per visa category, the EOR-unwind mechanics, the multi-country timeline, and the cross-module coordination plan with the US-side layoff playbook.
9. **A defensible ownership-boundary map** — "does this international question belong here, in a sibling module ([mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/), [mod-102](../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/), [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/), [mod-104](../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/), [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/), [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/), [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/), [mod-108](../mod-108-culture-employee-experience-and-dei/), [mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/), [mod-110](../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/), [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/), [mod-112](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/), [mod-114](../mod-114-operations-function-design/)), or with a specialist advisor (Big-Four / Baker McKenzie / Fenwick for transfer pricing, country-specific local counsel, Fragomen / BAL for immigration, Deloitte GES / KPMG GMS for global mobility, § 102 trustees, specialist privacy / anti-bribery / sanctions / export-control counsel)?"
