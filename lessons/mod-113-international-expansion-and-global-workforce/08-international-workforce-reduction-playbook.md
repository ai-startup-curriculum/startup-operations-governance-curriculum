# 8. International workforce-reduction playbook

> A US workforce reduction happens in a day. A French reduction above the PSE threshold happens over months and requires a formal plan. The international reductions playbook is not a translation of the US one — it is a different operating playbook with country-specific timing, consultation, and severance obligations that cannot be shortcut by executive urgency.

## Motivation

Workforce reductions are rare events that must execute flawlessly on first run. The US-side playbook — WARN Act notice where triggered, severance terms per the exec severance template, release and separation agreement, benefits-COBRA transition — sits in [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) and is designed around the US at-will baseline ([chapter 05](./05-international-employment-law-variance.md)). None of that transposes cleanly to an international footprint. In most of the world, employers cannot:

- Terminate without cause (jurisdiction-defined "fair" or "objective" grounds required).
- Shorten statutory notice by contract.
- Avoid works-council / employee-representative consultation where thresholds are met.
- Package severance below statutory formulas.
- Compress the timeline to a single-day announcement for groups above collective-consultation thresholds.

A US-native reductions announcement that treats the international footprint as "same action with translated letters" produces predictable failures: breach of German Betriebsrat consultation rights (voiding the dismissals), breach of French PSE obligation (voiding the dismissals and triggering reinstatement orders), breach of UK collective-consultation obligation (producing a protective-award liability of up to 90 days' pay per affected employee).

This chapter is the international reductions playbook — the country-specific timelines, consultations, notices, and severance mechanics the group runs in parallel with the US-side action. It also addresses the three subsidiary problems: **immigration-status implications** for terminated US foreign-national employees, **EOR-relationship unwind** for workforce held through an EOR, and **cross-module coordination** with the US-side playbook.

**Ownership boundary before we begin.** This chapter owns the **international reductions playbook**. It defers to [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) for the US-side layoff and offboarding mechanics; to [chapter 04](./04-us-immigration-programme.md) for the immigration-status mechanics; to [chapter 05](./05-international-employment-law-variance.md) for the underlying country-specific employment-law substrate; to [mod-112](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/) for EPLI and D&O exposure; and to local employment counsel in each jurisdiction for the formal letter templates and process certifications.

## The international reductions decision frame

Before executing in any jurisdiction, resolve three questions. The answers drive timeline, cost, and process.

### Question 1 — individual, small-group, or collective

In most jurisdictions, the obligation set differs sharply based on the number of proposed dismissals in a defined period:

- **Individual** — a single-employee termination. Driven by country-specific individual-dismissal rules (cause / notice / severance). Shortest path; still requires process compliance.
- **Small group** — more than one but below the collective-redundancy threshold. Driven by individual rules applied to each worker, plus any country-specific small-group consultation obligations.
- **Collective** — above the jurisdiction's threshold. Triggers formal collective-consultation, representative-body consultation (works council, trade union, elected representatives), government notification, and in some jurisdictions a mandatory restructuring plan (France PSE).

The jurisdictional threshold is specific:

| Jurisdiction | Collective threshold | Notice / consultation period |
|---|---|---|
| **UK** | 20+ proposed dismissals at one establishment in 90 days (TULRCA 1992 s. 188) | 30 days (20–99); **45 days (100+)** |
| **Germany** | Mass-dismissal (Massenentlassung) threshold by establishment size — 6+ in est. 20–59 employees; 10% or 26+ in est. 60–499; 30+ in est. 500+ (KSchG § 17) | Betriebsrat consultation + Massenentlassungsanzeige to Agentur für Arbeit |
| **France** | 10+ dismissals for economic reasons in 30 days in a company of ≥ 50 employees — **PSE (plan de sauvegarde de l'emploi)** required; smaller operations: lighter consultation | 2–4 months typical for PSE; DIRECCTE (DREETS) homologation |
| **Netherlands** | 20+ dismissals in a 3-month period in one UWV district (Wet melding collectief ontslag) | 30-day waiting period; works-council consultation |
| **Spain** | ERE (expediente de regulación de empleo) thresholds (Art. 51 ET) | 30-day consultation; authority intervention possible |
| **Italy** | 5+ dismissals in 120 days in units with 15+ employees (Legge 223/1991) | 75 days total procedure |
| **Canada (Ontario)** | 50+ terminations at one establishment in a 4-week period (ESA s. 58) | Formal notice to Director of Employment Standards; 8–16 weeks statutory notice |
| **Canada (Quebec)** | 10+ terminations in a 2-month period (LSA) | Notice to Minister; 8–16 weeks |
| **Australia** | 15+ dismissals (Fair Work Act s. 530) | Notice to Services Australia; consultation with Fair Work-registered employee organisations |

### Question 2 — reason characterisation (cause / performance / economic / role-elimination)

Jurisdictions distinguish between reasons for dismissal in ways the US at-will baseline does not. For economic / operational reductions (the typical startup layoff), the characterisation matters because:

- **Germany.** KSchG recognises three categories — personal (**personenbedingt**), conduct (**verhaltensbedingt**), and operational (**betriebsbedingt**). A role-elimination layoff is **betriebsbedingt**; the employer must prove the operational need, apply the statutory **social selection (Sozialauswahl)** criteria (tenure, age, maintenance obligations, severe disability), and avoid redundancy via available alternative positions. Works-council consultation is a precondition.
- **France.** The economic grounds (**licenciement pour motif économique**) require (i) economic cause — financial difficulties, technological change, operational safeguard, or cessation of activity; (ii) reclassification efforts within the group; (iii) PSE if above threshold; (iv) priority re-employment rights for 12 months.
- **UK.** Redundancy as defined in Employment Rights Act 1996 s. 139 — closure of workplace, cessation of business, or diminished requirement for work of a particular kind. Fair procedure includes consultation (collective where triggered, individual regardless), fair selection criteria, and consideration of alternative employment.
- **Canada.** No statutory "cause" distinction for economic terminations; common-law "reasonable notice" applies at common law, with statutory minimum notice overriding where more generous. Mass-termination thresholds trigger specific statutory notice regimes.
- **Australia.** Genuine redundancy defence to unfair-dismissal claims requires that the job is no longer required to be performed by anyone, that the employer has consulted per any applicable modern award or enterprise agreement, and that redeployment within the enterprise has been considered.

### Question 3 — representative-body consultation and government notification

The representative-body consultation is the governance step a US-native playbook most frequently misses. For each affected jurisdiction identify:

- **Does a works council / employee representative body exist?** Germany Betriebsrat; France CSE; Netherlands Ondernemingsraad; Austrian / Belgian / Nordic analogues. Where it does, the body is a procedural party to the dismissal; failure to consult voids the dismissals (France) or produces a protective-award (UK trade-union consultation parallel).
- **Is government notification required?** UK HR1 to the Insolvency Service for 100+ redundancies; France DREETS notification of PSE; Germany Massenentlassungsanzeige to the Agentur für Arbeit; Netherlands UWV notification; Australia Centrelink notification.
- **Is union consultation required?** Where a union is recognised (statutory recognition or voluntary), the collective-consultation duty runs to the union. In jurisdictions with sectoral collective bargaining (France, Italy, Spain, Nordics), the sectoral agreement may add consultation obligations.

## Country-specific reductions playbooks

### United Kingdom

**Governing law.** Employment Rights Act 1996; Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992 (TULRCA 1992 ss. 188–198A); ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures (consultation guidance).

**Individual redundancy process:**

1. **Place employee at risk.** Written notification of selection pool and provisional selection.
2. **Fair selection criteria.** Objective, verifiable, non-discriminatory (performance ratings, skills matrix, attendance subject to disability law, length of service).
3. **Individual consultation.** Normally 2–3 meetings — initial notification, selection consultation, dismissal confirmation.
4. **Alternative employment.** Reasonable search for alternative role within the group; four-week trial in alternative role if offered.
5. **Statutory redundancy pay** for employees with ≥ 2 years' service — formula based on age × service × weekly pay with a statutory weekly cap.
6. **Statutory notice** per s. 86 ERA 1996 (1 week per year of service, max 12 weeks), or payment in lieu.
7. **Settlement agreement** (contracting out of statutory claims including unfair dismissal) is standard practice at any reasonably senior level; must be in writing, must be advised by an independent legal adviser (solicitor or ACAS-approved).

**Collective redundancy (20+):**

1. **Trigger.** 20+ proposed dismissals at one establishment in 90 days.
2. **Consultation period.** 30 days (20–99) or **45 days (100+)** minimum before first dismissal takes effect.
3. **HR1 filing** with the Insolvency Service for 100+ dismissals.
4. **Consultation partner** — recognised trade union, or elected employee representatives if no union recognised. Elections must be conducted fairly; the ACAS guide applies.
5. **Mandatory consultation topics** — avoiding dismissals, reducing number, mitigating consequences.
6. **Protective-award liability** for failure — up to 90 days' pay per affected employee.

**Settlement agreements** typically wrap individual-package negotiation, confidentiality (subject to statutory whistleblower protection carve-outs), return of equipment, mutual non-disparagement, and release of claims. See the US-side settlement discipline at [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) for the structural analogue.

### Germany

**Governing law.** Kündigungsschutzgesetz (KSchG); Betriebsverfassungsgesetz (BetrVG); BGB §§ 622 (notice), 626 (termination for cause); Sozialgesetzbuch III § 17 KSchG (mass-dismissal notification).

**Individual role-elimination (betriebsbedingt):**

1. **Operational need.** Document the business reason (business closure, departmental elimination, outsourcing, headcount reduction with associated workflow redesign). Judicial review of the operational decision is limited but the reduction itself must be demonstrated.
2. **Sozialauswahl (social selection).** From the pool of comparable employees, apply statutory criteria — length of service, age, maintenance obligations (dependents), severe disability — and select the socially least-protected. Collective agreements may vary the weighting; works-council agreement to a social-selection scheme is common.
3. **Betriebsrat consultation (BetrVG § 102).** The works council must be heard before any dismissal; failure voids the dismissal. The council may object (one week); objection does not block the dismissal but creates protection against termination during the Kündigungsschutzklage.
4. **Notice period.** BGB § 622 — statutory 4 weeks baseline, extending with tenure up to 7 months after 20+ years. Contract may extend.
5. **Severance.** No general statutory severance for individual dismissal (contrary to common misconception). Severance arises from works-council social plan (**Sozialplan**), collective bargaining, settlement agreement under § 1a KSchG (0.5 months' pay per year of service in exchange for forfeiture of Kündigungsschutzklage right), or negotiated termination agreement (**Aufhebungsvertrag**).
6. **Aufhebungsvertrag.** Negotiated mutual termination agreement — frequent in Germany to resolve dismissals without litigation. Carries employee-side unemployment-benefit implications (sperrzeit) that must be structured carefully.

**Massenentlassung (mass dismissal, KSchG § 17):**

- **Thresholds.** 6+ dismissals in establishment of 20–59 employees; 10% or 26+ in establishments of 60–499; 30+ in establishments of 500+.
- **Massenentlassungsanzeige.** Formal notification to the Agentur für Arbeit before any dismissal takes effect. Freiwillige Betriebsvereinbarung with Betriebsrat on the mass-dismissal plan.
- **Sozialplan.** Where a works council exists and the operational change constitutes a Betriebsänderung (business change under BetrVG § 111 — covers mass dismissals, relocations, significant restructuring), a Sozialplan negotiated with the Betriebsrat is required. The Sozialplan covers severance, retraining, outplacement, and other mitigations. Negotiations frequently route to the **Einigungsstelle (conciliation board)** if parties do not agree.

### France

**Governing law.** Code du travail (livre II, chapitre III for individual economic dismissal; chapitre IV for collective procedure); PSE regime under L. 1233-61 et seq.; CSE (comité social et économique) under L. 2311 et seq.

**Individual economic dismissal (licenciement pour motif économique) — < 10 in 30 days or < 50-employee company:**

1. **Economic cause.** One of the enumerated grounds — financial difficulties, technological change, operational safeguard, cessation of activity.
2. **Reclassification.** Documented group-wide search for alternative role; priority on skills and geography.
3. **Convocation to pre-dismissal interview (entretien préalable).** Five business days' notice minimum.
4. **Dismissal letter.** Must state the economic grounds with specificity.
5. **CSP (contrat de sécurisation professionnelle)** proposal in companies of < 1,000 employees — a government-run retraining and support programme in lieu of standard termination notice.
6. **Notice period and severance.** Statutory notice per Code du travail; severance per L. 1234-9 (1/4 month per year ≤ 10 years, 1/3 month per year > 10 years, as a minimum) or collective agreement (frequently more generous).
7. **Priority re-employment.** 12-month right of priority re-employment if the employer re-opens the role.

**Collective economic dismissal (≥ 10 in 30 days in ≥ 50-employee company) — PSE:**

1. **Plan de sauvegarde de l'emploi (PSE).** A formal restructuring plan covering job preservation measures, reclassification, retraining, severance in excess of statutory minimum, outplacement, mobility aids.
2. **CSE consultation.** Mandatory consultation with the CSE (and the CSE central if group-level) on the proposed restructuring and the PSE itself.
3. **DREETS (previously DIRECCTE) involvement.** The administrative authority either **homologates** a unilaterally-adopted PSE (strict legality review) or **validates** a PSE adopted by collective agreement (lighter review). Decision within 15 or 21 days respectively.
4. **Minimum content.** Formal headcount reduction scope, selection criteria, reclassification plan, severance scheme, mobility measures. Litigation around minimum content and reclassification is common.
5. **Timeline.** 2–4 months typical from PSE launch to first dismissals; longer for complex cases.

**Economic grounds and group reach.** Economic grounds assessment considers the **group's** economic situation, not just the French entity. A profitable US parent is a factor against the French subsidiary's economic defence; French courts routinely re-characterise economic dismissals as unfair when the US-parent group is profitable. Specialist French employment counsel required for any US-parent subsidiary restructuring.

### Netherlands

**Governing law.** Burgerlijk Wetboek (Book 7); Wet werk en zekerheid (WWZ) and successor Wet arbeidsmarkt in balans; Wet melding collectief ontslag (WMCO).

**Individual dismissal.** Dutch law requires either:

- **UWV (Uitvoeringsinstituut Werknemersverzekeringen)** dismissal permit — for economic / role-elimination grounds; UWV reviews reasonableness.
- **Cantonal court dissolution** — for performance / cause / other grounds.
- **Mutual termination agreement (vaststellingsovereenkomst)** — negotiated termination with a reflection period (14 or 21 days) during which the employee may revoke.

**Transition payment (transitievergoeding).** Statutory severance applies to dismissals initiated by the employer — 1/3 month's gross salary per year of service, no cap, from the first day of employment.

**Collective dismissal (WMCO):** 20+ dismissals in a 3-month period in one UWV district triggers notification to UWV, notification to trade unions, consultation with the works council (where present) and the Ondernemingsraad consultation obligation (WOR).

### Canada

**Governing law.** Provincial Employment Standards statutes (Ontario ESA 2000; Quebec LSA; British Columbia Employment Standards Act; others); common law of reasonable notice; Canadian Human Rights Act and provincial human-rights statutes.

**Individual termination (without cause):**

1. **Statutory notice** — per province (Ontario ESA s. 57: 1–8 weeks; longer for mass terminations).
2. **Common-law reasonable notice** — the greater of statutory minimum or Bardal-factor reasonable notice (age, tenure, position, re-employment prospects). Senior roles may draw 12–24 months of reasonable notice at common law.
3. **Severance pay** — Ontario ESA s. 64: additional severance for employers with ≥ $2.5M Ontario payroll and employees with ≥ 5 years' service.
4. **Written release and settlement agreement** — standard practice, requires employee independent legal advice for enforceability.

**Mass termination.** Province-specific thresholds; Ontario ESA s. 58 (50+ at one establishment in 4 weeks) triggers 8–16 weeks' notice to the Director of Employment Standards + statutory notice to employees.

**Quebec specifics.** Collective-dismissal procedure under the LSA; language-of-dismissal letters subject to the Charter of the French Language (Loi 101 as amended by Loi 96).

### Australia

**Governing law.** Fair Work Act 2009 (FWA); National Employment Standards; modern awards; enterprise agreements.

**Genuine redundancy.** Defence to unfair-dismissal claim requires:
- Job no longer required to be performed by anyone because of operational changes
- Compliance with any consultation requirement in applicable modern award or enterprise agreement
- Reasonable consideration of redeployment within the enterprise or associated entities

**Statutory redundancy pay.** NES provides 4–16 weeks' pay based on service, up to a 12-week ceiling for 10+ years of service (sector / award variations). Small-business employers (fewer than 15 employees) exempt from NES redundancy pay (but not from unfair-dismissal protection).

**Notification.** Fair Work Act s. 530 — employer must notify Services Australia (previously Centrelink) when terminating 15+ employees.

### Other EU jurisdictions

**Ireland.** Protection of Employment Act 1977 (collective-redundancy threshold at 5+ dismissals in 500-employee establishment; variable thresholds); 30-day consultation with employee representatives.

**Spain.** ERE regime under Art. 51 Estatuto de los Trabajadores — 30-day consultation period; thresholds based on company size and dismissal numbers. Collective-bargaining-agreement framework common.

**Italy.** Mandatory procedure under Legge 223/1991 for collective dismissals of 5+ workers in 120 days; 75-day procedure including trade-union consultation.

**Nordic countries (Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland).** Strong trade-union coverage drives collective-bargaining-agreement-level consultation; works-council / medbestemmelse requirements; statutory consultation periods vary.

## Immigration-status implications

A workforce reduction that terminates US foreign-national employees has immigration consequences that cascade from the termination date. See [chapter 04](./04-us-immigration-programme.md) for the underlying visa framework.

### H-1B

**60-day grace period.** 8 CFR § 214.1(l)(2) — after cessation of H-1B employment, the worker has up to **60 consecutive calendar days or until the end of the authorised validity period, whichever is shorter**, to find a new H-1B sponsor, change status, or depart the US. The 60-day period may be used once per authorised validity period.

**Change of employer.** New H-1B petition portable upon receipt by USCIS (AC21 portability).

**Change of status.** B-2 visitor status filing is possible but discretionary; F-1 study with change-of-status filing is possible but complex.

**Dependents.** H-4 dependents' status terminates with the principal's status.

**Employer obligations.**

- **Reasonable costs of return transportation to the home country** — INA § 214(c)(5)(A), 8 CFR § 214.2(h)(4)(iii)(E). Required where the employer dismisses before the end of authorised stay. Document the offer; it is specific-statute-driven.
- **Withdrawal of the H-1B petition** by filing a withdrawal request with USCIS. Without withdrawal, the LCA remains attached to the terminated worker's record and prevents clean closure.
- **Last-paycheck date** interacts with the status-ending date. Guidance from USCIS and DOL on when status cessation starts is in motion; follow the current USCIS Policy Manual (Volume 2) and immigration counsel's advice.

### TN

**No formal grace period.** TN status is specifically tied to the employment covered in the petition. Termination of employment ends status; the worker must depart or change status immediately. Practical USCIS tolerance is limited; immigration counsel advises on the specific case.

### L-1

**60-day grace period** per 8 CFR § 214.1(l)(2), parallel to H-1B. L-1A executive / manager and L-1B specialised-knowledge both covered.

**Return to foreign affiliate.** The L-1 relationship is premised on the qualifying corporate relationship between the US petitioner and the foreign affiliate. On US-side termination, the worker typically returns to the foreign affiliate (where their L-1-qualifying prior employment originated); the foreign employment relationship is handled under the foreign jurisdiction's law.

### O-1

**60-day grace period** per 8 CFR § 214.1(l)(2) applies to O-1 as well.

### F-1 OPT / STEM OPT

**90-day unemployment aggregate limit** for post-completion OPT; **150-day aggregate limit** for 24-month STEM OPT (8 CFR § 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E)). Exceeding the aggregate ends F-1 status.

### Green-card process in flight

**I-140 petition.** If approved, I-140 priority date is preserved; worker can port under AC21 § 106(c) if the priority date is current.

**I-485 adjustment pending.** H-1B worker who has filed I-485 for 180+ days can port under AC21 § 106(c) to a "same or similar" occupation with a new employer.

**PERM in process.** PERM certification tied to the terminating employer is lost; the new employer restarts the PERM for a new green-card process (if applicable).

### Playbook integration

The international reductions playbook integrates the immigration-status analysis per affected worker, before the termination announcement:

1. **Immigration-status census.** Per affected US-foreign-national employee: current visa category, status expiration date, dependents' status, priority date and I-485 status if applicable, grace-period eligibility.
2. **Severance package design.** Build in **immigration-counsel-assistance support** (typically a specified allowance for new-employer sponsorship or departure-logistics counsel), **extended health benefits via COBRA / local equivalent** through grace-period expiry, **statement of grace-period rights** in the separation documentation.
3. **Return-transportation offer** per the H-1B / L-1 regulatory requirement.
4. **H-1B / L-1 / O-1 petition withdrawal** by immigration counsel following termination.
5. **PERM / I-140 status review** per worker with pending green-card process.

## EOR workforce-reduction unwind

Workforce held through an Employer of Record ([chapter 01](./01-eor-vs-peo-vs-direct-entity-decision-framework.md)) is terminated through the EOR, not directly. The client-services agreement governs the operational mechanics.

### Pattern

1. **Client-side decision.** The US-parent client decides to reduce the workforce in a given country and notifies the EOR under the client-services agreement.
2. **EOR-side employment termination.** The EOR, as the legal employer, runs the local termination process — statutory notice, severance per local law, consultation obligations if the local workforce reaches collective-threshold scale, local-regulator notification where required.
3. **Pass-through billing.** The client pays statutory severance and associated costs (notice-pay-in-lieu, accrued-vacation-payout, local-tax-and-social-insurance remittances) through the EOR under the pass-through billing mechanics.
4. **Transition options.** The affected worker is either (a) terminated by the EOR, (b) transferred to a successor employer (another EOR, the group's new direct subsidiary, or a third-party acquirer), or (c) continued under a renegotiated contract where the client-services relationship persists.

### Collective-dismissal through EOR

An EOR structure does not eliminate the collective-dismissal consultation obligation. If the group reduces 25 EOR-employed UK workers in a 90-day period, the EOR as the legal employer must comply with TULRCA 1992 s. 188 — consult with the appropriate representatives (elected representatives in the absence of a recognised union); file HR1 if 100+. The EOR coordinates the consultation; the US-parent client funds the process.

Where the EOR aggregates workers across multiple clients, the collective-dismissal threshold analysis is normally run per-client (same employer, same establishment analysis is fact-specific). The EOR's compliance team drives the analysis; the client should confirm the EOR's methodology before relying on it.

### IP and chain-of-title at EOR exit

The intercompany IP-assignment chain (EOR-employed worker → EOR → US-parent client, per the EOR client-services agreement — [chapter 01](./01-eor-vs-peo-vs-direct-entity-decision-framework.md)) survives termination. Confirm with the EOR that the termination documentation includes:

- Final IP-assignment confirmation.
- Return of client-owned confidential information and equipment.
- Non-solicit / non-compete release or re-affirmation per contract.
- Exit-interview-equivalent record where the EOR operates one.

## Cross-module coordination

Workforce reductions in a multi-jurisdiction group are rarely single-country events. Common coordination requirements:

- **Timing alignment with the US-side action.** [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) owns the US-side layoff mechanics (WARN Act notice, OWBPA release language for 40+ workers, selection-criteria discipline, severance package). International actions typically run slower; the US-side action completes before the international actions reach the dismissal date. Communications must handle the staggered timing — a group-wide announcement that references "effective date" without country-specific nuance creates employee-relations friction and legal exposure.
- **Communications governance.** A single global announcement with country-specific annexes; translated letters where statutorily required (France, Quebec); works-council / employee-representative pre-notification per jurisdiction (Germany, France, Netherlands) typically before the public announcement.
- **Severance-cost budget.** Non-US statutory severance is higher per capita than US practice. A reduction budget calibrated to US severance typical practice will under-fund the international component by a multiple. Confirm severance-cost estimates per jurisdiction with local counsel before the executive decision is finalised.
- **EPLI / D&O coverage.** EPLI (employment practices liability) covers wrongful-termination and related claims — see [mod-112 chapter 02](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md). International coverage is a specific endorsement; confirm the policy's territorial scope before a workforce reduction event. D&O can be implicated if plaintiffs allege management misled stakeholders about operations.
- **Equity-plan impact.** Terminations trigger post-termination exercise windows under the equity plan; some jurisdictions (UK EMI, Israeli § 102) impose plan-specific requirements on termination events ([chapter 06](./06-international-benefits-and-equity-comp.md)).
- **Privacy / employee-data retention.** HR files, performance records, and separation documentation have country-specific retention periods and employee access rights under GDPR / country equivalents ([chapter 07](./07-international-privacy-and-compliance-overlay.md)).
- **Internal communications and employee-representative obligations.** Where a works council exists (Germany, France), consultation precedes any employee communication. A US-timed all-hands that announces the reduction before the Betriebsrat has been informed is a BetrVG violation.

## Timeline integration — a representative multi-country reduction

A US Series-B startup reducing 15% of global headcount — 60 US employees, 10 UK employees, 8 German employees, 6 Canadian employees, 4 French employees, 2 Indian employees, 4 EOR-employed workers across Spain / Poland / Portugal — would follow approximately this timeline:

- **Day -60 to -30 (planning).** Reduction decision finalised with GC, CFO, CPO, CEO, board; per-jurisdiction scope confirmed with local counsel; severance budget per jurisdiction confirmed; immigration-status census for US foreign nationals; EOR unwind plan drafted; EPLI confirmation.
- **Day -30 to -14 (consultation initiation).** France PSE launched — CSE consultation starts; Germany Betriebsrat consultation starts (and Sozialplan negotiation if the operation constitutes a Betriebsänderung); UK HR1 filing prepared if applicable; Netherlands WMCO notification if applicable.
- **Day 0 (public announcement).** US-side WARN notices (where triggered); US employee communications and separations begin; UK consultation period continues (30 or 45 days); French PSE continues; German Betriebsrat consultation continues; Canadian notifications; Indian notifications; EOR-side terminations initiated.
- **Day +30 to +60 (international dismissal effective dates).** UK dismissals effective at the end of consultation period plus statutory notice; German dismissals effective after Betriebsrat consultation plus statutory notice; French dismissals follow DREETS homologation; Canadian dismissals effective at end of statutory notice; Indian dismissals per Industrial Disputes Act (if applicable) or standard termination; EOR terminations effective per local law.
- **Day +60 to +180 (post-separation).** Immigration-status transitions managed (H-1B grace periods, L-1 returns, O-1 transitions); COBRA / local-health-continuation administered; outplacement services run; green-card porting assistance per retained worker commitments; litigation watch and settlement responses.

## Anti-patterns

- **Executing US-timed layoffs across the international footprint simultaneously.** The resulting breach of consultation obligations voids dismissals and creates protective-award / reinstatement liability substantially larger than the targeted severance saving.
- **Treating EOR workers as reductions-free.** EOR-employed workers are legally employed under the local regime and the EOR must run the local termination process (statutory notice, severance, consultation where applicable). The EOR passes through the costs; the client funds them.
- **Ignoring the immigration grace-period mechanics.** H-1B workers terminated without a documented 60-day grace-period communication, without return-transportation offer per the regulatory requirement, and without H-1B withdrawal filing create immigration-compliance exposure.
- **Running the French PSE as a lightweight consultation.** Insufficient PSE content and documentation routinely results in judicial nullification of the dismissals, reinstatement orders, and in some cases personal liability for the employer's representatives on specific procedural failures.
- **Not budgeting German Sozialplan.** The Sozialplan is negotiated with the Betriebsrat for restructurings that constitute Betriebsänderungen. Severance scales well above statutory minimum; the Einigungsstelle imposes a plan if agreement is not reached.
- **Communications before consultation.** Publicly announcing a reduction before the works council has been consulted is a BetrVG / CSE violation. Internal all-hands communications sometimes serve as "constructive public announcement" to a works council — err on the side of consulting first.
- **Severance offered below statutory minimum conditioned on signing a release.** The statutory severance is owed regardless; only amounts above statutory minimum can be conditioned on release.
- **Working from the US settlement-agreement template.** Non-US releases must comply with local-law release mechanics (UK settlement-agreement conditions, French transaction mechanics, German Aufhebungsvertrag mechanics). The US template signed in a non-US jurisdiction is unenforceable in that jurisdiction.

## Summary

- **Three framing questions** govern every reductions decision: individual vs. collective, reason characterisation (cause / performance / economic / role-elimination), and representative-body / government-notification obligations.
- **Per-country playbooks** differ sharply — UK TULRCA s. 188 (30 / 45-day consultation), Germany KSchG § 17 + BetrVG § 102 (Massenentlassung + Betriebsrat), France PSE with DREETS homologation (2–4 month process), Netherlands WMCO + UWV, Canada provincial mass-termination, Australia Fair Work Act consultation. Each has specific statutory severance mechanics.
- **Immigration-status cascading** affects every US foreign-national termination — H-1B / L-1 / O-1 60-day grace period, TN immediate termination, F-1 OPT unemployment-aggregate limit, I-140 / I-485 portability under AC21.
- **EOR-held workforce** is terminated through the EOR under local law, with pass-through severance billing — the EOR relationship does not eliminate statutory consultation or notice obligations.

- **Timeline discipline.** International reductions take 60–180 days from decision to final dismissals, not the one-day US-native cadence; budget and communications must track the slower path.
