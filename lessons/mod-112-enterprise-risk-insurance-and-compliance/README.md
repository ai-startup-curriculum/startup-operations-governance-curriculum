# mod-112 — Enterprise Risk, Insurance & Compliance

> The enterprise-risk-management operating framework (taxonomy, register, appetite statement, quarterly executive review, annual audit-committee review) that sits above every individual risk programme and gives the board a *Marchand v. Barnhill* answer; the stage-triggered startup insurance stack (General Liability, Workers' Comp, Tech E&O + Cyber, EPLI, D&O Sides A/B/C, Umbrella, Fiduciary Liability, Crime / Fidelity, Representations & Warranties) with the occurrence-vs-claims-made and application-answers-are-warranties disciplines that make the policies actually pay at claims time; the cyber-insurance operating layer where the carrier is a live participant in the incident and the panel of pre-approved breach counsel, forensics firms, notification vendors, and ransomware negotiators is socialised inside the security team *before* an incident; and the sanctions and export-controls compliance programme (OFAC country and list-based programmes under 31 CFR Chapter V, the EAR at 15 CFR Parts 730–774 with the Commerce Control List / Country Chart / License Exceptions, the ITAR at 22 CFR Parts 120–130, and the self-serve-signup OFAC-screening pattern that keeps a US-based SaaS from transacting into a sanctioned jurisdiction by default) — the risk-transfer and compliance infrastructure the corporation needs so every board-level *Caremark* question, every carrier at renewal, every enterprise customer's vendor-risk review, every OFAC inquiry, and every breach-response clock can be met with a documented programme rather than a founder's memory.

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) stood up the Delaware C-corp, the officer-authority framework, and the board-resolution mechanics that give risk-transfer and compliance commitments their force. [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) authored the employment-contract substrate the EPLI and workers-comp posture sits on. [mod-109](../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/) authored the customer MSA / SLA / DPA / Security Addendum bundle whose indemnity caps, super-caps, and audit-rights language the insurance tower underwrites against. [mod-110](../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) wrote the privacy-programme operating model and the regulatory-notification clocks (GDPR Article 33, HIPAA Breach Notification Rule, the 50-state matrix, NYDFS Part 500, SEC Item 1.05) that the cyber-insurance carrier coordinates its incident-response panel against. [mod-111](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/) owns the board operating layer, the D&O programme design detail, the audit-committee charter, and the *Caremark* / *Marchand* doctrinal frame the enterprise-risk conversation lands inside.

What none of those modules does is answer the questions every carrier at renewal, every outside director on the way in, every CISO in the first hour of a ransomware incident, and every OFAC Enforcement Division letter eventually asks: *what is the integrated top-ten risk picture the executive team and the board look at on a cadence, which lines of insurance are in force at this stage and which are warranty-defensible, which carrier do we call first when the production Postgres cluster is encrypted, and how do we prove — on the record — that a paying customer inside a sanctioned jurisdiction did not slip through the self-serve signup flow?*

This module owns that layer.

Enterprise risk, insurance, and compliance decompose into four operating decisions the corporation has to make on purpose, not by drift:

1. **The enterprise-risk-management operating framework.** The fixed nine-to-eleven-category taxonomy (strategic / operational / financial / compliance / reputational / cybersecurity / AI / geopolitical / people / product) with a named owner per category; the risk register as the artefact with a fixed schema (ID, statement, category, owner, inherent L × I, controls, residual L × I, trend, cadence, last-reviewed, open actions) in a single tool of record; the risk appetite statement (board-adopted, qualitative-plus-limited-quantitative, annually re-affirmed) and the risk-tolerance operationalisation at the metric level; the quarterly 60–90-minute executive risk review (top-10 residual walk, new / escalated / closed risks, control-effectiveness signals, emerging-risks watchlist) with dated minutes; and the annual audit-committee risk review that holds the *Marchand* conversation on mission-critical risks under NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.07(b)(iii)(D). The reference-framework vocabulary — **ISO 31000:2018** as the enterprise operating framework, **COSO ERM 2017** as the pre-IPO migration target, **COSO Internal Control 2013** for the ICFR / SOX-lite programme, and **NIST CSF 2.0** (with the new Govern function) as the cyber sub-framework — is picked piecewise so the programme is legible to auditors, insurers, and directors without the compliance apparatus of a Fortune-500.
2. **The startup insurance stack, by stage.** The stage-triggered assembly map — **General Liability** (occurrence-triggered, often bundled as a BOP, baseline at the first office / event / COI-requesting vendor) and statutory **Workers' Compensation** (first W-2 hire in every state that has a comp system) at pre-seed; **Technology Errors & Omissions + Cyber** (claims-made, bundled, first paying customer), **Employment Practices Liability Insurance** (claims-made, with the wage-and-hour sub-limit the broker can source), and **Directors & Officers** (claims-made, Sides A/B/C, first outside board seat) at seed; **Umbrella / Excess** and **Commercial Auto / Hired & Non-Owned** at Series A; **Fiduciary Liability** (first ERISA-covered plan — the 401(k)), **Crime / Fidelity** with an affirmative social-engineering endorsement, and **Employed Lawyers** at Series B; and the **R&W** / IPO-track D&O uplift / **Key-Person Life** at pre-IPO. Two first-principles disciplines sit underneath: the **occurrence-vs-claims-made distinction** (claims-made policies must be continuously renewed or an Extended Reporting Period tail purchased, or historical exposure goes uninsured), and the **application-answers-are-warranties** discipline (every material representation on the application — MFA on administrative access, wage-and-hour compliance, no known incidents, EDR on all endpoints — is a warranty the carrier reads at claims time, and misrepresentation even innocent can void coverage). The broker relationship, the carrier-selection discipline, the audit-committee reporting on the tower, and the full **D&O design detail** (Broad-Form vs. traditional, retention structure, priority-of-payments, Side-A DIC, the DGCL § 145 indemnification-agreement interplay) sit in [mod-111 chapter 06](../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md); this module records which line enters the stack at which stage, which warranties sit on the application, and why the two-policy seam between Crime and Cyber is where social-engineering losses fall.
3. **Cyber insurance and incident-response coordination.** The post-2020 cyber market shape — ransomware-driven hard market, control-requirement underwriting, tightened ransomware and funds-transfer-fraud sub-limits, rewritten war-and-cyber-war exclusions (Lloyd's Market Association LMA5564–LMA5567 state-backed-cyber-war clauses <!-- needs-research: verify current LMA state-backed-cyber-war clause revisions and any US-market ISO equivalents and cite. -->), and the systemic-cyber-event exclusion — and the policy anatomy that falls out of it: **first-party** coverage (breach-response costs, business interruption and contingent BI with the waiting-period and period-of-restoration mechanics, cyber extortion with the OFAC ransomware-payment analysis, data restoration, funds-transfer fraud, regulatory defence and fines where insurable) and **third-party** coverage (network-security liability, privacy liability, media liability for AI-generated content, PCI assessment defence). The incident-response operating layer is the point of the chapter — the carrier's 24×7 hotline, the panel of pre-approved breach counsel / forensics firms / notification vendors / ransomware negotiators, the coverage-under-reservation letter pattern, the privilege posture on the forensic report under *Capital One* and *Rutter's*, the notification-clock coordination with the GDPR Article 33 / HIPAA / 50-state / NYDFS Part 500 / SEC Item 1.05 regulatory stack owned in [mod-110 chapter 05](../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/05-breach-response-playbook.md), and the ransomware-decision tree against the OFAC October 2020 and September 2021 advisories on ransomware payments. The CISO and GC walk this with the carrier *before* an incident — the first phone call is not the first conversation.
4. **The sanctions and export-controls compliance programme.** The three-regulator landscape — **OFAC** (Treasury, 31 CFR Chapter V; country / regional programmes covering Iran / Cuba / North Korea / Syria / the occupied-Ukraine regions / Russia; list-based programmes on the SDN, Consolidated, SSI, NS-MBS, and CAPTA lists); **BIS** (Commerce, the EAR at 15 CFR Parts 730–774; the Commerce Control List with ECCN classification and the EAR99 residual, the Country Chart, and the License Exceptions at 15 CFR Part 740 including ENC for encryption items; the Entity List, Denied Persons List, Unverified List, Military End-User and Military-Intelligence End-User Lists; and the 2022–2024 advanced-computing and semiconductor controls targeting China <!-- needs-research: verify current version of 15 CFR § 744.23 and advanced-computing controls after the January 2025 IFR and any 2025–2026 amendments. -->); and **DDTC** (State, the ITAR at 22 CFR Parts 120–130; the U.S. Munitions List at § 121.1; registration under § 122.1 the moment the company is engaged in the business of defence-article export, regardless of whether a single export has occurred). The operating-programme pattern — a written policy signed by the CEO; a screening architecture that runs every customer, vendor, and foreign-national hire against the consolidated OFAC lists and the BIS Entity / Denied-Persons / Unverified Lists at a cadence; an EAR classification determination for every exportable product with the ECCN, the License Exception where used, and the annual self-classification report to BIS where required; a geo-blocking and IP-geolocation control that excludes signups from comprehensive-sanctions jurisdictions; a payment-processor-screening overlay; a deemed-export analysis for foreign-national hires under 15 CFR § 734.13(a)(2) (covered in detail in [mod-113 chapter 04](../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/04-us-immigration-programme.md)); a voluntary-self-disclosure playbook; and the record-retention discipline that lets the programme survive a BIS end-use check or an OFAC subpoena. Internal-programme failures are strict-liability civil penalties on the OFAC side and can reach denied-persons designation that ends the company's ability to export at all.

A fifth problem-space closes the loop:

5. **The ownership-boundary map.** The reference that decides — for any risk / insurance / compliance question — whether it belongs to mod-112, to a sibling module (mod-101 for the entity-level authorisations the insurance tower and compliance-programme policies run under; mod-103 for the employment-contract substrate the EPLI, workers-comp, and wage-and-hour posture rests on; mod-106 for the benefits / 401(k) substance the Fiduciary Liability line insures; mod-109 for the DPA / Security Addendum clauses the cyber-insurance tower underwrites against; mod-110 for the privacy-regulatory depth and the breach-notification clocks that drive the cyber IR coordination; mod-111 for the full D&O programme-design detail, the audit-committee charter role in risk oversight, and the *Caremark* doctrinal frame; mod-113 for the international privacy / FCPA-equivalent overlay — UK Bribery Act 2010, Canadian CFPOA, French Sapin II — and non-US export-control regimes such as EU 2021/821 and UK ECJU; mod-114 for the risk-adjacent systems-selection and vendor-procurement operating model), or to a non-role track (`security-learning` for the security-engineering substance underneath the cyber-insurance underwriting posture and the incident-response technical forensics; `head-of-ai-governance-learning` / `chief-ai-officer-learning` / `ai-risk-engineer-learning` for AI-model-risk substance underneath the AI line in the risk taxonomy; `startup-exit-curriculum` for the R&W-insurance mechanics and the insurance / compliance diligence workstreams at M&A or IPO).

Get this right and the corporation compounds — the quarterly executive risk review produces a dated minutes document the audit committee can read on an annual cadence that sits inside the *Caremark* oversight expectation; the insurance stack is assembled stage-by-stage against documented triggers with a broker who answers the hotline and defends every warranty on the application; the cyber policy has been walked with the carrier before the first ransomware event, the panel firms are on retainer, and the forensic-investigation privilege posture is pre-wired against discoverability; the OFAC-screening and BIS-classification programme runs daily on the customer base, the vendor base, and the foreign-national hire pool, and the voluntary-self-disclosure path is rehearsed; and the board annual-risk conversation, the carrier renewal, and a BIS end-use check can each be answered from the same record. Get it wrong and the corporation absorbs the predictable failure set — a *Marchand* complaint that alleges the board utterly failed to oversee a mission-critical risk because no risk register or cadence was ever stood up; a claims-made policy that lapsed between the loss-triggering act and the first-reported claim with no tail purchased; a cyber-application warranty on MFA that the forensic investigation surfaces as false on half the administrative accounts and voids coverage on an eight-figure incident; a ransomware payment made without the OFAC advisory check to a sanctioned-nexus negotiator; a self-serve signup that onboarded a paying customer in Iran for six months before a routine screening sweep caught it; an advanced-computing export under a licence exception that the end-use checks later determine did not apply; and an ITAR registration the company owed under § 122.1 but never filed because the programme-ownership question never got answered.
