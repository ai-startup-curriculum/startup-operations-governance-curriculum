# Exercise 02 — Startup insurance-stack assembly and application-warranty drill

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [02 — The startup insurance stack, by stage](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md) (with D&O-programme detail deferred to [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md) and cyber-insurance operational substance deferred to [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md))

## Problem statement

Pennyroyal Technologies Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a workflow-automation platform into mid-market and lower-enterprise financial-services, insurance-broker, and professional-services customers. The corporation is 215 FTE across a San Francisco headquarters, a New York sales office, and a Toronto engineering office opened eleven months ago; closed a $70M Series B thirteen months ago; books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions; and signed its first two paying customers four quarters after seed-close three years back. The current insurance footprint is the legacy of the seed and Series-A placements and has not been reassembled against the Series-B posture.

Pennyroyal's current stack is a digital-first-broker-placed bundle written three years ago at seed — General Liability + BOP, statutory workers-comp (California, New York, Washington, Massachusetts, Illinois, Texas, Georgia, Colorado, Oregon, and three further states added at Series A), Tech E&O + Cyber on a combined form with the carrier the digital-first broker places as its default primary, EPLI with a wage-and-hour sub-limit the broker has not re-benchmarked since issuance, D&O Side A/B/C with a primary and one modest excess layer added at Series A when the Series-A lead director took the seat, Umbrella at $5M, and Commercial Auto with a hired-and-non-owned endorsement added when a sales-team member's personal-vehicle use surfaced in a near-miss. There is **no** Fiduciary Liability policy in place even though the 401(k) plan went live nine months ago, **no** Crime / Fidelity policy even though a social-engineering near-miss in Q3 last year was caught by the controller after the wire was drafted but not released, and **no** Employed Lawyers policy even though the general counsel and two in-house counsel have been in seat for over a year. The incumbent broker has not raised these gaps on the last two renewals; the general counsel surfaced them herself when she pulled the full policy binder to answer a diligence question from the Series-B investor's operations team.

Three operating realities have forced the stack-reassembly question onto the general counsel's agenda. **(i)** The three-year-old Tech E&O + Cyber policy is up for its fourth renewal in eleven weeks. The CISO has flagged — in the quarterly risk review ([exercise 01](./exercise-01-erm-operating-framework-authoring.md) Part D) — that the control-attestation grid the cyber-renewal application will require is materially harder than the grid signed three years ago, and that at least two of the warranties on the expiring application (MFA coverage, EDR coverage) were defensible when signed but would require remediation and documented exception-handling to be defensible today ([chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) covers the controls crosswalk). **(ii)** The Series-B lead director has asked the audit-committee chair — through the governance subcommittee — whether the D&O programme is adequate against the growth-trajectory exposure and whether the Side-A DIC layer the audit-committee chair's prior public-company director experience makes her expect is in place (reference [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md) for the Side-A DIC substance; the Pennyroyal programme does not yet have one). **(iii)** The Series-B lead investor's operations team flagged the missing Fiduciary Liability, Crime / Fidelity, and Employed Lawyers lines in its post-close risk review and asked for a dated remediation plan.

The general counsel has twelve weeks — three weeks before the T-90 kickoff with the broker and the renewal clock in eleven weeks — to walk the audit committee through a reassembled stack, a disciplined application-warranty workflow, a broker RFP and selection memo, and a renewal cadence the executive team will actually run. She owns the end-to-end package; the CFO co-owns the premium-and-retention budget; the CISO owns the cyber-application-warranty inventory; the Chief People Officer owns the EPLI and wage-and-hour inputs; the COO owns the GL / BOP / Auto / workers-comp inputs. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Stage-triggered stack assembly and current-state gap analysis

Author `stack-assembly-and-gap-analysis.md` — the specification the audit committee will adopt as Pennyroyal's reassembled stack. Cover:

1. **The stage-by-stage assembly map, as-is vs. target.** The chapter 02 reference assembly — General Liability + BOP and Workers' Comp at pre-seed; Tech E&O + Cyber, EPLI, and D&O (Sides A/B/C) at seed; Umbrella / Excess and Commercial Auto / Hired-and-Non-Owned at Series A; Fiduciary Liability (on 401(k)), Crime / Fidelity with affirmative social-engineering endorsement, and Employed Lawyers at Series B; R&W and D&O IPO-track uplift and Key-Person Life at pre-IPO. Author the map as a table with columns for (line, stage trigger per chapter 02, Pennyroyal's current status, gap vs. target, and remediation owner). Mark the Fiduciary Liability, Crime / Fidelity, and Employed Lawyers lines as the three Series-B-stage gaps the audit committee's remediation plan will close.
2. **The occurrence-vs-claims-made discipline per line.** The chapter 02 first principle. Name per line whether the trigger is occurrence or claims-made: GL and BOP occurrence; workers-comp statutory per state; Tech E&O, Cyber, EPLI, D&O, Fiduciary, and Employed Lawyers claims-made; Crime / Fidelity typically discovery-based or occurrence depending on the form. Name the retroactive-date preservation discipline on every claims-made line Pennyroyal is renewing — the retroactive date on the current Tech E&O + Cyber form, the EPLI, the D&O; name the Extended Reporting Period / tail decision Pennyroyal would owe if a claims-made line were replaced by a different carrier at renewal (continuous renewal on the same tower or an ERP tail purchased from the expiring carrier; otherwise historical exposure goes uninsured).
3. **The three Series-B gap lines in detail.** For each — Fiduciary Liability, Crime / Fidelity, Employed Lawyers — name the specific trigger that fired at Pennyroyal (the 401(k) plan going live nine months ago; the social-engineering near-miss in Q3; the general counsel and two in-house counsel in seat); name the specific coverage the line responds to (ERISA fiduciary-duty claims the D&O programme excludes by design; employee dishonesty / funds-transfer fraud / computer fraud / affirmative social-engineering coverage; in-house-attorney professional liability); and name the retroactive-date / prior-acts coverage posture the broker should negotiate to preserve pre-bind exposure. The Crime / Cyber seam — the chapter 02 and chapter 03 recurrent discussion — is where social-engineering losses fall. Author the operative discipline: the affirmative social-engineering endorsement is placed on Crime with a meaningful sub-limit, vendor-impersonation and payroll-diversion patterns are named covered, and the parallel Cyber coverage for FTF / social-engineering is not relied on as primary.
4. **The umbrella / excess re-sizing.** The current $5M umbrella, placed at Series A, is three financings behind the current revenue base and the current customer-contract limit requirements. Name the current umbrella posture, the customer-contract-required limits across the top-10 customers (the GC runs the limit-pull against the MSA / Order Form from [mod-109 chapter 01](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/01-customer-contract-suite-msa-sla-dpa-security-aup.md)), and the recommended umbrella structure at Series-B scale. 5. **The workers-comp multi-state posture.** Pennyroyal operates in a dozen US states and in Ontario. Name the statutory-coverage posture per US state (workers-comp is statutory in every state except Texas where subscription is a default; Texas-non-subscriber posture is a documented decision, not a default) and the Ontario-equivalent (WSIB) posture under the chapter-02 international-insurance deferral to [mod-113](../../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/). The detailed multi-state classification-code and premium-audit discipline is deferred to the forthcoming chapter 07 of this module; flag the deferral with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` where the forthcoming-chapter substance would otherwise land.
6. **The D&O cross-reference discipline.** The D&O programme-design detail — Broad-Form vs. traditional, retention structure, priority-of-payments, Side-A DIC — lives in [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md). ### Part B — Application-assembly workflow and warranty-defensibility drill

Author `application-workflow-and-warranty-drill.md`. The chapter 02 discipline — application answers are warranties the insurer reads at claims time; misrepresentation, even innocent, can void coverage. Cover:

1. **The application-assembly workflow.** The named single owner per application (Head of Ops, GC, or CFO) who assembles inputs from each attesting function. For each line, name the attesting function and the specific inputs the application pulls — CISO for cyber-controls attestation (MFA, EDR, backup, IR plan, vendor-risk, patch cadence, training — chapter 03's security-controls crosswalk is the inventory); Chief People Officer for wage-and-hour and headcount and prior employment-practices claims; CFO for revenue, revenue-recognition method, financials, and prior loss history; GC for prior claims, pending litigation, regulator correspondence, and no-known-circumstances attestations. Author the workflow as a one-page swim-lane with named owner, named input source, and named signatory per line.
2. **The source-from-operating-reality rule.** The chapter 02 discipline — do not answer "MFA on all administrative access" if a break-glass account bypasses MFA; either fix the bypass before signing or answer accurately with the exception documented in the application supplement. Author the pre-sign reconciliation checklist each attesting function runs against operating reality before signature — the CISO's pre-sign MFA / EDR / backup / IR-plan verification pass; the Chief People Officer's wage-and-hour audit against the state-by-state posture ([mod-103](../../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/)); the CFO's revenue-recognition and prior-loss-history reconciliation; the GC's litigation-docket and regulator-correspondence sweep.
3. **The signatory-reads-the-application rule.** The CEO / CFO signatory reads the questions and the answers, not just the signature page. Author the pre-sign walk-through the signatory runs with the general counsel in the days before the renewal bind — the question-by-question read of the material representations, the discussion of any attestation where operating reality creates exposure, the explicit sign-off that each answer is accurate as of the attestation date.
4. **The application-supplement discipline.** The named discipline for documenting exceptions — the MFA-exception on a specific break-glass account; the EDR-exception on a specific class of legacy endpoint; the wage-and-hour-exception for a specific role class being remediated. The chapter 03 discipline: carriers understand exceptions and price for them; they do not tolerate discovery of undisclosed exceptions after loss. Author the exception-documentation template the CISO and the Chief People Officer fill before the CEO signs.
5. **The retention discipline.** The chapter 02 discipline — retain the application and every supporting attachment in the corporate insurance record for the full statute-of-limitations period on any coverage question. Name the retention owner (the GC's legal-ops team or the Head of Ops), the retention medium (the corporate secretary's insurance binder or a controlled drive), and the retention period (at minimum the statute-of-limitations period on the claims-made policy; practical guidance of at least seven years is defensible, with the specific figure carrying `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` against the applicable state's statute of limitations on insurance-contract claims).
6. **The warranty-defensibility pre-bind drill.** For each of the four most load-bearing attestations on the expiring Tech E&O + Cyber application — MFA on all administrative access, EDR on all endpoints / servers monitored 24×7, immutable tested backups with a documented RTO / RPO and annual restore test, and the no-known-incidents attestation — the CISO (or Chief People Officer where applicable) runs a pre-bind drill: produce the operating-reality evidence for the attestation, identify any exception, document the exception in the application supplement, and either remediate before bind or disclose and accept the underwriter's pricing response. Author the drill as a working document.

### Part C — Broker RFP and selection memo

Author `broker-rfp-and-selection-memo.md`. The chapter 02 discipline — a startup-focused broker understands the stage-by-stage assembly, has depth on Tech E&O / Cyber / D&O forms, and has claims-advocacy staff who represent the insured's interests when a claim goes into dispute. The incumbent digital-first broker is likely still a serviceable fit at the primary layers but may not have the depth the Series-B Side-A DIC and the Fiduciary / Crime / Employed Lawyers additions require. Cover:

1. **The selection criteria.** The named criteria the broker must satisfy — documented stage-appropriate placement track record for Tech E&O + Cyber, D&O, EPLI, and Fiduciary / Crime / Employed Lawyers; dedicated claims-advocacy practice distinct from the placement team; coverage-analysis capability producing a line-by-line coverage-comparison memo at renewal (not just quotes); market access to the full slate of primary, excess, and specialty-line carriers across the three gap lines; a named service team with defined response SLAs for notice-of-circumstance events and quarterly review cadence; demonstrated depth on the application-warranty-defensibility discipline (the broker that recommends writing a soft warranty is not the right broker). Reference broker candidates by role only — **incumbent digital-first broker**, **challenger broker A** (a second digital-first-or-hybrid competitor), **challenger broker B** (a full-service broker with public-company depth for the trajectory from Series B through pre-IPO).
2. **The two-broker interview discipline.** The chapter 02 discipline — interview at least two brokers for any placement over $50k in annual premium. Author the question set the general counsel and CFO will carry into each broker interview. At minimum: what is the carrier shortlist you propose for each gap line and why; what is your claims-advocacy posture on a Tech E&O / Cyber dispute at renewal and in-claim; what is the coverage-comparison memo you produce at renewal and can we see a redacted specimen; what is the quarterly-review cadence and the service-team composition; what is your recommendation on the Side-A DIC placement cross-referencing [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md).
3. **The split-placement discipline where justified.** The chapter 02 rule-of-thumb — at seed and Series A, a full-service digital-first broker is usually sufficient; by Series B or when the D&O programme becomes complex, a larger broker brings depth on public-company forms, R&W, and specialty markets. Pennyroyal is at the transition point. Author the posture the general counsel will adopt — retain the incumbent for the primary stack lines where the service has been acceptable, run a challenger broker on the Side-A DIC and the three gap lines, and re-evaluate the full stack at the following renewal.
4. **The ownership-boundary rule.** The chapter 02 discipline — the broker is the intermediary; the company (Head of Ops or GC + CFO combination) owns the risk-transfer strategy; never delegate the read of the policy to the broker alone. Author the specific hand-off and read-back discipline — the broker drafts the application and the coverage-comparison memo; the general counsel reads the policy specimen; the signatory reads and attests; the audit committee reviews the tower against the risk register at the annual review (reference [exercise 01](./exercise-01-erm-operating-framework-authoring.md) Part E for the annual audit-committee review mechanic).
5. **The selection memo.** Author the one-page memo the general counsel and CFO will circulate to the audit committee at the T-90 point, naming the broker(s) selected, the selection criteria that drove the pick, the service-team composition, the claims-advocacy named lead, and the fees / commission posture.

### Part D — 90-day renewal cadence and owner matrix

Author `90-day-renewal-cadence.md`. The chapter 02 discipline — a disciplined renewal cadence is the difference between a placed renewal and a scramble, and hard-market Cyber and D&O want 120 days or more. Cover:

1. **The cadence schedule.** The chapter 02 reference schedule — T-120 broker kickoff; T-90 application drafts to internal signatories, loss-run pulled, financials assembled; T-60 applications submitted, broker markets, underwriter Q&A begins; T-45 first indications, accept-negotiate-expand-markets decision; T-30 terms finalised, binding order to broker; T-15 policy documents received and read against binding order; T-0 renewal effective. Author the cadence as a calendar with named owners at each stage, mapped to the three gap lines being newly placed (Fiduciary, Crime, Employed Lawyers) as well as the renewing lines.
2. **The hard-market-earlier-start trigger.** The chapter 02 discipline — Cyber and D&O in a hard market should start at T-150 or earlier. The current cyber-market shape (reference [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) for the post-2020 hard-market shape with ransomware-driven control-requirement underwriting and tightened sub-limits) justifies starting the Cyber renewal at T-150. Name the trigger Pennyroyal will apply at every future renewal and the governance for an early-start decision (the CISO's recommendation, the GC's approval, the CFO's budget cover).
3. **The owner matrix.** For each cadence milestone, name the single owner (RACI-style). The GC is accountable for the overall placement; the CFO is accountable for the budget and the signatory attestation on financial lines; the CISO is accountable for the cyber-application-warranty inventory; the Chief People Officer is accountable for EPLI; the broker is responsible for market-side execution; the audit committee is consulted at T-30 for lines above a defined premium threshold and is informed at T-0.
4. **The audit-committee gating at T-30.** The chapter 02 reference — the policy must be read against the binding order for material differences. Author the audit-committee or governance-subcommittee review point at T-14 — the GC presents the coverage-comparison memo, the tower structure, the premium-and-retention posture against budget, and any warranty on the application the signatory is uncomfortable attesting to; the committee ratifies the bind or asks for amendment.
5. **The 15-day read discipline.** The chapter 02 discipline — policy documents received at T-15 are read against the binding order for any material deviation before bind. Name the owner (GC with outside coverage counsel for the D&O and Side-A DIC lines; GC solo on the primary lines), the read-against artefact (the binding order plus the coverage-comparison memo from Part C), and the escalation path when a deviation is found (back to the broker; back to the underwriter if material; delay bind if unresolved).
