# Exercise 01 — Enterprise-risk-management operating-framework authoring

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — The enterprise-risk-management operating framework](../01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md) (with insurance-stack cross-reference to [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md) and the *Marchand* doctrinal setting in [mod-111 chapter 05](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md))

## Problem statement

Harbourline AI Systems Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling an AI-infrastructure inference-fabric product into mid-market and enterprise customers in regulated verticals (healthcare payer, regional bank, and federal-civilian systems-integrator customers). The corporation is 230 FTE, closed a $95M Series B nine months ago, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, runs its production inference-fabric out of two US regions and a Frankfurt region stood up two quarters ago to serve two German customers, and has four foundation-model providers wired as upstream dependencies across the serving stack. The board is now seven seats: the founder-CEO, three investor-designated directors (Seed, Series A, Series B), and three independent directors seated against the audit / compensation / governance committee charters stood up at the Series-B financing. The audit committee was constituted at the Series B and its charter under [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md) names risk-oversight as a standing responsibility.

Harbourline's current risk-management posture is the artefact of the pre-Series-B period. The CISO runs a security-risk register in a Notion database; the GC keeps a compliance-risk list in a spreadsheet the general counsel updates before board meetings; the CFO maintains a treasury-and-runway watchlist in the weekly finance review; the Head of AI Safety is standing up a model-risk register against the governance substrate the `head-of-ai-governance-learning` curriculum describes; the Chief People Officer tracks a key-person and harassment-complaint list in the HRIS. None of the five lists use the same taxonomy, none sits in a single tool of record, none produces a residual-score that is comparable across functions, and the board has never seen an integrated top-ten risk view — the quarterly board pre-read includes each function's narrative update but no cross-function residual walk.

Three operating realities have forced the ERM-framework question onto the general counsel's agenda. **(i)** The new audit-committee chair, a public-company former CFO, routed a memo at her first committee meeting asking for "the risk register, the risk-appetite statement the board has adopted, the quarterly cadence under which the executive team reviews residual risk, and the annual framework the audit committee will use to hold the *Marchand* conversation on mission-critical risks." None of those artefacts exists in canonical form. **(ii)** The Series-B lead investor's portfolio-operations team ran a routine risk-review two months ago and asked, as a diligence-equivalent follow-up, for "the enterprise risk taxonomy, the owner per category, and the escalation protocol when a residual exceeds appetite." **(iii)** The two German customers' vendor-risk teams each sent a 60-item risk-management questionnaire attached to the renewal. The questionnaires ask for the ISO 31000 or COSO ERM 2017 reference frame, the named reporting line from the risk function to the board, the risk-appetite statement's version and adoption date, and a specimen quarterly risk-review minutes document.

The general counsel has eight weeks to walk into the next audit-committee meeting with an enterprise-risk-management operating framework the corporation will actually run. She owns the end-to-end package; the CFO co-owns the appetite statement and the quantitative anchors; the CISO, Head of AI Safety, Chief People Officer, and CTO each own a category of the taxonomy and the residual-score inputs for their category's rows on the register; the audit-committee chair approves the framework at the meeting. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Risk taxonomy and ownership matrix

Author `risk-taxonomy-and-ownership-matrix.md` — the taxonomy the audit committee will adopt and the ownership map every risk-register row will read from. Cover:

1. **The fixed taxonomy.** Adopt a nine- to eleven-category taxonomy using the chapter 01 reference list — strategic, operational, financial, compliance and regulatory, reputational, cybersecurity, AI / model risk, geopolitical, people and culture, product / safety and physical risk. The exact category list is less important than fixing it; name any deviation from the chapter 01 reference and defend the deviation against Harbourline's business model and footprint.
2. **The named owner per category.** For each category, name the single executive-team role that owns category-level residual (CEO for strategic; COO or Head of Operations for operational; CFO for financial; GC / CCO for compliance; executive team collectively with Head of Communications as lead for reputational; CISO for cybersecurity; Head of AI Safety for AI / model risk; executive team collectively with GC for geopolitical; Chief People Officer for people and culture; Head of Product or VP Engineering for product / safety). Name the deputy who runs the register for the category when the executive-team owner is unavailable.
3. **The dual-category disallow rule.** The chapter 01 discipline — every risk on the register maps to exactly one category. Author the tie-breaking rule the DPO-equivalent will apply when a risk could plausibly sit in two categories (a ransomware event is cybersecurity, not compliance, even though its downstream notification obligations are compliance; an OFAC-exposure incident is compliance, not cybersecurity; an AI-model safety failure is AI / model risk, not product, even where the failure manifests in a customer-facing feature). Name three worked examples the executive team will use to calibrate the tie-break.
4. **The reporting-line map.** The named path each category owner uses to escalate residual change — into the quarterly executive risk review (Part D), into the audit-committee pack (Part E), and into off-cycle board notification when a Sev-0 risk event materialises. The AI / model risk reporting line specifically connects to the AI-risk sub-committee stood up in [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md) where that committee exists; name the interface.

### Part B — Risk register (schema + top-10 specimen)

Author `risk-register.md`. The register is the artefact every downstream review reads from. Cover:

1. **The schema in operational form.** Author the register's schema as a table definition with one column per chapter 01 field — ID, statement (actor-verb-object), category, owner (role plus named deputy), inherent likelihood (1–5), inherent impact (1–5), controls (terse, referenced to the control library), residual likelihood, residual impact, trend (↑ / → / ↓ vs. last review), review cadence (quarterly default; monthly for top-N and mission-critical), last-reviewed date, and notes / open actions. Adopt one scale explicitly — 1–5 or Low / Medium / High / Very High — and defend the pick.
2. **The single-tool-of-record discipline.** Name the tool Harbourline will adopt (Airtable, Notion database, Google Sheets, or a GRC platform — LogicGate, Onspring, AuditBoard, Hyperproof, Drata's risk module) and defend the pick against Harbourline's current Series-B stage, headcount, and the chapter 01 anti-pattern (a register touched only the week before the quarterly review). Name the off-platform-copy prohibition and the discovery mechanism the DPO-equivalent runs quarterly to confirm no shadow copies exist.
3. **The top-10 specimen register.** Author the top-10 residual-sorted register for Harbourline at its current stage. The chapter 01 concrete example (R-2026-001 through R-2026-010) is a reference, not a template — Harbourline's top-10 must reflect its own posture: the AI-inference-fabric business model, the regulated-vertical customer concentration, the two-US-region plus Frankfurt-region footprint, the four foundation-model upstream dependencies, the 230-FTE scale, and the German customer base. At minimum the top-10 must cover a strategic risk (foundation-model deprecation or hyperscaler bundling), an operational risk (upstream foundation-model-provider outage, key-engineer concentration), a financial risk (runway, revenue concentration), a compliance risk (GDPR / HIPAA / US-state privacy — [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) — or sanctions / export-controls — [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)), a cybersecurity risk (ransomware, FTF, insider misuse — [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)), an AI / model risk (prompt injection with cross-tenant exfiltration, hallucination in a high-stakes clinical or financial decision, model-safety-eval regression), a reputational risk, a geopolitical risk (export-control tightening on advanced-compute end-users; see [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)), and a people risk (key-person resignation, harassment / discrimination claim).
4. **Residual-score commentary per row.** For each row, a one-paragraph commentary naming the inherent-score reasoning, the controls currently in place (referenced by name to the control library, not re-authored), the residual score, the trend, and any open action with an owner and a due date. The commentary is what makes the register readable to a director who is not fluent in the operating detail.
5. **The register-refresh cadence.** The chapter 01 discipline — a living register refreshed continuously as new information arrives, with the quarterly review as a checkpoint on a running artefact, not a from-scratch assessment. Name the owner (the DPO-equivalent or the Chief Compliance Officer, reporting through the GC) and the specific triggers that refresh a row off-cycle (a near-miss event, a regulator inquiry, a customer-reported incident, a control-attestation delta from SOC 2 or ISO 27001 — see [mod-110 chapter 05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/05-soc-2-audit-readiness-programme.md)).

### Part C — Risk appetite statement and quantitative anchors

Author `risk-appetite-statement.md`. The appetite statement is the board's. It is short (one to three pages), qualitative-plus-limited-quantitative, and the audit committee re-affirms it annually. Cover:

1. **The overall risk posture.** A single paragraph that names Harbourline's posture against the chapter 01 reference — a growth-stage company that accepts meaningful strategic and product-execution risk to pursue category leadership and holds conservative appetite for compliance, safety, and financial-control risk that would compromise its ability to operate or fund. Defend the posture against Harbourline's specific business model, customer base, and runway position.
2. **The category-by-category appetite.** One paragraph per taxonomy category from Part A, naming the appetite level (high / moderate / low / very low) and the operating implication. Strategic high; compliance low; AI / model risk context-dependent — low for high-stakes clinical and financial deployments, moderate for low-stakes internal-tooling deployments; cybersecurity low; financial low outside runway trade-offs. The appetite statement is not a restatement of the taxonomy — it is a statement of **how much** risk the board will accept in each category.
3. **The bright-line prohibitions.** The chapter 01 zero-tolerance list. At minimum: no knowing violation of law; no shipping a model that fails its documented safety evaluation; no customer contract that indemnifies for unbounded consequential damages (reference the [mod-109 chapter 02](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/02-contract-playbook-and-fallback-position-matrix.md) fallback-matrix cap posture); no transaction with an OFAC-blocked party (reference [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)); no payment of a sanctioned-nexus ransomware demand without the OFAC analysis (reference [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)); no customer deployment into a comprehensive-sanctions jurisdiction (Iran, Cuba, North Korea, Syria, Crimea / DNR / LNR / Kherson / Zaporizhzhia — reference [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)). Author the prohibition list as operative language the audit committee will adopt.
4. **The quantitative anchors.** Five to six numbers, no more. The chapter 01 reference set — runway floor; single-customer-concentration ceiling (ARR share and headcount share); cyber-incident severity thresholds requiring board notification; maximum uninsured retention on any single insurance line; a foundation-model-provider concentration ceiling (percent of inference traffic through any one upstream provider). Each specific number carries `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` because the right anchor depends on Harbourline's own runway, revenue mix, cyber-tower limits (reference [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md)), and the board's appetite — the exercise names the anchors; the CFO and audit committee finalise the figures at adoption.
5. **The adoption and re-affirmation cadence.** The board resolution that adopts the appetite statement on management's recommendation; the audit-committee annual re-affirmation under NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.07(b)(iii)(D); the minutes-documentation discipline that makes the adoption available as a legal defence later (reference [mod-111 chapter 03](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/03-corporate-secretary-function.md) for the minutes discipline).

### Part D — Quarterly executive risk review (agenda + minutes template)

Author `quarterly-exec-risk-review.md`. The quarterly executive risk review is a 60–90-minute working meeting with agenda, owners, and dated minutes that feeds the annual audit-committee review. Cover:

1. **The attendee list and facilitation.** Name the attendees — CEO, COO, CFO, CTO, GC / CCO, CISO, Chief People Officer, Head of AI Safety where the role exists — the facilitator (GC or CCO), and the register owner who drives the material. Name the quorum discipline when a category owner is unavailable (the deputy from Part A attends; the owner reads the minutes).
2. **The 60-minute agenda.** The chapter 01 reference agenda — (5 min) prior-quarter actions closed with past-due items called out by name; (15 min) top-10 residual walk sorted by residual score, with trend and control status; (10 min) new and escalated risks tied to the event that surfaced them; (10 min) closed and retired risks; (10 min) control-effectiveness signals (SOC 2 findings, security-questionnaire delta, EPLI claims, near-miss reports — see [mod-110 chapter 05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/05-soc-2-audit-readiness-programme.md) and [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md)); (10 min) emerging-risks watchlist. Author the agenda as a working document the GC can hand to the facilitator.
3. **The minutes template.** The named sections — attendance; decisions taken; actions assigned with owners and dated due dates; items to escalate to the audit committee; appetite-exceedances flagged for board notification. Author the minutes template as a one-page form. The chapter 01 discipline: the minutes go into the corporate record.
4. **The off-cycle trigger discipline.** The named events that convene an off-cycle executive risk review — a Sev-0 incident, a material-change in runway, a regulator-inquiry receipt, a ransomware event (reference [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)), an OFAC-list hit on a customer or vendor (reference [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)), a foundation-model-provider outage affecting production inference. Name the owner of the trigger decision and the notification path to the audit-committee chair.
5. **The risk-register-and-minutes retention discipline.** The dated-minutes retention in the corporate record (reference [mod-111 chapter 03](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/03-corporate-secretary-function.md) for the corporate-secretary minutes retention mechanic) and the register-version retention. The *Marchand* defence turns on dated minutes and a living register; both must be preserved for the statute-of-limitations window on any claim.

### Part E — Annual audit-committee risk review (pack + Marchand deep-dive)

Author `annual-audit-committee-risk-review.md`. The annual audit-committee risk review is the moment the board holds the *Marchand* conversation on mission-critical risks. Cover:

1. **The pack structure.** The chapter 01 reference structure — (10 min) framework-and-methodology recap; (20 min) register in summary form (heat map by category, top-10 residual list, year-over-year trend); (15 min) mission-critical risks in detail; (15 min) appetite-statement review and re-adoption; (10 min) insurance and risk-transfer posture mapped against residual (reference [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md) and the D&O-tower cross-reference to [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md)); (10 min) executive session. Author the pack as a slide deck specification — slide titles, slide content bullets, slide owner, and the one-page summary the full board receives from the audit committee.
2. **The Marchand mission-critical risks deep-dive.** The chapter 01 framing — for the two or three risks that would materially threaten the company, name the reporting system that surfaces the risk, name what management is doing about it, and name what the audit committee needs to see quarterly. Reference *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019), and the *Caremark* line in [mod-111 chapter 05](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md); the deep-dive is the direct answer to a plaintiff who alleges the board utterly failed to oversee a mission-critical risk.
3. **The mission-critical-risk shortlist.** Name the two or three risks Harbourline's audit committee will treat as mission-critical at the current stage. Candidates include: the AI-model-safety-failure-in-a-high-stakes-deployment risk (the AI line in the register); the cross-tenant-data-exfiltration risk (the cybersecurity line); the HIPAA-or-GLBA-breach-notification-clock risk tied to a cybersecurity event (the compliance line, running through [mod-110 chapter 04](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md) and [chapter 07](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/07-incident-response-and-breach-notification-playbook.md) and coordinated with [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) on the cyber-insurance IR side); and the foundation-model-provider-outage risk (the operational line). The general counsel defends the shortlist against the register's residual-score walk.
4. **The appetite-statement re-adoption discipline.** The annual re-affirmation at the audit committee, with any amendments proposed by management and approved by the committee. Any risk currently exceeding appetite is a named line item with the committee's disposition — tolerate under a dated remediation plan, drive to within appetite by a dated action, or formally expand appetite with a documented rationale.
5. **The insurance-and-risk-transfer posture slide.** The one-slide summary showing the current stack against residual — General Liability / Workers' Comp baseline (reference [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md)); Tech E&O + Cyber with the sub-limit watch (reference [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) for the ransomware / FTF / social-engineering / war-and-cyber-war exclusion-watch); EPLI; D&O Sides A/B/C with the Side-A DIC deferred to [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md); Umbrella; Fiduciary Liability on the 401(k) (reference [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/)); Crime / Fidelity with the affirmative social-engineering endorsement. The slide names which residuals are risk-transferred, which are retained, and which are mitigated through controls rather than insurance.
6. **The executive-session discipline.** The audit committee meets without management for the last ten minutes. Name the questions the committee chair prepares in advance — any risk the chair has heard about from a third-party source that is not on the register; any appetite-exceedance management has not flagged; any control-effectiveness signal that is contradicted by an outside source (a SOC 2 opinion letter, a security-questionnaire failure, an insurance-renewal declination).

### Part F — Framework-selection memo (ISO 31000 + COSO + NIST)

Author `framework-selection-memo.md`. The reference-framework vocabulary is the thing that makes Harbourline's ERM legible to auditors, insurers, and directors who came from larger companies. Cover:

1. **The three reference frameworks.** Name each — ISO 31000:2018 as a principles-and-process framework; COSO ERM 2017 as the five-component framework (governance and culture, strategy and objective-setting, performance, review and revision, information communication and reporting) with its twenty supporting principles; NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2 paired with NIST CSF 2.0 (identify, protect, detect, respond, recover, plus the govern function added in CSF 2.0). Cite each framework's publishing body and current revision explicitly.
2. **The recommended pattern.** The chapter 01 reference pattern for a Series-B startup — **ISO 31000 as the enterprise-wide operating framework**, **COSO 2013 for the ICFR / SOX-lite programme** when that programme is stood up pre-IPO, and **NIST CSF 2.0 as the cyber sub-framework** (tying into [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) and [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)). Name Harbourline's pick and defend it against the current stage.
3. **The COSO ERM 2017 migration trigger.** The chapter 01 reference — migrate to COSO ERM 2017 as the enterprise framework 12 to 18 months before the IPO decision. Name the trigger Harbourline will use (the first Series-D board meeting, the engagement of an IPO-track audit firm, or a dated pre-IPO governance-readiness decision from [mod-111 chapter 09](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/09-ipo-readiness-and-transition-to-public-governance.md)).
4. **The legibility discipline.** The framework pick is not about adopting any of the three wholesale. It is about using their vocabulary so an auditor, an insurer underwriter, or a new independent director can read Harbourline's register, appetite statement, and review cadence in terms they already know. Name the specific vocabulary Harbourline borrows from each — ISO 31000's "risk" definition ("effect of uncertainty on objectives") and process stages; COSO 2013's control-environment / risk-assessment / control-activities / information-and-communication / monitoring; NIST CSF 2.0's functions as the cyber-sub-register's organising structure.
