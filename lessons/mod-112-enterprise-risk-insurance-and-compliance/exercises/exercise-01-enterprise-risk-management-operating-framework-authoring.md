# Exercise 01 — Enterprise-risk-management operating-framework authoring

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [01 — The enterprise-risk-management operating framework](../01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md)

## Problem statement

Halcyon Synthesis Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling an AI-infrastructure orchestration platform (model-serving, fine-tuning, and evaluation pipelines) into regulated-industry customers — a 14-hospital US health system, two mid-market US banks, a Texas-based energy trading firm, and three EU design partners (one Dublin-headquartered private-banking group, one Frankfurt-headquartered insurer, one Amsterdam-headquartered pharma analytics vendor). The corporation is 240 FTE, closed a $90M Series B nine months ago led by a growth fund with a healthcare and infrastructure thesis, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, is actively operating a Dublin engineering office opened five months ago (18 FTE), and is on an eighteen-to-twenty-four-month pre-IPO trajectory with the first underwriter conversations scheduled for the next quarter.

The risk footprint is already heavy and uncoordinated. Security runs its own top-five list; legal runs its own litigation docket; finance runs its own counterparty-risk and runway-risk view; the head of AI safety runs an evaluations-and-red-team backlog; the people function runs its own harassment / discrimination / retention watchlist; the head of infrastructure runs an SRE incident-review log. None of the six lists speak to each other, and none of them has reached the board as a single artefact. The audit committee's most recent minutes record a request from the independent chair for "an integrated risk view" the committee can read at its annual-risk-review meeting; the GC has eight weeks to deliver one.

Three operating realities have forced the ERM-operating-framework question onto the GC's agenda. **(i)** The investor-designated directors' pre-IPO readiness memo flagged the absence of a documented enterprise-risk-management programme as a *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019), exposure that an S-1 risk-factor section will have to address if it is not remediated. **(ii)** A ransomware near-miss last quarter (phishing → credential theft → lateral movement caught before encryption) surfaced the fact that the executive team has no shared view of how the cyber risk would score against financial, compliance, reputational, or operational risks on the same page. **(iii)** The chief AI officer has flagged, in a one-on-one with the CEO, that the AI line (prompt-injection, cross-tenant leakage, hallucinated outputs in high-stakes decisions, model drift, training-data provenance) is not on any current risk register and that the Head of AI Safety role does not report into a defined ERM cadence.

The GC owns the end-to-end ERM-framework package; the CFO co-owns the financial and runway anchors in the appetite statement; the CISO owns the cyber sub-framework; the chief AI officer owns the AI line; the Head of People owns the people-and-culture line; the COO owns the operational line; the corporate secretary owns the minutes and the audit-committee reporting surface. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Risk taxonomy and ownership map

Author `risk-taxonomy-and-ownership-map.md` — the single-page reference the executive team will read from at every quarterly review. Cover:

1. **The fixed taxonomy.** Adopt a nine-to-eleven-category taxonomy covering **strategic**, **operational**, **financial**, **compliance and regulatory**, **reputational**, **cybersecurity**, **AI / model risk**, **geopolitical**, **people and culture**, and **product / safety and physical risk**. For each category, name the one-sentence scope, the named executive owner by role (CEO, COO, CFO, GC / CCO, Head of Communications, CISO, Head of AI Safety / Chief AI Officer, Chief People Officer, Head of Product), and the sibling module or non-role curriculum that owns the substantive methodology behind the category (e.g., [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/) for privacy law, [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) for cyber IR, [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md) for sanctions, `head-of-ai-governance-learning` for AI-model-risk methodology).
2. **The one-category-per-risk discipline.** Name the rule that every risk in the register maps to exactly one category — not two — so ownership is unambiguous. Name the escalation path when a candidate risk straddles categories (the GC adjudicates the category; the trend column records the secondary-category effect).
3. **The named owners are individuals in role, not functions.** Name the convention that owner cells carry "CISO — [Name]" rather than "Security team," and the escalation path when an owner departs or a role is vacant (interim owner named by the CEO; audit-committee notification if the vacancy exceeds thirty days on a top-ten risk).
4. **Boundary with sibling modules.** Name the deferrals explicitly — the privacy regulatory depth lives in [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/); the sanctions and export-controls programme substance lives in [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md); the fiduciary-duty doctrinal frame lives in [mod-111 chapter 05](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md); the D&O programme-design detail lives in [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md); the security-engineering substance lives in the `security-learning` curriculum; the AI-model-risk methodology lives in `head-of-ai-governance-learning`, `chief-ai-officer-learning`, and `ai-risk-engineer-learning`. The taxonomy references these owners; it does not re-author them.

### Part B — Risk register schema and tool-of-record discipline

Author `risk-register-schema.md` — the single-tool-of-record artefact that every category owner reads and writes from. Cover:

1. **The fixed row schema.** Name the required columns at a minimum: **ID** (stable identifier, e.g., `R-2026-014`); **statement** (one sentence, actor-verb-object form — "A ransomware attack encrypts the production Postgres cluster and demands payment," not "Cyber risk"); **category**; **owner** (role plus named individual); **inherent likelihood** (1–5 or Low / Medium / High / Very High, assessed before controls); **inherent impact** (same scale); **controls** (terse; references the control library); **residual likelihood** (after controls); **residual impact** (after controls); **trend** (↑ / → / ↓ vs. last review); **review cadence** (quarterly default; more frequent for top-N and mission-critical); **last reviewed** (date); **notes / open actions** (any control gap under remediation with an owner and a due date). Pick one scoring scale (1–5 or L/M/H/VH) and apply it consistently.
2. **The single-tool-of-record discipline.** Name the operating rule that no risk lives in a Slack channel, a one-off deck, or a private spreadsheet — if it is not in the register, it is not a risk the corporation is tracking. Name the tool candidates (Airtable, Notion, Google Sheets, a GRC platform — LogicGate, Onspring, AuditBoard, Hyperproof, Drata's risk module) with `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current feature parity and pricing against Halcyon's Series-B stage. Do not fabricate a pricing number or a feature claim the GC has not verified against current vendor documentation.
3. **The continuously-refreshed vs. quarterly-reconstructed anti-pattern.** Name the anti-pattern explicitly — a register that is only touched the week before the quarterly review, in which the previous quarter's rows are unchanged and the executive team is doing risk assessment from cold memory in a two-hour meeting. Name the operating rule that the register is refreshed continuously as new information arrives (a near-miss, a regulator inquiry, a customer security-questionnaire delta, an evaluations-suite failure); the quarterly review is a checkpoint on a running artefact, not a from-scratch assessment.
4. **The retire-deliberately discipline.** Name the rule that a risk is retired by positive decision — the owner proposes retirement in the pre-read for the quarterly review, the executive team confirms, and the register records the retirement date and reason. Risks are not retired by attrition. The retired-risk list remains in the register (filtered-out by default) so a *Marchand* plaintiff who later alleges "the board stopped tracking this risk" is answered with the dated retirement entry.
5. **Change-control discipline.** Name the gate for category reassignment or scope change — who proposes (the owner), who approves (the GC adjudicates; the executive team is notified), the audit trail (prior category, new category, date, reason), the downstream-system re-propagation path (any dashboard that filters by category refreshes; the audit-committee pack picks up the reassignment at the next quarterly review).
6. **Example rows.** Author at least eight example rows that span the taxonomy from Part A — including a ransomware risk, a prompt-injection / cross-tenant-leakage risk, a HIPAA-breach-notification-clock risk, an OFAC-self-serve-signup-exposure risk, a key-person-concentration risk (the 10-%-of-critical-infra-knowledge engineer scenario), a runway-compression risk, a foundation-model-provider-deprecation risk, and an export-controls-tightening risk on advanced computing. Every row carries its category, its named owner by role, a residual (L × I) score, and a trend.

### Part C — Risk appetite statement and quantitative anchors

Author `risk-appetite-statement.md` — the one-to-three-page statement the board will adopt on management's recommendation and re-affirm annually at the audit-committee risk review. Cover:

1. **The appetite-vs-tolerance distinction.** Name the two distinct concepts the appetite statement rests on — **risk appetite** is the amount and type of risk the organisation is willing to accept in pursuit of its strategy (board-set on management's recommendation; qualitative-plus-limited-quantitative; not a stochastic bank-capital limit); **risk tolerance** is the acceptable variation around a specific objective or metric (operationalises appetite at the metric level — "cash runway must remain above eighteen months at all times" or "no single customer contract may exceed 15 % of ARR"). Name why conflating the two produces an appetite statement that is either unenforceable (no metric) or over-quantified (every category has a dollar number).
2. **The overall posture paragraph.** Author the one-paragraph statement of overall posture — Halcyon is a growth-stage AI-infrastructure corporation; it accepts meaningful strategic and product-execution risk to pursue category leadership and will tolerate controlled experimentation on new regulated-industry verticals; it holds conservative appetite for compliance, safety, cyber, and financial-control risk that would compromise the ability to operate, fund, or meet statutory and contractual obligations.
3. **The category-by-category appetite paragraphs.** For each category in Part A's taxonomy, author a one-paragraph statement of how much risk the corporation will accept — strategic → high; operational → moderate with named operational-resilience floors; financial → low outside the runway-trade-off decisions explicitly authorised by the board; compliance → low; reputational → low; cybersecurity → low with named control floors (MFA on all administrative access, EDR on 100 % of endpoints, immutable backups, documented and tested IR plan — the same list the cyber-insurance application in [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) warrants); AI → context-dependent and low for high-stakes deployments (healthcare, financial-services, employment-related decisions); geopolitical → low on sanctioned-jurisdiction exposure and export-controls-tightening scenarios; people → low on harassment / discrimination / wage-and-hour exposure; product / safety → very low.
4. **The bright-line prohibitions.** Name the acts the corporation will never take regardless of upside: knowingly violate law; ship a model that fails safety evaluation at the agreed-to threshold; sign a customer contract that indemnifies for unbounded consequential damages; accept payment from an OFAC-blocked party; onboard an employee or vendor without the Part A sanctions-screening pass; pay a ransom to a sanctioned-nexus negotiator or an OFAC-blocked ransomware operator without the Part C ransomware decision tree from [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md); operate a production inference pipeline for a high-stakes customer use case without a documented human-in-the-loop posture. Bright-line prohibitions are zero-tolerance; they are not subject to balancing.
5. **The five-to-six quantitative anchors.** Name the quantitative anchors the appetite statement will defend — the runway floor, the single-customer-concentration ceiling, the cyber-incident-severity threshold that triggers board notification, the maximum uninsured retention on any single insurance line, the AI-safety-evaluation pass threshold below which no production deployment occurs, the SLA-credit-exposure ceiling on any single customer contract. Keep the list to five or six anchors; more is theatre. Every dollar figure or percentage carries `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` — the runway-floor number, the concentration ceiling, the retention ceiling, and the SLA-credit ceiling are management-recommended-and-board-approved against the current operating plan and insurance-stack economics; do not fabricate a number.
6. **The adoption and re-affirmation cadence.** Name the operating rule — the board **adopts** the appetite statement on management's recommendation at a named meeting; the audit committee **re-affirms** it annually at the audit-committee risk-review meeting; the corporate secretary records the adoption and every re-affirmation in the minutes so the appetite statement is available as a legal defence later.

### Part D — Quarterly executive risk-review operating pattern

Author `quarterly-executive-risk-review-operating-pattern.md` — the 60-to-90-minute working meeting the executive team runs on a quarterly cadence. Cover:

1. **The cadence and attendees.** Name the quarterly cadence; the 60-to-90-minute duration; the standing attendees (CEO, COO, CFO, CTO, GC / CCO, CISO, Chief People Officer, Chief AI Officer, Head of Communications — scaled to Halcyon's current executive team); the GC / CCO as facilitator; the register owner (typically the GC's chief of staff or a named programme manager) driving the material.
2. **The 60-minute agenda.** Author the agenda with named timeboxes: **(5 min) Prior-quarter actions closed** — rapid roll-up of open remediation items; anything past due called out by name. **(15 min) Top-10 residual risks** — the register sorted by residual score, walked one at a time; for each, owner / current control status / trend / changes since last review. **(10 min) New and escalated risks** — risks added this quarter or that moved category or severity, tied to the event that surfaced them where possible. **(10 min) Closed and retired risks** — risks retired by positive decision, not attrition, consistent with Part B. **(10 min) Control-effectiveness signals** — SOC 2 findings, security-questionnaire delta, EPLI claims, near-miss reports, evaluations-suite failures, DPIA residual-risk changes. **(10 min) Emerging-risks watchlist** — risks not yet on the register but to be tracked (new regulator action, market movement, geopolitical event, foundation-model release).
3. **The minutes discipline.** Name the output — a short minutes document capturing decisions taken, actions assigned with owners and dates, and any items to escalate to the audit committee at the next meeting. The minutes go into the corporate record; the corporate secretary files them on a cadence consistent with [mod-111 chapter 03](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/03-corporate-secretary-function.md). Decisions-and-actions discipline — every action has an owner, a due date, and a success criterion.
4. **The privilege posture.** Name the operating posture — the quarterly review is a working meeting of the executive team and is not inherently privileged; where a specific risk requires privileged legal analysis (threatened litigation, a Wells-notice-risk analysis, a regulator-inquiry response), the GC routes that analysis through a separate privileged workstream rather than embedding it in the risk-review minutes. The corporate secretary and the GC co-own the sequencing so that the risk-review minutes are defensible as a *Caremark*-compliance record without disclosing privileged material.
5. **Escalation into the audit-committee cycle.** Name the mechanic for pulling items out of the quarterly review into the audit-committee pack — any risk with residual impact 5 or trend ↑ is a candidate for audit-committee attention; the GC reconciles the quarterly-review minutes against the audit-committee agenda at the quarterly committee-materials-prep deadline, consistent with [mod-111 chapter 02](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/02-board-cadence-and-materials.md).

### Part E — Annual audit-committee risk-review pack

Author `annual-audit-committee-risk-review-pack.md` — the 60-to-90-minute discrete agenda item at the audit-committee meeting, consistent with NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.07(b)(iii)(D) on audit-committee risk-assessment and risk-management oversight. Cover:

1. **The regulatory anchor.** Name the NYSE Listed Company Manual § 303A.07(b)(iii)(D) requirement that the audit committee discuss policies with respect to risk assessment and risk management; name the Nasdaq Rule 5605(c) comparator for a dual-listing-capable readiness posture. Name *Caremark International Inc. Derivative Litigation*, 698 A.2d 959 (Del. Ch. 1996), and *Marchand v. Barnhill*, 212 A.3d 805 (Del. 2019), as the doctrinal setting the audit-committee risk review sits inside; defer the full fiduciary-duty substance to [mod-111 chapter 05](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/05-fiduciary-duties-of-directors.md).
2. **The structure of the 60-to-90-minute agenda.** Author the agenda with named timeboxes: **(10 min) Framework and methodology recap** — taxonomy, scoring, cadence, repeated annually because directors rotate and refreshers matter. **(20 min) The register in summary form** — heat map by category; top-10 residual list; year-over-year trend. **(15 min) Mission-critical risks in detail** — the two or three risks that would materially threaten the corporation, with the *Marchand* conversation (what is the reporting system for this risk, what is management doing about it, what does the audit committee need to see quarterly). **(15 min) Appetite-statement review** — re-adopt the appetite statement as-is or amended; discuss any risks currently exceeding appetite and management's plan. **(10 min) Insurance and risk-transfer posture** — a summary of the insurance stack from [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md) and how it maps to residual risk, with the D&O programme summary from [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md). **(10 min) Executive session** — the audit committee meets without management to raise questions.
3. **The mission-critical-risks selection discipline.** Name the operating rule — two or three risks at most; selection anchored to residual impact 5 or trend ↑ on a top-ten line; the GC and CEO align on selection in the pre-read-prep cycle. For Halcyon at Series B with regulated-industry customers, name representative candidates: a ransomware-encrypts-production scenario; a prompt-injection / cross-tenant-leakage AI-safety scenario; a HIPAA-breach-notification scenario; an export-controls-tightening-blocks-strategic-customer scenario. Each candidate has a documented reporting system (what the audit committee sees quarterly), a documented management plan, and a documented control-effectiveness signal.
4. **The one-page summary the audit-committee chair delivers to the full board.** Name the one-page format — the taxonomy snapshot, the top-ten with residual and trend, the appetite-statement re-affirmation disposition, the mission-critical-risks summary, and any escalation items the full board must act on. Any director must be able to absorb the one-page in the pre-read pack.
5. **The pre-read-prep calendar.** Name the T-21 / T-14 / T-7 cadence for the audit-committee pack — T-21 the GC circulates the draft register and the draft mission-critical-risks list to the executive team; T-14 the executive team confirms selection and the appetite-statement re-affirmation disposition; T-7 the final pack is in the directors' hands via the board portal consistent with [mod-111 chapter 02](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/02-board-cadence-and-materials.md) and the committee charter from [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md).

### Part F — Reference-framework vocabulary memo

Author `reference-framework-vocabulary-memo.md` — the short memo the GC circulates to the executive team and the audit-committee chair explaining which reference framework the ERM programme reads from, and why Halcyon does not adopt any of them wholesale. Cover:

1. **ISO 31000:2018 — Risk management — Guidelines.** The principles-and-process framework defining risk as "the effect of uncertainty on objectives" and structuring the process into scope-and-context → risk assessment (identification, analysis, evaluation) → risk treatment → monitoring and review, wrapped in communication and consultation. Name why ISO 31000 is Halcyon's enterprise operating framework at Series B — terse, non-prescriptive, adaptable, and a common vocabulary that is legible to auditors and insurers without the compliance apparatus of a Fortune-500.
2. **COSO Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017).** The five-component framework — Governance & Culture, Strategy & Objective-Setting, Performance, Review & Revision, Information, Communication & Reporting — with twenty supporting principles. Name the operating rule that Halcyon migrates to COSO ERM 2017 as the enterprise framework **twelve to eighteen months before the IPO decision** so auditors and the audit committee are fluent in the COSO vocabulary at listing. Name the sequencing — the taxonomy, the register, the appetite statement, and the review cadences authored in Parts A–E translate directly into the COSO vocabulary; the migration is a re-label, not a re-author.
3. **COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013).** The ICFR / SOX framework. Name the deferral — Halcyon runs a SOX-lite programme against COSO 2013 on the financial-reporting-control side; the substantive methodology is handed off to the finance organisation's ICFR programme and is not re-authored in the ERM framework. Name `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current SOX-lite timing against Halcyon's IPO trajectory.
4. **NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2 and NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0.** The federal-origin vocabulary (categorize → select → implement → assess → authorize → monitor) and the NIST CSF 2.0 six functions — **identify**, **protect**, **detect**, **respond**, **recover**, **govern** (the Govern function added in CSF 2.0). Name NIST CSF 2.0 as Halcyon's cyber sub-framework; defer the cyber-engineering substance to [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) and to the `security-learning` curriculum. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current NIST CSF 2.0 version date and any subsequent revisions.
5. **The picked-piecewise rule.** Name the operating rule that Halcyon picks vocabulary from ISO 31000 (enterprise), COSO 2013 (ICFR / SOX-lite), NIST CSF 2.0 (cyber), and — at the twelve-to-eighteen-month-pre-IPO trigger — migrates the enterprise layer to COSO ERM 2017. Halcyon does **not** adopt any framework wholesale at Series B; adopting wholesale buys compliance apparatus the corporation cannot operate without over-engineering the register and the review cadence.
