# Exercise 02 — Startup insurance stack assembly drill

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [02 — The startup insurance stack, by stage](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md)

## Problem statement

Thornbrook Logistics AI Inc. is a Series-A Delaware C-corp selling a fleet-optimisation and routing-decision product (ML-driven dispatch, load-matching, and fuel-cost optimisation) into mid-market US trucking and last-mile delivery customers. The corporation closed a $24M Series A ten months ago led by an industrials-and-logistics-focused venture fund, books ARR in the mid-single-digit millions, and is 95 FTE across a San Francisco HQ (62 FTE), a Chicago office opened two months ago for the trucking-customer sales motion (11 FTE), and a Boston engineering satellite (22 FTE). Headcount is on plan to cross 140 FTE by the end of the next year as the Series-B thesis attaches to a 401(k) rollout, an EU go-to-market experiment through a Dublin sales office, and an in-house legal team build-out.

The insurance stack is the inherited artefact of a seed-era placement handled on a thirty-day pre-expiry scramble by the CFO with a general-purpose commercial broker. The current programme is a Business Owner's Policy bundling General Liability and office-contents coverage; a workers'-comp policy covering the three states of operation; a claims-made Tech E&O + Cyber bundle written at $3M aggregate with no explicit social-engineering endorsement; a claims-made Employment Practices Liability policy with a $100k wage-and-hour defence-only sub-limit; and a claims-made Directors & Officers policy written at $3M aggregate covering Sides A/B/C after the first outside board seat was taken by the seed lead. Everything else — Umbrella / Excess, Commercial Auto / Hired-and-Non-Owned, Fiduciary Liability, Crime / Fidelity, Employed Lawyers — is absent.

Four operating realities have forced the insurance-stack question onto the GC-and-CFO agenda. **(i)** The 14-truck pilot customer in Ohio has sent a vendor-risk questionnaire asking for proof of (a) a $5M Commercial Auto / Hired-and-Non-Owned coverage line (because Thornbrook field-service engineers drive to customer yards in personal vehicles), (b) a $10M Umbrella attaching above the GL and Auto, and (c) a Certificate of Insurance naming the customer as additional insured. **(ii)** The CFO is scheduled to start the 401(k) rollout in six months as part of the Series-B total-rewards motion, which will trigger the Fiduciary Liability line the moment the plan is sponsored. **(iii)** The CFO's controller forwarded a near-miss last month — a wire-transfer-fraud attempt impersonating the CFO instructed the controller to wire $187k to a new vendor; the controller caught it at the callback step — and asked whether the Tech E&O + Cyber policy and the (absent) Crime / Fidelity policy would have responded if the wire had gone out. The honest answer is "probably not cleanly, and the social-engineering seam is why." **(iv)** The GC is a one-headcount in-house hire who started last quarter; she will not stay a one-headcount through Series B, and the Employed Lawyers line enters the stack the moment the second attorney is hired.

The GC has eight weeks to walk into the board governance subcommittee with a stage-triggered insurance-stack specification, a renewal-cadence operating pattern, an application-warranty-discipline memo, a broker-selection memo, a social-engineering-seam analysis between Crime and Cyber, and a cross-reference to the D&O programme-design detail in [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md). She owns the end-to-end package; the CFO co-owns the premium indications, the retention analysis, and the broker-selection decision; the Head of Ops co-owns the application-input assembly from each attesting function; the corporate secretary owns the board-resolution and placement-authorisation mechanic from [mod-101](../../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/). Author the full package.

### Part B — Occurrence-vs-claims-made and tail-management discipline

Author `occurrence-vs-claims-made-and-tail-management-discipline.md`. Cover:

1. **The first-principles distinction.** Name the two triggers — **occurrence-triggered** policies cover events that happen during the policy period, whenever the claim is later made (General Liability is typically occurrence); **claims-made** policies cover claims first made against the insured during the policy period, subject to a retroactive date (Tech E&O, Cyber, EPLI, D&O, and Fiduciary are claims-made). Name why the distinction matters at termination, not just at bind — an occurrence policy that has expired still covers events that occurred during its policy period; a claims-made policy that has expired without a tail leaves historical exposure uninsured if the claim is first made after expiration.
2. **The tail-at-termination discipline.** Name the operating rule — **claims-made policies must be continuously renewed or an Extended Reporting Period (tail) purchased at termination**, or historical exposure becomes uninsured. The operative scenarios that can trigger an uncured gap: a claims-made policy non-renewed by the carrier at renewal; a claims-made policy replaced by a different carrier at renewal with a mismatched retroactive date; a claims-made policy terminated by the insured at a change-of-control, acquisition, or dissolution without a run-off tail.
3. **The retroactive-date discipline.** Name the operating rule — the retroactive date on a replacement claims-made policy must cover the full history of exposure the insured intends to preserve. A replacement policy with a retroactive date of "policy inception" wipes out coverage for every wrongful act before inception; a replacement policy with full-prior-acts coverage preserves it. The broker and the insured's counsel read the retroactive date at every renewal.
4. **The run-off tail at change-of-control.** Name the operating rule — at a change-of-control, acquisition, or dissolution, a run-off tail (an Extended Reporting Period, typically one, three, or six years) preserves the claims-made policy's ability to respond to post-close claims arising from pre-close wrongful acts. The 6-year run-off tail at IPO is the specialised version of this discipline and is handled in [mod-111 chapter 06](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/06-do-insurance-program.md). For Thornbrook at Series A, the operative event is a surprise acquisition or an inverse-triangular merger that terminates the claims-made D&O / E&O / EPLI / Cyber stack; the GC's playbook names the tail-purchase decision point in the term-sheet-to-close sequence.
5. **The continuous-renewal operating discipline.** Name the operational rule — the GC-and-CFO calendar tracks every claims-made expiration date and the retroactive-date reconciliation at each renewal; the broker produces a line-by-line retroactive-date-reconciliation table at the T-30 quote delivery; any deviation from full-prior-acts coverage is called out in the governance-subcommittee pre-read before bind.
6. **Worked example — Thornbrook's current stack.** For each claims-made line Thornbrook currently carries (Tech E&O + Cyber, EPLI, D&O), name the current retroactive date (`<!-- needs-research: ... -->`), the operational risk if the line is non-renewed, and the tail-purchase cost indication expressed as a multiple of the expiring annual premium with the multiple flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.

### Part C — Application-answers-are-warranties discipline memo

Author `application-warranty-discipline-memo.md`. Cover:

1. **The first-principles rule.** Name the operating rule — every material representation on an insurance application is a **warranty** the insurer will read at claims time, reliance on which the carrier issued the policy. Misrepresentation, even innocent, can **void coverage**; deliberate misrepresentation can constitute fraud. The signatory (typically the CEO, CFO, or Head of Ops) attests personally to the accuracy of every answer.
2. **The single-owner-per-application discipline.** Name the operating rule — each application has a **single owner** (typically the Head of Ops, GC, or CFO) who assembles inputs from each attesting function: CISO for cyber controls, VP People for wage-and-hour and headcount, CFO for revenue and financials, GC for prior claims and litigation, chief AI officer for any AI-governance attestation on applications that have adopted AI-governance questions. The single owner reconciles conflicts between the attesting-function inputs before the signatory signs; the signatory does not reconcile conflicts from a stack of memos at the signature page.
3. **The source-the-answers-from-operating-reality rule.** Name the operating rule — do not answer "MFA on all administrative access" if MFA is bypassed on the break-glass account; either fix the bypass first or answer accurately with the exception noted. Name the Cyber-and-AI-renewal pattern — applications have hardened materially since 2020 on cyber (see [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)) and since 2023 on AI (some carriers now require attestations about model-governance and AI-safety programmes). Assume the questions will get harder each renewal. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on the current AI-attestation pattern in the market.
4. **The signatory-reads-before-signing rule.** Name the operating rule — the CEO or CFO who signs is attesting personally. The signatory reads the questions and the answers, not just the signature page. The Head of Ops or GC sits with the signatory for the read to adjudicate any question the signatory flags.
5. **The retention-of-application-and-supporting-attachments rule.** Name the operating rule — retain the application and every supporting attachment in the corporate insurance record for the full statute-of-limitations period on any coverage question, consistent with [mod-109 chapter 07](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/07-clm-stack-and-legal-ops-graduation.md) (where the CLM-and-insurance-record discipline sits in the legal-ops stack). If a claim arises three years later and the carrier alleges misrepresentation, the record is the defence.
6. **Worked example — the Thornbrook Tech E&O + Cyber renewal application.** Author a checklist of the five most-load-bearing attestations on the Tech E&O + Cyber application and the attesting-function owner per attestation: MFA on all remote / administrative / privileged / email / VPN access (CISO); EDR on 100 % of endpoints and servers, monitored 24×7 (CISO); immutable, tested backups with documented RTO and RPO, annual restore test (CISO); written IR plan tested annually via tabletop (CISO + GC); vendor / supply-chain risk-management programme with tiered risk assessment, SOC 2 collection, and contractual security requirements (CISO + GC); wage-and-hour compliance (VP People); no-known-incidents and no-known-circumstances-that-could-give-rise-to-a-claim attestation (GC); revenue-recognition method and financials (CFO). For each attestation, name the sourcing function, the evidence the signatory will ask for before signing, and the exception-disclosure discipline where a control is deployed with a documented exception.

### Part E — Social-engineering seam between Crime and Cyber

Author `social-engineering-seam-crime-vs-cyber-memo.md`. Cover:

1. **The seam definition.** Name the operating problem — **social-engineering / impersonation fraud** is the "CEO email tells the controller to wire $200k to a new vendor" pattern (the Thornbrook near-miss in the problem statement is the canonical instance). The loss sits in a well-known gap between **Crime / Fidelity** policies (which traditionally cover employee dishonesty, funds-transfer fraud under a strict-definition trigger that may or may not reach the socially-engineered-but-authorised wire) and **Cyber** policies (which cover network-security-triggered funds-transfer fraud but may exclude losses where the insured's own employee authorised the wire).
2. **The read-the-two-policies-together rule.** Name the operating rule — read the Crime policy's funds-transfer-fraud and computer-fraud insuring agreements alongside the Cyber policy's funds-transfer-fraud sub-limit and social-engineering endorsement at every renewal. Confirm affirmative social-engineering coverage on **one** of the two policies; prefer an **affirmative social-engineering endorsement** on the Crime policy (rather than on the Cyber policy) because the Crime policy's loss-prevention sub-limits are typically higher for this pattern, and because the Cyber policy's social-engineering sub-limit is often the lowest on the policy ($250k–$500k is common at the mid-market — flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on the current market).
3. **The affirmative-endorsement checklist.** Author the checklist the broker-and-GC run against the Crime policy at the Series-B placement: affirmative social-engineering coverage with no requirement that the fraud originate from a "cyber event" as a trigger; coverage for vendor-impersonation patterns (fraudulent-instruction-to-change-payment-details — the BEC vendor-invoice fraud pattern); coverage for payroll-diversion patterns (fraudulent-instruction-to-reroute-direct-deposit); a meaningful sub-limit; a defined callback-verification condition the policy will read against the insured's control (the Thornbrook controller caught the near-miss because callback verification was the policy-and-practice — name the callback-verification control as the condition-precedent the policy will ask the insured to attest).
4. **The call-back and dual-authorisation controls.** Name the control-side rule — the social-engineering exposure is one of the few where the control is a near-perfect preventive and the insurer prices for the presence of the control. Callback verification on any new vendor payment-instruction; dual-authorisation on any wire above a named threshold; out-of-band confirmation on any change-of-payment-details request; segregation-of-duties between the person who sets up the vendor and the person who authorises the wire. Reference the finance-controls programme owned by [mod-114](../../mod-114-operations-function-design/) and defer the full operating-controls build-out there.
5. **The Thornbrook retroactive analysis.** Walk the near-miss through the current Tech E&O + Cyber policy to document what would have happened if the wire had gone out: whether the Cyber policy's funds-transfer-fraud sub-limit would have triggered (likely yes but at a modest sub-limit); whether the Cyber policy's social-engineering endorsement (absent in the current programme) would have triggered (no); whether a Crime / Fidelity policy (absent in the current programme) would have triggered (no). Name the gap as the specific reason Thornbrook adds Crime / Fidelity with an affirmative social-engineering endorsement at Series B and does not wait for a loss to prove the seam exists.
