# Exercise 03 — Cyber-insurance and incident-response coordination drill

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — Cyber insurance and incident-response coordination](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md)

## Problem statement

Sablefin Clinical Intelligence Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a diagnostic-decision-support product (ML-driven differential-diagnosis summaries, test-ordering suggestions, and discharge-note draft generation) into US mid-market hospital systems and large specialty clinics. The corporation is 180 FTE across a Boston HQ (115 FTE), a Chicago go-to-market satellite (26 FTE), and a Dublin engineering office opened seven months ago (39 FTE). Sablefin books ARR in the low-tens-of-millions, closed a $55M Series B eleven months ago led by a healthcare-and-infrastructure growth fund, is HIPAA Business Associate on every live US deployment, and is on an eighteen-month pre-IPO readiness track with a planned S-1 confidential-filing conversation in four quarters.

The cyber-insurance stack is a Series-A-era claims-made Tech E&O + Cyber bundle written at $5M aggregate with a $1.5M ransomware sub-limit, a $250k funds-transfer-fraud sub-limit, a $250k social-engineering endorsement, a $25k retention, and panel-provider conditions on breach counsel, forensics, notification, and ransomware-negotiation. The renewal is in sixteen weeks; the T-120 broker kick-off has produced a pre-renewal strategy note flagging five items: **(i)** the hard market has tightened ransomware sub-limits further and the carrier has signalled a sub-limit cap below the current $1.5M at the next renewal unless Sablefin can defend its control posture on EDR, backups, and IR-plan-tested-annually; **(ii)** the LMA state-backed cyber-war exclusion (LMA5564 / LMA5565 / LMA5566 / LMA5567 family — flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current versions) has been written into the Cyber form and the carve-back is a Sablefin-specific negotiation; **(iii)** the widespread-event / systemic-cyber-event exclusion has been introduced; **(iv)** the war-and-cyber-war exclusion's attribution-to-a-state-actor language is a policyholder-dangerous construction Sablefin's GC wants read carefully; **(v)** the SEC Item 1.05 of Form 8-K four-business-day cybersecurity-materiality disclosure rule and the GDPR Article 33 72-hour DPA-notification clock and the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule 60-day clock and the 50-state breach-notification matrix and the NYDFS Part 500 72-hour clock all sit on the regulatory-stack owned in [mod-110 chapter 05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/05-breach-response-playbook.md).

Three operating realities have forced the full cyber-IR-coordination package onto the CISO-and-GC agenda. **(i)** A ransomware near-miss three months ago (phishing → credential theft → lateral movement caught at the EDR step before encryption) exposed the fact that the CISO and GC have never walked the carrier's 24×7 hotline, the panel-breach-counsel firm, or the panel-forensics firm with the executive-IR team; the pre-briefed-shortlist step from chapter 03 is a to-do, not a done. **(ii)** The design-partner hospital customer in the problem statement has asked, in its vendor-security review, for the privilege posture Sablefin maintains on any forensic report it would produce in an incident — the customer's counsel has cited *In re Capital One Consumer Data Security Breach Litigation* (E.D. Va. 2020) and *In re Rutter's Data Security Breach Litigation* (M.D. Pa. 2021) as the case law the forensic-report privilege posture must survive. **(iii)** The CFO has asked whether the ransomware-decision-tree against the OFAC October 2020 and September 2021 advisories on ransomware payments has been rehearsed — the honest answer is no, and the CISO flagged that the ransomware-negotiator panel-provider includes sanctions-screening of the wallet address and the ransomware family as a standard preflight step, but Sablefin's own written policy on the ransom-payment decision does not exist.

The CISO and GC co-own the end-to-end IR-coordination package; the CFO co-owns the wire-and-payment mechanic for any ransom-payment-authorised scenario and the premium-and-retention analysis for the Cyber renewal; the corporate secretary owns the audit-committee reporting surface for cyber incidents from [mod-111](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/); the CEO owns the external-communications call for any public-disclosure event. Author the full package.

## Requirements

### Part A — Coverage-specification and exclusion-watch matrix

Author `coverage-specification-and-exclusion-watch-matrix.md` — the renewal-ready coverage specification the GC and CFO will circulate to the governance subcommittee. Cover:

1. **First-party coverage parts.** Author the first-party parts with named insuring-agreement scope per part: **breach-response costs** (forensic investigation, breach counsel, individual and regulator notification, credit monitoring and identity-protection services, call-centre services, crisis-communications / PR support — the most-used part of the policy); **business interruption and contingent business interruption** (lost income during a cyber-caused outage of Sablefin's systems and of a covered supplier's systems, with the **waiting period** (typically 8–24 hours) and the **period of restoration** (hours-or-days-based) named and flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` against current carrier form); **cyber extortion** (ransomware negotiation, ransom-payment reimbursement where legal, extortion-consultant fees — one of the most heavily sub-limited areas of the policy); **data restoration** (costs to restore or recreate data damaged, corrupted, or destroyed by a covered event); **funds-transfer fraud** (funds transferred out of Sablefin's account as a result of fraudulent instruction, with the Crime-vs-Cyber seam from [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md) called out explicitly); **regulatory defence and fines** (defence costs for regulatory proceedings — FTC, state AG, HHS OCR for HIPAA, EU DPA for GDPR — and, where insurable by law, the fines themselves; flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current EU-member-state positions on insurability of GDPR Article 83 fines).
2. **Third-party coverage parts.** Author the third-party parts: **network-security liability** (damages payable to third parties for unauthorised intrusion, malware transmission, DDoS); **privacy liability** (damages payable for a privacy violation — breach of PII, breach of contractual privacy obligation, CCPA private right of action for certain breaches); **media liability** (defamation, copyright infringement, trademark infringement in Sablefin's content, including increasingly relevant AI-generated-content exposure); **PCI assessment defence** (defence and, where covered, indemnity for PCI-DSS assessments arising from a card-brand-mandated forensic investigation — coverage varies materially, flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`).
3. **The exclusion-watch matrix.** Author the matrix in operational-table form with one row per exclusion and columns for (exclusion name, carrier-form default language the broker has flagged, the carve-back Sablefin will insist on, the fallback position the broker may accept). At minimum cover: **ransomware sub-limits** (frequently sub-limited to a fraction of aggregate — a $10M aggregate may cap ransomware at $2M–$5M, flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` against Sablefin's renewal); **funds-transfer-fraud sub-limits** (typically the smallest sub-limit on the policy — $250k–$500k for a mid-market startup, flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`); **social-engineering / impersonation-fraud coverage** (affirmed by endorsement only; confirm vendor-impersonation and payroll-diversion patterns are covered, confirm the sub-limit is meaningful, cross-reference the Crime / Fidelity seam from [chapter 02](../02-startup-insurance-stack-by-stage.md)); **war and cyber-war exclusions** (LMA5564 through LMA5567 — flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current LMA state-backed-cyber-war clause revisions and any US-market ISO equivalents, including CG 21 06 or the corresponding cyber-form endorsement); **widespread-event / systemic-cyber-event exclusions** (count-of-insureds-affected or dollar-of-market-loss triggers — carve-back Sablefin will insist on the attribution standard and the proportional-allocation posture); **unencrypted-mobile-device exclusions** (BYOD-relevant if applicable to Sablefin); **prior-and-pending-litigation and known-circumstances exclusions** (why the application's no-known-incidents attestation matters); **regulatory-fine exclusions for specific regulators or jurisdictions** (read the schedule); **bodily-injury and property-damage exclusions** (Cyber generally excludes BI and PD; the seam matters for cyber-physical incidents — confirm the intercoverage answer).
4. **The war-and-cyber-war attribution problem.** Name the operating problem — attribution to a state actor is a legal question and the policyholder rarely controls it. The LMA5564–LMA5567 clauses push the attribution burden onto the insured; the carve-back Sablefin will insist on is the proportional-impact / ratio-of-affected-insureds standard rather than the single-attribution-determination standard. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current LMA versions and any superseding US-market form language.
5. **The retention and sub-limit optimisation discipline.** Name the operating rule — the retention is the lever the corporation negotiates against the limit and the exclusion carve-backs; accepting a higher retention to preserve aggregate limit and the ransomware sub-limit and the social-engineering endorsement is a defensible trade. Accepting a higher retention **and** a weaker exclusion schedule is not. Every retention / sub-limit / aggregate figure carries `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`.

### Part B — Carrier-panel socialisation pack

Author `carrier-panel-socialisation-pack.md` — the reference pack the CISO and GC circulate to the executive-IR team so the first phone call in an incident is not the first conversation with the panel. Cover:

1. **The panel-provider inventory.** Name the four panel-provider categories and the roles the CISO-and-GC will pre-brief: **breach counsel** (specialised law firms — the carrier publishes a panel at bind; Sablefin will obtain the roster in writing and nominate one primary and one alternate per renewal cycle); **digital-forensics firms** (the CISO will obtain the roster and nominate one primary and one alternate); **notification vendors** (large-volume individual notification, credit monitoring, call-centre services — the GC nominates a primary against the state-by-state notification requirements the vendor will operationalise); **PR / crisis-communications firms** (the Head of Communications nominates a primary); **ransomware negotiators** (the CISO nominates a primary with sanctions-screening capability named as a condition of the pre-brief). Do not invent specific firm names; reference by role only — "panel breach-counsel firm," "panel forensics firm A," "panel forensics firm B," "panel ransomware-negotiation firm."
2. **The off-panel-requires-consent rule.** Name the operating rule — using off-panel providers usually requires **carrier consent** and may result in reduced-rate reimbursement or coverage denial for the off-panel spend. If Sablefin has a strong relationship with an off-panel forensics firm or breach-counsel firm, the GC obtains pre-approval in writing from the carrier **at bind or at renewal**, not in the first hour of an incident.
3. **The pre-briefed-shortlist operational discipline.** Name the operating rule — at policy bind and again at renewal, Sablefin obtains the panel roster in writing, socialises it with the CISO, GC, and executive-IR team, and pre-briefs the shortlist via a no-cost preparatory call with the breach-counsel firm the GC would call first and the forensics firm the CISO would engage first. The relationship is established **before** the incident. Add the 24×7 hotline number to the IR runbook as a laminated card in the SOC, a page in the runbook, and a saved contact in the incident-commander's phone.
4. **The pre-loss touchpoint checklist.** Author the checklist of the five things to confirm with the carrier before an incident: **(i)** the 24×7 incident-response hotline number, and any separate hotlines for extortion / ransomware events; **(ii)** notification deadlines (typically "as soon as practicable" paired with a hard outside window — 30 days is common; late notice is a coverage-defence pathway, do not test it); **(iii)** sub-limits and retentions per coverage part (know **before** the incident whether ransomware, funds-transfer, or business-interruption has a lower cap or retention than aggregate); **(iv)** trigger definitions (many policies now require formal "incident" or "claim" declaration by breach counsel to trigger coverage — a suspected intrusion under internal investigation may not itself be a covered event); **(v)** reservation-of-rights protocol (what a coverage-under-reservation letter looks like from this carrier and how it interacts with panel-provider engagement).
5. **The runbook integration.** Name the runbook-level artefact — a one-page "carrier panel + hotline + notification clock" reference card that sits at the front of the IR runbook; the laminated card in the SOC; the saved contact in the incident-commander's phone; the pager-escalation path to the CISO-and-GC that includes the 24×7 hotline as a parallel step. Reference the IR-runbook authoring discipline to the `security-learning` curriculum for the technical-containment substance underneath.

### Part C — Privilege-posture memo on the forensic report

Author `privilege-posture-memo-on-the-forensic-report.md` — the memo the GC will circulate to the executive-IR team so the privilege posture is pre-wired before an incident. Cover:

1. **The case-law anchor.** Cite *In re Capital One Consumer Data Security Breach Litigation*, 2020 WL 3470261 (E.D. Va. June 25, 2020) (holding the forensic report produced by Mandiant for Capital One was **not** protected by attorney-work-product because Mandiant's underlying engagement was a business-not-litigation retainer); cite *In re Rutter's Data Security Breach Litigation*, 2021 WL 3733137 (M.D. Pa. July 22, 2021) (holding the forensic report was not work-product because the engagement did not satisfy the "because of" litigation test). Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current subsequent-opinion status and any circuit-split developments.
2. **The engage-breach-counsel-first-for-privilege rule.** Name the operating rule — breach counsel is engaged **before** forensics so that forensics work product is directed by counsel and covered by attorney-client privilege and attorney-work-product doctrine. Engaging forensics directly (without counsel-direction) leaves the forensic report discoverable in later litigation.
3. **The engagement-letter structure.** Name the structural elements the engagement letter must carry to survive the *Capital One* / *Rutter's* line: breach counsel is named as the engaging party; the forensics firm is engaged on behalf of the insured **for the purpose of providing legal advice** (not for business or ordinary-course purposes); the deliverable is a forensic report the purpose of which is to inform counsel's legal advice; the forensics firm is not simultaneously under a separate ordinary-course services contract covering the same incident scope; billing runs through counsel; the engagement predates (or does not retroactively re-characterise) prior ordinary-course relationships. The engagement letter is reviewed by outside litigation counsel if an incident involves threatened or anticipated multi-party litigation.
4. **The dual-track-reporting discipline.** Name the operating rule — the forensic team may produce a privileged "legal-advice-informing" report for counsel and a non-privileged "technical-remediation" report for the engineering organisation. The privileged report describes the root-cause, scope, and legal-risk-informing analysis; the technical report describes the remediation steps the engineering organisation will take. Name why the two-track approach is not a universal cure (courts have scrutinised whether the privileged report is sufficiently distinct from the business-purpose report) and flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current circuit case law.
5. **The parallel-workstream sequencing.** Name the operating rule — technical containment (endpoint isolation, credential rotation, network segmentation) runs in parallel with the legal / notification / regulatory workstream; containment cannot wait for legal, but external notification cannot precede legal review. The breach-counsel-plus-incident-commander pairing sequences the two. Every substantive carrier communication runs through counsel; unrepresented company personnel do not make direct statements to the carrier about the incident.
6. **The retention-and-production discipline for the forensic report.** Name the operating rule — the privileged forensic report is retained in the GC's privileged-materials repository with the engagement letter and the counsel-direction-memorandum; the non-privileged technical report sits in the engineering-remediation record. Discovery requests are assessed against the engagement-letter structure and the two-track distinction; no privileged material is produced without a counsel-reviewed privilege-log entry.

### Part D — Ransomware-decision tree against the OFAC advisories

Author `ransomware-decision-tree-against-ofac-advisories.md` — the decision tree Sablefin runs before any ransom-payment authorisation, consistent with the Treasury OFAC advisories on ransomware payments and coordinated with [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md). Cover:

1. **The regulatory anchor.** Cite Treasury OFAC's **October 1, 2020** "Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments" and the **September 21, 2021** "Updated Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments." Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on exact current URLs on ofac.treasury.gov, any superseding or clarifying advisory since 2021, and the current state of OFAC ransomware licencing practice. Name the framework the advisories set out — victims and their agents (insurers, incident-response firms, financial institutions facilitating payment) can face civil enforcement **even without knowledge** of the sanctioned status of the recipient; the regime is strict-liability civil penalties per violation.
2. **The pre-payment sanctions-screening pass.** Name the operating rule — the carrier and the ransomware-negotiation firm will screen **(i)** the wallet address, **(ii)** the ransomware family, and **(iii)** any identifiable actor against OFAC's SDN List, the Consolidated List, and any relevant country / regional-programme jurisdiction before any payment. A **hit is a hard stop**: payment cannot proceed without an OFAC licence, and licences for ransomware payments are, by policy, presumptively denied.
3. **The decision tree in operational form.** Author the tree with named nodes: **(1) Incident confirmed as ransomware with a payment demand → engage breach counsel → engage panel ransomware-negotiator; (2) Pre-payment sanctions screen on wallet / family / actor → SDN or sanctioned-nexus hit → hard stop, no payment, licence-application analysis only; (3) No sanctions hit → continue to viability analysis: decryption-key-reliability check (does the known family deliver working decryption keys), data-restoration alternative check (do the backups cover the encrypted systems), continuing-business-interruption-cost analysis; (4) Viability analysis supports no payment → execute the no-payment decision, restore from backups; (5) Viability analysis supports payment → GC convenes the decision group (CEO, CFO, CISO, GC, outside sanctions counsel) → document the decision rationale → authorise through the CFO payment mechanic → execute the self-disclosure path regardless of payment outcome**.
4. **The report-to-OFAC-and-CISA-regardless rule.** Name the operating rule — **timely voluntary self-disclosure** to OFAC and to CISA is a **mitigating factor** even if payment occurs; failure to report can eliminate the mitigation. Mitigating factors identified in the advisories include a strong sanctions-compliance programme, timely and complete self-reporting to OFAC and to CISA, and cooperation with law enforcement. Name the reporting path — OFAC via the Enforcement Division; CISA via the 24×7 operations line (`report@cisa.gov` / 1-888-282-0870, flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current channels and intake forms); the FBI local field office or IC3 as a parallel law-enforcement reporting path.
5. **The pre-payment GC-and-outside-counsel involvement rule.** Name the operating rule — the GC and outside sanctions counsel are involved **before** the CISO makes a payment recommendation to the executive team. The sanctions-programme substance (OFAC country and list-based programmes under 31 CFR Chapter V, screening architecture, voluntary-self-disclosure playbook under OFAC's Economic Sanctions Enforcement Guidelines at 31 CFR Part 501 Appendix A and under BIS at 15 CFR § 764.5) is owned in [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md); this part names the ransomware-specific decision tree that reads from the chapter-04 programme.
6. **The written policy artefact.** Name the artefact Sablefin will produce — a written **Ransomware Payment Decision Policy** signed by the CEO, reviewed annually, naming the decision group, the escalation path, the self-disclosure trigger, the pre-payment sanctions-screen gate, and the authorised-payment-mechanic through the CFO. The policy is a corporate-record artefact consistent with [chapter 01](../01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md) Part C's risk-appetite statement; "pay a ransom to a sanctioned-nexus negotiator or an OFAC-blocked ransomware operator without the Part C ransomware decision tree from chapter 03" is a Part C bright-line prohibition.

### Part F — 72-hour claims-workflow operating pattern

Author `72-hour-claims-workflow-operating-pattern.md` — the operating pattern the CISO, GC, and incident commander execute from detection through carrier engagement. Cover:

1. **The T + 00:00 through T + 72:00 sequence.** Author the sequence against the chapter-03 72-hour timeline: **T + 00:00** EDR alerts on suspicious lateral movement; **T + 00:45** SOC escalates to incident commander, triage confirms real intrusion; **T + 01:30** incident commander notifies CISO and GC, internal incident bridge stood up; **T + 02:00** GC contacts breach-counsel panel firm using the pre-briefed contact from Part B, verbal engagement confirmed with written engagement letter to follow; **T + 03:00** breach counsel notifies the cyber carrier via the 24×7 hotline, case number assigned, reservation-of-rights letter expected within 48 hours; **T + 04:00** breach counsel engages panel forensics firm on behalf of the insured under the Part C engagement-letter structure, kickoff call at T + 06:00; **T + 06:00** forensics kickoff with scope defined (root-cause, blast-radius, data-exfiltration determination); immediate containment proceeds in parallel under CISO direction; **T + 12:00** preliminary containment with compromised credentials rotated and affected endpoints isolated; **T + 24:00** forensics preliminary read (intrusion vector identified); **T + 48:00** forensics preliminary determination on exfiltration scope, breach counsel begins state-by-state notification analysis from Part E, first carrier update, executive-team briefing; **T + 72:00** notification decision matrix drafted, regulatory-notification clocks plotted against statutory deadlines, board audit-committee notification prepared from Part E's sequencing.

5. **The controls-crosswalk discipline at renewal.** Name the operating rule — the cyber-insurance application warranties (MFA on all remote / administrative / privileged / email / VPN access; EDR on 100 % of endpoints and servers monitored 24×7; immutable and tested backups with documented RTO and RPO and annual restore test; written IR plan tested annually via tabletop; vendor and supply-chain risk-management programme with tiered risk assessment and SOC 2 collection; vulnerability-management and patch cadence; security-awareness training and phishing simulation; email security with DMARC / SPF / DKIM; segmentation with privileged-access workstations; logging and monitoring with centralised aggregation) are the **same list** the security programme builds against SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, ISO 27001 Annex A, and NIST CSF 2.0.
