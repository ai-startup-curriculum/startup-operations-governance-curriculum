# Exercise 04 — Sanctions and export-controls programme authoring

> Estimated time: **~10 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — Sanctions and export-controls compliance programme](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)

## Problem statement

Pallantir Compute Fabric Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling an AI-infrastructure orchestration platform (model-serving, fine-tuning pipelines, and an inference-optimisation layer that bundles proprietary CUDA-adjacent kernels) into mid-market US and EU enterprise customers. The product is self-serve for development tiers (a signup page issues an API key after email verification and credit-card capture), and enterprise-sales-gated for production tiers (single-tenant deployment with an MSA, DPA, and Security Addendum under [mod-109](../../mod-109-commercial-contracts-ip-and-legal-ops/)). The corporation is 215 FTE across a San Francisco HQ (118 FTE), a New York go-to-market satellite (34 FTE), and a Dublin engineering office opened eight months ago (63 FTE). Pallantir books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, closed a $85M Series B thirteen months ago led by an infrastructure-and-AI growth fund, and is on an eighteen-to-twenty-four-month pre-IPO readiness track. The product ships TLS-standard encryption plus a bring-your-own-key option for enterprise tenants; the inference-optimisation layer includes proprietary kernels that target advanced-node accelerator hardware. The engineering organisation includes seven foreign-national H-1B employees in the San Francisco HQ working on the kernel-optimisation layer and three foreign-national employees in the Dublin office on EU-origin research-and-development visas.

Four operating realities have forced the sanctions-and-export-controls programme onto the GC-and-CCO agenda. **(i)** A self-serve signup last quarter was routed into a sales-cycle review when a customer-success engineer noticed the billing address was a freight-forwarder in a non-sanctioned country but the IP-geolocation of the signup event clustered in a comprehensively-embargoed jurisdiction; the account was suspended on day eleven of the trial, and the GC does not yet have a documented self-serve-signup OFAC-exposure control. **(ii)** The pre-IPO underwriter's diligence memo flagged the absence of a documented sanctions-and-export-controls compliance programme as an S-1 risk-factor exposure the corporation will have to address before listing. **(iii)** The CTO is scoping an advanced-computing enterprise customer in a Country-Group-D destination; the broker-channel partner's end-use certification has arrived with the phrase "the end-user is a research consortium whose members include affiliated universities" — the GC's screening pass against the BIS Entity List returned two hits on the named affiliated entities, and the deal is on hold pending a licence analysis. **(iv)** The Dublin engineering office has added two of the three foreign-national employees to the kernel-optimisation team without the deemed-export analysis under 15 CFR § 734.13 for the EAR technology and under 22 CFR § 120.17 for any ITAR-adjacent technology; the chief AI officer flagged that one of the kernels was originally derived from a research project funded by a defence agency and may trigger the ITAR § 121.1 USML determination.

The GC-and-CCO has eight weeks to walk into the board governance subcommittee with a sanctions-and-export-controls compliance programme that maps cleanly to Treasury OFAC's "A Framework for OFAC Compliance Commitments" (May 2019) five-component structure. She owns the end-to-end package; the CTO co-owns the EAR classification exercise and the ITAR § 121.1 triage; the Head of Product owns the geo-blocking and payment-processor-screening control; the Head of People owns the deemed-export analysis for the H-1B and EU-work-visa population; the corporate secretary owns the CEO-signed written policy and the board-resolution calendar from [mod-101](../../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/). Author the full package.

### Part C — EAR classification and licensing operating-pattern memo

Author `ear-classification-and-licensing-operating-pattern-memo.md` — the operating pattern the CTO and GC-and-CCO run against every exportable product, consistent with chapter 04's six-step EAR programme. Cover:

1. **The six-step EAR programme.** Author the six steps with Pallantir-specific application: **(1) Classify the product** against the Commerce Control List (15 CFR Part 774, Supplement 1) by Export Control Classification Number (ECCN), or via a formal Commodity Classification (CCATS) request to BIS under 15 CFR § 748.3(e); items not otherwise classified are EAR99. **(2) Determine the licence requirement** by applying the ECCN and the Country Chart (15 CFR Part 738, Supplement 1) and the applicable licence exceptions (15 CFR Part 740 including License Exception ENC under 15 CFR § 740.17 for encryption items). **(3) Screen the end-user** against the Entity List, Denied Persons List, Unverified List, MEU List, MIEU List. **(4) Screen for red flags of a prohibited end-use** against the BIS "Know Your Customer" Guidance and Red Flag Indicators at 15 CFR Part 732, Supplement 3 — vague end-use, reluctance to answer end-user questions, mismatch between the customer's business and the product ordered, requests for non-standard shipping instructions. **(5) Apply for licences where required** via the SNAP-R electronic filing system (flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current SNAP-R URL at snapr.bis.doc.gov). **(6) Retain records** under 15 CFR Part 762 for five years from the date of the export, re-export, or in-country transfer (or from the date of expiration of a licence, whichever is later).
2. **The Pallantir product-triage against the three-bucket EAR question.** Author the triage against chapter 04's "startup EAR / ITAR triage": **(i) Encryption above mass-market strength** — Pallantir ships TLS-standard encryption plus a bring-your-own-key option; the classification exercise is required and the annual self-classification report to BIS under 15 CFR § 740.17 may be required depending on ECCN and categorisation (flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current § 740.17 reporting cadence and covered categories). **(ii) AI models and model access above the controlled threshold** — the inference-optimisation layer bundles proprietary CUDA-adjacent kernels targeting advanced-node accelerator hardware; the October 7, 2022, controls on advanced-node semiconductors and the October 17, 2023, Interim Final Rule and subsequent amendments refined the advanced-computing / semiconductor controls, including the Notified Advanced Computing thresholds, and the January 2025 IFR and any 2025–2026 amendments carry `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`. The classification exercise is required; end-user and destination restrictions are likely; the Country-Group-D destination in the problem statement is the trigger. **(iii) Defense customers or USML items** — one of the kernels was originally derived from a research project funded by a defence agency; the ITAR § 121.1 USML-item determination is the Part D triage. **(iv) EAR99 baseline** — EAR99 items cannot be exported to comprehensively-embargoed destinations without a licence, and cannot be exported to Entity-List or Denied-Persons parties; every Pallantir product is at minimum in this bucket.
3. **The ECCN classification record.** Author the record artefact per exportable product: the ECCN, the License Exception where used, the Country Chart cross-reference, the annual self-classification report submission date where required, and the retention path (five-year record under 15 CFR Part 762). Every record is in a controlled file; the Sanctions Compliance Officer owns the file.
4. **The end-user and end-use screening at the enterprise-sales gate.** Name the operating rule — before any enterprise-sales contract signs for a destination outside Country-Group A countries (`<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current Country-Group designations), the Sanctions Compliance Officer runs the end-user screening against the Entity / Denied-Persons / Unverified / MEU / MIEU lists, the end-use screening against the BIS Red Flag Indicators, and the Country-Chart licence-requirement determination; a licence application via SNAP-R is filed where required. The problem-statement advanced-computing opportunity in the Country-Group-D destination is the live case: the two Entity-List hits on the affiliated-university entities are the hard stop until the licence analysis is resolved.
5. **The licensing-timeline discipline.** Name the operating rule — licence applications via SNAP-R run on BIS's timeline, not Pallantir's deal timeline. The GC-and-CCO documents the end-user screening, end-use screening, Country Chart analysis, and licence-filing timeline in the enterprise-sales-forecast review with the CFO and CEO so the deal-forecast slippage is acknowledged upstream, not surfaced at contract-sign. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current BIS licence-processing norms.
6. **The post-export recordkeeping discipline.** Name the operating rule — retain records under 15 CFR Part 762 for five years from export / re-export / in-country transfer or from licence expiration, whichever is later. Records include the classification analysis, the end-user screening, the end-use screening, the licence application and licence grant, the shipping documentation, and any post-export end-use-check correspondence from BIS.

### Part D — ITAR § 121.1 and § 122.1 triage memo

Author `itar-121-triage-memo.md` — the triage memo the CTO and GC-and-CCO run against the kernel-optimisation layer's defence-agency-funded research lineage, consistent with chapter 04's ITAR section. Cover:

1. **The ITAR substantive scope.** Cite 22 CFR Parts 120–130 (ITAR), the **U.S. Munitions List** at 22 CFR § 121.1, and the definitions at 22 CFR Part 120. Name the ITAR scope — defense articles, defense services, and related technical data enumerated on the USML. Name that ITAR is a specialist domain and the triage memo is not a programme substitute; a positive USML determination requires dedicated ITAR compliance counsel.
2. **The registration-before-transaction rule.** Cite 22 CFR § 122.1 — any US person who manufactures, exports, or brokers defense articles or defense services must register with DDTC, **regardless of whether any export or brokering activity actually occurs**. Registration alone is triggered by manufacture of a USML item. The Pallantir question — does the kernel-optimisation layer's defence-agency-funded-research lineage carry a USML designation that triggers § 122.1 registration before Pallantir has exported to anyone? — is the triage question.
3. **The USML classification triage.** Author the triage against the USML categories potentially in scope — most commonly Category XI (Military Electronics), Category XII (Fire Control, Laser, Imaging, and Guidance Equipment), Category XIII (Materials and Miscellaneous Articles) as a catch-all for specialised algorithms. Name the operating rule — the triage is a desk-level initial assessment the CTO and chief AI officer run against the kernel's actual lineage, output of which is either **(a) likely-not-USML** (the kernel is a general-purpose inference optimisation with no defence-article-specific function); **(b) possibly-USML** (the kernel targets or was derived from defence-specific algorithmic work); **(c) likely-USML** (the kernel implements a specific USML-enumerated function). A (b) or (c) output escalates to outside ITAR counsel for a formal determination.
4. **The Commodity Jurisdiction request.** Name the operating path — where the USML determination is ambiguous, Pallantir may file a **Commodity Jurisdiction request** under 22 CFR § 120.12 to obtain DDTC's binding determination of whether an item is subject to the ITAR or to the EAR. Name the operating rule — the CJ request is filed by outside ITAR counsel; the process is deliberate and may take months; Pallantir does not export in the interim. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current CJ processing timelines.
5. **The deemed-export analysis for ITAR-controlled technical data.** Cite 22 CFR § 120.17 (export includes releasing technical data to a foreign person in the United States or abroad) and the parallel EAR deemed-export rule at 15 CFR § 734.13(a)(2). Name the operating rule — ITAR-controlled technical data cannot be shared with a foreign-national employee — including an H-1B employee inside the United States — without a licence or exemption. The visa admits the person; it does not authorise the technical-data disclosure. The Pallantir question — the seven H-1B employees in the San Francisco HQ working on the kernel-optimisation layer and the two Dublin foreign-national employees recently added to the team — is the deemed-export triage. Name the operating rule — pending the USML determination, the kernel-optimisation team access is restricted to US-person employees; if the USML determination returns negative (EAR), the deemed-export analysis under 15 CFR § 734.13(a)(2) still applies to the extent the technology is controlled.
6. **The broker-registration question under 22 CFR Part 129.** Name the operating rule — brokers who solicit, promote, negotiate, or otherwise facilitate defence-article transactions must register under 22 CFR Part 129. The Pallantir broker-channel partner in the Country-Group-D opportunity from Part C is the triage question — if the kernel-optimisation layer is determined to be ITAR, the broker-channel partner may itself trigger broker-registration, which is a counterparty-diligence question for the Sanctions Compliance Officer.
7. **The DECCS licensing path.** Name the operating path — licensing for defence-article exports runs via DDTC's DECCS portal at pmddtc.state.gov/deccs (flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current URL and workflow); outside ITAR counsel owns the filing. The DECCS workflow is not a self-serve regime.

### Part E — Hard-stop escalation pattern memo

Author `hard-stop-escalation-pattern-memo.md` — the memo the GC-and-CCO circulates to the executive team and the product-and-engineering leads so the "who does what when" for a hit is a written policy, not a head-held reference. Cover:

1. **The hard-stop definition.** Name the operating rule — a **true positive** on any list (SDN, Entity List, Denied Persons List, Unverified List, MEU / MIEU) or a suspected in-country user in a comprehensively-embargoed jurisdiction is a **hard-stop escalation**. A customer is not informed, a transaction is not refunded, a politely-decline message is not sent, until legal has reviewed. A refund of a payment that was itself blocked property can constitute a further violation.
2. **The five-step escalation sequence.** Author the sequence: **(1) Freeze the transaction / access** — do not proceed further; do not confirm receipt to the counterparty. **(2) Escalate to the Sanctions Compliance Officer** (the GC / CCO). **(3) Engage outside sanctions counsel** — they will assess whether blocking, rejection, licence application, or self-disclosure is required. **(4) Assess self-disclosure obligations** — OFAC's Economic Sanctions Enforcement Guidelines at 31 CFR Part 501, Appendix A treat voluntary self-disclosure as a mitigating factor; BIS treats voluntary self-disclosure under 15 CFR § 764.5 similarly. **(5) Report as required** — SDN-blocked property is reported to OFAC within 10 business days under 31 CFR § 501.603; rejected transactions may also require reporting; any BIS-required disclosure runs on its own timeline. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current OFAC § 501.603 reporting mechanics and current BIS § 764.5 voluntary-self-disclosure practice.
3. **The CISA-and-OFAC reporting coordination on ransomware payments.** Cross-reference [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) Part D's ransomware-decision tree — the CISA notification channel and the OFAC Enforcement Division reporting path are the same instruments that run in the ransomware scenario. The cyber-IR workstream and the sanctions-programme workstream converge at the OFAC-licence-and-self-disclosure gate.
4. **The self-serve-signup OFAC-hit worked example.** Walk the problem-statement self-serve-signup escalation through the five-step sequence — the geolocation signal and the billing-address correlation produce a presumptive hard-stop; the Sanctions Compliance Officer is notified; outside sanctions counsel is engaged; the SDN-blocked-property assessment runs against the trial-tier credit-card capture (even an un-authorised trial charge may constitute blocked property); the self-disclosure analysis is filed with OFAC; the credit-card payment is held, not refunded, pending OFAC guidance. Name the operating rule — the trial-tier credit-card refund is not a safe default; the Sanctions Compliance Officer owns the hold-pending-OFAC-guidance decision.
5. **The BIS-Entity-List worked example.** Walk the problem-statement Country-Group-D advanced-computing opportunity escalation through the five-step sequence — the two Entity-List hits on the named affiliated-university entities produce a hard stop; the deal is on hold; the Sanctions Compliance Officer engages outside sanctions counsel; the licence-application analysis under SNAP-R is scoped; the deal timeline is communicated to the CFO and CEO; the enterprise-sales-forecast slippage is acknowledged at the executive-team cadence.
6. **The audit-committee reporting surface.** Name the operating rule — the Sanctions Compliance Officer's escalation log (hits, dispositions, open items) is a standing read-out at the annual audit-committee compliance review consistent with [mod-111 chapter 04](../../mod-111-corporate-governance-board-operations-and-officer-duties/04-standing-committees.md) and with [chapter 01](../01-enterprise-risk-management-operating-framework.md) Part E's audit-committee risk-review pack. Any hit above a materiality threshold (named in the Part A policy, flagged `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`) is reported out-of-cycle to the audit-committee chair.

### Part F — Deemed-export analysis under 15 CFR § 734.13(a)(2)

Author `deemed-export-analysis-for-foreign-national-hires.md` — the operating analysis the Head of People, the CTO, and the GC-and-CCO run before any foreign-national employee or contractor is added to a team working on EAR-controlled technology, coordinated with [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/) and [mod-113 chapter 04](../../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/04-us-immigration-programme.md) once that chapter is authored. Cover:

1. **The deemed-export substantive rule.** Cite 15 CFR § 734.13(a)(2) — "release" of technology or source code includes release to a foreign person in the United States or abroad, including visual inspection, oral or written exchange, or the application of technology to situations abroad. Name the operating rule — a release of EAR-controlled technology to a foreign-national employee **in the United States** is a "deemed export" treated as an export to the foreign person's country of most recent citizenship (or, in some cases, most recent permanent residence). The visa admits the person; it does not authorise the technical-data disclosure.
2. **The foreign-national population inventory.** Name the four populations the deemed-export analysis covers at Pallantir: **(i)** US-based H-1B employees (the seven San Francisco HQ engineers on the kernel-optimisation team); **(ii)** US-based employees on any other work-authorisation basis (TN, O-1, L-1, F-1 / OPT, EAD); **(iii)** Dublin-office foreign-national employees whose access to US-exported technology constitutes a cross-border release (deemed-export analysis under 15 CFR § 734.13 applies even outside the US where the technology originates from the US); **(iv)** contractors, interns, and visiting researchers.
3. **The pre-hire deemed-export triage.** Name the operating gate — before a foreign-national employee or contractor is added to a team working on EAR-controlled technology, the Head of People and the CTO run the deemed-export triage with the Sanctions Compliance Officer: **(a)** the technology-classification check (what ECCN is the technology; is the controlled attribute the kernel-optimisation layer, the encryption, or both); **(b)** the citizenship-and-residence check (the deemed-export licence analysis against the foreign national's country of most recent citizenship); **(c)** the licence-or-exemption determination (a licence under SNAP-R, a licence exception where applicable, or a technology-access-restriction that cabins the foreign-national's access to uncontrolled portions of the stack).
4. **The role-and-access-control mechanic.** Name the operating rule — the access-control implementation is a product-and-engineering control, not a legal-advice document. The Head of Engineering and the CISO own the role-based-access-control mechanic that restricts access to EAR-controlled technology to US-person employees pending the deemed-export licence; the Head of People owns the hire-flow gate that routes any foreign-national hire into the deemed-export triage before the first day of work.
5. **The Dublin-office operating rule.** Name the operating rule — the Dublin office is not a safe harbour. EAR-controlled technology does not become non-controlled by being used in Dublin; the deemed-export rule applies to the release of US-origin technology to foreign nationals regardless of location. The Dublin-office access-control posture is the same as the San Francisco HQ posture for the kernel-optimisation layer pending the Part D USML determination and the Part C ECCN classification.
6. **The I-129 export-controls certification.** Name the operating rule — USCIS Form I-129 (H-1B / H-1B1 / L-1 / O-1A / O-1B) Part 6 asks the petitioner to certify that a deemed-export licence either is not required or has been (or will be) obtained before the beneficiary is given access to controlled technology. The petitioner's signature is a material representation; the Sanctions Compliance Officer reviews every Pallantir I-129 Part 6 certification before filing. Flag `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` on current I-129 Part 6 language and any USCIS guidance revisions.
7. **The recordkeeping discipline.** Name the operating rule — the deemed-export analysis file (technology classification, foreign-national citizenship-and-residence record, licence-or-exemption determination, access-control implementation, I-129 Part 6 reconciliation) is retained in the Sanctions Compliance Officer's recordkeeping file for five years under 15 CFR Part 762.
