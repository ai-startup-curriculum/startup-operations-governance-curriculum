# Exercise 04 — Sanctions and export-controls compliance programme authoring

> Estimated time: **~12 hours** · Related chapter: [04 — Sanctions and export-controls compliance](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md) (with the OFAC ransomware-payment gate in [chapter 03](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md), the ERM-register / appetite-statement interface in [exercise 01](./exercise-01-erm-operating-framework-authoring.md), and the deemed-export and international-expansion overlay deferred to [mod-113](../../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/))

## Problem statement

Thornhill Compute Platforms Inc. is a Series-A Delaware C-corp selling a managed-inference API and a browser-based model-tuning console into developer and ML-engineering buyers across US, Canadian, UK, EU, Japanese, Australian, and Singaporean customers. The product exposes a public self-serve signup flow (email + credit card; no enterprise-sales gate below a usage threshold), a developer-tier API with standard TLS and per-request authentication, and — for paying tiers — access to a set of model endpoints that includes two model families hosted on advanced-compute GPU hardware in US-East-1 and Frankfurt regions. The corporation is 55 FTE, closed a $28M Series A six months ago, books ARR in the mid-single-digit millions, and has pulled 11,200 self-serve signups and 420 paying customers in the last twelve months. Four of the engineering hires in the last three months are foreign-national H-1B and TN-visa holders working from the US on the inference-serving and model-tuning stacks.

Thornhill's current sanctions and export-controls posture is the artefact of founder intuition and a security-questionnaire answer the CEO drafted in the Series-A data room. There is **no** written Sanctions Compliance Policy, **no** written Export Controls Policy, **no** named Sanctions Compliance Officer, **no** screening architecture running against OFAC and BIS lists at a documented cadence, **no** EAR self-classification analysis for the two advanced-compute-hosted model families, **no** geo-blocking or IP-geolocation control on the self-serve signup flow, **no** payment-processor-screening overlay beyond what Stripe's default screening performs, **no** deemed-export analysis for the four foreign-national engineering hires, **no** voluntary-self-disclosure playbook, and **no** records-retention discipline against the EAR Part 762 and OFAC 31 CFR § 501.601 five-year retention periods <!-- needs-research: verify current OFAC recordkeeping period under 31 CFR §§ 501.601–501.602 and BIS retention under 15 CFR § 762.6, and confirm any longer requirements for specific programmes. -->.

Three operating realities have forced the sanctions-and-export-controls question onto the general counsel's agenda. **(i)** A routine quarterly audit of the customer base against the OFAC SDN List surfaced four signup accounts whose declared email domain, IP geolocation, and credit-card billing address all resolve to Iran — comprehensive sanctions under 31 CFR Part 560 — and that have been active for between three and seven months. Two of those accounts are active paying customers at the mid-tier. The GC's immediate posture is a hard-stop freeze (no refund, no polite decline, no further communication until outside sanctions counsel reviews — reference the chapter-04 escalation on a true-positive hit); the broader question is how the self-serve signup flow onboarded them in the first place. **(ii)** The largest enterprise customer, a US-headquartered advanced-compute hyperscaler, routed a 23-item export-controls questionnaire attached to the renewal. The questionnaire asks for Thornhill's ECCN classification for each product, the License Exception the company relies on for encryption-item exports, the BIS annual self-classification report filing history for the ENC-item set, the Entity List and Military End-User and Military-Intelligence End-User List screening cadence, the deemed-export analysis for any foreign-national-held access to controlled technology, and the written Export Controls Policy. **(iii)** The Series-A lead investor's post-close operations review flagged, in a one-page memo to the general counsel, that "an advanced-compute product with self-serve signup and foreign-national engineering hires that has no written sanctions or export-controls programme is on the wrong side of a predictable enforcement question" and asked for a dated programme-standup plan at the next board meeting.

The general counsel has eight weeks to walk the next board meeting through a written sanctions-and-export-controls compliance programme, a triage of the regulatory landscape against Thornhill's specific exposure, a screening architecture and product-side control design that catches the self-serve-signup pattern before onboarding, a voluntary-self-disclosure playbook for the four Iran signups already in-house, and a training curriculum and records-retention discipline that let the programme survive a BIS end-use check or an OFAC subpoena. She owns the end-to-end package; the CTO owns the geo-blocking and IP-geolocation control implementation; the Head of People owns the deemed-export analysis for the foreign-national engineering hires in coordination with [mod-104](../../mod-104-hiring-onboarding-and-hr-operations/); the CFO owns the payment-processor-screening overlay; outside sanctions counsel runs point on the Iran-signup voluntary-self-disclosure. Author the full package.

### Part E — Escalation matrix and voluntary-self-disclosure playbook

Author `escalation-and-vsd-playbook.md`. The chapter-04 discipline — a true-positive hit is a hard-stop escalation; voluntary-self-disclosure is a documented mitigating factor in the OFAC and BIS enforcement frameworks. Cover:

1. **The escalation matrix in operational form.** A table with one row per hit type (SDN hit, Entity List hit, Denied Persons List hit, in-country user in a comprehensively-embargoed jurisdiction, suspected export of a controlled item to a restricted end-user, suspected deemed-export release to a foreign-national employee) and columns for (immediate action, owner, escalation-within-24-hours, outside-counsel-engagement trigger, regulator-disclosure determination).
2. **The chapter-04 standard escalation walk.** (i) Freeze the transaction / access — do not proceed further, do not send a polite decline message, do not refund, do not confirm receipt until legal has reviewed, since a refund of a payment that was itself blocked property can constitute a further violation; (ii) escalate to the Sanctions Compliance Officer; (iii) engage outside sanctions counsel to assess blocking, rejection, licence application, or self-disclosure; (iv) assess self-disclosure obligations under OFAC's Economic Sanctions Enforcement Guidelines (31 CFR Part 501, Appendix A) and BIS's voluntary-self-disclosure provisions (15 CFR § 764.5); (v) report as required — SDN-blocked property reported to OFAC within 10 business days under 31 CFR § 501.603, rejected transactions as applicable, any BIS-required disclosure on its own timeline. Author the walk as the operational discipline the SCO follows at every hit.
3. **The Iran-signup VSD execution.** Author the specific voluntary-self-disclosure plan for the four Iran-nexus signups already in-house — immediate freeze of the accounts (no refund; no further communication); engagement of outside sanctions counsel on the day of discovery; preparation of the voluntary-self-disclosure to OFAC under 31 CFR Part 501 with the chapter-04-named mitigating factors (strong sanctions-compliance programme standing up as of the disclosure date; timely and complete self-reporting; cooperation with law enforcement); CISA notification where any cyber-nexus applies; internal post-incident-review coordinated with [exercise 01](./exercise-01-erm-operating-framework-authoring.md) Part D's off-cycle executive risk review trigger.
