# Exercise 03 — Cyber-policy coverage audit and incident-response coordination drill

> Estimated time: **~12 hours** · Related chapter: [03 — Cyber insurance and incident-response coordination](../03-cyber-insurance-and-incident-response-coordination.md) (with the privacy-regulatory and breach-notification substance owned in [mod-110](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/), the sanctions interaction in [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md), and the risk-register / audit-committee interface in [exercise 01](./exercise-01-erm-operating-framework-authoring.md))

## Problem statement

Stablewood Data Systems Inc. is a Series-B Delaware C-corp selling a data-lineage-and-observability platform into mid-market and enterprise data-engineering teams across US, UK, EU, and Australian customers. The corporation is 180 FTE, closed a $55M Series B eight months ago, books ARR in the mid-tens-of-millions, and runs 2,400 customer tenants on a multi-tenant control plane that ingests customer-side metadata (schema, column-level profiles, query logs, identity bindings) and executes a read-only API against the customer's warehouse. Approximately ~110 customers are US financial-services firms subject to GLBA Safeguards, 7 are NYDFS-Covered-Entity customers under 23 N.Y.C.R.R. Part 500, 14 are HIPAA Covered Entities or Business Associates under a signed BAA from Stablewood's healthtech-customer segment, and 38 are SEC-reporting companies that depend on Stablewood's breach-notification posture for their own Item 1.05 four-business-day disclosure clock. Stablewood is itself a private company and not an SEC registrant.

Stablewood's cyber insurance tower is the artefact of the Series-A placement, written two renewals ago at a $10M aggregate limit, bundled with the Tech E&O form on the digital-first broker's default carrier panel, placed in a market shape that no longer exists. Since then: the post-2020 hard market tightened ransomware and funds-transfer-fraud sub-limits; carriers introduced revised state-backed-cyber-war exclusions (the Lloyd's Market Association LMA5564 through LMA5567 clauses <!-- needs-research: confirm current LMA state-backed-cyber-war clause revisions and any US-market ISO equivalents and cite. -->) and systemic-event exclusions; control-requirement underwriting hardened materially on MFA, EDR, backup, and vendor-risk attestations; and the carrier's incident-response panel was refreshed twice. The CISO's control-attestation grid for the upcoming renewal is materially harder than the grid signed two renewals ago. The CISO and the general counsel have never walked the carrier hotline workflow, the panel-counsel engagement letter template, or the privilege posture on the forensic report with the carrier before an incident.

Three operating realities have forced the policy-audit and IR-coordination question onto the general counsel's agenda. **(i)** The Tech E&O + Cyber tower renews in ten weeks on the renewal cadence from [exercise 02](./exercise-02-startup-insurance-stack-assembly-and-warranty-drill.md). The CISO has run the chapter-03 security-controls crosswalk against operating reality and surfaced three warranties on the expiring application where the "yes" answer is no longer defensible without documented exception-handling — the MFA-on-all-administrative-access answer against a break-glass service-account bypass; the EDR-on-100%-of-endpoints-and-servers answer against four legacy build-server endpoints in the engineering lab; and the immutable-tested-backups annual-restore-test answer that was last tested fourteen months ago. **(ii)** The SOC detected an anomalous login against a privileged service account from an unusual ASN at 02:18 UTC on a Sunday six weeks ago; initial triage containment ran fine, but the GC discovered — at the post-incident review — that no one had notified the cyber carrier, no breach counsel had been engaged, and the engineering team's forensic investigation had been performed by the SOC in its ordinary-course capacity without counsel direction (reference the *In re Capital One Consumer Data Security Breach Litigation* (E.D. Va. 2020) discoverability posture in [mod-110 exercise-05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/exercises/exercise-05-breach-response-playbook-authoring.md) Part C). The post-incident review concluded that the incident was not a breach but that the handling was a failure. **(iii)** The Series-B lead investor's operations team flagged, in the post-close risk review, that the cyber tower had not been walked against the current coverage shape and that the panel roster had not been socialised inside the CISO and GC teams.

The general counsel has ten weeks to walk the audit committee through a cyber-policy coverage audit, a pre-loss panel-socialisation and IR-coordination runbook, a 72-hour incident walk-through against a concrete scenario, an OFAC-ransomware-decision tree against the October 2020 and September 2021 advisories <!-- needs-research: cite exact URLs for the October 1, 2020 "Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments" and the September 21, 2021 "Updated Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments" on ofac.treasury.gov; confirm no superseding advisory since. -->, and a post-incident-review template that closes the loop back to the risk register. She owns the end-to-end package; the CISO owns the controls-crosswalk and the IR-team-side runbook; the CFO owns the premium-and-retention budget; the broker runs the market-side; outside breach counsel is being pre-selected from the cyber-insurance panel. Author the full package.

### Part C — Pre-loss panel socialisation and carrier IR setup

Author `pre-loss-panel-and-carrier-ir-setup.md`. The chapter-03 discipline — the panel providers, hotline numbers, notification deadlines, sub-limits, and trigger definitions must be socialised inside the security and legal teams **before** an incident. Cover:

1. **The pre-loss touchpoint list.** The chapter-03 five pre-loss touchpoints: (i) the 24×7 incident-response hotline number, with any separate hotline for extortion / ransomware events; (ii) the notification deadlines (framed as "as soon as practicable" typically with a hard outside window — 30 days is common; late notice is a coverage-defence pathway); (iii) the sub-limits and retentions per coverage part; (iv) the trigger definitions (many policies require formal "incident" or "claim" declaration by breach counsel to trigger coverage); (v) the reservation-of-rights protocol. Author each as a one-line working entry the CISO and GC confirm with the carrier before renewal bind.
2. **The panel roster in writing.** The chapter-03 discipline — obtain the panel roster in writing at bind and at renewal. Author the panel-roster categories — **breach counsel** (panel-counsel specialised law firms); **digital-forensics firms**; **notification vendors** (large-volume individual notification, credit monitoring, call-centre); **PR / crisis-communications firms**; **ransomware negotiators**. Name the category entries by role only (panel breach counsel firm A / B / C; panel forensics firm A / B / C; panel notification vendor A / B; panel ransomware negotiator A). The chapter-03 observation that off-panel providers require carrier consent and may result in reduced-rate reimbursement or coverage denial is the operative constraint.
3. **The pre-brief shortlist.** The chapter-03 discipline — pre-brief the breach-counsel firm the GC would call first and the forensics firm the CISO would engage first; some breach-counsel firms will run a no-cost preparatory call to establish the relationship. Author the shortlist and the no-cost prep-call plan.
4. **The IR-runbook hotline integration.** The chapter-03 artefact — the 24×7 hotline number on a laminated card in the SOC, a page in the IR runbook, a saved contact in the incident-commander's phone. Author the runbook-section-specification for the hotline integration.
5. **The IR tabletop cadence.** The chapter-03 application-warranty discipline requires an annual tabletop. Author the quarterly scaled cadence — one annual full-scope tabletop walking a specific scenario end-to-end with breach counsel attending; quarterly tabletop focused on one of containment, forensics engagement, carrier notification, panel-provider engagement, or regulatory-notification clock coordination. The chapter-03 socialisation discipline runs on this cadence.

### Part D — 72-hour incident walk-through (test scenario)

Author `72-hour-incident-walkthrough.md`. Walk the test scenario — a SOC alert at T+00:00 on EDR-detected suspicious lateral movement from a compromised customer-support laptop (the chapter-03 concrete 72-hour timeline is the reference). The compromise ultimately shows no customer-data exfiltration but the IR runs the full playbook; the walk-through produces the decisions, not the analysis. Cover:

1. **T+00:00 through T+06:00 — detection, counsel, carrier.** The chapter-03 sequence: SOC detection; incident-commander mobilisation; CISO and GC notification; GC contacts breach-counsel panel firm using the pre-briefed contact; breach counsel notifies the cyber carrier via the 24×7 hotline; case number assigned; reservation-of-rights letter expected within 48–72 hours; breach counsel engages panel forensics firm on behalf of the insured for the purpose of providing legal advice (the standard structure to preserve privilege). Author the sequence as a dated / timestamped log with named owner per entry.
2. **T+06:00 through T+24:00 — forensics kickoff and containment.** Forensics kickoff at T+06:00 with scope (root-cause, blast-radius, data-exfiltration determination). Immediate containment — endpoint isolation, credential rotation — proceeds in parallel under CISO direction. The SOC continues to operate in its ordinary-course capacity; the forensic vendor runs a separate investigation under counsel's direction; the SOC-side and vendor-side artefacts stay distinct in the document-management posture (reference [mod-110 exercise-05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/exercises/exercise-05-breach-response-playbook-authoring.md) Part C on the dual-track-report pattern).
3. **T+24:00 through T+48:00 — characterisation and carrier updates.** Forensics preliminary read — intrusion vector identified; data-exfiltration determination under investigation. All substantive communications with the carrier run through breach counsel. Reference the chapter-03 rule — do not let unrepresented company personnel make direct statements to the carrier about the incident.
4. **T+48:00 through T+72:00 — notification decision matrix.** The notification-decision matrix chapter-03 names — state breach-notification laws; HIPAA if PHI is implicated (reference [mod-110 chapter 04](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/04-hipaa-compliance-for-healthtech.md)); GLBA for the ~110 US financial-services customers (reference [mod-110 chapter 03](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/03-us-state-privacy-landscape-and-rolling-registration.md) and the sector-overlay substance); GDPR Article 33 72-hour clock (reference [mod-110 chapter 01](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/01-gdpr-compliance-programme.md)); CCPA § 1798.29 / § 1798.82; NYDFS Part 500.17(a) for the 7 NYDFS-Covered-Entity customers; SEC Item 1.05 for the 38 SEC-reporting customers who depend on Stablewood's breach-notification posture. The exercise owns the **sequencing**, not the regulatory substance — the [mod-110 exercise-05](../../mod-110-privacy-data-governance-and-sector-compliance/exercises/exercise-05-breach-response-playbook-authoring.md) owns the regulator-notification-matrix construction. Reference by cross-link.
5. **The decision at T+72:00.** The scenario produces a determination that the incident is not a breach — the credential compromise did not result in exfiltration. Author the specific decision Stablewood makes — document the chapter-03 post-incident review (root cause, controls change, governance cadence review, lessons-learned-publication, cyber-insurance-claim-coordination post-incident), the risk-register update with the new control entry, and the carrier-close-out notification with breach counsel coordination.

### Part E — OFAC ransomware-decision tree

Author `ofac-ransomware-decision-tree.md`. The chapter-03 discipline — ransomware payment is not a straightforward "buy the decryption key" transaction; OFAC prohibits payments to Specially Designated Nationals and to persons subject to comprehensive sanctions; a payment to a designated party without an OFAC licence is a strict-liability violation; many ransomware operators have been designated (Conti, LockBit affiliates, Evil Corp, and others historically). Cover:

1. **The decision-tree in operational form.** Author the tree starting at the moment the incident-commander is informed of a ransomware demand. First branch: is this a ransomware event under the policy's definition (data-encryption with demand; data-exfiltration-and-threat-to-publish as a separate branch; funds-transfer-fraud-with-ransomware-pretext as a third branch). Second branch: is the policy's ransomware sub-limit sufficient against the demand and what is the retention. Third branch: has the carrier been notified and is panel ransomware-negotiator engagement pre-approved. Fourth branch: has OFAC screening been run against the wallet address, the ransomware family, and any identifiable actor against the SDN List, Consolidated List, and the comprehensively-embargoed jurisdiction set (Iran, Cuba, North Korea, Syria, Crimea / DNR / LNR / Kherson / Zaporizhzhia — reference [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md)).
2. **The hard-stop on a sanctions hit.** The chapter-03 discipline — a hit is a hard stop; payment cannot proceed without an OFAC licence; and licences for ransomware are, by policy, presumptively denied. Author the escalation on a hit — freeze the payment decision, escalate to the GC and outside sanctions counsel, assess licence feasibility (presumptively denied), document the no-payment decision and the operational implications (data recovery from backups, business-interruption invocation, regulatory notification).
3. **The OFAC and CISA reporting discipline regardless of payment.** The chapter-03 discipline — report to OFAC and CISA regardless of payment; timely voluntary self-disclosure is a mitigating factor even if payment occurs; failure to report can eliminate the mitigation. Cite the October 1, 2020 OFAC "Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments" and the September 21, 2021 Updated Advisory <!-- needs-research: cite exact URLs for the October 1, 2020 and September 21, 2021 OFAC advisories on ofac.treasury.gov; confirm no superseding advisory since. --> and the voluntary-self-disclosure substance in [chapter 04](../04-sanctions-and-export-controls-compliance-programme.md).
4. **The GC-and-sanctions-counsel-before-CISO-recommendation rule.** The chapter-03 discipline — the GC and outside sanctions counsel are involved before the CISO makes a payment recommendation. Author the operational hand-off the IR runbook will carry — the CISO's technical read on the demand and the data at risk; the GC's legal read on the payment legality under OFAC and the carrier-policy ransomware coverage; the outside sanctions counsel's confirm-or-decline read; the CFO's funds-authorisation posture. The payment decision is not the CISO's unilateral call.
