# 1. The sourcing operating model by stage

> The wrong sourcing model at the wrong stage is not a "recruiter" problem. It is a founder-time-allocation problem, a candidate-signal problem, and a cash-burn problem all at once.

## Motivation

Every hire that closes came from *somewhere*. The unglamorous fact of a growing startup's org chart is that it is the exhaust of a sourcing operating model — a specific set of people, tools, and cadences that produce candidates in the top of the funnel, screen them, and hand them to hiring managers.

That model is not the same at every stage. At pre-seed and seed the founders themselves are the sourcing engine, and the highest-signal channels are the founders' networks, targeted outbound from the founders, and referrals from the first ten employees. At Series-A the founder's calendar is the binding constraint, and the first recruiter (in-house or fractional) shows up because the alternative is that hiring becomes CEO's full-time job. At Series-B the hiring volume outpaces one recruiter and a talent-acquisition function starts to form — with a sourcer / recruiter ratio, a referral programme, and fractional or agency support in specific domains. At growth the exec-search partnership becomes a real relationship, not a transactional engagement.

Getting the model *wrong for the stage* is expensive in specific ways:
- **Too much recruiter, too early.** A full-time recruiter hired at seed with 6–8 open roles across engineering, product, and GTM will not close any of them faster than the founder would have, will burn ~$150k–$220k of annualised comp, and will consume founder-time coaching the recruiter on the domain, the pitch, and the closing conversation.
- **Too little recruiter, too late.** A founder still sourcing every candidate at Series-B is not doing the seven other jobs the founder is supposed to be doing. Roles stay open for months. Compensation drifts because the founder has no benchmarking discipline. The eventual first-recruiter hire arrives to find an unusable pipeline history and no scorecards on record.
- **Contingent recruiter fees, uncapped.** A single mid-market contingent engagement at 25% of first-year cash on a $220k account executive is a $55k invoice — often signed by the hiring manager without a comp-committee-visible line item. Repeat that across five roles and the corporation has spent $275k on external recruiting without a plan.
- **Retained exec search, mispriced.** A retained search at $80k–$150k per seat that lands the wrong VP is a two-quarter setback plus a re-engagement plus the eventual severance package. The retained-search decision is where the largest single sourcing-line-item lives.

This chapter builds the model stage-by-stage and names the cost-and-signal trade-off at each step.

## The four canonical stages

The stages below are archetypes, not rigid boundaries. Real corporations blur them — a seed-stage AI-infra company with a technical founder-CEO who has run recruiting before may skip the "first recruiter at A" stage; a Series-B enterprise-sales-heavy company may keep large contingent-recruiter engagements even after the in-house TA team is fully staffed. The stages exist to make the *transition* deliberate rather than accidental.

### Stage 1 — Founder-led sourcing (pre-seed / seed, headcount 1–15)

**Who sources.** The founders. Every top-of-funnel candidate is either in the founders' network, referred by an existing team member, or sourced by a founder writing a personal outbound message on LinkedIn / X / GitHub / a domain-specific community.

**Who screens.** The founder who will manage the role. The other founder does a second screen. If the role is a first-of-its-kind for the company (first designer, first DevRel, first sales rep), external advisors — angels, ex-boss, executive coach — help calibrate.

**Who closes.** The founder-CEO. Closing conversations at seed are almost always founder-to-candidate. The candidate is joining because they believe in the founder and the wedge, not because a recruiter positioned the role well.

**Tools.** An ATS from day one (see [chapter 02](./02-ats-selection-and-integration.md)) — even at ten open reqs, "spreadsheet + inbox" produces a corporate-record hole that will haunt the eventual first-recruiter hire. LinkedIn Recruiter Lite or a shared Recruiter seat. A basic scheduling tool (Calendly, Cal.com, or the ATS's native scheduler).

**Referral programme.** Informal at first — the founder asks every new hire "who else should we talk to?" in the first-week 1:1. A referral-bonus policy can be adopted once headcount crosses ~10, but the founder-driven "who do you know" cadence produces most of the pipeline before that.

**Cost model.** Founder time is the primary cost — and it is the constraint that eventually forces the graduation. External recruiting spend is deliberately near-zero; the corporation is protecting cash, and founder network is the highest-signal channel available.

**When to graduate.** The graduation trigger is not headcount — it is *sustained founder-hours-on-recruiting per week*. When the founder-CEO is spending more than ~15–20 hours per week on top-of-funnel sourcing, screening, and scheduling, and the founder-CEO's other work is visibly slipping, the corporation should start the recruiter search. Series-A funding usually correlates with this trigger, not causes it.

### Stage 2 — First recruiter (Series-A, headcount 15–50)

**Who sources.** The first recruiter — typically titled "Recruiter," "Talent Partner," or "Head of Talent" depending on how the corporation expects the role to grow. The founder still sources for exec roles, GTM leadership, and any role where the founder's network is the natural channel. The recruiter owns the *rest* of the pipeline plus all of the operating machinery (ATS discipline, scorecards, scheduling, offer-close).

**Full-time vs. fractional.** The two viable models at Series-A:
- **Full-time in-house recruiter** at approximately <!-- needs-research: confirm the Series-A recruiter total-comp band (base + equity + bonus if any); a defensible band as of the 2024–2026 US market is roughly $130k–$220k base for a solo generalist recruiter, subject to geo. --> total comp. Owns the entire funnel across ~5–12 concurrent open reqs. Best when the hiring plan is dense (10+ hires in the next 12 months) and cross-functional (engineering + GTM + product + design).
- **Fractional recruiter** at <!-- needs-research: confirm the market range for fractional-recruiter engagements; commonly quoted structures are $12k–$25k/month retainer or a $150–$300/hr consulting rate, plus in some cases a per-close bonus. --> monthly retainer. Best when the hiring plan is narrower (2–4 open reqs at a time), the roles are specialised (fractional engineering-recruiter for a hard-to-source domain, fractional GTM-recruiter for a first-sales-hire moment), or the company wants to defer the "first FTE recruiter" hire by one more cycle.

**Who screens.** The recruiter runs the first conversation (30–45 minutes, a mix of role-fit and pitch). The hiring manager runs the second conversation. The founder-CEO stays involved in the on-site loop for every hire until roughly headcount 40–50.

**Who closes.** The recruiter *quarterbacks* the close. The founder-CEO stays in the closing conversation for senior IC and manager hires; for junior and mid-level IC, the hiring manager plus the recruiter can close without the founder-CEO in the room.

**Contingent-recruiter augmentation.** External contingent recruiters are used *selectively* for hard-to-fill or urgent roles — typically at 20–30% of first-year cash, invoiced on offer acceptance with a 60–90-day guarantee (if the candidate leaves within the guarantee window, the fee is refunded or credited toward a replacement). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current-market contingent-fee band across engineering, GTM, and executive roles; historically 20–25% is common for individual contributor engagements and 25–30% for senior manager / director engagements. --> The in-house recruiter owns the vendor relationship, the fee negotiation, and the decision to engage.

**Referral programme.** A formalised referral bonus is standard by Series-A. A common structure: $2k–$5k for successful IC referrals, $5k–$15k for senior manager and director referrals, paid on the referred hire's 90-day anniversary. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current-market referral-bonus band; the numbers above reflect commonly cited practice for US venture-backed companies at Series-A / B but should be checked against a recent survey. --> The recruiter tracks referral source-of-hire in the ATS.

**Cost model.** In-house recruiter total comp is the biggest single line item. Contingent-recruiter spend is a variable overlay — budgeted quarterly, escalated when a specific role is stuck. Referral bonuses are a rounding error at this stage but earn disproportionate goodwill. Tooling (ATS + LinkedIn Recruiter + scheduling) is roughly <!-- needs-research: confirm the current-market annual tooling cost; a defensible Series-A range for ATS + LinkedIn Recruiter + scheduling + assessment tooling is $20k–$60k/year depending on user count and product mix. -->.

**When to graduate.** Graduate to Stage 3 when the recruiter is running >8–10 concurrent reqs and (a) time-to-fill is drifting upward, (b) the recruiter is doing "everything" instead of the highest-leverage work, and (c) at least one specialisation (executive, engineering, GTM) is producing a bottleneck the generalist recruiter cannot break alone.

### Stage 3 — In-house TA team plus fractional overlay (Series-B, headcount 50–200)

**Who sources.** A small in-house talent-acquisition team that has begun to specialise. A common target-state for the Series-B TA team:

- **A Head of Talent** (or Director of Talent Acquisition) — owns the function, the plan, the budget, and the reporting relationship to the CEO or Head of People.
- **Two to four recruiters** split by function — engineering, GTM, and G&A / operations. Some corporations add product / design as a fourth specialisation once product hiring accelerates.
- **One to two sourcers** who focus exclusively on top-of-funnel outbound. The sourcer / recruiter ratio is a commonly discussed operating benchmark; a defensible range for a startup at this stage is <!-- needs-research: confirm sourcer-to-recruiter ratio benchmarks from a recent survey (LinkedIn Talent Solutions, Ashby benchmarks, Handshake Access reports); commonly cited ranges are 1 sourcer per 2–3 recruiters at high-volume shops and 1:1 or lower where recruiters do their own sourcing. -->.
- **A recruiting coordinator or scheduler** — owns the interview-scheduling stack (see [chapter 02](./02-ats-selection-and-integration.md)) and the candidate-experience touchpoints (confirmation emails, on-site logistics, offer-package sends).
- **Fractional recruiters** engaged for specialised functions (a fractional exec recruiter to support VP hires without triggering a retained-search fee; a fractional recruiter for a specific vertical the in-house team does not yet cover).

**Who screens.** The recruiter for the function owns the first conversation. The hiring manager owns the second. The on-site loop is designed by the recruiter and the hiring manager together (see [chapter 03](./03-structured-interviewing.md)) rather than assembled ad hoc.

**Who closes.** The recruiter for the function quarterbacks the close. The hiring manager is the primary closing voice for IC and manager roles. The founder-CEO stays in the loop for exec / senior-director hires. The Head of Talent is involved in complex negotiations (competing offers, comp-band exceptions, immigration).

**Retained-search augmentation.** VP-and-above searches move to retained exec search (Heidrick, Spencer Stuart, True Search, Daversa, Bolster — see [chapter 08](./08-executive-hiring-playbook.md)). Retained-search fees are typically 30–33% of first-year cash comp, invoiced in three equal instalments (engagement, midpoint, close) — the classic "one-third / one-third / one-third" cadence. A per-seat retained search commonly falls in the $80k–$150k range at the market rate for a startup VP, higher for a C-suite hire. <!-- needs-research: confirm current-market retained-search fee structure and typical per-seat range for startup executive hires. -->

**Referral programme.** Institutionalised, with source-of-hire dashboards. A best-practice benchmark is that referral hires make up 20–35% of total hires; a healthy referral programme also has a high "referrer-participation rate" (fraction of employees who referred at least one candidate in the trailing 12 months). <!-- needs-research: confirm typical referral-hire fraction and referrer-participation rate benchmarks from a recent industry survey; the numbers above are commonly cited but should be sourced. -->

**Cost model.** The TA team payroll is now visible as its own line item on the operating plan. External spend (contingent + retained + fractional) is budgeted quarterly against the headcount plan. Tooling includes the ATS, LinkedIn Recruiter, LI Talent Insights, an interview-scheduling product (e.g., GoodTime, Calendly, ModernLoop), an assessment tool for engineering (CoderPad, CodeSignal, HackerRank), and analytics — often the ATS-native reporting plus a data pull into the corporation's BI stack.

**When to graduate.** Graduate to Stage 4 when the corporation is hiring at a rate that justifies a dedicated exec-search *relationship* (not a per-search transaction), and the operating model needs a Head of People (or CHRO) sitting above the Head of Talent.

### Stage 4 — In-house TA plus in-house exec-search partnership (growth stage, headcount 200+)

**Who sources.** A mature TA function reporting to a Head of People or CPO. In-house recruiters own most roles up through senior director. VP-and-above roles are worked through a small set of *preferred exec-search firms* with whom the corporation has an ongoing relationship — not one-off engagements.

**The exec-search partnership.** The corporation typically settles into a two-to-four firm shortlist:
- A large, general-purpose retained firm (Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, Egon Zehnder, Russell Reynolds) for board-visible searches (CEO, CFO, GC).
- A specialist retained firm for the functional need (True Search for engineering / product; Daversa Partners for GTM; ON Partners for board; Bolster for on-demand exec placements). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current market positioning of Bolster, True, Daversa, ON Partners, Heidrick, Spencer Stuart, Egon Zehnder, Russell Reynolds against the exec-search categories. -->
- A boutique firm for a specific vertical or diversity mandate.

The relationship is characterised by (a) preferred-partner pricing, (b) a shared understanding of the corporation's talent bar and values, (c) the exec-search partner sitting in on board-level talent-planning conversations, and (d) an off-cycle "who's out there we should know about" rhythm even when no active search is running.

**Referral programme.** Mature and instrumented — referral source-of-hire is a KPI, referrer participation is tracked, referral bonuses are tiered by role level, and a formal "referred-to-hire" conversion metric is reported to the head of people.

**Cost model.** TA function payroll is a material line item — typically <!-- needs-research: confirm typical TA-function operating cost as a percentage of total people-cost at growth stage; a commonly cited benchmark is TA operating cost at 1–3% of total payroll but this varies substantially by hiring rate. -->. External retained-search spend is planned annually and tracked per-seat. Contingent spend has dropped to near zero except in specific niches.

**When to graduate.** Beyond this stage the operating model is a mature people-operations function; further evolution is about scale (regional TA teams, international TA hubs — handoff to [mod-113](../mod-113-international-expansion-and-global-workforce/)) rather than structural change.

## The cost-and-signal trade-off

At each stage, the corporation is trading three quantities:

1. **Founder time** — the most expensive asset the corporation has at seed, the most leveraged asset it has at Series-A, and a scarce resource it should selectively deploy on senior / exec hires at Series-B and beyond.
2. **Cash** — recruiter comp, tooling, contingent fees, retained-search fees, referral bonuses. Cash spend on recruiting is highly visible on the operating plan and often signal-poor when spent reactively.
3. **Signal** — the specificity and reliability of the information the corporation has about each candidate. Founder-network hiring is signal-rich (the founder knows or knows-someone-who-knows the candidate). A cold contingent-recruiter submission is signal-poor by construction — the recruiter's incentive is to submit as many candidates as possible for the corporation to evaluate.

The operator's job is to spend the *right* mix of these three at each stage. Founder-led sourcing at seed is signal-rich and cash-cheap but constrained by founder time. Contingent recruiting is cash-expensive and signal-poor but scales fast when needed. Retained exec search is cash-expensive per seat but signal-rich (the retained firm has done deep referencing and calibration before presenting a shortlist). Referral programmes are cash-cheap per hire but require an existing employee base large enough to matter.

## A worked example — a Series-A SaaS company with 22 employees

The corporation just closed a $16M Series-A. The hiring plan is 22 → 55 over 18 months across engineering (10 new hires), GTM (12 new hires), product / design (4 new hires), and G&A / operations (7 new hires). The current sourcing model is founder-led with an offer of "we'll figure it out."

**Recommended model.**

- **Hire a first in-house recruiter.** A generalist Head of Talent (title deliberately senior — the person will hire the next TA hires within 12 months). Target start within 60 days of close.
- **Add a fractional GTM recruiter** to run alongside the first-in-house recruiter for the first two GTM roles (VP Sales and first AE / SE), which the founder-CEO will co-source. Fractional engagement terminates when the in-house recruiter is up to speed on the GTM domain or a permanent GTM-specialist recruiter is added.
- **Reserve a $150k contingent-recruiting budget** for the year, held by the Head of Talent, drawable against specific hard-to-fill roles with founder-CEO sign-off above $40k per seat.
- **Adopt a referral-bonus policy** — $3k for IC, $7.5k for senior IC / manager, $15k for director+, paid on the 90-day anniversary. Founder-CEO announces at the next all-hands.
- **Stand up the ATS discipline** — every candidate the founders currently have in email or on a spreadsheet gets migrated into the ATS in week 1. Every open req has a scorecard by week 2 (see [chapter 03](./03-structured-interviewing.md)).
- **Founder-CEO's calendar.** Preserve founder-CEO for closing conversations on senior IC and manager roles; pull founder-CEO out of the sourcing loop for junior IC roles within 30 days of the recruiter's start.

**Anticipated cost.** In-house recruiter total comp ~<!-- needs-research: confirm range --> $180k. Fractional GTM recruiter ~$18k/mo for four months → $72k. Contingent budget $150k (with actual spend likely $60k–$100k if the recruiter operates the vendor relationships tightly). Tooling ~$40k. Referral bonuses assumed at ~$40k across the plan. Total year-one people-ops recruiting spend: ~$500k. On a $16M raise with a plan to hire 33 people, that is ~$15k per hire on the direct recruiting line — a defensible number for a Series-A operating plan.

## Summary

- Sourcing is a *staged* operating model, not a fixed function. Founder-led at seed; first recruiter at Series-A; specialised TA team + fractional overlay at Series-B; in-house TA + exec-search partnership at growth.
- Each transition has a graduation trigger — a specific signal in the corporation's operating pattern (founder-hours per week, time-to-fill, req concentration, exec-search cadence) — not a headcount number.
- The three cost quantities the operator is always trading are founder time, cash, and candidate signal. Contingent recruiting is cash-expensive and signal-poor; founder-network sourcing is signal-rich but founder-time-expensive; retained exec search is cash-expensive per seat but signal-rich.
- Contingent-recruiter fees are typically 20–30% of first-year cash comp; retained-search fees are typically 30–33% invoiced one-third / one-third / one-third; referral bonuses are typically $2k–$15k depending on role level. Confirm current-market numbers before committing on the operating plan.
- The corporation should adopt an ATS on day one even at seed. The graduation triggers between ATS products are covered in [chapter 02](./02-ats-selection-and-integration.md); the structured-interviewing operating norms are covered in [chapter 03](./03-structured-interviewing.md).
