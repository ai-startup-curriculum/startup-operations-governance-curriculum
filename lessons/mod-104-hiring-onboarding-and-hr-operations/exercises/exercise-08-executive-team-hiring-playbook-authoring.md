# Exercise 08 — Executive-team hiring playbook authoring

> Estimated time: **~6 hours** · Related chapter: [08 — The executive-team hiring playbook](../08-executive-hiring-playbook.md)

## Problem statement

Trailhead Networks is a Delaware C-corp at Series-B, 110 employees, based in San Francisco with distributed engineering. The founder-CEO has run the company from formation through the current round. The next 12 months bring three concurrent executive hires that the CEO has committed to the board:

1. **VP Sales** — first VP Sales hire. Trailhead has 6 AEs, 3 SEs, 2 SDRs, and 1 CS lead today, all reporting up to a Head of Revenue (a promoted early hire). The Head of Revenue is a strong operator but is not the person to build the GTM function to $50M ARR. The VP Sales will manage this team, hire aggressively, and own the number.
2. **Chief Financial Officer** — first CFO hire. Trailhead has run finance through a fractional CFO (Elena Advisory LLC, retained since seed) plus a full-time Controller hired at Series-A. The board's audit committee (formed at Series-B) has asked for a full-time CFO to lead the corporation through Series-C, potential enterprise-customer expansion, and a possible IPO horizon.
3. **General Counsel** — first GC hire. Trailhead has run legal through Cooley (formation counsel) plus a fractional GC (a former startup GC, ~10 hours/week). The volume of commercial contracts, HR-legal escalations, and board-governance matters has grown to the point where fractional coverage is no longer sufficient.

Two constraints matter:

- **The founder-CEO is a first-time CEO.** She has hired VPs of Engineering and Product but has never hired a VP Sales, a CFO, or a GC. The retained-search consultant relationship is unfamiliar territory.
- **The board has one specific ask.** Trailhead's Series-A and Series-B lead investors are aligned that Trailhead needs to hire at least one executive from an under-represented background across these three roles. This is not a formal quota; it is a stated expectation that shapes the sourcing brief and the shortlist expectations.

Produce, for each of the three executive hires: (a) a retained-search-partner selection, (b) a search agreement summary, (c) an interview panel design, (d) an executive offer package, and (e) an executive onboarding programme (100-day plan, board-relationship establishment, CEO-partnership norms). Plus a cross-role synthesis memo.

## Requirements

### Part A — Retained-search-partner selection (for each of the three roles)

For each role, produce a search-partner-selection memo covering:

1. **The role-appropriate firm archetype** — large general-purpose retained firm (Heidrick, Spencer Stuart, Egon Zehnder, Russell Reynolds, Korn Ferry), startup-focused specialised firm (True Search, Daversa Partners, ON Partners), on-demand exec platform (Bolster), or boutique / specialty firm.
2. **The shortlist of 2–3 firms** to interview for the specific role. For each, justify why they are on the shortlist against the role's requirements (VP Sales — GTM specialty; CFO — audit-committee-visible search, board interaction; GC — legal-community connectivity, general-counsel specialty).
3. **The consultant reference-check plan** — the search-quality is driven by the specific consultant, not the firm. Reference-check the consultant against 2–3 prior searches they personally led. Include the specific questions the reference-check will ask.
4. **The selection decision** — one firm chosen per role, with a specific reason.
5. **The DEI-alignment requirement** — how each firm's diversity slate policy (their own commitment to presenting a diverse slate) intersects with the board's stated expectation. Be honest about the limits — search firms cannot manufacture candidates who do not exist in the market, but they can shape the sourcing brief.

### Part B — Search agreement summary (for each of the three roles)

For each role, produce a summary of the search-agreement terms Trailhead will insist on:

1. **Fee.** The percentage of first-year cash comp (30–33% is the market default; VP Sales and CFO are commonly at 30–33%; C-suite / GC can vary). Flag as `<!-- needs-research -->` if you cite a specific number and cannot confidently source it.
2. **Instalment cadence.** The one-third / one-third / one-third structure — engagement (paid on signing, non-refundable), midpoint (paid on delivery of initial slate or a defined milestone), close (paid on offer acceptance).
3. **Guarantee.** The period during which the firm will replace the executive at no or reduced fee if the executive leaves. 12 months is the common default.
4. **Off-limits.** The period during which the firm agrees not to recruit *from* Trailhead. 12–24 months is common.
5. **Exclusivity.** The firm has exclusive rights to work the search; Trailhead does not run a parallel search through another firm.
6. **Expenses.** Reasonable out-of-pocket expenses reimbursed at cost; unusual expenses require pre-approval.
7. **Cancellation.** Cancellation terms — commonly the corporation forfeits paid instalments if it cancels unilaterally.
8. **The search kick-off deliverables.** The written position specification, the candidate profile, the competency model, and the target-company list — all agreed with the consultant at kick-off.

### Part C — Interview panel design (for each of the three roles)

For each role, produce a panel design:

1. **Panel members** — the specific interviewers, with role at Trailhead and interview-slot ownership:
   - Founder-CEO (always; multiple touches — an initial framing conversation, a mid-loop deep-dive, a final closing conversation).
   - Search-partner screen (the retained firm's initial in-depth interview against the position specification, delivered as a written candidate assessment).
   - Peer executive(s) — for VP Sales: the VP Engineering, VP Product, VP Marketing (if applicable). For CFO: the COO / Head of Ops, the incoming VP Sales (as the CFO will partner closely on GTM finance), the outgoing fractional CFO for context. For GC: the Head of Product (as the GC will own commercial-contract review), the CFO (co-officer relationship), and — carefully — the outgoing fractional GC.
   - Board director(s) — at least one, and for CFO / GC (both officer-visible) the full board typically meets before offer. VP Sales's board interview may be one board member (the lead investor).
   - Cross-functional interview(s) — a rising-manager or senior-IC-level leader from a partner function. Provides bottom-up signal.
   - Case interview / work-sample — the highest-signal single interview. Design specifically per role (see below).
   - Founder-CEO close — the final conversation, typically over dinner or a long meeting.
2. **The case interview / work-sample** — designed per role:
   - **VP Sales.** A structured "first 90 days" GTM plan presented to the founder-CEO and one board member. Rubric: hiring plan, comp-plan design, pipeline-generation strategy, forecasting cadence.
   - **CFO.** A written memo on Trailhead's path from current state to Series-C readiness — headcount plan, capital efficiency, board-of-directors reporting cadence, audit-committee posture. Delivered as a written memo followed by a discussion with the founder-CEO and the audit-committee chair.
   - **GC.** A case teardown on a specific commercial-contract escalation (Trailhead provides a redacted MSA with a proposed customer). Rubric: risk identification, negotiation posture, alternatives, communication strategy.
3. **Reference calls** — 8–12 references per candidate, mix of on-panel and back-channel, coordinated with the retained firm. Include the specific behavioural-question rubric per role (leadership under a specific hard moment; operating style with the CEO / board; team-building track record; results-shipping track record).

### Part D — Executive offer package (for each of the three roles)

For each role, produce the offer package design:

1. **Base salary.** Benchmarked against the market for the specific role, level, geography, and stage. Cite Compensia / Radford / Carta Executive as the executive-comp benchmark providers (see [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/)). Flag as `<!-- needs-research -->` if you cite a specific number.
2. **Signing bonus.** Whether to offer, sized to cover forfeited equity from the exec's prior role, with a clawback if the exec leaves within 12–24 months.
3. **Target performance bonus.** Percentage of base (commonly 30–40% for VP-level; VP Sales's variable can be much higher and structured as commission on the corporation's revenue goal). Defined performance metrics.
4. **Equity.** Grant size benchmarked against Compensia / Radford / Aon executive-equity data; 4-year vest with 1-year cliff (or a shorter-cliff / monthly-from-day-one variant if negotiated). *Handoff to [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/) for grant-guideline substance.* Include:
   - Refresh expectation — annually starting in year 3 or on promotion / re-leveling.
   - Change-of-control acceleration — single-trigger (rare, disfavoured by investors; note the CEO-specific exceptions), double-trigger (market default), percentage accelerated (typically 50–100% for exec double-trigger; specifics vary by role and stage).
5. **Severance.** On involuntary termination — commonly 6–12 months of base plus pro-rata bonus plus COBRA subsidy (CEO severance often more generous, 12+ months). On good-reason termination — heavily negotiated definitions. Consideration required: severance in exchange for a signed release of claims (see [mod-107](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/) for the separation-agreement mechanics).
6. **Officer appointment.** Whether the role is a corporate officer (VP Sales — likely; CFO — yes, officer with signing authority for finance scope; GC — yes, officer with signing authority for legal scope). Board consent required to appoint. Indemnification agreement (DGCL § 145) executed at start. D&O tower notified.
7. **Board seat.** Rare for non-CEO exec hires; note whether the CFO or GC has any board-observer role (typical practice is no; audit-committee support is a common CFO role but not a board seat).
8. **Contingent conditions.** Background check (see [chapter 04](../04-reference-and-background-checks.md) — the FCRA process applies to executives, and reference checks are deeper), reference checks completed, I-9 completed. Every offer letter is contingent.

### Part E — Executive onboarding programme (for each of the three roles)

For each role, produce the executive onboarding programme:

1. **The 100-day plan.** Structure per the chapter (first 30 days — listen and diagnose; days 30–60 — hypothesis and plan; days 60–100 — first structural moves). Include:
   - **VP Sales.** 1:1s with every AE, SE, SDR, CS lead; diagnostic of pipeline, comp plan, close cadence, forecast accuracy; hypothesis and plan on team, priorities, tooling, hiring; first structural moves.
   - **CFO.** 1:1s with the audit-committee chair, the outgoing fractional CFO, the controller, key operating leaders; diagnostic of financial-close cadence, board-reporting quality, capital-efficiency posture; hypothesis and plan on team, controls, systems, Series-C readiness; first structural moves.
   - **GC.** 1:1s with formation counsel (Cooley), the outgoing fractional GC, key operating leaders; diagnostic of commercial-contract volume, HR-legal escalation pattern, board-governance posture; hypothesis and plan on team (if any hires within 100 days), outside-counsel relationships, commercial-terms library; first structural moves.
2. **The board-relationship establishment.** 1:1s with each director in first 30 days; first board presentation at the first board meeting after start (day ~30–45 depending on board cadence). Board-committee involvement — CFO to audit committee (required); GC to nominating-and-governance committee (typical); VP Sales generally no committee.
3. **The CEO-partnership norms.** Weekly 1:1 (60 minutes, agenda co-owned); working-style norms (Slack DM / email / quick calls / on-demand); escalation pattern; executive-team operating cadence; day-90 structured CEO-partnership review.
4. **New-hire NPS overlay.** The exec still gets the day-14 / day-30 / day-60 / day-90 new-hire NPS (see [chapter 06](../06-first-90-day-onboarding.md)) — the exec onboarding programme sits on top of, not instead of, the general 90-day programme.

### Part F — Cross-role synthesis memo

Produce a 2–3 page memo for the founder-CEO covering:

1. **Search-firm relationships.** How the three retained-search engagements collectively shape Trailhead's exec-search relationship posture — a single firm for all three (rare and usually not right — different specialties), a preferred-partner shortlist that Trailhead maintains going forward (the chapter's Stage-4 target-state), or three one-off engagements.
2. **Total cost.** The total retained-search spend across the three roles. Flag as `<!-- needs-research -->`; show the model.
3. **Board sequencing.** Which of the three hires goes first, second, third — with reasoning. Consider whether the CFO should be in place before Series-C fundraising begins, whether the VP Sales should be in place before the next revenue quarter closes, and whether the GC should be in place before a specific board-visible event.
4. **The DEI-alignment posture.** How Trailhead's search briefs to the three firms operationalise the board's stated expectation without becoming a formal quota. What the corporation does if the shortlists across all three roles do not deliver.
5. **The founder-CEO's own development.** Given that this is her first VP Sales / CFO / GC hire, the specific supports the CEO puts in place — an outside executive coach; a board mentor for each hire; the CFO / GC / VP Sales peer network among portfolio-company CEOs.
6. **The 12-month risk register.** The three-to-five most-likely failure modes across the three searches (a bad search consultant; a bad offer misaligned with the market; a bad first 100 days for the wrong CEO-partnership norm; a candidate reneges at the offer stage; a wrong hire that requires severance and re-engagement within 12 months). For each, the mitigation.

## Starter guidance

- Chapter 08 is the primary reference. The exec-search-partner selection, retained-search fee structure, exec-interview panel design, offer package, and executive onboarding programme are all covered.
- The chapter's worked example (Series-B VP Engineering hire) is analogous in structure. Trailhead has three concurrent hires; the exercise stresses cross-role synthesis.
- Do not manufacture retained-search fee ranges, executive-comp benchmarks, or firm positioning. Where you cite specifics, either take from chapter 08 or flag `<!-- needs-research -->`.
- The equity-package mechanics (grant size, refresh, acceleration) are the substance of [mod-105](../../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); this exercise designs the *hiring-process* mechanics. Do not draft the grant-guideline substance; identify what needs mod-105 review.
- The comp-benchmarking methodology is [mod-106](../../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/); this exercise names the benchmark sources and flags the specific numbers as needing mod-106 methodology.
- The severance-and-separation-agreement mechanics are [mod-107](../../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/); this exercise names the severance structure and flags the separation-agreement substance as mod-107 territory.
- The D&O coverage confirmation is [mod-112](../../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/); this exercise names the requirement and defers substance.
- The founder-CEO's first-time-CEO context matters. A first-time-CEO exec search that skips consultant-reference-checking, that under-invests in the case interview, or that lets the retained firm drive the offer will produce a predictable outcome. The synthesis memo (Part F) is the place to name these risks.
- The DEI-alignment expectation is real and delicate. Search briefs to firms can explicitly ask for a diverse slate; sourcing plans can target under-represented professional communities; interview panels can be designed for diversity. Formal quotas at the offer stage are legally risky. Balance the specifics.
- The board sequencing question (Part F item 3) is not a right-or-wrong answer; it is a judgment call the CEO owns. Structure the analysis, land on a recommendation, and be clear about the trade-off.

## Acceptance criteria

The package is acceptable if:

1. Each role has a specific search-firm archetype identified, a shortlist of 2–3 firms with role-specific justifications, and a single selected firm with a consultant reference-check plan.
2. Each search agreement summary covers fee, instalment cadence (one-third / one-third / one-third), guarantee period, off-limits period, exclusivity, expenses, and cancellation.
3. Each interview panel includes the founder-CEO (multiple touches), the search-partner screen, peer executives, at least one board director, at least one cross-functional interview, a case / work-sample interview, and a founder-CEO closing conversation.
4. Each case / work-sample interview has a specific prompt and rubric — the VP Sales's first-90-day GTM plan, the CFO's Series-C-readiness memo, the GC's commercial-contract teardown.
5. Reference-call posture (8–12 references, mix of on-panel and back-channel) is specific for each role.
6. Each offer package addresses base, signing bonus, target bonus, equity (with change-of-control acceleration mechanics), severance, officer appointment, indemnification, D&O, and contingent conditions.
7. Each onboarding programme has a 100-day plan (30 / 60 / 100 structure), a board-relationship-establishment cadence, and CEO-partnership norms.
8. The CFO onboarding explicitly includes audit-committee involvement; the GC onboarding includes nominating-and-governance-committee involvement.
9. The cross-role synthesis memo (Part F) covers search-firm relationships, total cost, board sequencing, DEI alignment, founder-CEO development, and a 12-month risk register.
10. The board-sequencing recommendation (Part F item 3) picks a specific order and defends it.
11. The equity, comp, severance, and D&O substance is deferred to mod-105 / mod-106 / mod-107 / mod-112 as appropriate, with the deferrals named explicitly.
12. Every market number cited (retained-search fee, exec-comp benchmark, offer-package specifics) is either taken from chapter 08 or flagged with `<!-- needs-research -->`.
13. Nothing left as `[TBD]` or `[FILL IN]`.
