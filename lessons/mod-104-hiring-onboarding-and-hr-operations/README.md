# mod-104 — Hiring, Onboarding & HR Operations

> Sourcing, interviewing, I-9 / E-Verify, and the HRIS / PEO stack — the operating layer that turns a headcount plan into a working workforce.

**Track:** Startup Operations & Governance (level 50) · **Stage:** SEED / SERIES-A / SERIES-B · **Pillar:** people-ops · **Hours:** 30

## Why this module exists

[mod-101](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/) stood up the entity. [mod-102](../mod-102-founding-team-legal-architecture/) authored the founder-side legal architecture. [mod-103](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/) authored the *employee-facing legal architecture* — the offer letter, the PIIA and NDA suite, the FLSA classification, the non-compete-and-alternatives clause pack, the arbitration-and-class-action-waiver. This module owns everything about *finding, deciding on, hiring, and onboarding* the people those documents govern.

The employment-law layer only fires *after* a candidate has been sourced, screened, interviewed, and offered. Everything upstream of the offer letter — the sourcing operating model, the ATS, the interview panel, the structured scorecard, the reference and background check, the I-9, the day-one laptop, the benefits-enrollment window, the 30/60/90-day check-ins, the HRIS or PEO the payroll runs through — belongs to this module.

Get the operating layer right and the corporation hires people who succeed, retains the people it hires, and passes a Series-A / Series-B people-ops diligence readback (structured interviewing on record, I-9s complete and on file, PEO or HRIS configured with a clean audit trail). Get it wrong and the corporation ships slow, mis-hires expensively, invites disparate-impact claims from unstructured hiring, fails an ICE Form I-9 audit, discovers on the day of a PEO-to-HRIS migration that the payroll data does not reconcile, or loses an executive hire because the exec-search-partner engagement was structured with the wrong incentives.

The module is also where **cost decisions are large and quiet**. A contingent recruiter fee at 20–30% of first-year cash on a $180k role is $36k–$54k. A retained executive search at the $80k–$150k per-seat range is a board-visible number. A PEO's benefits pass-through at seed removes months of work but ties the corporation to that PEO's benefits broker and payroll rails; the PEO-to-HRIS migration at Series-B is a full-quarter project. Every one of these decisions has a graduation trigger, and this module teaches the operator how to see the trigger before it becomes an incident.

## What "done" looks like

By the end of this module you can, for a hypothetical hiring plan across multiple stages, produce and defend:

1. A staged sourcing operating model — who sources at pre-seed vs. Series-A vs. Series-B vs. growth, with the recruiter cost model, referral-programme design, and sourcer / recruiter ratio benchmarks the plan assumes.
2. An ATS selection decision with a defensible graduation trigger — Ashby vs. Greenhouse vs. Lever vs. Workable at the current stage, plus the integration wiring into the HRIS, LinkedIn Recruiter, LI Talent Insights, and the interview-scheduling stack.
3. A structured interview kit for a specific role — job-scorecard architecture, panel design, competency-anchored questions, interviewer-training playbook, and debrief-and-consensus norm.
4. A reference-check playbook and a background-check operating policy — the FCRA disclosure and authorization forms, the adverse-action letter templates, the ban-the-box / fair-chance-act analysis for the corporation's hiring footprint, and the international overlay.
5. An I-9 and E-Verify operating procedure — the intake form, the three-business-day rule enforcement, the acceptable-documents list, the retention schedule, the reverification triggers, and the state-specific E-Verify overlay.
6. A first-day / first-week / first-90-day onboarding programme with pre-boarding communications, laptop / SSO provisioning, benefits-enrollment window, PIIA / handbook sign-off, buddy assignment, 30 / 60 / 90 check-ins, and a new-hire-NPS instrument.
7. A PEO-to-HRIS graduation decision memo — the trigger analysis, the target-state HRIS selection, the migration project plan, and the benefits-broker selection.
8. An executive-team hiring playbook for a specific role — retained-search partner selection with fee structure, exec-interview panel design, exec-offer package (cash + equity + performance + severance + acceleration), and 100-day plan.
9. A defensible ownership-boundary map for every question the head of people / COO / GC will encounter — "does that belong here or in mod-103 / mod-105 / mod-106 / mod-107 / mod-113 / GTM / CTO?"
