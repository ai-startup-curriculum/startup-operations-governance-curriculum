## Immigration and work authorisation (Form I-9 and E-Verify)

- **Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 (IRCA)** — 8 U.S.C. § 1324a (employer verification obligation and unauthorised-hire prohibition).
- **8 C.F.R. § 274a** — implementing regulations for employer verification.
  - **§ 274a.2** — Form I-9 completion, retention, and electronic-storage requirements.
  - **§ 274a.2(b)(2)(ii)** — three-business-day response window for a Notice of Inspection.
  - **§ 274a.2(e)** — electronic-storage compliance requirements.
  - **§ 274a.10** — civil penalty schedule (indexed for inflation via the DHS Federal Civil Penalties Inflation Adjustment Act — verify the current-year figures). <!-- needs-research: confirm the current I-9 civil penalty schedule as most recently published by DHS. -->
- **USCIS Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification.** https://www.uscis.gov/i-9
- **USCIS Handbook for Employers, M-274** — the operational reference on I-9 completion, acceptable documents, edge cases, error correction, and reverification. https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central/handbook-for-employers-m-274
- **DHS "Alternative Procedure" for remote document examination** — announced at 88 Fed. Reg. 47990 (July 25, 2023); available to qualifying E-Verify-enrolled employers in good standing. <!-- needs-research: confirm current eligibility criteria and any subsequent DHS guidance amending the procedure. -->
- **E-Verify** — https://www.e-verify.gov
  - **E-Verify Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** — the employer-participation agreement; defines the tentative-nonconfirmation (TNC) process, poster requirements, and prohibited practices (no pre-hire prescreening, no selective use, no reverification of green cards).
  - **E-Verify User Manual for Employers.** https://www.e-verify.gov/employers/employer-resources
- **FAR 52.222-54** — Federal Acquisition Regulation Employment Eligibility Verification clause (mandatory E-Verify for federal contractors and subcontractors). 48 C.F.R. § 52.222-54.
- **State E-Verify mandates** (verify current statutory text and any amendments before quoting scope thresholds):
  - **Alabama** — Beason-Hammon Alabama Taxpayer and Citizen Protection Act (Ala. Code § 31-13-15).
  - **Arizona** — Legal Arizona Workers Act (Ariz. Rev. Stat. § 23-214).
  - **Florida** — Fla. Stat. § 448.095 (private employers with 25+ employees for hires on or after July 1, 2023).
  - **Georgia** — O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6 and related provisions.
  - **Mississippi** — Mississippi Employment Protection Act (Miss. Code § 71-11-3).
  - **Missouri** — Mo. Rev. Stat. § 285.530 (state contractors).
  - **North Carolina** — N.C. Gen. Stat. § 64-25 et seq. (private employers with 25+ employees).
  - **South Carolina** — S.C. Code § 41-8-20 (all employers).
  - **Tennessee** — Tenn. Code § 50-1-703 (varies by employer size).

## Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) and consumer-report background checks

- **Fair Credit Reporting Act** — 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.
  - **§ 1681b(b)(2)(A)(i)** — standalone-disclosure requirement.
  - **§ 1681b(b)(2)(A)(ii)** — written-authorisation requirement.
  - **§ 1681b(b)(1)** — employer certification to the CRA.
  - **§ 1681b(b)(3)** — pre-adverse-action requirements.
  - **§ 1681m(a)** — adverse-action notice content requirements.
  - **§ 1681n and § 1681o** — statutory and actual damages for willful and negligent violations.
- **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)** — "A Summary of Your Rights Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act." https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/ <!-- needs-research: confirm the current version and correct URL for the CFPB Summary of Rights document required in pre-adverse and adverse-action packets. -->
- **FTC business guidance — "Using Consumer Reports: What Employers Need to Know."** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/using-consumer-reports-what-employers-need-know
- **State-analogue FCRAs** (verify current text before drafting jurisdiction-specific templates):
  - **California ICRAA** — Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act, Cal. Civ. Code § 1786 et seq.
  - **California CCRAA** — Consumer Credit Reporting Agencies Act, Cal. Civ. Code § 1785 et seq.
  - **New York** — NY Gen. Bus. Law Article 25.
  - **Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, Washington** — state-specific consumer-reporting statutes and adverse-action requirements.
- **Leading FCRA standalone-disclosure decisions** — the *Syed v. M-I, LLC*, 853 F.3d 492 (9th Cir. 2017) line established that extraneous information (including a liability waiver) in the disclosure document is a willful violation as a matter of law; subsequent circuit and district decisions have refined the "extraneous" analysis. <!-- needs-research: confirm the current controlling authority for standalone-disclosure and pre-adverse-action-timing claims in the corporation's operating jurisdictions before drafting a compliance policy. -->

## Interviewing and hiring-science references

- **Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter, J. E. (1998).** "The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology." *Psychological Bulletin*, 124(2), 262–274. Foundational meta-analysis on the predictive validity of hiring methods (structured interview, work sample, cognitive ability, integrity test).
- **Schmidt, F. L., Oh, I. S., & Shaffer, J. A. (2016).** Updated Schmidt-Hunter meta-analysis (unpublished / working paper form).
- **Sackett, P. R., Zhang, C., Berry, C. M., & Lievens, F. (2022).** "Revisiting meta-analytic estimates of validity in personnel selection: Addressing systematic overcorrection for restriction of range." *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 107(11), 2040–2068. Re-estimates several of the Schmidt-Hunter validity coefficients.
- **U.S. Office of Personnel Management, "Structured Interviews: A Practical Guide."** https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/guide.pdf
- **Google re:Work — Guide to Structured Interviewing.** https://rework.withgoogle.com/guides/hire-use-structured-interviewing/
- **Smart, G. & Street, R. (2008).** *Who: The A Method for Hiring.* Ballantine. Popularised the "scorecard" and "outcomes-first" framing used across venture-backed startups.
