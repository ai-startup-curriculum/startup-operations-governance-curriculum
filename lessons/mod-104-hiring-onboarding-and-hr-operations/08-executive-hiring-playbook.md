# 8. The executive-team hiring playbook

> An IC hire is a talent decision. A VP hire is a talent decision plus a comp package, plus a search-firm engagement, plus a board conversation, plus a 100-day plan. Treat it like the operating change that it is.

## Motivation

Every executive hire — VP Engineering, VP Product, VP Sales, VP Marketing, CFO, GC, CPO — is a discontinuous event in the corporation's operating history. The specific person hired reshapes the function, the operating cadences, the culture of that side of the company, and the CEO's own bandwidth. A great exec hire is one of the highest-return moves the corporation can make. A bad exec hire is a two-to-three-quarter setback plus a severance package plus a re-search.

The mechanics of hiring an executive are also different from hiring an IC:

- **Sourcing.** The market for VP+ candidates is small, closed, and mostly not on LinkedIn. In-house recruiters cannot reliably source these candidates alone; retained exec-search firms exist to work the closed market.
- **Panels.** The interview panel spans the board, the founder-CEO, peer executives, cross-functional partners, and reference calls back into the executive's ecosystem. This is a bigger loop than any IC hire.
- **Offer.** The offer package is materially more complex than an IC offer — cash, equity (with specific vesting and acceleration mechanics), performance bonus, severance, and change-of-control terms are negotiated individually.
- **Onboarding.** The executive is expected to lead a function from day one. A 100-day plan, a board-relationship-establishment process, and a CEO-partnership norm are all part of the onboarding.

This chapter walks the executive-hiring playbook end to end: partner selection, search-firm fee structure, interview panel design, offer package, and executive onboarding.

## The exec-search-partner decision

### When to use retained exec search

For roles at approximately director-plus (typically VP+, sometimes senior director in specific functions), the corporation typically engages a **retained executive-search firm** rather than working the search through in-house recruiters or contingent search. Reasons:

- **Access.** Retained firms have long-standing relationships with senior executives who are not actively looking. In-house recruiters can approach only the market they can reach cold; retained partners can approach the executives who take the retained partner's call.
- **Depth.** A retained search includes not just candidate presentation but also market mapping (who exists in this role at what companies), candidate assessment against a defined competency model, deep referencing (both on-panel and back-channel), and compensation calibration.
- **Signal.** A candidate presented by a reputable retained firm has been through a vetting layer before the corporation ever meets them.
- **Focus.** The retained firm works the search exclusively — no simultaneous contingent submissions from the same firm, no split attention.

The tradeoff is cost. Retained search fees are meaningfully larger per seat than contingent search — a retained VP search is typically in the $80k–$150k range at the market rate for a venture-backed startup, higher for C-suite hires or specialty markets. <!-- needs-research: confirm current retained-search fee ranges by role tier and firm; the specific per-seat cost varies with base compensation and firm positioning. -->

### The retained-search fee structure

Retained-search fees are typically calculated as a percentage of the executive's first-year cash compensation (base + target bonus, sometimes plus other cash elements), commonly in the **30–33% range**. The fee is invoiced in three equal instalments — the **one-third / one-third / one-third** cadence:

1. **Engagement (one-third).** Paid on signing the search agreement. Non-refundable.
2. **Midpoint (one-third).** Paid at a defined midpoint milestone — commonly upon delivery of the initial slate of candidates (typically 4–8 weeks into the search) or on the completion of a defined milestone in the search plan.
3. **Close (one-third).** Paid on the executive's acceptance of the offer, sometimes tied to a start-date confirmation.

The retained-search contract also typically includes:

- **Guarantee.** If the executive leaves within a defined period (commonly 12 months), the search firm will replace the executive at no additional fee (or, in some contracts, at a reduced fee).
- **Off-limits period.** The firm agrees not to recruit *from* the corporation for a defined period (commonly 12–24 months) after the close.
- **Exclusivity.** The firm has exclusive rights to work the search; the corporation agrees not to run a parallel search through another firm or contingent recruiters.
- **Expenses.** Reasonable out-of-pocket expenses (candidate travel, background-check costs) are reimbursed at cost; unusual expenses require pre-approval.
- **Cancellation.** Cancellation terms — commonly the corporation forfeits paid instalments if it cancels the search unilaterally.

### The exec-search firm landscape

The retained-exec-search market for venture-backed startups clusters around four archetypes:

- **Large general-purpose retained firms.** Heidrick & Struggles, Spencer Stuart, Egon Zehnder, Russell Reynolds Associates, Korn Ferry. These are the go-to firms for board-visible searches (CEO, CFO, GC), for C-suite hires at growth-stage / pre-IPO companies, and for board searches. Fees are at the top of the market; the process is rigorous and the shortlist is deep.
- **Startup-focused specialised firms.** True Search (technology executive search across engineering, product, and GTM), Daversa Partners (GTM and product leadership in tech), ON Partners (board and C-suite for venture-backed and growth companies). Focused on the venture-backed startup market; often faster and more culturally aligned than the large firms for a Series-B / growth VP search.
- **Bolster and on-demand exec platforms.** Bolster positions itself as an on-demand exec platform — fractional exec placement, board placements, and full-time exec placements sourced through a curated network of executives. Fees and models vary from the classic retained structure; useful especially for fractional or interim roles.
- **Boutique / specialty firms.** Function-specific (e.g., a boutique for VP People, a boutique for VP Finance, a boutique with diversity mandate). Often the right choice for a specialised search where a large firm's shortlist would be generic.

### Selecting the search partner

For a specific search, the corporation:

1. **Meets 2–3 firms.** Each firm pitches the search — proposed candidate profile, timeline, references from similar searches, and fee proposal.
2. **Checks references on the search consultant, not just the firm.** Retained-search quality is largely driven by the specific consultant assigned; a great firm with the wrong consultant produces a bad search. Reference-check the consultant against prior searches they personally led.
3. **Confirms cultural fit and process.** The corporation and the search consultant will work together for 3–6 months. The founder-CEO's working style needs to be compatible with the consultant's process.
4. **Signs the search agreement.** Fee, instalment cadence, guarantee, off-limits, exclusivity, expenses, cancellation. The GC or outside counsel reviews the terms.
5. **Kicks off the search.** Written position specification, candidate profile, competency model, and target-company list agreed with the consultant.

## The exec-interview panel

The executive interview loop is larger than an IC loop. A canonical panel:

1. **Founder-CEO.** Always. Multiple conversations — an initial framing conversation, a mid-loop deep-dive, and a final closing conversation.
2. **Search-partner-facilitated screen.** The retained firm typically does the first "in-depth" interview against the position specification, delivering a written candidate assessment to the corporation.
3. **Peer executive(s).** The executives with whom the new hire will work most closely — head of engineering meets a VP Product candidate, head of sales meets a VP Marketing candidate, CFO meets a GC candidate. Two or three peer-exec interviews.
4. **Board interview.** At least one board director (often the lead investor's partner and, for C-suite hires, the independent director closest to the function) meets the candidate. For CEO / CFO / GC hires, the full board typically meets the candidate before an offer is extended.
5. **Cross-functional interviews.** One or two rising-manager or senior-IC-level leaders from a partner function. This provides bottom-up signal about the exec's ability to work at a level beyond their immediate peers.
6. **Case interview / work-sample.** For executives, this is typically a structured "future-state" exercise — the candidate presents a 60- or 90-day plan for the function, a strategic memo, or a case teardown that reflects the actual problem the exec is being hired to solve. This is one of the highest-signal single interviews.
7. **Founder-CEO close.** The final conversation, typically over dinner or a long meeting, is the closing conversation. The founder-CEO makes the corporation's case; the exec makes their commitments.

The panel is designed *before* the first candidate is screened, in coordination with the search partner. Every panelist has read the position specification and the competency model.

### References — deep, and both directions

For every serious exec candidate, the corporation and the search partner together conduct **8–12 reference calls** — a mix of on-panel references (named by the candidate), back-channel references (via the search partner's network), former direct reports, former peers, former managers, and former board members if applicable.

Reference calls at the exec level follow the same structured template as IC references (see [chapter 04](./04-reference-and-background-checks.md)) but go deeper:

- Specific behavioural examples of leadership.
- How the executive handled specific hard moments (a major layoff, a strategic pivot, a public-market moment, a board conflict).
- Their operating style with the CEO / board.
- Their track record building and retaining teams.
- Their track record shipping results against a plan.
- What they left behind (a healthier or weaker function than they inherited?).

The candidate also references the corporation. Expect them to talk to board members, prior executives, and possibly a small set of employees. This is normal; a strong candidate is diligencing the corporation the same way the corporation is diligencing them.

## The executive offer package

An exec offer is a designed package, not a template. The components:

### Cash compensation

- **Base salary.** Benchmarked against the market for the specific role, level, geography, and stage. Executive comp benchmarking uses different sources than IC benchmarking — a specialised exec-comp benchmark (Compensia, Radford Executive, Carta Executive) is often engaged for VP+ hires. See [mod-106 — Compensation Architecture & Total Rewards](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/) for the compensation methodology.
- **Signing bonus.** Sometimes offered to cover the executive's forfeited equity from their prior role, or to close a gap the base cannot close. Signing bonuses commonly include a clawback if the executive leaves within 12–24 months.
- **Target performance bonus.** Annual bonus at a target percentage of base (commonly 20–40% for VP-level, higher for CEO), paid against defined performance metrics (function-specific KPIs, company OKR attainment, board discretionary component).
- **Relocation.** If applicable, relocation support (either grossed-up cash, a benefit through a relocation-management vendor, or both).

### Equity

- **Grant size.** Benchmarked against the executive-equity market for the role, level, and stage. Common ranges published by Compensia, Radford, Aon, and others — but every corporation ultimately makes a stage-and-role decision.
- **Vesting.** Standard 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff is the default, though senior exec hires sometimes negotiate a shorter cliff, a monthly-from-day-one vesting schedule, or a specific vesting acceleration on named events.
- **Refresh.** A "refresh" grant expected at a defined future date (typically annually starting in year 3 or on a promotion / re-leveling event).
- **Change-of-control acceleration.** The most negotiated single equity term for exec hires:
  - **Single-trigger acceleration** — acceleration on change of control alone. Rare and disfavoured by investors; typically reserved for CEO or a specific negotiated case.
  - **Double-trigger acceleration** — acceleration on change of control *and* involuntary termination (or termination for good reason) within a defined window. The market default for exec hires.
  - **Percentage accelerated** — 25%, 50%, 100% acceleration. The market default has moved toward 50–100% for senior exec double-trigger; specifics vary by role and stage.

Equity depth belongs to [mod-105 — Equity-Compensation Policy & the Compensation Committee](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); this chapter names the exec-hire-specific mechanics.

### Severance

- **Severance on involuntary termination.** For senior exec hires, a severance package on involuntary termination (without cause) is typical — commonly 6–12 months of base salary plus a pro-rata bonus and health-benefits continuation (COBRA subsidy). CEO severance is often more generous (12+ months + acceleration + benefits continuation).
- **Severance on good-reason termination.** Some exec offers extend severance to "good reason" termination — the executive resigning under defined circumstances (material demotion, material comp reduction, material relocation). Good-reason definitions are heavily negotiated.
- **Consideration required.** Severance in exchange for a signed release of claims (a separation agreement — see [mod-107 — Performance, Promotion & Offboarding](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/)).

### Other terms

- **Board seat (rare, and only for CEO in specific circumstances).** Most non-CEO exec hires do not receive a board seat.
- **Officer status.** VP+ hires are often officers of the corporation (a title with DGCL implications — signing authority, indemnification, D&O coverage). Confirm the officer-appointment mechanics in the board consent (see [mod-101 chapter 03](../mod-101-legal-entity-formation-and-corporate-structure/03-corporate-record-and-compliance-calendar.md)).
- **Indemnification agreement.** Every officer receives an indemnification agreement referencing DGCL § 145; standard form.
- **D&O coverage confirmation.** The exec hire is added to the corporation's D&O tower (see [mod-112 — Enterprise Risk, Insurance & Compliance](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/)).
- **Contingent on background check, references, and I-9.** Every offer is contingent, per the [mod-103 chapter 03](../mod-103-employment-law-and-contract-design/03-offer-letter-architecture-and-pay-transparency.md) offer-letter architecture.

## The executive onboarding programme

An executive who arrives without a designed onboarding programme falls behind their team's expectations in the first 30 days. The three components:

### The 100-day plan

The executive arrives with a written 100-day plan — either produced during the interview process (as part of the case interview) or produced in the first 2 weeks against a template.

A canonical 100-day plan structure:

- **First 30 days — listen and diagnose.** 1:1s with every direct report, key peer, board director, and cross-functional partner. Diagnostic of the function's operating cadence, team, key OKRs, and gaps. No major structural changes in the first 30 days unless the executive was hired specifically to make one.
- **Days 30–60 — hypothesis and plan.** The executive forms and validates a hypothesis about what the function needs — team, priorities, process, tooling, budget. Written plan shared with the CEO. Board director briefed.
- **Days 60–100 — first structural moves.** The executive begins executing the plan — filling gaps, restructuring where needed, revising priorities, retiring things that should not survive. Public commitments to the team about what will change and what will not.

The 100-day plan is a working document, iterated with the CEO in weekly 1:1s.

### The board-relationship establishment

The executive establishes a working relationship with the board within the first 60 days:

- **1:1s with each director.** The executive meets each director individually — introductions, background, expectations, communication norms.
- **First board presentation.** The executive presents at the first board meeting after their start date — a function overview, current state, initial plan.
- **Board committee involvement** (if applicable) — CFO to audit committee, VP People to compensation committee, GC to nominating and governance committee.

### The CEO partnership

The exec-CEO working relationship is designed, not accidental:

- **Weekly 1:1.** A recurring 60-minute weekly 1:1 with the CEO from week one. Agenda co-owned.
- **Working-style norms.** How the exec and the CEO communicate outside the 1:1 (Slack DM, email, quick calls, on-demand). How escalation works. How disagreement is handled — publicly, privately, in writing, in person.
- **Executive team norms.** The corporation's executive-team operating cadence — weekly staff meeting, monthly strategy review, quarterly planning, offsite cadence — is explained to the exec in week one.
- **CEO-partnership review.** At day 90, a structured conversation between the exec and the CEO on what is working, what is not, and what to adjust.

## A worked example — a Series-B VP Engineering hire

The corporation is at 80 employees, has just closed Series-B, and is hiring its first VP Engineering. The founder-CTO is stepping into a Chief Architect / co-founder role and hiring a peer to lead the engineering organisation. This is the largest single hire in the company's history to date.

**Search partner selection.**

- Interview three firms: True Search (tech VP+ specialty), Daversa Partners (broader tech leadership, adjacent GTM strength), and a boutique with a strong track record in AI-infra engineering leadership. Reference-check the specific consultant proposed at each firm.
- Select True Search. Reasons: the consultant proposed has led three comparable VP Eng searches for portfolio companies of one of the corporation's investors, has references who speak to specific rigor and cultural fit, and the firm's off-limits and guarantee terms are competitive.
- Engagement fee: <!-- needs-research: confirm current True Search retained-fee structure --> 30% of first-year cash comp, one-third / one-third / one-third cadence, 12-month guarantee, 24-month off-limits.

**Position specification.**

- Role: VP Engineering, reporting to CEO, peer to founder-CTO (Chief Architect).
- Team: 24 engineers today, plan for 60 in 18 months. Multiple sub-team leads to hire.
- Priorities: scale the engineering organisation; establish engineering-management cadence and career-ladder; ship the Series-B product commitments.
- Competencies: proven scaled-eng-org building; ship-focused; strong CEO / peer-exec partnership; low ego; ability to partner with the founder-CTO on architecture without displacing them.

**Interview panel.**

- Founder-CEO (multiple touches).
- Founder-CTO (multiple touches — this is a peer relationship the exec must be able to build).
- Head of Product.
- Head of GTM.
- Two engineering managers on the current team (bottom-up signal).
- Two board directors (lead investor and one independent director with engineering-leadership background).
- Case interview: a written 100-day plan on the corporation's current engineering-org state, presented and discussed with founder-CEO and founder-CTO.

**Reference calls.**

- 10 references total — 4 on-panel (candidate-provided), 6 back-channel (via True Search's network and the corporation's own network). Mix of former CEOs, former direct reports, former peer executives.

**Offer package.**

- Base salary: benchmarked against Compensia executive data for Series-B VP Eng in the corporation's geography.
- Target bonus: 30% of base.
- Equity: benchmarked against Compensia; 4-year vest, 1-year cliff, double-trigger 100% acceleration on change of control with involuntary or good-reason termination within 12 months of change of control.
- Severance: 9 months base + 9 months COBRA subsidy + pro-rata bonus on involuntary termination without cause or good-reason termination.
- Officer appointment: added by board consent as an officer with signing authority for engineering-organisation scope.
- Indemnification agreement executed at start.
- D&O tower notified.

**Onboarding programme.**

- 100-day plan drafted during the case interview; iterated with CEO in week 1 and week 4.
- 1:1s with each board director in first 30 days.
- Presentation at the next board meeting (day 45).
- Weekly 1:1 with CEO, weekly 1:1 with founder-CTO, weekly engineering-leadership team meeting from day one.
- Day-90 structured CEO-partnership review.
- New-hire NPS instrumented at day 30 / 60 / 90.

## Summary

- Every exec hire is a designed process — search partner, interview panel, offer package, and onboarding programme — because the risk-adjusted downside of a bad exec hire is disproportionately large.
- Retained exec search is the tool for VP+ hires. Fee is typically 30–33% of first-year cash comp, invoiced one-third / one-third / one-third (engagement / midpoint / close), with a 12-month guarantee, an off-limits period, and exclusivity.
- Search-partner landscape: large general firms (Heidrick, Spencer Stuart, Egon Zehnder, Russell Reynolds, Korn Ferry) for board-visible / C-suite; specialised startup firms (True Search, Daversa, ON Partners); on-demand platforms (Bolster); and boutiques for specific verticals or diversity mandates.
- Interview panel: founder-CEO (multiple touches), search-partner screen, peer executives, board director(s), cross-functional partners, and a case interview / work-sample (typically a written 100-day plan). Reference calls are deep and both directions.
- Offer package is designed component-by-component: base, bonus, signing (sometimes), equity with acceleration mechanics, severance, officer appointment, indemnification, D&O.
- Executive onboarding has three components: the 100-day plan, the board-relationship establishment, and the CEO-partnership norms. All three are designed, not left to accident.
- The exec-hire equity mechanics are handed off to [mod-105](../mod-105-equity-compensation-policy-and-comp-committee/); the exec-hire compensation-benchmarking methodology is handed off to [mod-106](../mod-106-compensation-architecture-and-total-rewards/); the exec-hire severance-and-separation-agreement mechanics are handed off to [mod-107](../mod-107-performance-promotion-and-offboarding/); D&O coverage to [mod-112](../mod-112-enterprise-risk-insurance-and-compliance/).
